The Cottage Hutch

A furniture-grade indoor rabbit home for the free-roaming bunny

🏡 Furniture-Style 🐇 Free-Roam Friendly ❤️ Bekah-Approved

The Concept

A painted wooden hutch that looks like a small side table or cabinet, not a cage. It sits in the living room as the rabbit's home base: sleeping spot, litter box station, food & water. The door stays open during the day for free-roaming, and closes at night or when you're away.

The key difference between this and a wire cage is aesthetics. Solid painted plywood sides, decorative trim molding along the base, and nice brass or black hardware make it look like it belongs in the house. The front is framed with a decorative door that has hardware cloth behind it for ventilation.

Build Steps

  1. Cut all 2×2s to length. Four at 36", eight at 24", two at ~15", four at 6". Miter saw or circular saw.
  2. Build the top and bottom rectangular frames. Two 36" rails + two 24" rails each. Pre-drill, screw at corners.
  3. Connect with corner posts. Four 24" vertical posts join top and bottom frames. You now have a 3'×2'×2' box skeleton.
  4. Attach legs. Screw four 6" 2×2 stubs to the bottom of each corner, pointing down. This lifts the hutch for airflow and easy sweeping underneath.
  5. Cut and attach plywood panels. Back wall, both side panels, floor, and top. Screw or nail into the 2×2 frame from outside. Use wood glue for extra strength on the top (it's a shelf surface).
  6. Build the front door frames. Three verticals (left edge, center, right edge) + two horizontals per door opening. This creates two rectangular openings.
  7. Attach hardware cloth. Staple wire mesh to the inside face of each door frame. Mesh faces inward, wood frame faces outward. This is what makes it look like furniture, not a cage.
  8. Hang doors. Mount decorative hinges on the outer edges. Attach cute latches in the center.
  9. Sand everything lightly. 120-grit, knock down splinters and sharp edges. Round over the corners.
  10. Paint! Prime bare plywood first (or use paint+primer combo). Two coats of Bekah's chosen color. Paint trim molding separately, then nail-gun or glue it to the base.
  11. Attach trim molding. Run it along the bottom front and side edges with small finish nails or wood glue. This is the detail that makes it look built-in.
  12. Set up inside. Place a waterproof tray or puppy pad on the floor panel. Add litter box, water bottle/bowl, hay feeder. Leave doors open for free-roaming!

Cottage Hutch vs. Wire Cage

The Cottage Hutch

Painted plywood, trim, brass hardware. Looks like a side table. Bekah puts a plant on top. Guests don't notice it's a rabbit enclosure until the bunny hops out.

Metal grid panels. Functional. Everyone knows it's a cage. Works fine, looks like a cage.
