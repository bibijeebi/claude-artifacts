Chapter 01

The Conversion

Ivanka Trump's conversion to Orthodox Judaism was the event that bound the Trump and Kushner families, gave Trump Jewish grandchildren, and created the personal stake in Israel that would shape American foreign policy.

Jared Kushner's family is Modern Orthodox Jewish. Under halacha (Jewish law), the principle of matrilineal descent determines Jewish identity: it passes through the mother, not the father. If Kushner married a non-Jewish woman, their children would not be recognized as Jewish by Orthodox authorities. For the Kushner family, this was non-negotiable.

Ivanka Trump began studying Judaism while dating Kushner. She converted under Rabbi Haskel Lookstein of Congregation Kehilath Jeshurun, a Modern Orthodox synagogue on Manhattan's Upper East Side. She took the Hebrew name Yael. The conversion was completed in July 2009, and the couple married on October 25, 2009 at Trump National Golf Club in Bedminster, New Jersey.

In his 2022 memoir Breaking History, Kushner described going to Trump Grill in Trump Tower to tell his future father-in-law about the engagement and the conversion. Trump's response revealed his unfamiliarity with the process.

"Well, let me ask you a question. Why does she have to convert? Why can't you convert?"

DONALD TRUMP, as recounted in Jared Kushner's Breaking History (2022)

Kushner explained it was Ivanka's own choice. Trump pivoted immediately.

"That's great. Most people think I'm Jewish anyway. Most of my friends are Jewish. I have all these awards from the synagogues. They love me in Israel."

DONALD TRUMP, same conversation, per Breaking History

Trump then mentioned that Tom Brady was also courting Ivanka. Per Kushner's memoir, Trump asked for a yarmulke to wear before walking Ivanka down the aisle and was "respectful of our Jewish traditions."

In a 2015 Vogue profile, Ivanka described her faith as deeply personal: "I always shied away from it being a public conversation because it's such a personal thing. We're pretty observant, more than some, less than others." She and Kushner keep kosher and observe Shabbat, disconnecting from sundown Friday through Saturday evening. They have three children: Arabella (b. 2011), Joseph (b. 2013), and Theodore (b. 2016). All are halachically Jewish.

How a marriage created an unelected foreign policy apparatus that outlasted every rival in the White House.

Trump's management style, documented across multiple administrations and biographies, prioritizes personal loyalty and family bonds over institutional expertise. In The Art of the Deal (1987), Trump wrote: "I leave my door open. You can't be imaginative or entrepreneurial if you've got too much structure." Chapter 03

The Security Clearance Override

Career intelligence officials said no. The CIA said no. White House counsel said no. The chief of staff said no. A father overruled them all for his daughter's husband.

Two career White House security specialists in the Personnel Security Office rejected Kushner's application for top-secret clearance after FBI background checks raised concerns about "potential foreign influence." Their supervisor, Carl Kline, overruled them. When Kushner's file reached the CIA for the higher SCI (Sensitive Compartmented Information) clearance, CIA officers "balked." One called the White House "wondering how Kushner got even a top-secret clearance." The CIA did not grant SCI access. [NBC News, Laura Strickler, Ken Dilanian, Peter Alexander, January 24, 2019]

White House Counsel Don McGahn recommended against granting the clearance and wrote an internal memo documenting CIA concerns. Chief of Staff John Kelly wrote a separate contemporaneous memo documenting that he had been "ordered" by Trump to grant it. [New York Times, Maggie Haberman, Michael S. Schmidt, Adam Goldman, Annie Karni, February 28, 2019]

This contradicted Trump's own statement to the New York Times in January 2019: "I was never involved with the security" clearances. It also contradicted Ivanka Trump's statement to ABC News: "The president had no involvement pertaining to my clearance or my husband's clearance. Zero."

What the FBI found

The FBI background check identified concerns about Kushner's contacts with foreign governments, particularly Israel, the UAE, and Russia. His family's real estate business carried massive debt on 666 Fifth Avenue ($1.2–1.8 billion). Kushner's initial SF-86 form, submitted January 18, 2017, listed zero foreign contacts. He amended it at least three times, eventually disclosing over 100 contacts with representatives of more than 20 countries. [Multiple outlets, congressional testimony]

Charles Phalen of the National Background Investigations Bureau testified: "I have never seen that level of mistakes."

The Washington Post reported that officials in at least four countries — the UAE, China, Israel, and Mexico — "privately discussed ways they can manipulate Jared Kushner... by taking advantage of his complex business arrangements, financial difficulties and lack of foreign policy experience." These discussions were detected through U.S. intelligence intercepts. National Security Adviser H.R. McMaster grew concerned that Kushner was having conversations with foreign officials that McMaster was "finding out about after the fact through intercepts." [Washington Post, Shane Harris, Carol D. Leonnig, February 27, 2018]

Whistleblower Tricia Newbold, an 18-year career White House security official, testified to the House Oversight Committee on March 23, 2019, that Kushner had "significant disqualifying factors, including foreign influence, outside activities, and personal conduct." She identified 25 individuals total whose clearance denials were overturned during the Trump administration. She was suspended without pay for two weeks. [House Oversight Committee testimony]

Chapter 08

Gaza 2035 and Project Aurora

Before Kushner unveiled "New Gaza," Netanyahu's office had already circulated plans for a free trade zone. Boston Consulting Group modeled $9,000 relocation packages. Tony Blair called it "a once-in-a-century opportunity."

Netanyahu's office circulated the "Plan for the Transformation of the Gaza Strip" internally in December 2023 — two months after the October 7 Hamas attack. Ynet reported it in March 2024. The plan proposed a "Gaza-Arish-Sderot Free Trade Zone," a 132-mile railway connecting Gaza to NEOM (Saudi Arabia's $500 billion mega-city), an electric vehicle manufacturing city in northern Gaza, offshore oil and gas extraction, solar fields, and desalination plants. It envisioned eventual Palestinian Authority "nominal administration" — but no statehood.

Boston Consulting Group's involvement

The Financial Times published its investigation on July 4, 2025 (Stephen Foley, lead). BCG was hired in October 2024 by Orbis, a Washington security contractor working for the Tachlith Institute, an Israeli think tank, to conduct "Project Aurora" — a feasibility study for the Gaza Humanitarian Foundation. More than a dozen BCG staff worked on the project; total contracted work exceeded $4 million over seven months.

BCG developed financial models including relocation packages of $9,000 per person for 500,000 Palestinians to leave Gaza "voluntarily" — total cost approximately $5 billion. The models estimated relocating Palestinians would cost $23,000 less per person than providing humanitarian support within Gaza. They projected 25% of Gaza's population would leave and most would never return.

BCG fired partners Matt Schlueter and Ryan Ordway. CEO Christoph Schweizer wrote: "A few people from BCG were involved in such work. They never should have been." Save the Children suspended its partnership with BCG. [Financial Times, July 4, 2025]

Tony Blair's role

The FT reported on July 6, 2025 that two Tony Blair Institute staff participated in a group connected to "The Great Trust" — a 38-page slide deck titled "From a Demolished Iranian Proxy to a Prosperous Abrahamic Ally." The deck proposed a "Trump Riviera" coastal development and an "Elon Musk Smart Manufacturing Zone." One TBI staffer authored a document describing the war as creating "a once-in-a-century opportunity to rebuild Gaza."

TBI initially denied involvement: "Your story is categorically wrong." When confronted with evidence, TBI revised: "We have never said TBI knew nothing about what this group was working on." By August 2025, Blair was developing the Gaza International Transitional Authority (GITA) concept. A leaked 21-page draft proposed Blair as chairman with "supreme political and legal authority" over Gaza for up to five years. On January 16, 2026, Blair was named to the Board of Peace. [Financial Times, July 6, 2025; Haaretz, September 28, 2025]

Chapter 12

Charles Kushner: Conviction, Pardon, Ambassador

18 criminal counts. A prostitute hired to entrap his brother-in-law. 14 months in federal prison. A presidential pardon. Then: U.S. Ambassador to France.

On August 18, 2004, Charles Kushner pleaded guilty in U.S. District Court in Newark to 18 counts: 16 counts of filing false tax returns, one count of retaliating against a cooperating witness, and one count of false FEC statements. The case was prosecuted by U.S. Attorney Chris Christie.

The witness tampering scheme: After learning his brother-in-law William Schulder was cooperating with prosecutors, Charles Kushner hired private investigators, paying them $25,000 in cash. When they could not find a willing participant for a honey trap, Kushner personally recruited a prostitute from an elite Manhattan escort service (where he was a client under the alias "John Hess"), offering her up to $10,000. The investigators wired a room at the Red Bull Motor Inn in Bridgewater, New Jersey. Per court documents: "In a conference room with an associate present, defendant Charles Kushner viewed the videotape and expressed satisfaction with it." He then had the tape mailed from Canada to his sister, timed to arrive before a family engagement party.

Judge Linares sentenced him to 24 months. Christie called it "one of the most loathsome, disgusting crimes that I prosecuted." Kushner served 14 months at Federal Prison Camp Montgomery, Alabama.

On December 23, 2020, Trump issued a full pardon to Charles Kushner, alongside Paul Manafort and Roger Stone. On November 30, 2024, Trump nominated him as Ambassador to France.
