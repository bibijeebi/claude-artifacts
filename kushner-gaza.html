I

The Conversion

Ivanka Trump's conversion to Orthodox Judaism bound the Trump and Kushner families, gave Trump Jewish grandchildren, and created the personal stake in Israel that would shape American foreign policy.

Jared Kushner's family is Modern Orthodox Jewish. Under halacha, the principle of matrilineal descent determines Jewish identity: it passes through the mother, not the father. If Kushner married a non-Jewish woman, their children would not be recognized as Jewish by Orthodox authorities. For the Kushner family, this was non-negotiable.

Ivanka began studying Judaism while dating Kushner. She converted under Rabbi Haskel Lookstein of Congregation Kehilath Jeshurun, a Modern Orthodox synagogue on Manhattan's Upper East Side, taking the Hebrew name Yael. The conversion was completed in July 2009. They married on October 25, 2009 at Trump National Golf Club in Bedminster, New Jersey.

In his 2022 memoir Breaking History, Kushner described going to Trump Grill in Trump Tower to deliver the news.

"Well, let me ask you a question. Why does she have to convert? Why can't you convert?"

Donald Trump, as recounted in Jared Kushner's Breaking History (2022)

Kushner explained it was Ivanka's own choice. Trump pivoted immediately: "That's great. Most people think I'm Jewish anyway. Most of my friends are Jewish. I have all these awards from the synagogues. They love me in Israel." He then mentioned that Tom Brady was also courting Ivanka. Per the memoir, Trump asked for a yarmulke before walking Ivanka down the aisle.

In a 2015 Vogue profile, Ivanka described her faith as deeply personal: "I always shied away from it being a public conversation because it's such a personal thing. We're pretty observant, more than some, less than others." She and Kushner keep kosher and observe Shabbat. They have three children: Arabella (b. 2011), Joseph (b. 2013), and Theodore (b. 2016). All are halachically Jewish.

II

The Power Pipeline

How a marriage created an unelected foreign policy apparatus that outlasted every rival in the White House.

Trump's management style prioritizes personal loyalty and family bonds over institutional expertise. In The Art of the Deal, he wrote: "I leave my door open. You can't be imaginative or entrepreneurial if you've got too much structure." This made Kushner, as the husband of Trump's eldest and reportedly closest daughter, the most durable power center in the administration.

An NBC News source stated in March 2017: "Ivanka Trump is all powerful." Trump took "the unprecedented step of seeking top-secret clearance for Kushner so he could attend Presidential Daily Briefings." [NBC News, 3/29/2017]

Kushner's first-term portfolio: the Israel-Palestine conflict, the opioid crisis, criminal justice reform, government modernization, relations with Mexico, relations with China, NAFTA renegotiation, and COVID-19 supply chain logistics. No Senate confirmation. No prior government experience.

"Why would you send Jared Kushner to the Middle East when you have Rex Tillerson and Mike Pompeo... You send him? Why? We found out the answer six months after he left office: $2 billion from the Saudis to Jared Kushner and Ivanka Trump."

Chris Christie, Republican presidential debate, 2023

The Washington Post estimated that during 2017, Kushner and Ivanka made $82 million in outside income while serving as senior White House advisors. [Washington Post]

III

The Security Clearance Override

Career intelligence officials said no. The CIA said no. White House counsel said no. The chief of staff said no. A father overruled them all for his daughter's husband.

Two career White House security specialists rejected Kushner's application for top-secret clearance after FBI background checks raised concerns about "potential foreign influence." Their supervisor, Carl Kline, overruled them. When Kushner's file reached the CIA, officers "balked." One called the White House "wondering how Kushner got even a top-secret clearance." [NBC News, 1/24/2019]

White House Counsel Don McGahn recommended against granting the clearance. Chief of Staff John Kelly wrote a memo documenting he had been "ordered" by Trump to grant it. The NYT reported Trump ordered Kelly in May 2018 to grant Kushner top-secret clearance, overruling intelligence officials and his own counsel. [NYT, 2/28/2019]

This contradicted Trump's January 2019 statement: "I was never involved with the security" clearances. It also contradicted Ivanka telling ABC News: "The president had no involvement pertaining to my clearance or my husband's clearance. Zero."

What the FBI found

Concerns centered on contacts with Israel, the UAE, and Russia, and massive debt on 666 Fifth Avenue ($1.2-1.8 billion). Kushner's SF-86 form listed zero foreign contacts. He amended it three times, eventually disclosing over 100 contacts with 20+ countries. Charles Phalen of NBIB testified: "I have never seen that level of mistakes."

The Washington Post reported officials in four countries — the UAE, China, Israel, and Mexico — "privately discussed ways they can manipulate Jared Kushner... by taking advantage of his complex business arrangements, financial difficulties and lack of foreign policy experience." Detected through U.S. intelligence intercepts. NSA McMaster was "finding out about" Kushner's foreign conversations "after the fact through intercepts." [WaPo, 2/27/2018]

Whistleblower Tricia Newbold, 18-year career official, testified Kushner had "significant disqualifying factors, including foreign influence, outside activities, and personal conduct." She identified 25 individuals whose denials were overturned. She was suspended without pay. [House Oversight Committee]

IV

"Jarvanka"

Their opponents were fired. They remained.

Steve Bannon coined "Jarvanka." In Vanity Fair (December 2017): "The railhead of all bad decisions is the same railhead: Javanka." Per Fire and Fury, the couple agreed that if the opportunity arose, Ivanka would run for president. Gary Cohn: she "thinks she's going to be president of the United States."

Chief of Staff John Kelly tried to curtail them, insisting they schedule through him. Per TIME: "For a time, Kushner's power seemed to wane. But nobody who tries to control Trump lasts very long. One by one, his antagonists fell by the wayside, and Kushner was there to fill the void." Kelly reportedly called the couple "just playing government." [TIME, 1/16/2020]

Secretary of State Tillerson told Kushner "that his interference had endangered the U.S." after Trump backed Saudi Arabia's Qatar blockade. Kushner had undermined Tillerson's efforts and pressured Trump to back the Saudis. Bannon, after a confrontation: "He goes from a little boy to, like, this fucking devil." [Kushner Inc.; NYT]

In late 2017, Kushner clashed with Tillerson and Mattis over the Jerusalem embassy move. They warned it would "ignite a conflagration." Trump sided with Kushner.

Axios scoreboard (July 2018): "Jared and Ivanka's opponents have largely been fired (Bannon and Tillerson) or have less sway (Kelly)... They have outlived their enemies. Sometimes we forgot the most stable truism of Trumpworld: It's a family business." [Axios, 7/26/2018]

V

The Middle East Portfolio

Netanyahu slept in Jared's childhood bedroom. Charles Kushner paid him $400,000. The Abraham Accords bypassed the Palestinians. The peace plan proposed Israeli annexation of 30% of the West Bank without consulting anyone who lived there.

The New York Times reported that Netanyahu stayed at the Kushner home in Livingston, New Jersey, sleeping in Jared's bedroom while the teenager moved to the basement. This was during Netanyahu's first term (1996-1999). In Breaking History, Kushner described the night: Netanyahu pulled Great Expectations from the shelf, read half, and asked to take it. [NYT, Jodi Kantor, 2/11/2017]

Charles Kushner paid Netanyahu approximately $400,000 in speaking fees (per American Oligarchs). The family foundation donated $58,500 to West Bank settlements (2011-2013) and $200,000+ to Friends of the IDF (2010-2011). Jared attended schools where "second graders were expected to draw maps of Israel from memory." A rabbi: "Israel wasn't a political discussion for him; it was his family, his life, his people."

The Abraham Accords (2020)

Normalized Israel's relations with the UAE, Bahrain, Sudan, and Morocco. Side deals: $23 billion F-35 sale to UAE, recognition of Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara. The Israeli think tank INSS: "The Palestinian issue — previously considered a prerequisite — was pushed aside." Abbas: "a stab in the back."

"Peace to Prosperity" (2019-2020)

The economic plan proposed $50 billion but contained no mention of Palestinian land, territory, state, or authority, no "refugee," no Jerusalem (per Arab Center DC). The political plan proposed Israel annexing 30% of the West Bank, including all settlements. Released without Palestinian participation. Abbas: "the slap of the century." Kushner told critics to "divorce [themselves] from all of the history" and doubted Palestinian capacity for self-governance.

VI

The Khashoggi Timeline

From the murder of a journalist to "standing with Saudi Arabia" to a $2 billion investment.

On October 2, 2018, Washington Post columnist Jamal Khashoggi was killed inside the Saudi consulate in Istanbul. A 15-member team, including MBS's personal security detail, tortured, murdered, and dismembered him with a bone saw.

The NYT detailed the Kushner-MBS relationship: WhatsApp, first-name basis, private channels bypassing protocols. Martin Indyk: "The relationship between Kushner and MBS constitutes the foundation of the Trump policy not just toward Saudi Arabia but toward the region." The Intercept: MBS boasted he had Kushner "in his pocket." [NYT, 12/8/2018; The Intercept]

Kushner became "the prince's most important defender inside the White House." On November 16, 2018, the CIA concluded with "high confidence" MBS ordered the killing. On November 20, Trump: "Maybe he did and maybe he didn't!" In 2020, Kushner called it one of "a couple of missteps." The ODNI declassified assessment (2/26/2021): MBS "approved an operation to capture or kill" Khashoggi. Six months after leaving office, PIF invested $2 billion in Kushner's fund.

VII

The Money

$2 billion from the Saudi sovereign wealth fund. Its own panel said no. MBS overruled them. Zero returns. $157.5 million in fees.

Kushner left the White House January 20, 2021. The next day he incorporated Affinity Partners. The PIF screening panel (June 30, 2021) cited his "inexperience," found operations "unsatisfactory in all aspects," called fees "excessive," and flagged "public relations risks." [NYT, 4/10/2022]

MBS's board overruled the panel. The response: "This investment aims to form a strategic relationship with the Affinity Partners Fund and its founder" and anything below $2 billion "may negatively affect the framework of the agreed strategic and commercial relationship."

VIII

Gaza 2035 and Project Aurora

Before "New Gaza," Netanyahu had plans. BCG modeled $9,000 relocation packages. Blair called it "a once-in-a-century opportunity."

Netanyahu's office circulated the "Plan for the Transformation of the Gaza Strip" in December 2023 — two months after October 7. It proposed a Gaza-NEOM railway, an EV city, offshore drilling, and PA "nominal administration" but no statehood. [Ynet, March 2024]

The Financial Times revealed BCG's "Project Aurora" on July 4, 2025. BCG modeled $9,000 relocation packages for 500,000 Palestinians. They projected 25% would leave and most would never return. BCG fired the partners involved. CEO Schweizer: "They never should have been." Save the Children suspended its partnership. [FT, 7/4/2025]

Two Tony Blair Institute staff participated in developing "The Great Trust" — a deck proposing a "Trump Riviera" and "Elon Musk Smart Manufacturing Zone." One called the war "a once-in-a-century opportunity to rebuild Gaza." TBI denied involvement, then revised when confronted: "We have never said TBI knew nothing." Blair was named to the Board of Peace in January 2026. [FT, 7/6/2025; Haaretz, 9/28/2025]

Reporting indicates Kushner conceived Trump's Gaza takeover proposal and crafted the remarks.

"The US will take over the Gaza Strip and we will do a job with it too. We'll own it and be responsible for dismantling all of the dangerous unexploded bombs... level the site and get rid of the destroyed buildings, level it out."

Donald Trump, press conference with Netanyahu, February 4, 2025

"I'm a real estate person at heart and it's all about location. And I said: 'Look at this location on the sea. Look at this beautiful piece of property.' ... It could be the Riviera of the Middle East."

Donald Trump, same press conference

Tara Palmeri of Puck News reported: "Trump's idea for the US to take over Gaza was first conceived by Jared Kushner. Kushner was involved in crafting Trump's prepared remarks. The request did not come from Bibi." [Puck News, 2/4/2025]

Nearly a year earlier, at Harvard (February 15, 2024), Kushner: "Gaza's waterfront property, it could be very valuable... from Israel's perspective I would do my best to move the people out and then clean it up." He added: "I am not sure there is much left of Gaza at this point" and called statehood "a super bad idea." [The New Republic, 3/18/2024]

A joint statement from Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, UAE, and Qatar reaffirmed "the continued presence of Palestinians in their homeland."

XI

"New Gaza"

170 towers. $112 billion. 70% of coastline monetized. Two years in the making — since before Gaza was destroyed.

On CBS's 60 Minutes (October 19, 2025), Witkoff stated a master plan had been "mooted for two years" — placing inception around late 2023, while Israel was actively destroying Gaza. They framed reconstruction as a $50 billion real estate play. [CBS; The Canary, 10/24/2025]

Kushner on visiting Gaza: "It looked almost like a nuclear bomb had been set off." On conflicts of interest: "What people call conflicts of interests, Steve and I call experience and trusted relationships."

Project Sunrise

WSJ (12/20/2025): $112 billion over 10 years, "sensitive but unclassified." U.S. anchors at $60 billion. By year 10, 70% of coastline monetized, $55 billion in projected returns. Gaza as "a smart city with tech-driven governance."

The Davos Presentation

January 22, 2026: Four phases. "New Rafah": 100,000+ housing units, 200 education centers, 75 medical facilities. Entire 26-mile coast: 170 towers for "coastal tourism." Behind them: data centers, manufacturing. "We've already started removing the rubble. There is no Plan B."

NPR: no reference to land deeds or displacement plans. No Palestinians consulted. Al Jazeera: the plan "necessitates the total erasure of Gaza's existing urban fabric." Urban planner Alraouf: "real estate fantasy rather than urban planning." UN: 60 million tons of rubble, 7+ years to clear. [NPR, 2/2/2026; Al Jazeera, 1/27/2026]

XII

Charles Kushner

18 counts. A honey trap. 14 months. A pardon. Ambassador to France.

On August 18, 2004, Charles Kushner pleaded guilty to 18 counts: 16 false tax returns, one witness tampering, one false FEC statement. Prosecutor: Chris Christie.

The scheme: After learning his brother-in-law was cooperating with prosecutors, Kushner hired PIs ($25,000 cash), then personally recruited a prostitute (alias "John Hess," up to $10,000 offered). They wired a room at the Red Bull Motor Inn. Court documents: "defendant Charles Kushner viewed the videotape and expressed satisfaction with it." He mailed the tape from Canada to his sister, timed before a family event.

The judge: "disgraceful and reprehensible." Christie: "one of the most loathsome, disgusting crimes I prosecuted." Kushner served 14 months.

December 23, 2020: full pardon from Trump. November 30, 2024: nominated as Ambassador to France. Confirmed 51-45. Former French Ambassador Araud: "He has no knowledge of our country. At least he will have access to the president."

Christie wrote that Kushner ordered Christie's own firing from the transition team "as revenge for what Christie had done to Kushner's father." [DOJ; Christie memoir]
