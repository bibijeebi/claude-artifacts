The Goal

The roof is the aquifer

The goal is water that does not depend on anyone. The good news arrives from above: about 40 inches of rain falls on this property every year, and the metal roofs catch it almost perfectly. One inch of rain on those roofs is more than 2,000 gallons.

The bad news sits below. The rock under Bates County is poor well country, which is why the house is on rural water in the first place. So this plan builds down from the sky, not up from the rock.

In 1936 the utility came to the farm on poles. In 2026 the second utility was on the roof the whole time.

Bulletin No. 1

What the ground will and will not give

  • 01Shallow wells here give little. The state geologists report that wells under 200 feet in this county produce small amounts of marginal water, at 3 to 10 gallons per minute.
  • 02Deep wells here hit salt. Past about 200 feet, the water turns too saline to use. More money buys worse water.
  • 03This is why the rural water district exists. The whole region drinks from pipes because the rock underperforms. The house is already connected. Keep that connection.
  • 04The sky out-delivers the rock. The roofs can catch about 75,000 gallons in a normal year. No well on this land will ever match that.
  • 05Do not pay the dowsing man. Rod-witching scores at chance in controlled tests. The state keeps real driller logs for every nearby well on a free public map, and the old well already proves water at 20 feet. That is dowsing with receipts.
Build down from the sky, not up from the rock.
Bulletin No. 2

One machine makes it drinkable

Every source on this plan feeds one treatment device: a reverse osmosis unit under the kitchen sink. The membrane is the machine. The other stages exist to protect the membrane and to polish the taste.

01

Sediment

Catches grit and rust before the filters.

02

Carbon

Removes chlorine and bad taste.

03

Carbon

Second pass. Protects the membrane.

04

Membrane

The real filter. Removes salts, nitrates, metals, microbes.

05

Polish

Final carbon pass for taste.

+1

Minerals

Adds calcium and magnesium back. This is the bottled-water taste.

Membrane water comes out flat and empty. The mineral cartridge puts the good part back, and that is the taste of the expensive bottled brands. It is the only stage past five that earns its keep. Units that advertise seven to ten stages with alkaline, hydrogen, or infrared claims sell words, not water.

The reject line from the unit carries 3 to 4 gallons per clean gallon. On city plumbing that is waste. Here, route it to a garden barrel and it is irrigation.

Declined · The Air-Water Machine

An atmospheric water generator is a dehumidifier with a markup. It costs $1,500 to $3,500, makes 5 to 10 gallons a day in humid weather, burns electricity worth $0.15 to $0.50 per gallon, and nearly stops in a dry Missouri winter. The roof catches 2,000 gallons from one inch of rain, for free. This purchase is declined.

The four sources, ranked

Rain · The Backbone

Scale the collection that already exists: leaf screens on the gutters, a first-flush diverter to dump the dirty first minutes of each storm, and a 2,500 gallon cistern.

Metal roofs are the ideal catchment surface. A full cistern plus normal rainfall covers drinking, cooking, and garden with room to spare.

FEEDS: the RO unit for drinking, the garden for the rest.
2$0

Rural Water · The Grid Tie

The house is already on the district. Keep it. Self-sufficiency means the district becomes the backup instead of the only source.

The same logic ran the signal plan: independence with a grid tie beats independence alone.

FEEDS: the house, as it does today. No change, no cost.
3≈ $240

The Old Well · Irrigation + Data

First it is an instrument: measure the water level, bail it, time the refill, and send a sample to the county lab. The test costs about $20 and nitrates matter in farm country.

If it refills well, clean it, drop a small pump, and bolt a sealed lid over the shaft. That ends the snakes and the fall hazard, and keeps the water for the garden. It is not drinking water.

FEEDS: garden and stock. Never the kitchen.
4$0

The Pond · Bulk Reserve

Garden, livestock, and a drafting point the fire department will thank you for. Thousands of gallons that already sit there.

In a true emergency, pond water through a sediment prefilter and the RO membrane is drinkable. As a daily source it chews up membranes, so it stays the reserve.

FEEDS: land and stock daily. The kitchen only in an emergency.
Decision for the demolition · Part two

The open shaft gets handled the same week the building comes down. Either a sealed lid (keep the water for the garden) or a proper plug (clean fill with a clay seal near the surface). An open 20 foot hole next to demolition traffic is the most expensive thing on this page.

Standing rules

  • Test before trust. Every source gets a $20 lab test before anyone drinks it. The membrane removes nitrates. Carbon filters do not.
  • A landowner can build a well on their own land in Missouri without a permit. The construction standards still apply.
  • If a new well ever happens, keep it about 50 feet from the septic tank and about 100 feet from the lateral field.
  • New wells on this land are irrigation projects, not drinking projects. The geology already voted.
  • The RO reject line goes to a garden barrel, not down the drain.
