The Bottom Line

It's Real, It's Free, and It's For Us

Trump Accounts are a new IRA for kids, created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. The government deposits $1,000 for babies born 2025-2028, the money gets invested in S&P 500 index funds with fees capped at 0.1%, and the kid can't touch it until they're 18. Then it converts to a regular IRA. We can also contribute up to $5,000/year on top of the seed money.

🏦

📈

Step by Step

How to Set It Up

1

Baby arrives, get their SSN immediately

The child needs a valid Social Security Number issued before you can file. You can request it at the hospital when filing for the birth certificate.

2

File IRS Form 4547

You can submit it with your tax return (easiest/fastest by e-filing), or later through the portal at trumpaccounts.gov expected mid-2026. Complete Parts I, II, and IV to open the account. Check Part III, Line 7 to request the $1,000 pilot program deposit.

3

Treasury sends activation instructions

After filing, the Treasury Department contacts you (starting May 2026) with instructions to authenticate and activate the account through their designated financial agent.

4

Government deposits $1,000

Once the account is confirmed open, Treasury deposits the pilot program contribution. No contributions from us allowed before July 4, 2026.

5

Optional: contribute up to $5K/year

After July 4, 2026 we can add our own money. Our contributions are after-tax, meaning only earnings get taxed on withdrawal. We can also rollover to a preferred brokerage later.

Who Gets What

The Three Tiers

That X post was mostly right but slightly off on the details. Here's how it actually breaks down.

$1,000
Born 2025 – 2028
Federal government deposits $1,000 into the account. Child must be a US citizen with a valid SSN. This is the pilot program contribution. This is us if baby #1 arrives before end of 2028.
$250
Born before 2025, age ≤ 10
This is NOT from the government. It's a charitable donation from the Michael & Susan Dell Foundation for up to 25 million kids in ZIP codes with median income below $150K. A private gift, not a federal entitlement.
$0
Any kid under 18
No seed money, but you can still open a Trump Account and contribute up to $5K/year. The account still gets the tax-deferred growth and 0.1% fee cap benefits.

Fine Print

Key Rules to Know

🏛️ Investment Restrictions

Funds can only go into eligible mutual funds or ETFs tracking the S&P 500 or similar US equity indexes. No crypto, no individual stocks, no bonds.

💵 Our Contributions = After-Tax

Money we put in has already been taxed. So when the kid withdraws, only the earnings portion gets taxed, not our original contributions.

🎂 At Age 18

The account converts to a standard traditional IRA. Normal IRA withdrawal and penalty rules apply from that point. Can be rolled into a traditional IRA.

👥 Doesn't Affect Our IRAs

Contributing to a kid's Trump Account does NOT reduce how much we can put in our own IRAs or Roth IRAs. Completely separate limits.

🏢 Employer Contributions

An employer can kick in up to $2,500/year tax-free to an employee's kid's account. Counts toward the $5K annual limit.

🔄 Transferable

Initially all accounts are held with Treasury's designated agent. After first funding, we can transfer to a preferred brokerage (Vanguard, Fidelity, etc.).

Expert Consensus

What Financial Advisors Say

The unanimous advice from every financial expert who's weighed in: take the free money, but think carefully about contributing your own beyond that.

The government's $1,000 is a no-brainer: accept it. For your own contributions beyond that, a 529 plan or custodial Roth IRA may offer more flexibility. The Trump Account's main value is the seed money and the fact that no earned income is required, making it ideal as a complement to other savings, not a replacement.

— Consensus from Vanguard, Chase, Cato Institute, Tax Foundation

For us specifically: with 6-8 kids planned, just taking the free $1,000 per baby born before end of 2028 is guaranteed value. Whether we contribute beyond that depends on how we want to split savings between Trump Accounts, 529s for college, and our own retirement accounts.

— The Benny Analysis
