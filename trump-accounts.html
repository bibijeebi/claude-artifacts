The Bottom Line

Four Things to Know

🏦

$1,000 Free

Government deposits $1,000 for every baby born 2025–2028 with a Social Security Number.

📈

S&P 500 Growth

Money is invested in low-cost U.S. stock index funds. Fees are capped at 0.1% per year.

🔒

Locked Until 18

No withdrawals allowed during the growth period. At 18, the account converts to a traditional IRA.

💰

$5K/Year Cap

Parents, grandparents, and anyone else can contribute up to $5,000 per year per child, starting July 4, 2026.

Getting Started

How to Open an Account

1

Baby arrives — get the SSN immediately

Request the Social Security Number at the hospital when you file for the birth certificate. The child must be a U.S. citizen with an SSN to qualify.

2

File IRS Form 4547

Submit it with your tax return or through the online portal at trumpaccounts.gov. Check Part III, Line 7 to request the $1,000 pilot program deposit (only for kids born 2025–2028).

3

Treasury activates the account

After processing, Treasury sends activation instructions. The account is initially set up with a Treasury-selected trustee. You can later transfer to a brokerage of your choice.

4

Government deposits $1,000

The pilot program contribution is deposited once the account is confirmed open. This money counts as pre-tax (it will be taxed when eventually withdrawn).

5

Family contributes up to $5K/year

Starting July 4, 2026, anyone can contribute. Contributions must be made by December 31 each year. No earned income required for the child.

The Important Part

Withdrawals & Penalties

The money isn't gone forever, but there are clear rules about when and how you can access it.

🔒

Before Age 18 No Access

Withdrawals are completely prohibited until January 1 of the year the child turns 18. No exceptions, no hardship withdrawals, no emergency access. The money is locked.

If the child passes away: Funds transfer to the child's estate or a designated beneficiary. This is the only scenario where funds move before age 18.

⚠️

Age 18 to 59½ Penalties May Apply

At 18, the account converts to a traditional IRA. The child can withdraw for any reason, but withdrawals of the taxable portion are subject to income tax plus a 10% early withdrawal penalty.

What's taxable: The $1,000 government seed, employer contributions, charitable contributions, and all investment earnings.

What's tax-free: Your own family contributions (you already paid taxes on that money).

The 10% penalty applies on top of income tax for any taxable withdrawal before age 59½, unless it qualifies for an exception (see below).

✓

After Age 59½ Penalty-Free

Standard IRA rules apply. Withdrawals are still taxed as ordinary income (on the taxable portion), but the 10% early withdrawal penalty no longer applies. Required minimum distributions begin at the standard RMD age.

Understanding Taxes

What Gets Taxed and What Doesn't

When money comes out of the account after age 18, the tax treatment depends on where the money came from.

Tax-Free on Withdrawal

Your Family's Contributions

Money contributed by parents, grandparents, or anyone else. You already paid income tax on it before contributing, so it comes out tax-free. This is the "after-tax" portion.

Taxed on Withdrawal

Everything Else

The $1,000 government seed, any employer contributions, charitable contributions (like the Dell Foundation $250), and all investment earnings. These are taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn, plus the 10% penalty if before age 59½.

How the math works (example): Say the account has $32,000 at age 18. Of that, $25,000 is family contributions, $1,000 is the government seed, and $6,000 is earnings. If the kid withdraws $8,000, only about $1,750 is taxable (the proportional share of the pre-tax money). The rest comes out tax-free. This is called the "common pot" rule.

Good News

Penalty-Free Exceptions (Before 59½)

The 10% early withdrawal penalty is waived for these qualified expenses. You still owe income tax on the taxable portion, but no penalty.

🎓

Higher Education

Tuition, fees, books, and other related costs for post-secondary education at an eligible institution.

🏠

First Home Purchase

Down payment on a first home. Standard IRA first-time homebuyer rules apply (up to $10,000 lifetime limit on the exception).

🚀

Starting a Business

Capital to start a small business. This exception is specific to Trump Accounts and was included in the original legislation.

The Smart Play

Roth IRA Conversion at 18

Trump Account

Tax-deferred growth for 18 years

→

Convert to Roth IRA

Pay income tax on pre-tax portion now

→

Tax-Free Forever

All future growth and withdrawals are completely tax-free

Why this is the move: At 18, your kid is probably in the lowest tax bracket they'll ever be in. Converting to a Roth means paying a small tax bill now so that decades of future growth are never taxed. If the account has $20,000 at 18 and the taxable portion is, say, $7,000, they'd owe maybe $700–$1,000 in taxes on the conversion. Then that full $20,000 grows tax-free for the rest of their life.

Important: After converting to a Roth, standard Roth IRA rules apply. The converted amount can be withdrawn penalty-free after 5 years. Earnings are penalty-free after age 59½.
