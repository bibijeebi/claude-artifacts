TrumpRx Claims Huge Discounts.
Here's the Real Math.

The new government prescription drug site compares against list prices almost nobody was paying. Here's what the numbers actually look like.

TrumpRx shows discounts of 72-85% by comparing to manufacturer list prices. But drug companies had already slashed cash prices throughout 2025 via their own platforms. The real additional savings from TrumpRx are $0-150/mo on injectables, not the $800-1,150/mo the site implies.

How Prices Actually Dropped

Drug companies were already racing to cut cash prices throughout 2025, driven by competition between Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly, expiring compounding pharmacy loopholes, and growing political pressure. TrumpRx is the latest step, not the first one.

March 2025
Novo Nordisk launches NovoCare direct-to-consumer pharmacy. Wegovy available for $499/mo cash, replacing a $650 savings card.
August 2025
Novo adds Ozempic to NovoCare at $499/mo cash pay.
October 2025
Costco starts selling Wegovy and Ozempic for $499/mo cash. Walmart rolls out similar deal for Zepbound.
November 6, 2025
Trump announces MFN deal with Novo and Lilly. TrumpRx average price target: $350/mo trending to $245 over 2 years.
November 17, 2025
Novo slashes NovoCare prices. Wegovy and Ozempic drop to $349/mo. New patients get $199/mo intro for first 2 months.
December 1, 2025
Lilly cuts Zepbound vials on LillyDirect. Starting dose drops to $299/mo, higher doses $399-449.
December 23, 2025
FDA approves Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide) for weight loss. First GLP-1 pill ever approved for obesity.
February 5, 2026
TrumpRx website launches. Shows prices of $149-299/mo against list prices of $1,028-1,349, claiming 72-85% discounts.

What TrumpRx Actually Is

TrumpRx is not a pharmacy. It doesn't sell drugs directly. It's a website built on GoodRx technology that links you to the manufacturers' own direct-to-consumer platforms (NovoCare, LillyDirect) or gives you discount coupons to take to a pharmacy.

When you click "get this price" for Zepbound on TrumpRx, it literally redirects you to LillyDirect, which has been offering that same price since December. For Wegovy, it redirects to NovoCare or provides a savings card.

Cash-pay only. These prices are for people paying without insurance. If you have insurance that covers these drugs, your copay might already be lower. And per the fine print, Novo requires you to agree not to count TrumpRx purchases toward your insurance deductible.

What's Genuinely New

Not everything is smoke and mirrors. The deal does include some real changes:

Medicare coverage of obesity drugs (mid-2026): For the first time, Medicare will cover Wegovy and Zepbound for patients with obesity and at least one comorbidity. The price to Medicare will be $245/mo, and beneficiaries will pay a $50/mo copay. This is a big deal for the 65+ population.

Medicaid access: State Medicaid programs get the $245/mo price. Most states didn't cover weight loss drugs before because of cost. Some may now start.

Future price trajectory: The deal commits manufacturers to trending cash-pay injectable prices down to $245/mo over the next 2 years. So prices should keep dropping.

Oral GLP-1s at $149-150: Both companies committed to pricing starting doses of any FDA-approved GLP-1 pills at ~$150/mo on TrumpRx. As more pills get approved (Lilly's orforglipron is expected later in 2026), this sets a floor.
