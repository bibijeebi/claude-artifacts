Patio Joint Re-Sand (Ant Lockout)

The brick patio was an ant colony with furniture on it. Pressure washing evicted them but also blew out every joint, so right now the bricks are loose and the joints are open invitations. We refill with polymeric sand that cures into a hard, flexible mortar-lite: locks the bricks, sheds water, and takes the free nesting real estate off the market. One Saturday, roughly a $30-60 bill.

Est. Cost $30–60
Difficulty 1/5

The Problem

The patio is laid in a basketweave-style interlocking brick pattern, and the joints were originally filled with plain sand. Plain sand is loose, drains fast, and stays dry underneath. To an ant colony that is a pre-excavated apartment complex. They moved in, and every rain washed a little more sand out, which made the joints even more hospitable.

The pressure wash killed the party and cleaned the bricks, but it also stripped the joints down to nothing. That was the right move for cleaning but it leaves the patio in its most vulnerable state: bricks can shift and rock underfoot, water pours straight through to the bedding layer, and any ant scout that walks by sees vacant units with the doors open. Refilling with the same loose sand just restarts the cycle.

Why Polymeric Sand Is the Answer

Polymeric sand is joint sand mixed with polymer binders. It sweeps in dry like regular sand, then a light misting activates the binder and it cures into a firm but slightly flexible joint, somewhere between sand and mortar. Cured joints resist washout, block weed germination, and deny ants the loose granular material they tunnel in. It also re-locks the bricks against each other so the pattern stops creeping.

The $5.97 trap. Right next to the polymeric sand at Home Depot sits Pavestone Paver Sand at $5.97 a bag. Five times cheaper, and it is exactly the loose sand the ants were living in. It is for bedding and leveling under pavers, not for joints on a patio with an ant history. Same for the $6.47 Paver Base, that is crushed aggregate for sub-base. Neither goes in our cart.

It is not permanent and nobody honest says it is. A good install lasts 5-10 years, then you top off low spots. It is ant-resistant, not ant-proof. But it converts the joints from "ideal habitat" to "hard work," and ants are lazy economists.

What We Buy

Verified in stock at the Fuquay-Varina Home Depot. One 35 lb bag covers roughly 30-60 sq ft of brick with typical narrow joints; freshly emptied joints eat sand, so plan on two bags unless the patio is tiny. Unopened bags return free.

The Plan

Wait for bone dry. Patio must be fully dry 24-48 hrs after the pressure wash, and the forecast needs 24 rain-free hours after install. Damp joints ruin polymeric sand, it clumps and cures wrong.
Pour and sweep. Dump sand on the bricks, sweep diagonally across the joint lines with the push broom until joints are filled to about 1/8" below the brick surface. Diagonal strokes pack the joints instead of skating over them.
Consolidate. No plate compactor needed for a small patio: tap bricks with the broom handle or walk it hard, then re-sweep to top off the joints that settled.
Blow the surface clean. Leaf blower on low, angled shallow, every grain off the brick faces. Any polymeric dust left on top hardens into a white haze that does not come off. This is the step people skip and regret.
Mist to activate. Hose on shower setting, light passes, 2-3 rounds a few minutes apart. Damp, not flooded. Puddling washes binder out of the joints.
Cure. No foot traffic 24 hrs, no rain 24 hrs, no furniture back for 48. Done.

The Bigger Ant Question

The patio might be the symptom, not the disease. Heavy ant pressure at the patio plus wood dying around the house at the same time is the classic carpenter ant signature. Carpenter ants nest in moist, decaying wood and run satellite colonies nearby, like, say, a sandy patio. If the ants are large and black, or there is fine sawdust-like frass near the failing wood, the main colony is in the wood and the patio was the suburb.

Cheap diagnostic before spending anything: poke the suspect wood with a screwdriver. Solid wood, no frass, small ants = probably just pavement ants and this proposal alone solves it. Punky soft wood or frass piles = add Advion carpenter ant bait gel (~$27) and start scoping wood repair plus the moisture source (grading, splash-back, gutter overflow) as a follow-on proposal. Baiting beats spraying because workers carry it home to the queen; spraying just kills the ants you can see and scatters the colony.

Decision for Bekah

This one is cheap, fast, and already half-done since the cleaning happened. The only real choices are tan vs gray sand color and which dry Saturday. The patio is loose and unprotected until this happens, so sooner beats later. If the screwdriver test on the dying wood comes back bad, that becomes its own proposal with its own budget.
