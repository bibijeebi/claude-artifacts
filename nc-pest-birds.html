NC PEST BIRD GUIDE

What You Can Legally Shoot on Your Property

✓ YEAR-ROUND • NO SEASON • NO LICENSE • NO LIMIT

TL;DR

Three invasive bird species — European Starlings, House Sparrows, and Rock Pigeons — are completely unprotected in North Carolina. You can shoot them year-round with any legal weapon, including a .22 rifle. No hunting license required. The only restriction is your county's firearm discharge ordinance.

THE THREE UNPROTECTED SPECIES

European Starling

Sturnus vulgaris

  • Robin-sized (~8.5 inches)
  • Glossy black with iridescent purple/green
  • Yellow beak in spring/summer
  • White speckles (heavier in winter)
  • Pink legs

Rock Pigeon

Columba livia

  • Largest of the three (~12 inches)
  • Gray body, darker head
  • Iridescent purple/green neck
  • Orange or red eyes
  • Two black wing bars

House Sparrow

Passer domesticus

  • Small (~6.5 inches, <1 oz)
  • Males: black throat "bib", gray crown
  • Females: plain brown, streaky back
  • Chunky body, short tail
  • Loud repetitive "chirp"

WHY THESE BIRDS?

These three species are invasive from Europe, not native to North America. They are explicitly excluded from the Migratory Bird Treaty Act because they cause significant ecological and economic damage:

Ecological Impact

Starlings and House Sparrows aggressively compete with native cavity-nesting birds like bluebirds, swallows, and woodpeckers. They will kill adult birds and nestlings to take over nest sites. Starlings were introduced in 1890 when ~100 birds were released in Central Park by someone who wanted every bird mentioned in Shakespeare's plays to exist in America. There are now over 200 million in North America.

LEGAL AUTHORITY

NC Wildlife Resources Commission explicitly lists these as unprotected:

"The exceptions are the English or house sparrow, pigeon, starling, mute swan, and Eurasian collared dove."
— NC Nongame Regulations
"Pigeons, starlings, and English sparrows do not require a state permit."
— NC Wildlife Depredation

WEAPONS

Unlike migratory game birds, there are NO weapon restrictions for these invasive species. You don't need a shotgun.

⚠ WAKE COUNTY: 300-YARD RULE

Critical Local Restriction

As of December 2022, Wake County requires you to be 300 yards (900 feet) from any dwelling, building, or livestock owned by others before discharging a firearm.

On a typical half-acre lot, this is physically impossible without neighbor permission.

You CAN shoot within 300 yards IF:

  • You have written permission from every affected neighbor
  • Permission is dated and valid for no more than 1 year
  • You have an adequate backstop (2ft thick, 4ft above target)

Other NC counties have different rules. Johnston, Harnett, etc. may be more permissive.

WHAT TO DO WITH THEM

🍖 EAT THEM

Pigeons: Squab is a delicacy. Dark meat, similar to duck. Breast out, wrap in bacon, grill. Cook to 165°F internal.

Starlings: Edible but tiny (~3oz). Need a dozen for a meal.

Sparrows: Barely worth it. Deep fry whole if desperate.

🎣 USE AS BAIT

Good for catfish trotlines, turtle traps, or predator trapping. Legal to use as bait in NC.

🪶 FEATHERS

Starling feathers are prized for fly tying due to their iridescence. Pigeon feathers work for arrow fletching.

🐕 PET FOOD

Cooked meat (no bones) is fine for dogs. Don't feed raw due to parasites.

🌱 COMPOST

If nothing else, bury them or add to compost. Return nutrients to soil.

🗑️ DISPOSE

NC law requires disposal in a "safe and sanitary manner" on the property where taken.

Question Answer
Do I need a hunting license? No. These are unprotected pest species.
Is there a season? No. Year-round, any time.
Is there a bag limit? No. Shoot as many as you want.
Can I use a .22 rifle? Yes. Any legal firearm works.
Do I need to eat them? No. Dispose safely on property.
Can I shoot on Sunday? Yes. No Sunday hunting restrictions apply.
What about at night? Check local ordinance. Wake County: no discharge 10pm-7am.
