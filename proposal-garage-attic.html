Finish the Garage Attic

What We Know

The garage has a full second floor accessible by stairs. It's currently unfinished attic space with exposed joists and only partial OSB subfloor. The ceiling below is drywall screwed to the underside of the joists. It's being used for storage right now, but the footing situation is hazardous: Ronnie already put a foot through the drywall ceiling by stepping off the subfloor onto an unsupported area between joists.

The space has real potential as a workshop, hobby room (Warhammer painting, reloading, whatever), or organized storage. But it needs a proper subfloor across the full area, insulation, lighting, and ideally climate control.

Likely Scope

Minimum viable (storage-grade): Full OSB/plywood subfloor across all joists, lighting (LED shop lights on existing circuit or extension), basic organization. No climate control, no drywall. This is the cheapest path and gets it safe and functional.

Mid-tier (workshop-grade): Subfloor + insulation (batts in exterior walls and roof rafters) + drywall or OSB on walls + dedicated electrical circuit (outlets, lighting) + MrCool mini-split for climate control. This makes it a year-round usable space.

Full finish (bonus room): Everything above + proper drywall/mud/paint + finished stairs + possibly a window or dormer for natural light. At this point you're basically adding a room to the house.

What Needs to Happen Before This Is Real

Measure the full attic footprint (length × width), count joists and spacing, assess existing electrical (is there a circuit up there already?), and decide which tier we're targeting. The cost difference between "safe storage" and "finished room" is probably $500 vs $3,000-5,000+.

From our past chats: The existing hole from the old water heater is a potential pass-through for a mini-split line set. The garage already has chalkboard-painted walls/ceiling and track lighting rails on the main floor. There's a DYMO LabelWriter 4XL for labeling storage.

Decision for Bekah

This is a "someday" project that needs scoping before we can price it. The immediate question is: what do we want this space to be? Safe storage is cheap and fast. A real room is a bigger commitment. Worth discussing before I go measure.
