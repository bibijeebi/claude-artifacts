The Distillation Hustle

Anthropic accuses China of stealing AI capabilities. There's just one problem: they built their models on pirated books. And the real threat isn't distillation at all.

Compiled from public reporting, benchmark data & industry analysis
Bottom Line Up Front

Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have accused Chinese AI labs (DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax) of "industrial-scale" distillation attacks, harvesting millions of model outputs to train competing systems. The internet's response: Anthropic just settled a $1.5B lawsuit for pirating half a million books to build those same models. The distillation isn't actually that threatening either. Chinese models score 4–12% on the benchmark that actually tests reasoning (ARC-AGI-2) while US models hit 53–77%. Meanwhile, China quietly controls 90% of the global humanoid robot market, ships at half the price, and is deploying armed robot dogs and autonomous drone swarms. Everyone's watching the wrong race.

01What Anthropic Is Claiming

On February 24, 2026, Anthropic published a blog post accusing three Chinese AI labs of running coordinated campaigns to extract Claude's capabilities through distillation. The numbers they reported are specific: 16 million exchanges generated through roughly 24,000 fake accounts, all in violation of terms of service and China-region access restrictions.

The three labs used commercial proxy services to bypass Anthropic's China geofence, routing traffic through "hydra clusters" that managed tens of thousands of accounts simultaneously, mixing distillation requests with normal traffic to avoid detection. The targets were Claude's most differentiated capabilities: agentic reasoning, tool use, and coding.

Most telling: when Anthropic released a new model during MiniMax's active campaign, MiniMax redirected nearly half its traffic to the new model within 24 hours. They were tracking Anthropic's releases in real time and pivoting instantly.

02The Hypocrisy Problem

Here's where Anthropic loses the moral high ground entirely. Six months before making these accusations, Anthropic settled the largest copyright lawsuit in US history for $1.5 billion after being caught downloading millions of pirated books from shadow libraries (Library Genesis and Pirate Library Mirror) to train Claude.

The timeline writes itself: Anthropic torrents millions of books from pirate websites to build Claude. A judge rules the piracy was not fair use. They settle for $1.5B covering ~500,000 books at $3,000 each. Then they turn around and accuse Chinese labs of "illicitly extracting" capabilities from the model they built on stolen content.

The internet noticed immediately. Reddit's r/singularity lit up with The Office quotes: "How the turn tables." The Register's headline was simply "Anthropic misanthropic toward China's AI labs," noting the company "built a business by remixing content created by others." CNBC quoted experts saying the boundary between legitimate and adversarial distillation is "often blurry" and that Anthropic's complaints are likely more about preserving competitive advantage than genuine security concerns.

OpenAI is playing the same game. They sent a memo to Congress accusing DeepSeek of distillation while being the defendant in 16 consolidated copyright lawsuits. The US AI industry's position is essentially: our extraction of others' work is innovation; their extraction of our work is theft.

03What Distillation Actually Extracts

The Chinese labs aren't getting access to model weights, architecture, or internal representations. They're getting Claude's text outputs, the same text any user sees. But they're using those outputs in specific, high-value ways:

High-Quality Training Data (SFT)

The simplest play. Send thousands of carefully crafted prompts targeting reasoning, coding, and tool use. Collect Claude's responses. Feed those prompt-response pairs into your own model as supervised fine-tuning data. This teaches a model to sound like Claude on common tasks, learning the formatting, reasoning style, and code structure. The telltale sign: massive volume concentrated in narrow capability areas with highly repetitive prompt structures.

Reinforcement Learning Signals

The more sophisticated approach. Use Claude's outputs as a high-quality reference for building reward models. Claude essentially becomes a judge that scores the Chinese model's own outputs during RLHF training, generating preference pairs that teach the student model which direction to optimize. This is more valuable than raw SFT because it gives the model a training signal to improve against, not just examples to memorize.

Chain-of-Thought Extraction

For reasoning models, the step-by-step thinking process is arguably more valuable than the final answer. When Claude works through a hard problem, that reasoning trace teaches a student model how to think, not just what to answer. This is especially relevant because Anthropic confirmed the campaigns specifically targeted "agentic reasoning, tool use, and coding."

Censorship Engineering

A DeepSeek-specific angle. Their accounts were used to generate "censorship-safe alternatives to politically sensitive queries," essentially asking Claude how it would handle questions about dissidents, party leaders, and authoritarianism, then using those responses to train their model to smoothly deflect away from topics the CCP censors. Anthropic theorized this trained DeepSeek's models to steer conversations away from sensitive topics without obviously refusing.

Key insight: The fact that these labs need to keep running campaigns proves the fundamental limitation. If distillation transferred full capability, they'd do it once and be done. The continuous siphoning tells you the distilled models decay relative to the frontier and require constant refreshing. They're copying answers, not understanding.

04The Benchmark That Exposes the Gap

Traditional AI benchmarks (MMLU, coding tests, math competitions) can be gamed by distillation. A model trained on enough Claude outputs will score similarly on well-known tasks. ARC-AGI-2 is different. Designed by François Chollet, it tests abstract reasoning through novel puzzles that specifically resist memorization. You can't game it by learning patterns from another model's outputs, you have to actually generalize.

That's a 6–19x performance gap on the test that actually measures reasoning. The Chinese models are cheap, yes, but they can't generalize to novel problems. They've memorized the teacher's homework without learning the subject.

The chess analogy: Distillation is like learning chess by memorizing a grandmaster's games rather than understanding positional theory. You'll play well in familiar positions but fall apart in novel ones. ARC-AGI-2 only tests novel positions, which is why the gap is so stark.

DeepSeek V3.2 does score impressively on narrower benchmarks like AIME (96% on math competitions) and performs competitively on standard coding tasks. These are exactly the kinds of well-defined problems you can optimize for with enough targeted training data from distillation. But they represent pattern matching on known problem types, not general intelligence.

The Military Dimension

Unitree's Go2 robot dogs have appeared in PLA military exercises with mounted rifles. The same robots have been purchased, weaponized, and shipped to the front lines in Ukraine by a British company. Beihang University has patented systems using DeepSeek AI to coordinate autonomous drone swarms for intercepting small aerial targets. State-owned defense firm Norinco has deployed the P60 combat support vehicle running on DeepSeek AI. The PLA is systematically integrating AI into autonomous target recognition, battlefield decision-support, and coordinated swarm operations.

The uncomfortable truth: Distilling chatbot responses to train a slightly-worse text model is annoying for US companies but strategically irrelevant. Building autonomous drone swarms and $5,000 humanoid robots at industrial scale while the West argues about benchmark scores? That's the real competition.
