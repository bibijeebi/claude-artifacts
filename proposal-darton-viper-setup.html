What You Can Hunt

At 70-80 lbs, this bow is legal for everything in NC that archery can take. NC requires a minimum 35 lbs for deer on a compound and minimum 7/8" broadhead cutting diameter. You have the Lifetime Sportsman License (disabled veteran), so all seasons are covered. No additional archery permits needed.

On Your Property (0.57 acres, Stamey's Walk)

Squirrel and rabbit with judo points year-round (check local ordinances for discharge distance from dwellings). Raccoon and possum with broadheads during trapping/hunting season. Deer is technically legal with archery on your property but the lot is small for deer. The real deer hunting happens on public land (Harris Game Land, etc.).

Setup Steps

Wax the string right now. It's dry and dusty. Apply bowstring wax liberally, work it in with your fingers. This is $6 and 5 minutes. Do it before anything else.
Measure your draw length. Wingspan (fingertip to fingertip with arms extended) divided by 2.5. At 5'11", you're approximately 28-29". This determines which module setting (B1-B4) the bow needs and what arrow length to buy.
Set the draw weight. If 70-80 lbs is too stiff to draw smoothly, back out the limb bolts evenly (same number of turns on each limb). Most guys shoot 55-65 lbs for deer, which is still way above the 35 lb minimum. Shoot what you can draw comfortably and hold steady.
Install the peep sight. Use a string separator tool to spread the string strands, insert the 3/16" peep, and tie it in with serving thread. Position at eye level when you're at full draw with a consistent anchor. Or: skip DIY and have the shop do it when you buy the pin sight.
Mount the pin sight. Bolts into the pre-drilled riser holes. Set pins at 10, 20, and 30 yards. Sight in at the range by adjusting pins (chase the arrow: move pin in the direction you want the arrow to go).
Buy arrows, cut to length. Go to Godwins. Tell them: "Darton Viper, 70 lbs draw weight, 29 inch draw length, need 340 spine arrows cut to 29 inches with inserts." They'll handle it. Buy a 6-pack to start.
Screw on tips, go shoot. Judo points for squirrel practice in the backyard. Broadheads for deer season when the time comes. Sight in at 10 and 20 yards first before moving back.

Why Not a Crossbow?

We originally considered a crossbow (Killer Instinct or TenPoint Boss). Crossbows are legal during all archery seasons in NC with no special permit. But now that we have a 70-80 lb compound for free, there's no reason to spend $300-500 on a crossbow. The Darton kills everything the crossbow would, and you learn actual archery skills in the process. A crossbow becomes a "nice to have" for specific situations (ground blinds, lending to guests, leaving cocked on stand) but it's not a need.

Decision for Bekah

The bow was free. $175-225 in accessories turns it into a complete hunting setup for every legal game animal in NC, from squirrels to whitetail deer. This replaces the need for a $300-500 crossbow purchase. It's a one-time shopping trip (Godwins or Academy), some backyard practice, and Benny has a second weapon for hunting season alongside the Gamo air rifle and the Remington 511. The ongoing cost is basically just replacement arrows when they break or get lost.
