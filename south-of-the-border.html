The Origin Story

The Original Building, Early 1950s
1949

A Jewish businessman named Alan Schafer had a simple idea: open a beer stand just south of the North Carolina border. Why? Because the NC counties to the north were dry counties. Thirsty Tar Heels drove south to grab a cold one.

The name was literal. Materials shipped to the site were addressed to "Schafer Project South of the (North Carolina) Border". He shortened it.

The Golden Age, Early 1960s
1960s

Business exploded. Schafer added fireworks sales (illegal in NC), a motel, restaurants, and gas stations. In 1964, I-95 was routed RIGHT BY the attraction. The KKK burned crosses on Schafer's lawn for serving Black customers. He didn't stop.

Hollywood celebrities like Joan Crawford and Rita Hayworth stayed at the motel and ordered champagne to their rooms.

Planning Pedro's Pleasure Dome, 1985
1980s+

Schafer kept building. Pedro's Pleasure Dome - a geodesic dome swimming pool - went up. Reptile Lagoon became the largest indoor cold-blooded animal exhibit in America. The 200-foot Sombrero Tower became a landmark visible for miles.

Alan Schafer died in 2001. His grandson Ryan runs it now.

Who the Hell is Pedro?

Pedro is NOT a real person. He's a cartoon mascot born from casual 1950s racism and possibly... Jewish self-portraiture?

Here's the story: Schafer traveled to Mexico to source trinkets. He brought back two Mexican men and hired them as bellboys. White guests started calling them "Pedro and Pancho" then just "Pedro." Schafer turned the nickname into a mascot.

But here's the twist: Academic P. Nicole King described Pedro as "a southern Jewish guy in brown face" possibly made in Schafer's own image. The "Mexican" mascot may actually look more like the Jewish owner than any Mexican person.

Today, every employee is called "Pedro" regardless of their actual race or gender. It's... a choice.

The Controversy

Let's address the sombrero-shaped elephant in the room. Yes, people have called it racist. In 1993, the Mexican Embassy sent an angry letter. Latino-Americans have called for boycotts. The old billboards with "Mexican-Speak" ("Beeg Deal!" "Too Moch Tequila?") have mostly been removed.

The Critics Say:

  • Pedro is a racist caricature of a "Mexican bandito"
  • The whole aesthetic exoticizes and mocks Mexican culture
  • The "Mexican-Speak" billboards were straight-up mockery
  • It's a relic that should've died in the 1960s

The Schafers Say:

  • "We didn't invent Pedro. Mexico sells the same imagery."
  • Alan hired Black, Latino, and Lumbee employees when others wouldn't
  • He helped register Black voters; KKK burned crosses on his lawn
  • He served as a delegate at the 1968 Democratic National Convention
"We didn't invent Pedro. We didn't invent that character. Even the one they complain about the most, Pedro sleeping beside the cactus - I still go to Mexico, I go four times a year, that's something they sell down there."
- Ryan Schafer, current owner (Alan's grandson)

Weird Facts

Ben Bernanke Worked Here

Yes, THAT Ben Bernanke. The former Federal Reserve Chairman had a summer job at South of the Border as a teenager. He declined to comment about it.

You Can Get Married in the Sombrero

The 200-foot observation tower has hosted actual weddings. Imagine saying "I do" with a bird's-eye view of a parking lot and I-95.

$40 Million in Billboards

At their peak, over 250 billboards stretched from Philadelphia to Daytona Beach. They still spend a fortune on highway advertising.

Largest Indoor Reptile Exhibit

Reptile Lagoon is the largest indoor cold-blooded animal attraction in the USA. Because why not add alligators to the chaos?

The KKK Hated Them

The Klan followed Schafer's beer trucks, burned crosses on his lawn, and harassed the family for years - partly because they served Black customers equally.

Drive-Through Pedro

The 104-foot Pedro statue has legs spread wide enough that you can literally drive your car through them. It's the I-95's Colossus of Rhodes.
