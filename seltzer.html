The Problem

Seltzer water is a scam at retail

Seltzer water is literally just tap water with CO₂ gas dissolved in it. That's it. No minerals, no flavoring (unless you want it), no magic. Yet grocery stores charge you a premium for the privilege of carrying heavy cans home and filling up your recycling bin.

At a typical grocery store, a 12-pack of store-brand seltzer runs about $4-6. If you're going through one pack a week, that's a quiet $250-312 draining out of your wallet annually. For water and gas.

Pro Tips

Things nobody tells you

🧊

Carbonate cold water

Cold water absorbs CO₂ much more efficiently than room-temp water. Chill your water in the fridge first for maximum fizz with less gas wasted. This alone can double the life of your tank.

⚗️

Food-grade CO₂ matters

Welding CO₂ and food-grade CO₂ are chemically identical, but food-grade is certified free of oil residue and contaminants from the fill process. Homebrew shops always carry food-grade. Don't overthink it, but don't skip it either.

🔧

Check your model first

Older SodaStreams use a screw-on cylinder. Newer ones (Terra, Art, Duo) use a quick-connect "pink" system. Make sure your adapter hose matches. Some adapters come with both fittings.

📍

Find your fill spot first

Before buying a tank, locate where you'll get it filled. Homebrew shops, Airgas locations, fire extinguisher services, and some welding suppliers all do food-grade CO₂. Confirm availability in your area.

📦

Go bigger if you have space

A 20lb tank costs more upfront (~$120-150) but is dramatically cheaper per fill and lasts forever. If you have garage or closet space and drink a lot of seltzer, this is the ultimate move. One fill could last you 2+ years.

🍋

DIY flavoring is easy

Skip SodaStream's overpriced flavor syrups. A squeeze of fresh lemon or lime, a splash of fruit juice, or a few drops of food-grade flavor extract gives you custom seltzer flavors for pennies.

FAQ

Common questions

Does it have to be a SodaStream specifically?
No. Any countertop soda maker works: Drinkmate, Aarke, generic Amazon brands, whatever. They all do the same thing (push CO₂ into a bottle of water). SodaStreams are just the most common on the used market, which makes them the cheapest to pick up secondhand. Drinkmate is actually better in one way: it can carbonate things other than plain water.
Is this safe? I'm hooking a pressurized tank to a kitchen appliance.
Yes, with basic precautions. Food-grade CO₂ tanks are DOT-certified pressure vessels. The adapter hoses are rated for the pressure. Do a leak test when you first set it up (charge the line, close the valve, wait a few hours, check if pressure holds). Don't overfill SodaStream cylinders if you're refilling them directly. The bulk tank + hose method is actually simpler and safer than DIY refilling small cylinders.
Where do I get the CO₂ tank filled?
Homebrew/home brewing supply shops are the easiest, they always stock food-grade CO₂ and are set up for small tank fills. Airgas has locations in most metro areas. Some fire extinguisher service companies do it too. Welding supply shops carry CO₂ but you'll want to confirm it's food-grade. A quick search for "CO₂ refill" or "homebrew supply" near you will turn up options.
How long does a 5lb tank last?
A 5lb CO₂ tank produces roughly 400 liters of carbonated water (about 1,125 cans worth). At one 12-pack per week of consumption, that's roughly 8-10 months. If you carbonate cold water and don't over-carbonate, you can stretch it even further. At your consumption level, one fill per year is realistic.
What about Costco's Kirkland sparkling water?
Costco sells a 35-pack of Kirkland Signature sparkling water for about $7-10, which is one of the better retail deals at ~23¢ per can. But it's all flavored (lemon, lime, grapefruit) and they don't carry plain unflavored seltzer in bulk. If you just want plain sparkling water for mixing drinks or everyday sipping, you're stuck with flavored variety packs or paying more for brands like San Pellegrino.
What if I only drink a few cans a week?
If you're going through less than half a 12-pack per week, the math gets tighter. Your annual can spend might only be $150, and the $80-110 setup cost takes longer to recoup. It still pays off within a year, but the urgency is lower. The calculator above will show you your exact break-even.
