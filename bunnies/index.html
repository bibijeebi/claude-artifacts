Lucky's Eight

Every kit with its current markings, what those markings say about its genes, and whether it's worth keeping as a backup sire. Born ~Sept 4 to Lucky. The sire is Yoseph or Thump, and that's still open.

8 alive 2 solid black 3 broken 1 chestnut 2 albino

Bottom line: if you keep a buck, keep a solid one. A Darrel is first choice, the chestnut second, and an albino is fine. The three brokens are fryers, not sires. Pick on growth, not color. Color only breaks a tie. Sex them at 6 weeks (~Oct 16) and confirm at 8 (~Oct 30). Weigh weekly and decide at 10 to 12 weeks (~Nov 13 to 27).

One photo could settle who the sire is. The brokens show textbook English broken marks: butterfly nose, eye circles, colored ears, spots along the spine. So one parent carried the broken gene. Thump was solid black and didn't. If Lucky has none of those marks herself (just a blaze, collar and socks), the brokens had to come from Yoseph, and it's his litter. If she does have a butterfly and eye circles, it stays open. A clear shot of Lucky's face and back answers it.

Keeper plan

Ranking, if a buck
  1. Black A or Black B. No spotting gene, the cleanest genetics in the litter.
  2. Chestnut. Same cleanliness, and easy to identify.
  3. White A or White B. Fine for meat, but his hidden genes are unknown.
  4. Brokens: no. Freezer at 10 to 12 weeks.

Growth beats this list. A fast-growing chestnut beats a slow Darrel. Look for weekly gain and a broad, meaty back.
