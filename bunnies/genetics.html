Paternity · Lucky's litter, born ~Sept 4, 2026

Who Sired the Eight

Yoseph or Thump, worked gene by gene. Both bucks are processed, so coat color is the only evidence left besides who was seen doing what.

Bottom line: the genes don't rule out Thump. The original case against him (the white kits were Vienna whites, which only Yoseph could make) died when the whites turned out to be red-eyed albinos. Read honestly, the coat genetics now lean slightly toward Thump, while behavior points to Yoseph, who was the one seen mounting Lucky over and over in early August. The thing that actually decides it is access: if Thump never shared space with Lucky around Aug 1 to 7, it's Yoseph and nothing below matters.

Oct 4 update: at 4 weeks the three marked kits show textbook English broken marks (butterfly nose, eye circles, colored ears, spots along the spine), so the white gene in play is broken (En), not Vienna. Thump couldn't pass En. If Lucky shows no broken marks herself, the brokens must be Yoseph's and this whole page is settled in his favor. One clear photo of Lucky's face and back decides it.

Ignore the two albinos (their Vienna genes are hidden). That leaves six colored kits, and zero of them are blue-eyed whites. Under Thump that's guaranteed. Under Yoseph each kit had a 1 in 4 shot at being one, so six misses in a row is (3/4)6, about 18%. That makes the litter roughly 5 to 6 times more likely under Thump on this gene alone.

Oct 4: the kits' marks turned out to be broken (En), not Vienna. The math is the same with En swapped in. Two copies make a Charlie instead of a blue-eyed white, and zero Charlies in six colored kits is the same 18% under Yoseph, if both he and Lucky were broken. The new wrinkle: if Lucky isn't broken, the En had to come from Yoseph, because Thump was solid. That flips this gene hard to Yoseph.

The catch: this only holds if Yoseph's white really was Vienna. If his white came from a different spotting gene (broken or Dutch), this square doesn't apply and the gene is a wash. The solid vs marked split among the colored kits fits both crosses either way.

Genes alone: lean Thump. Behavior: lean Yoseph. Net is close to a coin flip unless one fact is pinned down. Was Thump ever loose with Lucky or in her pen in late July or early August? If no, it's Yoseph. If yes, it's genuinely open, and a split litter from both bucks is also possible since a doe can carry kits from two sires.

How the call changed

Sept 14 · first read

Kits described as all black and white. No blues gave a slight edge to Thump, a coin flip.

Sept 14 · Charlie theory

Mostly-white kits read as Charlies (two broken genes), which Thump couldn't make. Called it Yoseph. Dropped when the white kits showed zero dark on ears and nose.

Sept 14 · Vienna theory

All-white with pink ears could be Vienna white (Yoseph only) or albino (either). Called it Yoseph at 85%, pending eye color.

Sept 21 · eyes open

Red eyes. Albino. The Yoseph-only explanation for the white kits is gone.

Oct 4 · this page

Rechecked the colored kits against the Vienna square. Missing blue-eyed whites now count slightly against Yoseph.

What still tells you something

Nothing more can come from Yoseph or Thump. The new buck can tell you about Lucky, which tightens the dilute question after the fact:

Breed Lucky to a blue (or any dd) buck. Any blue kits prove she carries d, which makes Yoseph a 1-in-64 longshot for this litter and hands it to Thump. All black across a decent litter says she's DD and the dilute gene goes neutral.

Breed Lucky to a New Zealand White. NZW is cc, so about half the kits come out albino. That re-confirms Lucky carries c but says nothing new about the old bucks.

For keeper selection it doesn't matter who the sire was: every kit carries Lucky's hidden albino gene at 50/50 odds or better (the albinos are cc, the colored kits are Cc two times in three). Expect albinos to keep popping up in this line.
