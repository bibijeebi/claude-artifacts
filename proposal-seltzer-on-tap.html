Seltzer on Tap

Stop paying $6/week for cans of fizzy water. Bulk CO2 tank, used SodaStream, unlimited seltzer for pennies per liter. Break even in 3 months.

The Problem

We go through roughly a 12-pack of store-brand seltzer water per week from Food Lion at about $5–6 per pack. That's $260–$312 per year on cans of water with bubbles in it. The cans pile up in recycling, take up fridge space, and we run out mid-week when we're mixing drinks at the bar. It's a consumable that never stops costing money.

Seltzer water is literally just water + CO2. There's no reason to keep buying it pre-packaged in aluminum cans shipped from a bottling plant when CO2 is one of the cheapest industrial gases on earth.

The Solution

A used SodaStream (or any generic soda maker) connected via a CGA320 adapter hose to a 5lb bulk CO2 tank. The soda maker is just a valve that pushes CO2 into a bottle of water. The adapter hose bypasses SodaStream's overpriced proprietary cartridges and connects directly to a bulk tank that costs $15–25 to refill and lasts 6–12 months at our usage level.

A 5lb CO2 tank produces roughly 400 liters of sparkling water. At one 12-pack per week (about 4.25 liters), that's nearly two years of seltzer on a single fill. The ongoing cost works out to about 1–2 cents per liter.

Grab a used SodaStream off Facebook Marketplace for $15–30 (they're everywhere, people buy them and forget about them). Buy a CGA320 adapter hose on Amazon (~$25–35). Get a 5lb CO2 tank filled at Machine & Welding Supply in Garner (~$25 swap) or Pye Barker Fire & Safety in Raleigh (~$25 fill). Done.

Setup Instructions

  • Browse Facebook Marketplace for "SodaStream" in the Raleigh area. Filter under $30. Confirm it uses the blue screw-in cylinder (NOT the pink quick-connect).
  • Order a CGA320 to TR21-4 adapter hose from Amazon. Get one with a pressure gauge and 60" length. ~$30.
  • Drive to Machine & Welding Supply in Garner. Buy/swap a filled 5lb CO2 tank. ~$25.
  • Wrap the CGA320 threads with plumber's tape. Connect hose to CO2 tank. Connect other end to SodaStream (where the blue cylinder normally goes).
  • Fill SodaStream bottle with cold water. Open CO2 tank valve. Press the carbonation button 3–5 times until you hear the buzz. Done.
  • Close the CO2 tank valve after each use. Some adapter seals leak slowly if left open.
Heads up from Amazon reviews: Some cheap hoses leak at the crimp or quick-disconnect. Wrap all threads with plumber's tape, tighten with a wrench (not just hand-tight), and close the tank valve between uses. If you get a dud hose, return it. The brass adapter itself is reliable, it's usually the hose that fails.

Why This Matters for the Bar

Half the cocktails on the drinks bar use seltzer, tonic, or sparkling water as a component. Running out of cans mid-session means a trip to Food Lion or a flat drink. With a bulk setup, there's always carbonated water on demand. Fill a bottle, carbonate, pour. No cans to chill, no recycling pile, no running out. It also opens up making homemade tonic water and flavored seltzers with syrups from the bar.

The Ask

This is a no-brainer money saver. The upfront cost is under $100 using Path B, it pays for itself in 3 months, and after that we're saving $280+ per year on something we buy every single week. The setup takes one afternoon and there's basically zero ongoing maintenance. One CO2 refill per year.
