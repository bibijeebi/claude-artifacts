Field Guide / Updated May 2026

Metabolic Pharma 2026

A field guide to the obesity and muscle pharmacology landscape. GLP-1s are the appetizer. The rest of this decade belongs to multi-agonists, oral pills, and the muscle-sparing drugs that stack on top of them.

00State of the field

Where we are in May 2026.

Bottom line up front

The single-target GLP-1 era is closing. The next 18 months bring three things: triple agonists pushing weight loss past 28%, amylin co-formulations finalizing for FDA review, and the first oral small-molecule GLP-1 hitting the market. The class that follows is muscle-sparing add-ons, myostatin and activin pathway inhibitors, designed to stack with GLP-1s and prevent the lean mass loss that comes with rapid fat loss.

The generation map

  • Gen 1: single GLP-1 receptor agonists, around 15 to 17% mean weight loss
  • Gen 2: dual GIP and GLP-1, around 20 to 22%
  • Gen 3: triple agonists (GLP-1, GIP, glucagon) and dual GLP-1 with glucagon, 17 to 29% depending on dose and population
  • Gen 4 (combinations): GLP-1 plus amylin co-formulations, GLP-1 plus muscle-sparing antibody add-ons

01The big picture

Six things to know if you only read one section.

Lean mass loss is the unsolved problem

Every modern obesity drug discussion now includes body composition. The simple rule of thumb (about 25 to 40% of GLP-1 weight loss is lean mass) was acceptable when GLP-1s were a niche T2D therapy. Now that they're projected to become the most-prescribed drug class globally, the long-term sarcopenia risk is real, especially in older adults and in people who don't strength train. Resistance training plus 1.6 to 2.0 g/kg lean body mass protein partially mitigates it but doesn't solve it. The myostatin inhibitor class exists to close that gap pharmacologically.

Cardiovascular outcomes data is still semaglutide's moat

The SELECT trial (semaglutide reduces major adverse cardiovascular events in non-diabetic obese patients with prior CVD) remains the only large-scale outcome data published in the class. Tirzepatide and retatrutide are running CV outcome trials. Until those read out, semaglutide retains the strongest evidence base for "this drug actually reduces hard endpoints, not just weight".

The pill question

Injectable peptides require cold chain, syringes, and clinical comfort with self-injection. Roughly half of patients refuse injectable options when asked. Orforglipron is the first true oral small-molecule GLP-1, and if the efficacy holds up in real-world use the access expansion is enormous. Novo's oral peptide path (Rybelsus, oral CagriSema) is more constrained by absorption logistics.

Compounded peptides exist in a legal grey zone

FDA approval is not the same as legal availability. Compounding pharmacies produced large quantities of semaglutide and tirzepatide during the 2022 to 2024 shortage period, and the FDA has been progressively closing those pathways now that the official shortage is over. Retatrutide cannot be legally compounded because it's not approved for any indication. The "research peptide" market for unapproved drugs operates outside the regulated supply chain entirely and has no manufacturing oversight.

Glucagon agonism is the differentiating mechanism

The reason retatrutide and survodutide hit harder than tirzepatide isn't just "more receptors". Glucagon receptor agonism actively raises basal metabolic rate (measured energy expenditure goes up by a real, meaningful percentage), and drives hepatic fat oxidation. This is the only mechanism in the entire class that increases energy out rather than just decreasing energy in.

Dysesthesia is the new side effect to watch

Around 21% of TRIUMPH-4 patients on 12 mg retatrutide reported skin tingling, sensitivity, or tenderness to the touch. Mostly mild. Not seen at this rate in earlier GLP-1 classes. The mechanism is unclear and probably tied to the glucagon component. If retatrutide approves, expect this to be a significant labeling consideration.

This page is informational and not medical advice. Drug development changes rapidly. Anything in clinical trials is by definition not approved, and approved drugs have specific labeled indications, contraindications, and risk profiles that aren't fully captured here. Talk to a qualified clinician before starting, stopping, or stacking any of these compounds.

04The biology

Two pathways do most of the work.

The incretin axis

Incretin hormones are released from the gut after eating and signal the pancreas to release insulin. The two main ones are GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) and GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide). Glucagon, despite the confusing name, is a counter-regulatory pancreatic hormone that raises blood sugar and ramps up energy expenditure. The drug class started by mimicking GLP-1 alone and progressively layered on more receptors.

  • GLP-1 receptor agonism: appetite suppression via brainstem and hypothalamus, slows gastric emptying, increases insulin secretion, decreases glucagon secretion
  • GIP receptor agonism: additive insulin secretion, controversial direct adipose effects, may improve nausea profile when combined with GLP-1
  • Glucagon receptor agonism: increases basal energy expenditure (real measurable metabolic rate increase), promotes hepatic fat oxidation, this is why dual and triple agonists hit harder than mono GLP-1
  • Amylin receptor agonism: separate pathway, signals satiety via the area postrema, slows gastric emptying, complements GLP-1 mechanism

The myostatin and activin pathway

Myostatin (gene name GDF8) is a TGF-beta family ligand secreted by muscle that signals through type II activin receptors (ActRIIA and ActRIIB) and tells muscle to stop growing. Knock it out and animals develop massive muscle hypertrophy. Belgian Blue cattle are the textbook example. The pathway also includes activin A, activin E, and GDF11, which work through the same receptors.

Three inhibition strategies exist:

  • Anti-myostatin antibodies bind myostatin directly and prevent receptor activation. Examples: trevogrumab, apitegromab, taldefgrobep alfa.
  • ActRII receptor blockers sit on the receptor and block multiple ligands at once including myostatin, activin A, activin E, and GDF11. The most efficacious approach but highest off-target risk. Example: bimagrumab.
  • Anti-activin antibodies like garetosmab block activin A specifically and are typically combined with anti-myostatin agents.
Why this matters now

GLP-1 weight loss is roughly 25 to 40% lean mass by some studies, with high inter-individual variability. Some of that loss is biologically appropriate (less mass to support requires less muscle), but a meaningful portion is unwanted sarcopenia, especially in older adults. Resistance training and adequate protein partially mitigate it, but not fully. Pharmaceutical muscle preservation is the obvious next move.

11Oral GLP-1 agents

No fridge, no needle, lower potency, much wider access.

Orforglipron LY3502970
Small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonist / Eli Lilly
Status
FDA target ~March 2026
Dosing
Daily oral, no food restrictions
Efficacy
~13 to 14% mean weight loss
First non-peptide GLP-1 to reach late-stage development. Unlike Rybelsus (peptide sema in oral form), orforglipron is a true small molecule, no absorption gimmicks needed, no fasting requirements. Big access unlock for the many patients who refuse injections or can't manage cold-chain logistics.
Rybelsus (oral semaglutide) PIONEER program
Peptide GLP-1 in oral form with absorption enhancer / Novo Nordisk
Status
Approved (T2D, weight pending)
Dosing
14 to 25 mg daily, fasted, with sips of water
Uses SNAC (sodium N-(8-(2-hydroxybenzoyl)amino)caprylate) absorption enhancer to push intact peptide across stomach lining. Restrictive dosing protocol (30-min fast before, 30-min after) is the price of using a peptide orally. Higher-dose obesity-targeted version filed but uses ~10x the active drug per pill compared to the injectable.

12Myostatin and activin pathway inhibitors

The muscle-sparing add-on class. None approved yet, all designed to stack with GLP-1s.

This is the class to watch through 2026 and 2027. The strategy across all candidates is the same: stack on top of a GLP-1 to keep total weight loss high while shifting the composition of that loss away from lean mass and toward fat. The historical track record of myostatin inhibitors in muscular dystrophy was disappointing, but obesity is a different problem (you don't need new functional muscle, you just need to not lose existing muscle) and early data is far more encouraging.

IBIO-600
Anti-myostatin antibody / iBio
Status
Phase 1 starting early 2026

Post-GLP-1 muscle preservation
San Diego biotech taking a different commercial angle: rather than competing with Lilly and Novo on co-administered combos, iBio is positioning IBIO-600 for after patients discontinue GLP-1s, when rebound weight gain often includes disproportionate fat regain.
Reality check

No myostatin or activin pathway inhibitor is FDA-approved for any indication as of May 2026. The class has a poor track record in muscular dystrophy trials, partly because new muscle bulk doesn't help if neural input is the bottleneck. Obesity is mechanistically different, but expect first approvals 2027 to 2028 at the earliest, and side effect profiles are still being characterized. Bimagrumab in particular has had mild liver enzyme elevations and pancreatic safety questions raised in earlier programs.

13SARMs and androgen modulators

Oral small-molecule alternative path to muscle preservation.

Enobosarm GTx-024, ostarine
Selective androgen receptor modulator / Veru
Status
Phase 2 (with GLP-1s)
Dosing
Daily oral
Notes
Same molecule as the lifter grey-market "ostarine"
FDA gave Veru "regulatory clarity" in September 2025 for the muscle-preservation Phase 2 program. SARMs selectively activate androgen receptors in muscle and bone while sparing prostate and other tissues, unlike traditional anabolic steroids. The grey-market reputation is a regulatory hurdle but the mechanism is well-characterized.
