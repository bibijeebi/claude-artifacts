Homemade Mac & Cheese

Creamy from-scratch mac and cheese using a simple roux and shredded Mexican blend. Better than the blue box, and you already have everything.

Prep
5 min
Cook
15 min
Total
20 min
Serves
2–3
Difficulty
Easy

Ingredients

8 ozelbow macaroni (or any short pasta)
2 tbspbutter
2 tbspall-purpose flour
1½ cupswhole milk
2 cupsshredded Mexican blend cheese
½ tspsalt (adjust to taste)
¼ tspblack pepper
pinchgarlic powder (optional)

Let's Make It

1

Cook your pasta

Boil your macaroni according to the package directions. Drain it and set it aside. You want it just barely al dente since it'll soak up some sauce.

Melt the butter over medium heat until it's foamy but not brown.
2

Make the roux

Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat. Once it's foamy, whisk in the flour. Stir constantly for about 1 minute. You want it bubbling and smooth but still pale/blonde, not brown.

What's a roux? It's just equal parts fat and flour cooked together. It's your thickening agent. The butter coats the flour so it doesn't clump when you add liquid.
3

Add milk slowly

Pour in the milk a little at a time, whisking constantly. It'll seize up and get thick and clumpy after the first splash. That's normal. Keep whisking and adding milk gradually until it's all incorporated and smooth.

Don't panic when it clumps. Just keep whisking. Every splash of milk will thin it out a bit more. Patience here = smooth sauce.
4

Thicken the sauce

Keep stirring over medium heat for 3–5 minutes. You'll feel it go from thin and watery to thick and silky. It's ready when it coats the back of a spoon and you can draw a line through it with your finger.

Add cheese in handfuls, letting each batch melt before adding more.
5

Melt in the cheese

Kill the heat or drop to the lowest setting. Add the Mexican blend cheese in 3–4 handfuls, stirring each one in until melted before adding the next. Season with salt, pepper, and garlic powder.

Low heat is key. Too much heat makes cheese proteins seize up and go greasy/grainy. The residual heat from the béchamel is plenty.
6

Toss with pasta & serve

Pour the cheese sauce over your drained pasta and stir until everything is coated. Taste it. If it needs more salt or a kick, now's the time. Serve immediately while it's hot and gooey.

🔬 Why This Works

The roux is your secret weapon. When you cook flour in butter, the fat coats the starch granules. This means when you add milk, the starches disperse evenly instead of clumping into lumps. As the sauce heats, those starch granules absorb liquid and swell, thickening everything into a béchamel (fancy French word for "white sauce").

The cheese melts into this stable, starch-thickened base. Without the roux, cheese proteins tend to clump together and squeeze out their fat, giving you a greasy, broken mess. The starch molecules physically get between the cheese proteins, keeping everything smooth and emulsified.

Mexican blend (cheddar, monterey jack, queso quesadilla, asadero) is milder and stretchier than straight cheddar. It melts beautifully but can taste a little flat on its own, which is why the salt and garlic powder matter.

Pro Tips

💥 If It Tastes Flat

A splash of hot sauce, a squeeze of mustard, or ½ tsp of mustard powder will wake it right up. The acid and sharpness cut through the richness.

🧈 Want It Richer?

Swap some of the milk for heavy cream, or stir in a tablespoon of cream cheese at the end. Stupid good.

🥓 Add-Ins

Crumbled bacon, diced jalapeños, or roasted green chiles lean into the Mexican blend flavor beautifully.

♨️ Reheating

Add a splash of milk when reheating on the stove. Low heat, stir often. Microwave works but stir halfway through.
