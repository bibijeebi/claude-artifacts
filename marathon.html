Marine Corps Marathon

Arlington, VA & Washington, D.C. — October 2026
$240
per person

"The People's Marathon." The third largest in the U.S., organized by the Marine Corps, no prize money. The course runs past the Lincoln Memorial, Jefferson Memorial, the Capitol, and finishes near the Iwo Jima Memorial with Marines placing medals on finishers. It's the obvious sentimental pick for us.

Entry is by lottery — registration opens April 6, 2026. No qualification, no prior marathon needed. There's also a guaranteed-entry option by running the 17.75K (11 miles) at Quantico in March, or through a charity partner bib. Being veterans doesn't give us priority — active duty/reservists only get early access.

City of Oaks Marathon

Raleigh, NC — November 1, 2026
~$100–140
tiered pricing

Raleigh's hometown marathon. The course starts downtown near NC State, runs along Hillsborough Street past the Bell Tower, through Pullen Park, and onto the Capital Area Greenway all the way to Shelley Lake before heading back. It's an out-and-back through neighborhoods and trails we actually know.

Open registration, no lottery, no qualification. You can register now and defer for free until July 1, 2026, so there's zero risk in locking it in. It's a Boston Qualifier if that ever matters. Post-race party has live music and free beer.

Tobacco Road Marathon

Cary, NC — March 2027
$90–$140
early = cheapest

The flat, fast one. Over 20 miles on the American Tobacco Trail — a converted rail-trail — with a downhill finish. Runner's World named it one of the top nine best new marathons. Up to 20% of runners qualify for Boston here because the course is that forgiving. All-volunteer staff, 100% of proceeds go to charity.

Open registration, no lottery, no qualification. Starts and finishes at the USA Baseball National Training Complex in Cary. The March 2027 date gives us almost a full year to train from right now.

Honolulu Marathon

Honolulu, Hawai'i — December 13, 2026
$138
US residents (early)

No time limit. Literally none. You can walk, run, crawl — aid stations stay open until the last person finishes. The course starts at 5 AM along Ala Moana Beach, goes through Waikiki, up around Diamond Head at sunrise, out to Hawai'i Kai, and finishes in Kapiolani Park. 25,000+ runners, massive international crowd.

Open registration year-round, no lottery, no qualification. The race entry is surprisingly cheap. The flights and hotel are the real cost — but if we're making a trip out of our first marathon, finishing on Waikiki Beach is tough to beat.

Rock 'n' Roll Nashville Marathon

Nashville, TN — April 25, 2026
$105–$135
presale / standard

Live bands on the course, running down Broadway past the honky tonks, finishing at Nissan Stadium. It's more party than race. Part of the Rock 'n' Roll Marathon series, which means great production value and a fun atmosphere. The course is undulating with some hills early, then mostly flat.

Open registration, no lottery, no qualification. The April 2026 date is tight for training (about a month away), but the 2027 edition in spring would give a full year. Nashville also makes a great weekend trip — ~7.5 hour drive or a short flight.

Grandma's Marathon

Duluth, MN — June 20, 2026
~$130–160
tiered pricing

Consistently rated one of the best first-timer marathons in the country. Point-to-point along the North Shore of Lake Superior, net downhill (starts at 740 ft, finishes at 610 ft), mild June weather in the 50s–60s. Incredibly well-organized with a smaller, supportive field of about 8,000 runners.

Open registration, no lottery, no qualification. 2026 is the 50th anniversary, so expect it to sell out earlier than usual. It's a flight to Minneapolis + a drive to Duluth, so a bit more logistically involved.

The important stuff

None of these require qualification — that's a Boston thing. Most have open registration you can sign up for right now. The MCM is the only lottery. Everything here is between $90 and $240 per person for the race itself, which is a fraction of what Disney charges before you even get into the park. Whatever we pick, we've got time to train and we're doing it together.
