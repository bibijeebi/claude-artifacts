The Night Shift

Dad's 1940s Remington 511 Scoremaster gets three upgrades in one gunsmith trip: a new sear so the bolt stops falling out of the gun, a threaded muzzle, and a $180 suppressor. The result is the quietest legal firearm combo that exists, pointed at a corn pile that raccoons and possums can legally be shot off of at 2 a.m. Parts are already ordered. This one's rolling.

The Situation

The bait station is live: a 50 lb corn pile in the backyard, gravity feeder build pending, patterning squirrels for the Oct 12 opener. But the corn doesn't just pull squirrels. Raccoons and possums will hammer it nightly, and NC law explicitly allows taking both at night, year-round hours-wise during their Oct 12 – Feb 28 season, with no possession limit on possums and 3/day on coons. That's a target-rich backyard from mid-October to March, day and night.

The right tool already lives in the safe: the Remington 511 Scoremaster, Dad's rifle, wearing a scope and fed on subsonic shorts. Two problems stand between it and night duty. First, the bolt sometimes rides straight out the back of the receiver mid-cycle, an 80-year-old-parts problem, not an operator problem. Second, an unthreaded muzzle means every shot on a 0.6-acre lot announces itself to the whole street at midnight.

Both problems die in one gunsmith visit.

Fix One: The Wandering Bolt

On the 500-series Remingtons the sear pulls double duty: it holds the firing pin AND its nose is the bolt stop, catching a groove milled in the bolt's belly. Eight decades of racking wears the hook round and the bolt skips right past it. Known model-wide disease with a known cure.

Every part that can cause the symptom is already ordered, Numrich order #169123, $46.18 shipped:

PartPriceRole
Sear, new reproduction (fitting req'd)$23.95The hook itself. THE broken part. Repro runs oversize on purpose so the smith files it to this rifle.
Sear pivot screw, used factory original$2.95The axle the sear swings on. Worn axle = drooping sear = same symptom even with a fresh hook.
Trigger spring, new factory original$2.95The muscle that shoves the hook up into the bolt groove. Tired spring = lazy hook.
Trigger spring plunger, new factory original$4.26The slug between spring and sear. Wears as a pair with the spring.
Why buy all of it: nobody knows which of the three (hook, axle, muscle) is the culprit until the action is apart on the bench. At these prices, the whole candidate set rides along in one baggie and the smith fixes whatever he finds. One trip instead of two.

Fix Two: The Thread and the Can

Muzzle threading, 1/2x28~$100–150

Standard rimfire suppressor thread, any competent smith cuts it on the 511's 25" barrel. Same visit as the sear fitting. Include a thread protector cap so the threads survive when the can's off.

YHM Mite 22 suppressor~$180 + tax

The budget king of rimfire cans: all stainless, full-auto rated, disassembles for the lead-scraping that every rimfire can needs. On a bolt gun with subsonics there is no audible difference between this and $450 cans.

The timing is historically stupid: the $200 federal tax stamp dropped to $0 on Jan 1, 2026, and an Aug 13, 2026 federal court order means Silencer Shop customers in free states skip the Form 4 entirely, just a 4473 and NICS at the dealer like a regular gun. NC qualifies. That ruling could get stayed on appeal, so sooner beats later, and even the fallback path is $0 and ~2 weeks.

Why this combo is the quiet ceiling: subsonic .22 (no supersonic crack) + bolt action (no cycling clatter, no ejection port noise) + suppressor. The action being worked by hand is louder than the muzzle. Neighbors hear nothing, and the animals on the pile don't scatter after the first shot.

The Payoff Math

Under $425 total converts an heirloom with a dangerous party trick into the exact right tool for a five-month backyard season, and every dollar lands on durable goods: the sear fix is permanent, threads are forever, and the can outlives the rifle. Compare against the "normal" path of a new threaded .22 ($280) plus a premium can ($450): this build beats it by hundreds while keeping Dad's rifle in service instead of in the safe.

Reality check: suppressed doesn't mean silent, it means "airsoft gun" instead of "gunshot." The corn pile will still educate the survivors after a night or two of shooting; expect the coons to go shy in waves and rotate the schedule. And the repro sear may take the smith two fittings to get the bolt stop AND trigger engagement both right, that's normal for 80-year-old tolerances, not a failed part.
