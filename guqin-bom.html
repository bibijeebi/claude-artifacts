▸ THE PLAN IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Seven cheap piezo discs go between the bridge and soundboard, one per string, held with foam pads and painter's tape. Each disc wires into one of seven 1/4" instrument cables that plug into a Behringer UMC1820's eight Hi-Z combo inputs. Mac sees one 8-channel input. The browser's Web Audio API reads all 7 channels in parallel. Software is trivial: per-channel onset detection, no pitch tracking, no FFT, no YIN. Latency: ~5-10ms. The channel that fires brightest IS the string that was plucked. Done.

01 Sensors

The thing that converts string vibration into electrons.

15PCS

27mm piezo discs, prewired with leads

Brass-backed ceramic transducer, ~50mm leads pre-soldered. We need 7 — buying 15 gives spares and a margin for the inevitable "oh I crushed one" moment. Don't get the leadless ones unless you want to solder.
Why these specs: 27mm hits the sweet spot for sensitivity vs. footprint. 20mm too small for clean attack capture, 35mm+ won't fit between strings on a guqin bridge. Brass plate is the standard contact-mic format.

02 Audio interface

The brain that turns 7 voltage signals into one USB stream.

1UNIT

Behringer U-Phoria UMC1820

8 Hi-Z/XLR combo inputs, 24-bit/96kHz, USB 2.0, class-compliant on Mac (no driver install). Each input switches to instrument-level Hi-Z mode at the press of a button.
Why this one: it's the cheapest 8-channel interface with Hi-Z on every input, which is what piezos need. Focusrite Scarlett 18i20 is twice the price for nicer preamps you don't need. Cheaper alternatives like multi-channel mixers don't have per-channel digital outs to USB. Buy used on Reverb/eBay for ~$150-200; they're abundant and reliable.

03 Cables

Wires from the piezos to the interface. Boring but necessary.

7CABLES

1/4" TS instrument cables, 6-foot

Standard mono guitar cables. The piezo's bare leads get soldered to one end (or use solderless 1/4" plugs). Other end goes into the UMC1820.
Why six feet: short = better signal. Piezos are high-impedance sources and long cables turn into capacitive losses. Don't get 20-foot stage cables. Six feet is plenty since the interface sits next to the instrument.
▸ Amazon (6-pack)
$256-pack + 1
7PLUGS

Solderless 1/4" TS plugs (alternative)

Skip soldering: get screw-terminal 1/4" plugs and just clamp the piezo's leads inside. Less elegant, faster.
Why optional: only buy these if you don't want to solder. If you've got the Hobart out anyway, just solder the piezo leads onto trimmed instrument cables — cleaner and more reliable. These are the lazy path.

04 Mounting

How the piezos stay in place without harming the instrument.

1PACK

Adhesive foam weatherstripping, 1/4" thick

Cut into 27mm squares, sandwich each piezo between foam and the soundboard. Foam compresses to hold the piezo in firm contact with the bridge area while absorbing crosstalk vibration.
Why foam: this is the actual installation mechanism, not the tape. The foam keeps the piezo firmly pressed against the wood without any glue or fastener. The tape is just a strap. Anything you have around the house works — pipe insulation, packing foam, Dr. Scholl's moleskin. Don't overthink this.
▸ Amazon (or Lowe's)
$8
1ROLL

Painter's tape, low-tack

3M ScotchBlue or equivalent. Strips of this hold the foam-piezo sandwich against the underside of the bridge. Comes off in seconds, leaves no residue, no adhesive contact with the lacquer.
Why painter's tape and not gaff/duct/electrical: low-tack means non-marking. Bekah's $279 instrument deserves a tape that won't pull lacquer. Test on an inconspicuous spot first.

USB cable, display, you've already got these.

USB-B to USB-C cable, 6-foot

For connecting the UMC1820 to your M1 MacBook Air. The interface ships with USB-A → USB-B; you need USB-C → USB-B since modern Macs are all USB-C.
Why this and not a hub: direct connection minimizes latency and avoids the powered-hub-fights-for-current issue Rocksmith users always complain about.

▸ HEADS UP — INSTALLATION

Do not glue piezos directly to the lacquer. The foam pad is non-negotiable. The foam serves three purposes: (1) holds the piezo in firm vibrational contact, (2) absorbs crosstalk between adjacent strings, (3) creates a mechanical buffer that's instantly removable. If you skip the foam and stick a piezo direct to the soundboard with adhesive, you'll either get crap signal (rigid contact = body resonance instead of clean string transient) or you'll damage the lacquer. Both bad.

(4) Test each piezo through the interface — squeeze it, watch the input meter spike. (5) Cut foam pads. (6) Place piezos under the bridge with foam, tape down.
