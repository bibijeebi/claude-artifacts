Bekah runs to Great Falls

What this is

Bekah's comeback: one daily mile in September to a half marathon in February.

Physical therapy cleared running on September 14, 2026 after about four months off. The first mile back went fine, so the goal became a real race with a real date. Not every-other-day, not a couch program. Six days a week, every day at least a mile, because the habit is the point and the distance follows.

The full marathon comes later. This is the half that proves the back holds.

The weekend

Staying with friends in Mount Airy, MD, about 50 minutes from the park and about 50 minutes from Baltimore the other direction. Race in the morning, done by 11, back to the house, then the Baltimore Symphony's The Planets HD Odyssey at the Meyerhoff that evening. Benny's show. Saturday has a 1:30 and a 7:30 performance.

Why this race

Twenty-two weeks from the first mile back. Long enough to build without rushing, short enough not to be ridiculous. The coast races in February (Oak Island, Wilmington) landed on or right after the Baltimore trip, so the race came to the trip instead. Ocracoke's half in April is the likely second one.

This week

Green with a check means it's in the log. Tap a day to tick it yourself before Claude logs it. Sunday is off.

The full plan

22 weeks. Six days, Sunday off, every run is at least a mile. Saturday is the long run because race day is a Saturday. Numbers are miles.

Rules

Every run counts, the point is showing up. Easy pace, walk breaks are fine.

Distance creeps on a few days a week, never on all six at once. Every fourth week or so holds flat.

If the back or knee goes from "feels it" to actually hurting, that day is a walk, not a skip. Streak intact, tissue protected. Still hurting at 3 miles, tell Tim.

Feeling good and the back's holding, the plan gets bumped. Tell Claude.
