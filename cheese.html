Tier 1

Your Core Rotation

These are the buy-every-trip cheeses. The ones that should always be in your fridge. You already love most of these.

Aged, Hard, Crystalline. Your Wheelhouse.

You two gravitate toward nutty, sharp, aged cheeses with that addictive crystal crunch. Parm, aged Gouda, Manchego, Dubliner. These are all in the same flavor family: complex, savory, with sweetness that develops from long aging. Once you know you love this category, the whole world of aged cheese opens up.

Kerrygold Dubliner
Ireland · Cow's Milk · ~12 Months

Nutty, sweet, sharp, with Parm-like crystals. Sits perfectly between cheddar and Parmesan. Snack it straight, melt on burgers, pair with apple slices. One of the best value-to-quality cheeses at Costco.

Costco Must-Buy Snacking Melting
Kirkland Manchego D.O.P.
Spain · Sheep's Milk · 6 Months

Nutty, buttery, salty finish. The real deal with D.O.P. certification from La Mancha. Pairs perfectly with membrillo (quince paste), Marcona almonds, or dry salami. Aged 12+ months ("viejo") is even more intense if you spot it.

Costco Must-Buy Snacking Boards
Parmigiano-Reggiano
Italy · Cow's Milk · 24+ Months

The undisputed king. Costco's Kirkland wedges are D.O.P. certified via Zanetti. Snack on chunks, grate over everything, toss the rind into soups for umami depth. Unbeatable price per pound at Costco.

Costco Cooking Snacking Highest Protein
Aged Gouda
Netherlands · Cow's Milk · 18+ Months

The good stuff develops caramel and butterscotch notes with crunchy crystals. Look for black or brown wax (= aged). Young Gouda with red/yellow wax is mild and boring by comparison. Costco carries Beemster 18-month online.

Costco (Sometimes) Snacking Boards
Pecorino Romano
Italy · Sheep's Milk · 8+ Months

Saltier and earthier than Parm. Essential for cacio e pepe, carbonara, and any pasta where you want aggressive salty punch. Also great broken into chunks on a board. Kirkland P.D.O. certified.

Costco Cooking Grating
Tillamook Sharp Cheddar
Oregon, USA · Cow's Milk

The fridge staple. 2.5 lb blocks at Costco run ~$11 ($0.28/oz), one of the best cheese deals anywhere. Sandwiches, burgers, mac and cheese, straight snacking. Always have a block.

Tier 2

Level-Up Picks

Once the core rotation is locked in, these are the ones that take your cheese game from good to genuinely impressive.

Don't Sleep on the Soft Stuff

You love the blueberry goat cheese, which means you're already into the soft/flavored category. Brie, Boursin, chèvre, and Fromager d'Affinois are all in this lane. They're the counterbalance to your aged cheeses on a board: creamy where the others are crystalline, mild where the others are sharp.

Gruyère AOP
Switzerland · Cow's Milk · 8-12 Months

Probably the single most versatile cheese in existence. Sweet, slightly funky, nutty. The ultimate melter: French onion soup, croque monsieur, fondue, gratins. Also fantastic as a snacking cheese. Costco carries legit Swiss AOP.

Costco Most Versatile Melting Cooking
Comté
France · Cow's Milk · 10-12 Months

French Alpine cheese, similar family as Gruyère but more complex. Nutty, fruity, sometimes with a hint of brown butter. Costco occasionally stocks it. If you see it, buy it. No hesitation.

Costco (Seasonal) Snacking Special
Kirkland French Brie
France · Cow's Milk · Soft-Ripened

Creamy, mild, buttery. ~$10 for a whole wheel at Costco. Bake it with honey and walnuts for a killer appetizer. Triple cream brie (Fromager d'Affinois or Saint André) is next-level richness if you spot it.

Costco Boards Baking Spreading
Boursin Herb & Garlic
France · Cow's Milk · Fresh

Soft, spreadable, garlicky, herby. On crackers, stuffed in chicken, mixed into mashed potatoes, spread on bagels. Costco sells the multi-pack. This is the "everybody loves it" cheese.

Costco Spreading Cooking
Goat Cheese with Fruit
Various · Goat's Milk · Fresh

You already love the blueberry one. Kirkland carries logs and sometimes cranberry or herb rounds. Plain chèvre is also great: tangy, creamy, crumbles over salads. The fruit varieties are instant board stars.

Costco Boards Salads
Havarti
Denmark · Cow's Milk · Semi-Soft

Buttery, mild, melts beautifully. Excellent sandwich cheese. Costco sells sliced Havarti. The dill-flavored variety is worth seeking out for something a little different.

Costco Sandwiches Melting
Sartori BellaVitano
Wisconsin, USA · Cow's Milk

A Parm-meets-cheddar hybrid. The plain/gold version is excellent. Skip the Cabernet Sauvignon one (tastes like chewing wine). Espresso and Balsamic are polarizing but worth one try. Costco rotates these seasonally.

Costco (Seasonal) Snacking
Edam
Netherlands · Cow's Milk · Semi-Hard

Gouda's underrated Dutch cousin. Semi-skimmed milk makes it denser but equally buttery. Costco carries Gayo Azul Edam in a fun big round ball. Milder than aged Gouda, great everyday slicer.

Tier 3

Adventurous Picks

When you're feeling bold. Blues, frying cheeses, burrata, and stuff that'll expand your palate.

Roquefort
France · Sheep's Milk · Blue

The "king of blues." Sharp, salty, tangy, crumbly. Pair with honey and walnuts to tame the intensity. Costco carries Papillon Roquefort online. Not for everyone, but incredible if you like bold flavors.

Costco (Online) Bold Boards
Burrata
Italy · Cow's Milk · Fresh

Fresh mozz shell filled with cream. Cut it open and the center oozes out. Drizzle olive oil, add tomatoes, basil, flaky salt. Pure luxury. Short shelf life, eat fast. Costco stocks it sometimes.

Salads Special Occasion
Queso de Freír / Halloumi
Various · Holds Up to Heat

Cheeses that don't melt when fried or grilled. Slice thick, pan-fry until golden, serve with honey or alongside eggs. Game-changing breakfast cheese. Costco carries Tropical brand.

Costco Frying Breakfast
Cotija
Mexico · Cow's Milk · Aged

Mexican "Parmesan." Salty, crumbly, dry. Essential for elote (street corn), tacos, enchiladas, chilaquiles. If you cook any Mexican food, keep this around.

Mexican Cooking Crumbling
Smoked Gouda
Netherlands · Cow's Milk

Campfire-like flavor layered onto that Gouda sweetness. Trader Joe's Dutch smoked version is great. Pairs well with dark beers and cured meats.

Snacking Boards
Stilton
England · Cow's Milk · Blue

English blue, milder and creamier than Roquefort. Great gateway blue cheese. Pair with port wine, pears, or walnuts. A good first step if you're blue-curious.

The takeaway: If cottage cheese is out at Costco, Kirkland nonfat Greek yogurt is the direct substitute with nearly identical macros. For hard cheeses, think of them as flavor delivery systems in small portions (grated Parm on salads) rather than protein sources.

Beyond Costco

Where to Buy

Costco is king for bulk value, but other stores fill the gaps nicely for variety and smaller quantities.

Costco

Best price per pound on staples. Kirkland cheeses are legit (D.O.P./P.D.O. certified imports). Commit to large quantities. Selection varies by region and season.

Trader Joe's

Unexpected Cheddar (the famous cheddar-Parm hybrid), triple cream brie with mushrooms, Dutch smoked Gouda, Toscano with black pepper. Smaller portions, great for experimenting.

Aldi

Surprisingly good cheese section. Happy Farms brand has solid Gouda, Havarti, brie at dirt-cheap prices. Seasonal "Aldi Finds" include legit European imports, especially around holidays.

Harris Teeter / Publix

Deli counter lets you buy by weight. Great for trying small amounts of expensive cheeses (aged Manchego, Comté, Gruyère) before committing to a Costco-sized block.

Lidl

Good European cheeses at low prices. Worth checking if there's one near Willow Spring. Similar to Aldi's approach but sometimes different imports.

Keep It Fresh

Storage Tips

Hard cheeses (Parm, Manchego, aged Gouda)

Last weeks to months. Wrap in parchment paper, then loosely in plastic or a zip bag. Never fully airtight; cheese needs to breathe.

Soft cheeses (brie, goat, Boursin)

Eat within 1-2 weeks of opening. Keep sealed in the fridge. These go bad faster than you'd expect.

Mold on hard cheese?

Cut it off (at least 1 inch around and below). The rest is completely fine. This is normal and expected.

Mold on soft cheese?

Toss it. Soft cheese has too much moisture; mold penetrates throughout. Don't risk it.

Freezing cheese

Hard cheeses freeze OK for cooking (grating, melting) but texture degrades for snacking. Shred before freezing for easy use later.

Serving temperature

Pull cheese out of the fridge 30-45 minutes before eating. Cold cheese = muted flavor. Room temp = completely different experience.
