Proposal #014

Tesla Model Y Fog Light Repair

Driver-side front fog light pushed in after a deer strike on Bekah's 2023 Long Range. One deer, three possible fixes, and a four-figure trap to avoid.

Total Cost $0–$90
Difficulty 1–2/5
Time 5–30 min
Category Automotive
Status Proposed

Bottom Line Up Front

The light is the driver-side front fog lamp, a small LED pod set into the lower bumper fascia. Do the push test first. Roughly half the time a deer tap just pops the clips loose and it snaps right back for $0. If the clips snapped, it is almost always a $40–$90 used-OEM pod swap you can do yourself in under 30 minutes. The only expensive outcome is a shop talking you into a $1,000+ bumper cover replacement. Do not let that happen.

The Problem

Benny hit a deer. The driver-side fog light, the small light below the main headlight, got pushed inward. The lens is not cracked, it is just sitting recessed and crooked. That tells us the impact was light and the damage is mechanical, not a shattered unit.

The fog pod is held into the bumper cover by 3 plastic mounting tabs, and the pod is also screwed to the fascia from behind with 3 screws. A knock like this breaks one of two things: the tabs on the pod, or the tabs on the bumper cover opening. Which one decides the fix and the cost. Everything below is a decision tree to figure out which path you are on.

The Three Paths in Detail

Path A — Reseat $0

The clips never broke. The deer just knocked the pod off its detents and it is hanging loose in the opening.

  1. Press the pod evenly back into the opening until you hear it click.
  2. Push all the way around the perimeter so every one of the 3 tabs engages.
  3. Do not force it. If it will not click and hold, you are actually on Path B or C.
  4. Confirm it sits flush with the surrounding fascia. Done. No teardown, no parts.
Path B — Replace the Pod $40–$90

The 3 plastic tabs on the fog pod snapped. The pod floats loose but the bumper cover opening is undamaged. This is the most common deer-tap outcome. No need to pull the whole bumper, you work through the wheel well.

  1. Turn the wheel for clearance and chock the car.
  2. Release the 3 push clips holding the front of the wheel-arch liner to the fascia (pry the center pin, then pull the clip).
  3. Release the 4 clips on the front fender garnish where it meets the wheel arch.
  4. Fold the liner back, reach up behind the fascia to the back of the fog pod.
  5. Unplug the harness: lift the red locking tab, then pull the connector.
  6. Remove the 3 screws holding the pod to the fascia, pull the old pod out the front.
  7. Read the white sticker on the back of the old pod for the exact part number.
  8. Fit the new pod, reuse the 3 screws, reconnect the harness until the red tab clicks down.
  9. Reseat the liner clips and fender garnish. Install is the reverse of removal.
Path C — Epoxy Mount ~$8

The tabs that broke are on the bumper cover, not the pod. The pod is good but has nothing to clip into. A shop will use this to sell a whole new bumper cover plus paint. Skip that. Bond the pod directly into the opening instead.

  1. Confirm the pod itself is intact and still lights up.
  2. Clean both mating surfaces with isopropyl alcohol so the bond holds.
  3. Apply 2-part epoxy (JB Weld or similar) to the pod's mounting flange.
  4. Seat the pod flush in the opening, hold or tape it square while the epoxy cures.
  5. Let it cure fully before driving. Ugly from behind, invisible from the front, saves four figures.

Permanent-ish: a future pod swap means cutting the bond. Acceptable trade for not repainting a bumper.

The Part (if Path B)

This is the driver/left-hand front fog lamp for a 2023 Model Y Long Range. The lamp is shared across Model 3 and Model Y from roughly 2021 on, so listings will say "Model 3/Y" and span "2017–2023". That fitment is real, just match the exact part number.

Verify before buying. The trailing letter (-B / -C / -D) is a revision code and it must match. Pull the old pod, read the white sticker on the back, and order off that number. A 2023 build could be C or D depending on production date. Do not order off the table above alone.

Tools & Materials

For Path A: nothing, just your hands. For Path B: a plastic trim/pry tool for the liner clips, and a screwdriver or socket for the 3 pod screws. For Path C: isopropyl alcohol, 2-part epoxy, painter's tape to hold the pod square while it cures.

Recommendation

Run the push test today before spending a dollar or ordering a part. If it clicks and holds, this proposal closes at $0. If it does not, fold back the liner and inspect: pod tabs broken means a sub-$90 DIY swap, cover tabs broken means an $8 epoxy job. Either way this is a driveway repair, not a body shop ticket. The single rule: if anyone quotes a bumper cover replacement, this proposal becomes Path C and they get nothing.
