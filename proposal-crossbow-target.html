The Target Decision

The crossbow was free. The target is therefore the entire cost of deer season, and there is exactly one target at Bass Pro that does both jobs the Parker needs: stop 365 fps bolts without eating them, and take the broadheads that have to be verified before opening day.

The Situation

The Parker Tornado XTreme landed free on 8/12: 165 lb draw, 365 fps, illuminated scope, bolts, broadheads, rope cocker. Rail lube and string wax are in hand, quiver arrives ~Thursday from Jeff and Perry. A hunt-ready rig like this runs $500+ new, and the entire out-of-pocket for deer season is now one target.

Deer archery season opens September 12. Buying this weekend means four weekends to zero the scope and verify broadhead point-of-impact. That last part is non-negotiable: fixed-blade broadheads fly differently than field points, and the ethical-kill standard is confirming where they hit before pointing one at an animal.

The Pick: Bigshot Iron Man 24" Xtreme 500

Bigshot Iron Man 24" Xtreme 500 Crossbow Target$159.99 → ~$144

Purpose-built crossbow target from a company that only makes archery targets. Rated to 500 fps (the Parker's 365 is mid-pack, not a stress case), takes field points AND broadheads, hand-pull bolt removal, 24" face with a deer-vitals side. 4.6 stars over 72 reviews.

Bass Pro's Legendary Salute takes 10% off every day, stacks on sales, and archery targets aren't excluded. That's the Iron Man for ~$144 + tax, cheaper than anywhere else sells it, using a discount already earned.

The 24" face matters: this is a used crossbow with an unproven zero. A bigger face means walk-in shots land on foam instead of in the yard. Bolts that miss at 365 fps bury themselves somewhere they're never coming back from, and replacement bolts run $8–12 each. Two saved bolts pay the size premium.

Why cheap targets die on crossbows: crossbow bolts are shorter and heavier than arrows and carry ~120 ft-lbs. In soft layered foam they bury to the fletching and weld in; in bag targets a broadhead shreds the fill on the first shot. "Buy an $80 target" really means "buy an $80 target twice, angrier."

The Payoff Math

One deer yields roughly 50–60 lbs of venison: $300–500 of ground meat, steaks, and roasts at grocery prices. The target pays for itself the first successful season and lasts years of field-point volume by rotating its faces. Meanwhile the practice itself is free forever, which is the whole reason a backyard target beats range fees.

Backstop plan: the retired Kia Optima parked broadside behind the target, door-and-fender line, stall mat or scrap plywood leaned against it for insurance. Free, already owned, and the 24" face makes it a formality at 20 yards.
