1998

A man sat in a Hawaiian rainforest and described
exactly what would happen to the world.

Nobody listened.

SCROLL

In October 1998, filmmaker John Hazard traveled to the Big Island of Hawaii to interview Terence McKenna. It was supposed to be the first of several sessions for a documentary. McKenna was diagnosed with a brain tumor shortly after. He died in April 2000.

This was the last long-form interview he ever gave.

It sat on YouTube for years, mostly ignored. No subtitles. Low view count. Just a man in a garden talking about what he saw coming.

Read what he said. Then remember: this was twenty-seven years ago.

· · · TRANSMISSION 01 · · ·
"The universe is getting its act together. It's connecting the dots. More and more is happening. And if you ask the question, what would be the ultimate state of connectivity or of happening—it's when all points are connected to all other points." Terence McKenna · October 1998 · Big Island, Hawaii
2004: Facebook launches. 2007: iPhone. 2016: Half the planet is online. 2023: GPT-4 connects the sum of human knowledge into a single queryable interface. 2025: 5.5 billion people connected. CONFIRMED
"Human technologies, languages, migrations, art movements, ideologies are not something different from nature. They're the same download of process that we see in the movement of continents, the evolution of new species of animals, except that these human novel emergent situations are happening much more quickly."
He said "download of process" in 1998. Before broadband. Before the cloud. Before anyone talked about technology as an evolutionary force. He described it as biology.
· · · TRANSMISSION 02 · · ·
"I have always doubted that evolutionary theory without purpose, without teleology, could produce as complex a world as we see around us in as short a time. It seemed more as though these processes were being channeled between high walls. Its forward direction was inevitable."
"The universe is under the sway of what I call the transcendental object at the end of time. All nature aspires for this state of perfect novelty. Nature abhors habit, and so it seeks the novel."
In 2023, researchers at Google DeepMind published evidence that AI systems spontaneously develop emergent capabilities at scale—abilities that were not programmed, not predicted, and not understood. The models are pulled toward complexity by the structure of the data itself. The language McKenna used in 1998 maps almost exactly onto how AI researchers describe emergence today. ACTIVE
· · · TRANSMISSION 03 · · ·
"Between now and 2012, I look for—"
"The invention of artificial life." He said this in 1998.
2010: Craig Venter creates the first synthetic cell. 2024: AI protein folding unlocks synthetic biology at scale. Self-replicating nanobots in development. CONFIRMED
"The cloning of human beings."
2018: He Jiankui gene-edits human embryos in China. 2025: Synthetic embryo models grown to 14 days. The technology exists. The line was crossed. CONFIRMED
"Possible contact with extraterrestrials."
2023: David Grusch testifies under oath to Congress that the U.S. government possesses non-human craft and biologics. Pentagon confirms UAP task force. Congressional hearings ongoing. ACTIVE
"Possible human immortality."
2025: Bryan Johnson's Blueprint protocol reverses biological aging markers. Yamanaka factors reprogram cells to embryonic state. Altos Labs funded at $3B to "solve death." ACTIVE
"And at the same time—appalling acts of brutality, genocide, race-baiting, homophobia, famine, starvation."
Rwanda. Darfur. Syria. Yemen. Uyghurs. Ukraine. Gaza. He didn't name the places. He didn't have to. CONFIRMED
"Because the systems which are in place to keep the world sane are utterly inadequate to the forces that have been unleashed."
2008: Global financial collapse. 2016: Brexit. Social media disinformation. 2020: Global pandemic. 2024: Institutions worldwide failing faster than they can reform. CONFIRMED
· · · TRANSMISSION 04 · · ·
"The collapse of the socialist world, the rise of the internet. These are changes so immense nobody could imagine them ever happening. And now that they have happened, nobody even bothers to mention what a big deal it is."
"The fact that there is no such thing as the Soviet Union, people never talk about it anymore. But when I was a kid, the notion that that would ever change was beyond conceiving."
He identified the pattern: Things that would have been inconceivable become normal overnight. The inconceivable becomes mundane. The impossible becomes boring. This is exactly what happened with AI, smartphones, social media, gene editing, cryptocurrency. Each one a revolution. Each one absorbed in months.
"As primates, we're incredibly adaptable to change.

It's a good thing, because we're going to be tested to the limits."
· · · TRANSMISSION 05 · · ·

Then the interviewer asked him what the near future would feel like.

"It's only going to get weirder.

The level of contradiction is going to rise excruciatingly, even beyond the excruciating present levels of contradiction.

So I think it's just going to get weirder and weirder and weirder.

And finally it's going to be so weird that people are going to have to talk about how weird it is."
He said this before 9/11. Before smartphones. Before social media. Before deepfakes. Before a pandemic shut down the planet. Before AI started writing poetry and passing bar exams.
· · · FINAL TRANSMISSION · · ·

Near the end of the interview, he said something he attributed to a psilocybin experience. Whether you believe in the source is irrelevant. Listen to the content.

"The mushroom said to me once—it said: This is what it's like when a species prepares to depart for the stars."
"You don't depart for the stars under calm and orderly conditions."
"It's a fire in a madhouse."
"And that's what we have. The fire in the madhouse, at the end of time."
"This is what it's like when a species prepares to move on to the next dimension. The entire destiny of all life on the planet is tied up in this. We are not acting for ourselves, or from ourselves. We happen to be the point species on a transformation that will affect every living organism on this planet at its conclusion." Terence McKenna · Final Interview · 1998

RECORDED: OCTOBER 1998

SUBJECT DECEASED: APRIL 3, 2000

He saw all of this coming from a garden in Hawaii, twenty-seven years before it happened.

What did he see that we still can't?
