How to read this chart

Each curve shows relative drug concentration in the blood over time. Retatrutide has a half-life of about 6 days, meaning half the drug is still in your body when the next weekly dose arrives. This causes accumulation: each dose stacks on top of what remains from previous doses.

Over 4–5 weeks of consistent dosing, blood levels reach a steady state where each new injection replaces exactly what was eliminated since the last one. The peak-to-trough swing shows how much concentration fluctuates within each dosing interval.

Shorter dosing intervals (e.g. every 5 days instead of 7) produce higher accumulation because less drug has cleared before the next dose. Higher starting doses produce proportionally higher concentrations since retatrutide has dose-proportional pharmacokinetics.

Why accumulation matters

Gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, diarrhea) are concentration-dependent. In clinical trials, groups that started at 4mg had significantly more GI events than groups starting at 2mg, even when both reached the same target dose. The Phase 3 TRIUMPH protocol starts at 2mg and steps up every 4 weeks (2 → 4 → 6 → 9 → 12mg) specifically to let the body adapt at each plateau.

Dosing more frequently than every 7 days or jumping dose levels too quickly produces higher peak concentrations before the body has adapted, increasing the likelihood and severity of side effects without meaningfully improving long-term efficacy.
