Proposal #021 — Draft

Side-Access Driveway Spur

Swing a concrete spur off the existing driveway around to the left side of the garage, and cut in a 36-inch man-door for side access.

Est. Cost $4,500–$12,000
Difficulty 4/5
Time Several weekends
Category Home
Status Draft

The Vision

Right now the driveway is a straight shot from Eagle Chase Dr to the two front garage doors, and that's it. The utility trailer lives on the grass. We want concrete that branches off the existing driveway and swings around to the left side of the garage (the open-yard side, away from the house), ending at a new walk-in door cut into the garage side wall. End state: a real side approach, a hard surface for the trailer, and a man-door you can walk through without raising a garage door.

What the Site Shows

Reading the Google Street View and satellite imagery of 7613 Eagle Chase Dr: brick-veneer house over a wood-framed structure, front-load two-car garage, single straight concrete driveway. The left side of the garage (toward the wooded vacant lot) is wide-open lawn on a gentle slope, no fence, no neighbor crowding it, which is the easy direction to build. The A/C condenser sits on that side wall and, as you noted, is set back deeper than the garage itself runs, so a spur that only needs to reach a garage-wall door naturally stops short of it. The lot falls gently toward the street, so drainage has to be designed, not assumed.

Proposed Layout

Schematic, not to survey scale. Confirm all dimensions on-site before forming.

The recommended approach is a sweeping L-spur: the new concrete peels off the left edge of the existing driveway with a generous fillet, curves roughly 90° to the left, runs alongside the garage's left wall to the new man-door, and terminates with about 3 ft of clearance before the A/C unit. Keep the inner radius of the turn at 15 ft minimum (18–20 ft is a noticeably easier swing), the spur 12 ft wide for comfortable maneuvering plus walking room, and widen to 14–16 ft if you want the trailer parked broadside rather than lengthwise.

The Two Work Packages

A. The Concrete Spur

Branch off the existing driveway's left edge, curve around, run to the garage side wall. Tie-in matters: saw-cut a clean straight edge on the old slab, then drill and epoxy #4 rebar dowels (about 16 in long, 16 in on-center) into that edge so the new pour shares load and won't settle into a lip. Use a 1/2-in isolation joint where the new concrete meets the garage and house foundation so the slab can move independently of the building.

The pour-and-finish is the hard part of any DIY slab this size. Realistic best move: DIY the demo, grading, base prep, and forms (the curved forms are fussy but doable with bender board), then hire a finish crew for pour day. That single split is the highest-leverage decision in the whole project.

Est: $3,500–$9,000 (DIY base + hired finish vs. fully contracted)

B. The Garage Man-Door

A 36-in pre-hung insulated steel exterior door in the garage's left side wall, placed in the front-to-middle third so the spur curve stays comfortable and it's well clear of the A/C unit. This is a real structural opening: the brick veneer above the door needs a steel lintel (loose angle iron, sized for span, ~4 in bearing each side on solid brick), and the wood-framed wall behind needs a proper header with king and jack studs. Header size depends entirely on whether that wall is load-bearing, so that gets confirmed before anything is cut.

Sequence: snap lines, score and remove brick, set the lintel, cut the framed wall, install header + studs, set and flash the pre-hung door (sill-pan flashing is non-negotiable), trim, caulk, paint. Keep the exterior concrete a step below the door threshold and below the brick weep holes. DIY-able for someone comfortable with framing; the lintel and header sizing should be verified, not guessed.

Est: $500–$3,000 (DIY install vs. contracted, incl. permit)

Concrete Spec

Code-compliant residential flatwork for a surface that will see a Tesla plus a loaded utility trailer:

Thickness4 in minimum; 5 in recommended given trailer loads
Concrete4,000 psi, air-entrained 5–7% (NC freeze-thaw, exterior)
Base4 in compacted ABC / crusher run over compacted subgrade, placed in lifts with a plate compactor
Reinforcement#4 rebar 18 in on-center each way, on chairs at mid-slab (better than wire mesh for trailer loads)
Control jointsTooled or saw-cut 1/4 of slab depth, every 10–12 ft, panels kept near-square (max 1.5:1)
Isolation joints1/2 in expansion material against garage, house, A/C pad, any post
SlopeMinimum 1/4 in per ft (2%) away from foundation; spur daylights into the side yard
Finish & cureBroom finish for traction; moist-cure or curing compound 7 days, keep traffic off 7 days, full strength at 28
Drainage is the make-or-break. New concrete sends water that used to soak into the lawn straight downhill. Pitch the spur to daylight into the open side yard, never toward the foundation, the garage doors, or the new man-door. The pad in front of the door pitches away from the door. If the uphill grade would dump runoff against the garage wall, add a shallow swale on the high side or a channel drain near the building. Get this wrong and you buy yourself a wet garage and a rotted sill plate.
The A/C unit. Don't pour concrete against the condenser pad. Leave a clear gap (grass or gravel), keep any nearby concrete below the unit's pad height so you don't trap water, and keep the electrical disconnect accessible. Since the door sits forward on the wall and the unit is set back past the garage depth, the spur should end with 3 ft or more of clearance with no special routing needed. Confirm the manufacturer's required service clearances on-site.

Permits & Approvals

Code-compliant means clearing these before breaking ground:

  • HOA architectural review — Stamey's Walk almost certainly has an ARC; a new driveway and a visible side door need approval first. Step zero.
  • Wake County building permit — required for the man-door (new opening + structural alteration in an exterior wall). The driveway flatwork itself typically does not need one.
  • NC 811 utility locate — mandatory and free; call before any digging. A/C lineset, electrical, and possibly irrigation run through that side yard.
  • Impervious surface check — southern Wake County has watershed overlays with impervious caps. ~600 sq ft is small, but worth confirming against the lot's allowed percentage, especially with the shed and porch proposals also on the board.
  • No new street curb-cut, so no NCDOT/county driveway permit needed.

Verify On-Site First

Before forms or saw blades: confirm whether the garage side wall is load-bearing (look in the attic at where the trusses bear) since that sets the header size; measure the driveway width, the run from driveway edge to garage wall, the side-wall length, and the A/C location and clearances; shoot the grade across the side yard so the drainage design is real numbers, not a guess; and call 811.

Cost & Reality Check

Option A lands roughly $4,500–$7,500 DIY-leaning (you do base, forms, and door; hire only the concrete finish) or $8,000–$12,000 fully contracted. The honest reality check: a curved 8–10-yard slab goes off fast and is genuinely hard to finish flat solo. Best case a DIY pour comes out clean; realistically, hiring the finish crew for that one day is cheap insurance against a lumpy driveway you stare at for twenty years.

Decision for Bekah

The fork is scope: a clean side spur that does the job (Option A, ~$5–7k DIY) versus a full side parking court (Option B, bigger and pricier) versus the no-curve budget pad (Option C). The man-door and the spur are independent enough to phase — door first or concrete first both work. Which matters more here: keeping it tight and cheap, or building the side yard into a real second parking and trailer area while the equipment is already on site?
