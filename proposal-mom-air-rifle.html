The Package

Mom's shoulders can't run a break-barrel springer, and she's never going to babysit a hand pump or compressor. That killed every conventional air rifle option twice over (researched Jan 2026, re-verified Aug 2026). The Komplete NCR is the only rifle on the market where she owns zero infrastructure: screw in a pre-filled 3,600 psi nitrogen cartridge, load the 10-round mag, shoot.

The .22 version pushes 11.9gr pellets at 975 fps for 25 fpe, which is genuine clean-kill energy on squirrels, not the marginal 600–700 fps CO2 zone. Nitrogen doesn't bleed off between sessions, so the rifle sits in the closet charged and ready for weeks. Comes with a 4x32 scope, two mags, and it's quiet enough for a residential backyard.

The License Fine Print

Three things worth knowing before buying the license, all NC-specific:

Landowner exemption

NC resident landowners can hunt their own property without a license. If the squirrels stay strictly in her backyard and she owns it, the license is technically optional. The license is still worth gifting: it makes her legal anywhere in the state, covers small game everywhere, and removes any gray area.

Hunter education: plan locked~$15–$30

NC requires a hunter ed certificate for first-time license buyers. The plan: run her through the hunter-ed.com NC online course, the same one Benny completed in March 2026 for the Fort Liberty permit. Fully online, prints a temporary certification card immediately on completion, no field day, done in an afternoon sitting with Benny. Fallback if she stalls: the apprentice permit lets her hunt under Benny's supervision in the meantime.

Senior pricing: RESOLVED, she qualifies$19 once

Confirmed: Mom hits the NC senior tier, so skip the $30 annual entirely. The Senior Lifetime Sportsman License is $19, once, forever, and it's the full sportsman package: statewide hunting including big game and game lands, waterfowl privileges, and all inland fishing including trout waters. The adult version of this license is $630, so this is one of the best license deals in the country. Buy it once and she never touches license paperwork again.

Season note: NC gray squirrel season runs roughly mid-October through February with generous bag limits. Off-season backyard squirrels doing property damage fall under depredation rules. In practice, an October gift is perfectly timed.

The Upgrade Path

If she shoots enough that cartridge cost starts to sting, the Umarex Komplete PCP Tank ($113) screws in where the cartridge goes and turns the rifle into a normal refillable PCP: 132cc at 4,500 psi with a rotating pressure gauge she can read herself, reviewers reporting 100+ shots per fill. Per-shot cost drops to just the pellet. Pairs with a hand pump (~$50–$70) or a cheap compressor, and she can swap back to NitroAir cartridges anytime. The cheaper alternative is the gauge-less Air Venturi cylinder ($100, 3,625 psi), but for $13 more the gauge and higher pressure make the Umarex tank the pick.

Reality check: the tank ships empty and neither bundle nor kit includes a fill source, so the pump purchase is what actually unlocks it. This is the Christmas follow-up gift if the rifle gets used. Umarex also sells a "Komplete Bundle" (rifle + cartridges + pellets + wipes + this tank, $364.75) that's a wash vs a la carte pricing: fine if committing to the full setup day one, skippable otherwise.

Why Not Something Else

Every alternative fails one of her two constraints. Break-barrels (Gamo, Crosman Fire, Barra TPR) need the exact shoulder motion she can't do, and a cocking aid only cuts force 20–30% while keeping the bad motion. CO2 rifles like the Crosman 1077 are effortless but 600–700 fps is marginal for ethical kills. Better PCPs (Notos, Avenger, Iconix) are stronger rifles per dollar but all need a pump or compressor on day one. The Komplete is the only zero-infrastructure option with real hunting power.
