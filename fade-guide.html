THE DIY FADE

A no-BS guide for guys who refuse to pay $35+ for a haircut. Medium fade, clippers, two mirrors, zero excuses.

GEAR CHECK

Before you touch your head, make sure you have everything. Nothing worse than being half-faded with no way to finish.

  • Clippers with guards #0.5 through #8 — Wahl, Andis, or Remington all work. The guard set is non-negotiable.
  • Two mirrors — A wall mirror + a handheld mirror. You cannot fade the back of your head blind. This is the #1 reason home fades look like shit.
  • A trimmer/edger — For your neckline and around the ears. Your clipper set probably came with one.
  • A barber cape or old towel — Hair gets everywhere.
  • A comb — For checking length and lifting hair to inspect your work.
  • Good lighting — Bathroom with overhead lights. If you can't see it, you can't blend it.
  • Spray bottle with water — Damp hair is easier to cut evenly.
Pro tip: Set up your handheld mirror behind you, angled toward the wall mirror so you can see the back of your head with both hands free. Lean it against something stable, or get a cheap mirror stand from Amazon. Some guys mount a second mirror on the wall opposite. Game changer.

KNOW YOUR GUARDS

The numbers on your guard combs correspond to hair length. Lower number = shorter cut. This is the most important thing to understand before you start.

GuardLengthWhat It Does
No guard~1/16"Near-skin. The very bottom of your fade. Stubble.
#0.51/16"Just above skin. Optional ultra-short zone.
#11/8"Your bottom fade zone. Very short but not bald.
#1.53/16"Blending between #1 and #2. Key for smooth transitions.
#21/4"Mid fade zone. The workhorse guard.
#33/8"Upper fade zone. Where sides meet the top.

Golden rule: You can always go shorter. You cannot go longer. Start with a bigger guard than you think you need and work down. If you're nervous, start with #2 as your shortest and #4 as your blend-out point.

THE FOUR ZONES

A medium fade divides the sides and back of your head into zones. Each zone gets a different guard. The magic is in the blending between them.

Side view — zone heights are approximate. Your head shape determines exact placement. The curve of your skull is your natural guide.

ZONE 1
#1
Neckline to just above the ears. ~1 inch tall.
ZONE 2
#2
Above ears to mid-temple. ~1.5 inches tall.
ZONE 3
#3
Mid-temple to where the head starts curving on top.
ZONE 4
#4–#7
The top. Whatever length you want up here.
Feel your skull. Run your hand up the side of your head. You'll feel where the head starts to curve inward toward the top, that's roughly where Zone 3 ends and the top begins. The fade should live below that ridge, not on top of it.

THE CUT — STEP BY STEP

0

PREP

Wash and towel-dry your hair. Slightly damp is ideal, not soaking wet. Comb everything down flat. Set up your mirrors. Drape your cape/towel. Deep breath.

1

TOP FIRST — SET YOUR LENGTH

Attach your longest guard (#7 for about 7/8" or whatever you want on top) and run it over the entire top of your head, front to back. Follow the contour of your skull. This sets your ceiling. Don't worry about the sides yet.

If you want the top longer than a #8 guard can handle, skip the clippers up top. Use scissors with a comb to trim to your desired length. Lift sections of hair between your fingers and cut to the same length all around.
2

SIDES & BACK — THE LONGEST GUARD FIRST

Put on your #3 guard. Starting from the bottom of your sideburns, run the clippers UP the side of your head. Stop about 2 inches above your ear, where you feel the head starting to curve. Go all the way around, sides and back. This is Zone 3 and everything below it. You're establishing the rough shape.

Use steady, even pressure. Don't dig in. Let the guard do the work. Go over each area 2–3 times to make sure you haven't missed spots.

3

DROP TO #2 — BUILD THE FADE

Switch to the #2 guard. Now cut from the bottom up, but only go about HALFWAY up the area you just cut with #3. That's roughly from the bottom of your ear to the top of your ear. This creates your first visible transition line. Don't worry if the line is visible right now, you'll blend it.

4

DROP TO #1 — THE SHORT ZONE

Switch to #1. Go from the bottom up, stopping about 1 inch above your ear/neckline. This is just the very lowest band. You should now have three distinct lengths on the side if you look closely.

5

THE BLEND — THIS IS WHERE THE FADE HAPPENS

This is the whole game. You're going to blur the lines between your zones. There are two techniques, and using both together gives the best result.

TECHNIQUE A: THE FLICK

Using whatever guard you're working with, start at the bottom of a transition line and move the clippers upward. As you hit the line, FLICK your wrist outward, away from the head. This scoops the hair at the transition rather than cutting a hard line. Think of it like a slight C-motion that curves away from your skull at the top of each stroke.

🤚

The motion: Start flat against the head. Push upward. At the blend line, rotate your wrist so the clipper peels away from your head. It's like scooping ice cream off the side of your skull. Start slow. The flick is gentle, not dramatic. You're creating a gradient, not carving a trench.

TECHNIQUE B: THE TAPER LEVER

Most quality clippers have a taper lever on the side. This is a cheat code. When the lever is OPEN (pushed away from the blade), the blade cuts longer. When CLOSED (flush against the blade), it cuts shorter. You get about half a guard size of range from this lever.

OPEN
Longest cut on this guard. Start here.
HALF
Medium. Blend the middle.
CLOSED
Shortest on this guard. Bottom of the section.

Here's how to use it: With your #2 guard on, set the lever to OPEN and cut the full Zone 2 area. Then close the lever HALFWAY and cut only the bottom half of Zone 2. Then close the lever ALL THE WAY and just hit the very bottom of Zone 2 with short flicks. This creates three micro-lengths within a single guard, and that's what makes it look blended instead of stepped.

Do this for every guard transition. Use both the flick motion AND the lever together. Each zone boundary gets the three-pass lever treatment plus the flick-out. This is how barbers get that buttery smooth gradient.
6

BLEND TOP INTO SIDES

The trickiest transition is where the sides meet the top. Use your #3 or #4 guard (depending on your top length) with the lever open, and feather upward into the top hair using the flick technique. Comb the top hair down and look for any harsh lines. If you see a line, use the next guard up with lever closed and lightly flick across it.

Take your time here. This is what people actually see. Comb, inspect, adjust. Repeat until the transition looks smooth.

7

THE BACK

This is where your two-mirror setup earns its keep. Hold your handheld mirror behind your head (or prop it up) and look at the reflection in your wall mirror. Do the exact same zone/blend process on the back as you did on the sides. Go slower here since you're working in reverse vision.

Common disaster zone: The back of the head where it meets the neck. Take extra care blending here. The occipital bone (that bump on the back of your skull) is where most people screw up because the contour changes. Use the flick technique aggressively around this area.
8

CLEAN UP — NECKLINE & EARS

Flip your trimmer on. Clean up the neckline by creating a slight rounded or squared-off shape at the back. Don't go too high, follow your natural neckline roughly. Trim around the ears carefully, pulling the ear down with your free hand. Edge up the sideburns to be even on both sides.

Neckline shape: Rounded looks the most natural and is the most forgiving. Squared off (blocked) is also fine. Avoid trying to do a sharp lineup on yourself, that requires precision that's very hard to achieve in a mirror.
9

FINAL INSPECTION

Brush off all loose hair. Comb everything down. Use your mirror setup to inspect every angle: both sides, the back, the top, the transitions. Look for lines, uneven spots, or patches. Touch up anything that stands out. But here's the most important thing:

STOP WHILE YOU'RE AHEAD. This is the #1 rule. 80% of DIY fade disasters happen during "touch-ups" where guys keep chasing perfection and end up cutting too short or creating new lines trying to fix old ones. If it looks 85% as good as a barbershop fade, that's a win. Put the clippers down. Walk away.

MISTAKES THAT WILL RUIN YOUR DAY

🚫

Going too short too fast. Starting with a #1 on the sides and realizing you wanted a #2 bottom. Now you're stuck. Always start longer.

🚫

Skipping the lever technique. If you just slap on guards and cut straight lines, you get steps, not a fade. The lever + flick combo is non-negotiable.

🚫

Cutting the back blind. "I'll just wing it." No. You won't. Set up your mirrors. Every time.

🚫

Inconsistent guard heights. The #2 zone should be the same height all the way around your head. Use your ears as landmarks. If Zone 1 ends at the top of the ear on the left, it ends there on the right too.

🚫

Perfectionism spiral. "Let me just fix this one spot..." Stop. It's hair. It grows back in a week. A 90% fade beats a 0% fade from overcorrecting into a buzz cut.

🚫

First attempt before an important event. Your first few self-fades will be rough. Practice when you have nowhere important to be for a few days.

QUICK REFERENCE CARD

Bookmark this or screenshot it. This is the compressed version for when you're standing in the bathroom ready to go.

MEDIUM FADE — CHEAT SHEET

1. Top first — #7 guard (or scissors), front to back
2. #3 guard — all sides & back, bottom to 2" above ear
3. #2 guard — bottom to top of ear only
4. #1 guard — bottom 1 inch only
5. Blend each line: lever open → half → closed + flick motion
6. #3/#4 lever-open to feather sides into top
7. Mirror check the back, repeat steps 2–6 there
8. Trimmer: neckline (rounded), ears, sideburns
9. Inspect all angles. Fix obvious stuff. STOP.

Time: 30–45 min your first few times. 15–20 min once you're comfortable.
Frequency: Every 2–3 weeks to keep it looking fresh.

THE LONG GAME

Your first self-fade is going to be mediocre. That's fine. Your fifth will be decent. Your tenth will be genuinely good. The savings add up fast: at $35 a cut every 3 weeks, that's over $600 a year you keep in your pocket. After a few months, you'll wonder why you ever paid someone else.

Things that will accelerate your learning: pay attention to how the hair falls when you brush it down after each pass. Develop a feel for where the guard is relative to your head even when you can't see it. Build muscle memory for the flick-out motion. And have Bekah check the back and call out any spots you missed before you put the clippers away.

Maintenance cuts are easier than fresh fades. Once you nail your first good fade, maintaining it every 2 weeks is much simpler since you're just following the existing shape and taking off a little growth. The hard part is the initial sculpting.
