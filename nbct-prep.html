The Four Components

What You'll Be Assessed On

Each component tests different aspects of accomplished teaching. Click to expand for full details, requirements, and strategy tips.

1

Content Knowledge

40% of score ▼

Format: Computer-based test at a Pearson VUE center. Testing window: March – June 2026.

What's on it:

  • 45 multiple-choice questions across all disability categories (not just your specialty)
  • 3 constructed response exercises: Numeracy, Assessment, and Collaboration

Selected Response Breakdown:

  • 20% — Standards I & II (Knowledge of Students + Philosophy/History/Law)
  • 20% — Standards III & IV (Diversity + Family Partnerships)
  • 30% — Standards VI & VII (Communication + Social Development/Behavior)
  • 30% — Standards V & VIII (Assessment + Curriculum & Instruction)

Strategy: This tests across ALL disability areas (Deaf/HoH, Visually Impaired, Gifted, Severe/Multiple) even if you only teach mild/moderate. Study the full spectrum. Use the practice test in the downloads section.

2

Differentiation in Instruction

20% of score ▼

Format: Portfolio entry, written narrative with student work samples.

What you submit:

  • Description and analysis of ONE featured student with complex needs
  • Evidence of how you differentiated content, process, and product
  • Student work samples showing growth over time
  • Reflection connecting your decisions to student outcomes

Key: You must use a DIFFERENT featured student than Components 3 and 4. Choose a student with complex needs who showcases your full range of differentiation skills. Every paragraph must DESCRIBE, ANALYZE, and REFLECT.

3

Teaching Practice & Learning Environment

20% of score ▼

Format: Portfolio entry with TWO video recordings of your teaching + written commentary.

What you submit:

  • Two videos showing different instructional approaches
  • Must show at least 2 of 3 grouping types: whole group, small group, one-on-one
  • Written analysis with specific video timestamps
  • Evidence of learning environment management, engagement, and responsiveness

Video tips: Choose lessons that show your BEST practice — active engagement, real-time responsiveness, multiple strategies, genuine differentiation. Use timestamps in your writing (e.g., "At 3:42, when Marcus struggled with..."). Assessors want to SEE what you describe.

4

Effective & Reflective Practitioner

20% of score ▼

Format: Portfolio entry. The longest and hardest component. Veterans say this one transforms you into an NBCT.

What you submit — evidence across 4 themes:

  • Assessment & Student Learning: How you use data to drive decisions
  • Family & Community Partnerships: Genuine collaboration beyond parent conferences
  • Professional Growth & Advocacy: How you grow and advocate for students
  • Collaboration & Leadership: Impact beyond your own classroom

Critical strategy: START THIS COMPONENT FIRST. It requires the most evidence gathering and the longest writing. Organize by THEMES, not chronology. Gather documentation NOW — emails, meeting notes, PD certificates, collaboration logs, parent communication records.

Foundation

The 12 ENS Standards

Everything in your certification is assessed against these standards. Know them cold. Every piece of writing should reference at least one.

Foundations for Effective Practice

I

Knowledge of Students

Use development & learning knowledge; insightful observation; recognize diversity; advocate for each student's unique needs

II

Philosophy, History & Law

IDEA, ADA, FAPE, LRE, due process; ethical practice; know the legal framework inside and out

III

Diversity

Equitable treatment, fairness, respect; ensure access for ALL students regardless of background

IV

Family Partnerships

Collaborate with families as true partners; culturally responsive communication; beyond the annual IEP meeting

Student Learning & Development

V

Assessment

Multiple assessment types; formal & informal; data-driven decisions; collaborative analysis with teams

VI

Communication

Language acquisition; unique communication strategies; AAC; specialist collaboration; BICS vs. CALP awareness

VII

Social Development & Behavior

Teach social skills; self-determination; FBA/BIP; positive behavior supports; safe environments

VIII

Curriculum & Instruction

Core + expanded curriculum; literacy/numeracy; differentiation; life skills; transition planning

Roles & Practices

IX

Learning Environment

Caring, stimulating, safe; democratic values; student responsibility and self-advocacy

X

Instructional Resources

Select, adapt, create varied resources; assistive technology integration; UDL principles

XI

Collaboration

Leadership; work with educators, specialists, families, and community; co-teaching models

XII

Reflective Practice

Analyze, evaluate, synthesize your practice; lifelong learning; stay current with research

Your Roadmap

14-Week Preparation Timeline

February through May 2026. This is aggressive but absolutely doable, especially with AI tools in your stack.

Phase 1 — Weeks 1-3 (Feb)
Foundation & Registration
Register + purchase all 4 components by Feb 28. Download all NBPTS materials. Read the 12 standards cover to cover. Start gathering evidence for Component 4 (emails, PD certs, collaboration logs, parent comms). Identify featured students for Components 2 & 3.
Phase 2 — Weeks 4-8 (Mar)
Portfolio Development
Start writing Component 4 FIRST (it's the longest). Record videos for Component 3 — film multiple lessons, pick the best. Draft Components 2 and 3. Use AI to review drafts against rubrics paragraph by paragraph.
Phase 3 — Weeks 9-12 (Apr)
Revision & Submission
Deep revision using rubric criteria as a checklist. Every paragraph must pass the "So What?" test. Upload to ePortfolio system. Submit all portfolio components by mid-May deadline.
Phase 4 — Weeks 13-14 (May-Jun)
Component 1 Testing
Schedule Pearson VUE appointment. Use practice tests and AI-generated quizzes to drill across ALL disability areas. Focus on constructed response writing — Numeracy, Assessment, Collaboration. Take the test.

Time estimate: The NBPTS says 200-400 hours. That was before AI existed. With the tools listed below, you're looking at 120-200 hours. That's roughly 10-15 hours per week for 14 weeks.

Money on the table: Every month you delay is salary you lose. A 12% raise in NC on a teacher salary is thousands per year, for the life of the certification. If you have a master's, it's 18%. Start now.

Know the Rubric

How Your Writing Gets Scored

The difference between passing and failing is the difference between describing WHAT you did and explaining WHY. Here's what each level looks like.

Level 1
Little or No Evidence

Score: 0.75-1.75. Vague descriptions, no analysis, no connection to standards or student needs. "I used small groups." That's it. No why, no how, no outcome.

Level 2
Limited Evidence

Score: 1.75-2.75. Describes what you did but doesn't explain reasoning. "I modified the worksheet for Marcus." Okay, but WHY that modification? What did you observe?

Level 3
Clear Evidence

Score: 2.75-3.75. Describes + explains WHY. Connects decisions to student data, standards, and research. "Because Marcus's assessment showed difficulty with place value at the tens level, I..."

Level 4
Clear & Convincing

Score: 3.75-4.25. Describes + analyzes + reflects with nuance. Multiple perspectives. Genuine insight. Acknowledges complexity and what you'd change. This is the gold standard.

The "So What?" Test: After every paragraph, ask "So what?" If you can't immediately explain why what you wrote matters for student learning, the assessor can't either. Rewrite until the answer is obvious.

Must-Know Law

Critical Legal Knowledge for Component 1

These will appear on the test and should be woven into your portfolio writing. Know them cold.

IDEA 2004

Individuals with Disabilities Education Act. Guarantees FAPE (Free Appropriate Public Education) in the LRE (Least Restrictive Environment). Requires IEPs. Transition planning by age 16.

Section 504

Civil rights law prohibiting disability discrimination. Broader eligibility than IDEA. Covers students who don't qualify for an IEP but need accommodations. 504 plans.

ADA

Americans with Disabilities Act. Prohibits discrimination in all public services. Ensures physical and programmatic accessibility. Broader than just education.

FERPA

Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act. Protects student record privacy. Rights transfer to student at age 18 or college enrollment. Governs who can access IEP data.

Manifestation Determination

Required for suspensions over 10 days. Team must determine if behavior is related to disability. If yes, student cannot be expelled — must revise BIP and placement instead.

Endrew F. v. Douglas County (2017)

Supreme Court case. IEPs must enable "progress appropriate in light of the child's circumstances." Raised the bar from "merely more than de minimis" educational benefit. Know this case.

LRE Continuum

Least Restrictive Environment. General education classroom is the default. Must justify any removal. Continuum: gen ed → resource → self-contained → separate school → residential.

RTI / MTSS

Response to Intervention / Multi-Tiered System of Supports. Three tiers of increasingly intensive support. Tier 1 = universal, Tier 2 = targeted, Tier 3 = intensive/individualized.

Insider Knowledge

Pro Tips & Magic Phrases

Words and phrases that assessors are trained to look for. Weave these into your portfolio writing naturally.

✅ Phrases That Signal Accomplished Teaching

✓ "Based on assessment data, I determined that..."
✓ "I differentiated by modifying the [content/process/product]..."
✓ "In collaboration with [specific person/role]..."
✓ "This decision was informed by [student's] IEP goal of..."
✓ "I reflected on this outcome and adjusted my approach by..."
✓ "To promote self-determination, I provided [student] with..."
✓ "The family partnership with [student's] parents included..."
✓ "Evidence of growth can be seen in [specific data point]..."

🚫 Words That Tank Your Score

✗ "All students" (too vague — name specific students)
✗ "I always" / "I never" (unrealistic absolutes)
✗ "Good job" / "Great lesson" (empty evaluation)
✗ "The worksheet" (which one? what was on it? why?)
✗ "I helped the student" (how? what strategy? why that one?)
✗ "It went well" (what data proves it? what would you change?)

Common Mistake #1: Writing a narrative instead of an analysis. Don't tell a story about your classroom. Describe what you did, explain WHY (connected to data/standards), and reflect on the impact. Every. Single. Paragraph.

Common Mistake #2: Using the same featured student in multiple components. You MUST use a different student for Components 2, 3, and 4. Plan this from day one. Choose students who showcase different aspects of your practice.
