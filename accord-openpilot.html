Teaching a 2017 Accord to drive itself.

The car already has the camera, the radar, and the electric steering. It was never impossible, comma just never shipped the 40 lines of config that tell openpilot how to talk to it. This is that missing work: sniff the bus, find the steering message, decode it, and ship the port back to opendbc.

// bottom line up front

A 2017 Honda Accord EX is a 9th-generation car on Honda's Nidec ADAS platform (the 2018 is a different 10th-gen Bosch car, don't confuse them). Nidec Hondas are well supported by openpilot, the Civic, CR-V, Pilot, Odyssey, Ridgeline and Acura ILX all run it. The Accord was just never added, because nobody ever submitted a finished port.

Three things make it a green light. One, the factory ADAS camera is dead and uncalibrated after a windshield swap Honda refused to recalibrate, so the comma device just replaces it, no fighting a working stock system. Two, openpilot bug #474 is literally a 9th-gen Accord responding to the standard Nidec steering command, so the electric steering already listens. Three, a Nidec port is almost entirely fingerprint + DBC data, not new software.

So the plan isn't "invent self-driving." It's reverse-engineer this specific car's CAN bus, write a DBC and a fingerprint, tune the steering, and open a pull request so the port lives in comma's repo and anyone with this Accord gets it.

Everything below the "what it looks like" line is simulated to show the exact process and outputs. The car hasn't been on jack stands yet, this is the map, drawn to scale.

01 what "drives itself" actually means

Level 2. You are always the driver.

openpilot is advanced lane-centering plus adaptive cruise, the same category as Honda Sensing or Tesla Autopilot, just open source and better tuned. On a Nidec car it does two jobs at once and does them well:

Lateral · steering

It reads the road from the comma device's camera and sends torque to the electric power steering to hold the lane. This is the part that needs the message we have to find.

Longitudinal · gas + brake

Nidec is special: openpilot drives the gas and brakes itself off the radar, full stop-and-go, instead of leaning on stock cruise like Bosch cars. Better following, smoother stops.

!

Not hands-off, not eyes-off. Hands stay on the wheel, eyes stay on the road, and you take over instantly. No insurer covers openpilot, treat it exactly like the driver assist it is. The upside is a system you understand top to bottom and tune yourself.

03 the hardware

One sniffer, one brain, one harness.

The research phase needs a cheap read-only sniffer. Running openpilot for real needs the comma device and the Honda harness. Three Ocelots are already on the shelf as a spare CAN-gateway path if the steering ever needs translation, but on this car it shouldn't.

red panda · $99

USB-C CAN sniffer. Plugs into OBD-II, streams every frame to the MacBook. This is the whole Phase-1 kit, you can start decoding before spending real money.

comma four · $999

The brain and the camera. Runs openpilot, watches the road, sends steering. Physically replaces the dead factory ADAS camera at the mirror.

Honda Nidec harness · $99

Plug-and-play loom that taps the camera connector, isolates the stock camera onto its own bus, and hands the comma device both F-CAN and the radar bus.

3× Ocelot · owned

STM32 CAN gateways, spare insurance. If the steering ever needed message translation, one sits inline and remaps it in firmware. Not expected here.

04 the core insight

It's mostly copy-paste. One message isn't.

Every Nidec Honda speaks the same dialect. Brake, gas, speed, gear, wheel speeds, radar, and even the steering command share IDs and formats across the whole family. That means ~70% of this Accord's port can be lifted straight from its Civic and Acura ILX siblings. The remaining slice is confirming this specific car's message layout and firmware, then tuning.

§

The golden rule. A Nidec Honda port is fingerprint + DBC, not new control code. If you catch yourself rewriting carcontroller.py, stop, you're fighting a data mismatch, not a real problem. The steering command 0xE4 path is shared and untouched.

05 the toolkit

Four scripts, built and verified.

These already exist and passed on synthetic CAN. You run them the moment a panda is plugged in. The first one is locked read-only so it physically cannot move the car.

capture.py — read-only logger

Forced into SAFETY_SILENT, cannot transmit. You drive and tap labels (wheel-left, brake, acc-set) as you go. Everything downstream reads its logs.

correlate.py — the money tool

For each labeled action, ranks which (bus, address, byte) moves in the window around the marker versus its resting behavior. The top hit is your steer / brake / gas / cruise message. Verified: it isolated the steer byte and rejected noise.

diff.py — engaged-vs-off isolator

Diffs a log with LKAS/ACC off against one with it on. Whatever appears only when engaged is a camera message. Verified: it surfaced 0xE4 as engaged-only.

decode_check.py — DBC validator

Runs a candidate DBC against a real log and prints every signal's min / max / last value plus checksum pass rate, so a wrong guess shows up as a nonsense range instantly.

07 why it's safe to attempt

Three walls between you and a mistake.

1 · read-only research

The entire decode phase runs with the panda in SAFETY_SILENT. It cannot transmit. You can log the bus for weeks without any risk of moving anything.

2 · firmware torque limit

The panda's Honda-Nidec safety model bounds steering torque in firmware, below the app. Even a buggy command gets clamped, as the jack-stand test showed. This limit is never loosened.

3 · the EPS itself

Honda's own steering rack ignores commands beyond its rate and torque envelope. A worst case is a cleared fault code, not a yanked wheel, and you're always holding it anyway.

08 definition of done

When is it actually finished?

  • ✓The car names itself. Plug in a comma four, it reads HONDA_ACCORD_9G_NIDEC, no forced fingerprint.
  • ✓It steers and paces traffic on the highway, lane-centered, stop-and-go cruise off the radar, tuned to hold within a few inches.
  • ✓The port lives in comma's repo. DBC, values, fingerprints, and tuning merged into commaai/opendbc, not a private fork.
  • ✓Anyone else with this Accord gets it for free, the port shows up in the supported-car list. That's the finish line, the work outlives the one car.
⚑

The one real risk left. Some Accord trims (the "serialsteering" ones noted in sunnypilot's docs) may not use the standard 0xE4 command. Phase 4's jack-stand test is the gate: if the wheel answers 0xE4, the whole path above holds. If it doesn't, an Ocelot goes inline to translate, and the timeline gets longer. Everything before that gate is already known-good.
