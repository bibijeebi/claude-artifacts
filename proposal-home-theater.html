Proposal #037

The Sound Plan

We own four JBL 305 studio monitors, a 10″ JBL sub, a good DAC and a pile of cables, and right now two speakers sit in the garage while the living room plays movies through the projector's built-in speaker. This puts all of it to work: bedroom first, one remote, no vibration; living room second, everything hidden in the walls.

What We Already Have

Four JBL 305P MkII powered monitors (two in the bedroom, two in the garage), the JBL LSR310S sub, the SMSL D-6 DAC, an HDMI audio extractor, two wall mounts for 305s, and XLR cables. Nothing in this proposal replaces any of it. The 305 is a real studio monitor, and a pair of them fed properly beats any soundbar in the price range several times over. The problem has never been the gear, it's been how it's hooked up.

Bedroom today: the TV is a 75″ TCL Roku (75S451) over the dresser, the two 305s are sitting on the dresser top with nothing feeding them. Living room today: the Roku Ultra (model 4802X, HDMI audio only) runs to the Optoma projector on the kitchen ceiling, and sound comes out of the projector's 3-watt speaker. The cables to the projector hang loose down the wall onto the kitchen shelf.

Phase 1: Bedroom

Speakers onto floor stands$69.99

Foam pads on the dresser were the first idea, and they'd still leave the dresser humming and the drawer pulls buzzing on bass. Floor stands take the speaker off the furniture entirely. There's room either side of the dresser once the guitar and the gun cabinet move. The pick is the Ultimate Support JS-MS70, a proper studio monitor stand: 9×9″ non-slip platform, 50 lb rating per stand (the 305 is 10.4 lb), pin-locked heights of 32.25″, 36.25″, 40.25″ and 44.25″, rubber feet for the hardwood.

Height math: our bed sits low, ear height is roughly 36″ lying back and 42″ sitting up. At the lowest pin the tweeter lands around 41″, right in that window, so no tilt needed, just toe the speakers in toward the bed. The Perlesmith stands on Amazon were checked and rejected: the $50 model is rated 11 lb for a 10.4 lb speaker on a 4.7 lb pole, and the $70 “22 lb” model is a fixed 27.56″ glass plate the 305 overhangs with no clamp.

One remote for volume$179.99

The whole reason this hasn't been done: studio monitors have no remote, and the TV's optical output is fixed volume. The fix is HDMI ARC, which carries the TV's volume commands along with the audio. The Fosi Audio ZD3 is a balanced DAC/preamp with an HDMI ARC input, XLR outputs that plug straight into the 305s, and Fosi confirms the TV remote controls its volume over CEC. $179.99 sold by Fosi on Amazon, free 30-day returns. The TCL remote then runs everything: power, volume, mute. Nothing else to pick up.

Why not the $18 headphone-jack cable: it works, but a TV's headphone output is a cheap amp feeding fixed-gain monitors, and any hiss it makes gets played all night. The ZD3 removes that risk entirely and keeps the single remote.

Setup, in order: both 305s off and switched to +4 dBu (the ZD3's XLR out is hot at 5 Vrms). HDMI from the ZD3 to the TV port labeled eARC. ZD3 bypass switch off, volume low, then power the 305s. On the TV: Settings → System → Control other devices (CEC), check ARC, System audio control, System standby. Settings → Audio → S/PDIF and ARC → PCM-Stereo (the ZD3 doesn't decode Dolby). If sound still plays from the TV speakers, pick the ARC device as the output by hand.
Two quirks on record: one owner reports the ZD3 waking itself back up over CEC about 30 seconds after the TV shuts off; if ours does that, uncheck System standby and leave the DAC on, it idles at nothing. And the 305s have no trigger input, so they stay powered; a $10 smart plug on both fixes that if it bothers anyone. Nobody online has run a ZD3 on a TCL Roku TV specifically, which is what the 30-day return window is for. Worst case, the ZD3 still has an optical input and we're back to a second remote, with a much better DAC than the headphone jack.

Phase 2: Living Room

The living room already has all the parts for a real 2.1 system. The two garage 305s go on the wall mounts pulled off the dead bedroom wall, either side of the screen at ear height, and the sub goes on the floor by the credenza. The chain is the one we used to run:

Roku Ultra → HDMI extractor → (HDMI to projector) + (optical to SMSL D-6) → XLR to LSR310S sub, crossover at 80 Hz → XLR to each 305

The D-6 in preamp mode has an IR remote for volume, so it lives on the credenza with line of sight to the couch. The Roku remote is RF and can hide anywhere. Roku audio set to Stereo/PCM so the extractor and the D-6 stay happy.

The part that makes it clean is moving every source box to the screen wall. Right now the Roku, extractor and cables all live at the ceiling end. Once they're on the credenza, the only things that go up to the projector are one HDMI cable and power. The 30 ft of loose cable draped down the kitchen wall goes away.

Into the walls, to code~$150–260

Projector run: a CL3-rated fiber HDMI cable from the credenza, up the screen wall, through the attic, down to the projector. Fiber because copper HDMI gets flaky past 25 ft; CL3 because that's the in-wall fire rating. It's directional, Source end at the extractor, Display end at the projector's HDMI 2.0 port.

Speaker locations: behind each wall-mounted 305, an Arlington TVBU505K recessed box, power on one side and low voltage on the other, so the plug and the XLR both disappear into the wall. The speakers' power cords can't just be fished through drywall (NEC 400.12 bans flexible cords concealed in walls), so each box gets a real receptacle fed with NM-B off the nearest existing outlet. The 305's power switch is on the back and unreachable once mounted, so both receptacles go on one smart switch.

Audio runs: Belden 9451 by the foot, a CMR-rated shielded pair (an allowed substitute for CL2/CL3), soldered to XLR ends. Runs go from the sub at floor level up inside the front wall to each speaker box, never through the attic.

Pass-throughs: VCE brush plates ($7–10 a pair) behind the credenza for the cable bundle and wherever the fiber HDMI enters and exits. Low voltage only, never in the same box as a receptacle unless the box is divided like the Arlington.

Scope check: this is a stereo-plus-sub system, not surround. The Roku-to-projector chain is stereo anyway, surround would mean buying a receiver with pre-outs, and it would leave the bedroom with nothing. On a 1080p projector, 2.1 done right is about 90% of the experience for the price of cable and boxes.

Order Of Operations

  1. Buy the stands and the ZD3. Set up the bedroom in one evening. If ARC misbehaves with the TCL, return the ZD3 inside 30 days and decide between the optical route and the $18 cable.
  2. Pull the two bedroom wall mounts and hang them either side of the living room screen. Move the two garage 305s in, run the chain surface-mounted first to confirm placement and levels.
  3. Weekend job: cut in the Arlington boxes, run NM-B to the new receptacles, pull the Belden and the fiber HDMI, brush plates, patch and paint. Move the Roku and extractor to the credenza.

Phase 1 stands on its own. Phase 2 can wait a month or a year and nothing about Phase 1 changes.
