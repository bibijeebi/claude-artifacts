Part 01

The Four Phases

The menstrual cycle is a recurring ~28-day hormonal program the body runs to potentially enable pregnancy. Day 1 is always the first day of actual bleeding, not spotting. Everything else counts from there.

🔴
Menstruation
Days 1 – 5
The uterine lining sheds because no pregnancy happened. This is the period. Day 1 = first day of real flow.
🌱
Follicular
Days 1 – 13
Overlaps with menstruation. The brain signals the ovaries to mature an egg. Estrogen rises. This phase varies the most in length.
⚡
Ovulation
~Day 14
LH spikes. The egg is released. Survives 12–24 hours. This is what LH strips detect — the spike happens 24–36 hours before release.
🌙
Luteal
Days 15 – 28
Progesterone rises to prep the lining for implantation. If no egg implants, progesterone drops and the cycle resets. Most consistent phase: 12–14 days.
Key Insight

The follicular phase (before ovulation) is unpredictable and can vary by weeks. The luteal phase (after ovulation) is a nearly fixed 12–14 day timer unique to each woman. This is why catching ovulation is more useful than just counting days.

Four hormones drive the whole system. Understanding when each rises and falls explains everything else.

Part 03

LH Strips — What They Are & How to Read Them

An LH strip tests urine for the luteinizing hormone surge that happens right before ovulation. The strip has two lines: a control line (always appears if the test works) and a test line (gets darker as LH rises).

How to Read the Lines

Negative Test line barely visible. LH is at baseline. Not ovulating soon.
Approaching Test line lighter than control. LH rising but not there yet.
Positive ✓ Test line as dark or darker than control. Ovulation in ~24–36 hours.

The Math After a Positive

Once you get a positive LH strip:


→ Ovulation happens ~24–36 hours after the surge


→ Luteal phase runs 12–14 days after that (most women are consistent at 13 or 14)


→ Next period = ovulation date + ~14 days, ±1–2 days


After a few cycles you'll learn her exact luteal length and narrow it down further.

Product Recommendation

Wondfo or Easy@Home bulk strips from Amazon — ~$15–20 for 50 strips. Same antibody chemistry as $30+ branded tests. No reason to pay more. Test once or twice daily starting around Day 10, using afternoon urine (not first morning — LH surges mid-morning, shows in afternoon urine).

Part 04

How to Actually Track

Two methods, from lowest to highest effort. Either works. Combining both gives the best picture.

1

Find past period start dates (if you can)

Scroll back through texts, photos, or calendar events where you can infer "day 1" — the first day of actual flow. Timestamps on those give you historical cycle lengths. Even 3–4 dates gives you a useful average.

2

Calculate her average cycle length

Cycle length = days from one Day 1 to the next Day 1 (don't count the end date). Average those. That number is your baseline prediction for future cycles. Add it to her last known Day 1.

3

Start testing with LH strips around Day 10

Test once in the afternoon daily. The moment the test line is as dark as the control line, log that date. That's your confirmed ovulation marker. Add 13–14 days and you have the next period prediction, accurate to ±1–2 days.

4

After each period, log Day 1

Just note the date. That's it. After 6 cycles you'll have her personal pattern locked in — cycle length, ovulation day relative to cycle start, luteal length. Predictions get tighter every month.

Important

Irregular cycles (PCOS, thyroid issues, high stress, significant weight change) can make even LH-based predictions less reliable because the follicular phase can extend unpredictably. If cycles are wildly irregular, that's worth discussing with a doctor.

Part 05

How Accurate Can You Get?

Accuracy depends on method and data volume. Here's the realistic picture:

Method / Data Prediction Window Notes
Calendar, 1–2 cycles ±7 days Basically a guess
Calendar, 3–5 cycles ±4–5 days Usable, rough
Calendar, 6–12 cycles ±2–3 days Solid for planning
LH strip, 1 confirmed ovulation ±2 days Better than months of calendar data
LH strip, 3+ confirmed ovulations ±1–2 days You know her luteal length exactly

For irregular women, no method reliably gets below ±4 days — the variability is biological, not a tracking failure.

Part 06

Glossary

Terms you'll encounter when reading about this stuff.

LH · Luteinizing Hormone
The hormone that spikes to trigger ovulation. LH strips measure this surge in urine.
FSH · Follicle Stimulating Hormone
Signals the ovaries to mature an egg during the follicular phase.
Estrogen
Rises during the follicular phase, building the uterine lining and peaking before ovulation.
Progesterone
Rises after ovulation in the luteal phase. Drop in progesterone triggers the period.
Follicle
A fluid-filled sac in the ovary that contains a developing egg. One matures per cycle.
Corpus Luteum
What the empty follicle becomes after releasing an egg. It produces progesterone.
Luteal Phase
Post-ovulation phase, days 15–28. Progesterone-dominant. Most consistent phase in length.
Fertile Window
The ~6 days each cycle when pregnancy is possible: 5 days before ovulation + ovulation day.
Follicular Phase
Pre-ovulation phase. Variable in length (10–20+ days). Where most cycle length variation comes from.
FAM · Fertility Awareness Method
Using cycle tracking (strips, temperature, cervical mucus) to understand fertility status.
BBT · Basal Body Temperature
Resting temperature. Rises slightly (0.2–0.5°F) after ovulation. Confirms ovulation happened.
PCOS
Polycystic Ovary Syndrome. Hormonal condition causing irregular or absent ovulation, making tracking harder.
