William Clay "Bill" Foreman

September 17, 1949 – August 16, 2022
Navy Veteran · Apollo 8 Recovery · Marathon Walker · Monument Toppler

A Navy veteran who survived being sucked into a jet intake, helped recover humanity's first lunar mission, walked a marathon at 64 to honor bombing victims, and at 70 toppled a Confederate monument because God told him to.

Bill Foreman was born in Richmond, Virginia, and grew up in Roanoke, where he remained a fixture for over six decades. A man of deep religious faith and sudden conviction, he lived a life defined by acts others might call reckless but he understood as righteous.

U.S. Navy Service (1966–1970s)

Bill enlisted in 1966 and served aboard the USS Yorktown (CVS-10) when it was designated as the primary recovery ship for the Apollo 8 mission in December 1968—humanity's first crewed spacecraft to leave Earth orbit, reach the Moon, and return safely.

He was later transferred to the USS Oriskany (CVA-34) operating off the coast of Vietnam. While serving there, Bill was seriously injured when he was sucked into the jet intake of an F-8 Crusader fighter aircraft—one of the rarest and most dangerous accidents that can occur on a carrier flight deck. He survived, recovered, and received an honorable discharge.

"He was a 17-year-old ninth-grader, one of eight kids, when he dropped out of Northside High School in 1967... His father had to sign him into the Navy."
— Dan Casey, Roanoke Times

The Boston Marathon Walk (April 2014)

After reading a Sports Illustrated article about the 2013 Boston Marathon bombing, Bill decided he had to do something. At 64 years old, with a bad knee and hip replacement surgery scheduled weeks away, he drove 12 hours overnight from Roanoke to Boston after church on Sunday.

He had no registration. No hotel reservation (he found one at the Liberty Hotel—$600/night). He talked his way in as a "volunteer walker" and began walking at 11:30 AM after all registered runners had started.

"From every age group to every race group to every religious group you could think of, and they all were there to have fun and to support the town of Boston."
— Bill Foreman, WDBJ7 interview

He crossed the finish line at approximately 11:00 PM—nearly 12 hours after he started. A Canadian couple became his "guardian angels" and walked him the last three blocks.

"I did it and I finished it."

The Confederate Monument (July 2020)

In summer 2020, following George Floyd's death and nationwide protests over Confederate monuments, Bill watched the escalating tensions with growing alarm. Roanoke had a modest Confederate monument—a 10-foot granite obelisk honoring Robert E. Lee, erected in 1960 by the United Daughters of the Confederacy just as the first two Black students were being enrolled in the city's all-white schools.

The City Council had already voted to legally remove it. But Bill feared the two-month process would spark violent clashes.

"I was touched by God to do something. I just had a vision, and it came to me."
— Bill Foreman, WDBJ7
Tuesday, July 21, 2020 — 8 PM
First Attempt

Police observed Bill tampering with the monument. They asked him to leave. He complied.

Wednesday, July 22, 2020 — 9 PM
Successful Toppling

Bill returned with a rented U-Haul, 50-pound chain, step ladder, and rope. He pulled down the monument, which broke into three pieces.

Friday, July 24, 2020
Voluntary Surrender

Bill turned himself in to police. Charged with Class 6 felony property damage.

January 7, 2021
Motion to Dismiss Hearing

The day after the Capitol riot. Prosecutor opposed dismissal: "Today more than ever, we need to remember we are a nation of laws."

January 3, 2022
Final Resolution

Pled nolo contendere to reduced misdemeanor. Paid $482.82 restitution + $500 fine.

"I wanted to prevent an incident that's going to, I believe happen. I call it the day of action, when they actually take it down or leave it up, and I believe that there's going to be both sides there with big opinions and a lot of pressure. I didn't want it to become like a mini-Richmond."
— Bill Foreman

The Aftermath: Henrietta Lacks Plaza

Lee Plaza was renamed Henrietta Lacks Plaza in honor of the Roanoke-born Black woman whose cancer cells—taken without consent in 1951—became the first immortalized human cell line and enabled countless medical breakthroughs.

On October 4, 2023, a bronze statue of Henrietta Lacks was unveiled on the spot where the Confederate monument once stood.

"On the very spot where stood a symbol of oppression, of slavery, now stands the sculpture of a Black woman."
— Sculptor Lawrence Bechtel

Life & Career

After his Navy service and medical recovery, Bill attended Virginia Tech on the GI Bill, graduating with a business degree in 1975. He spent his career at First National Exchange Bank (later Dominion Bank), rising to Commercial Banking Manager for the Roanoke Valley region and Senior Commercial Banking Relationship Manager.

He was a lifelong member of Southview Methodist Church (3539 Peters Creek Rd NW) for more than 60 years.

Bill passed away peacefully on August 16, 2022, at age 72. His Celebration of Life was held August 20, 2022, at Southview Methodist Church.
