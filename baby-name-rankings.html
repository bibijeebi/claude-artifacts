Patterns & Insights

What the data reveals about naming preferences.

⚡
Short & Punchy Wins
Dean, Eli, Rhett, Io, Luna, Mabel, Ruby, Eva. The top names are mostly 1–2 syllables. Longer names that survive (Harrison, Sterling, Oliver) have strong consonant sounds.
🌿
Nature Names Dominate Girls
Violet, Hazel, Ruby, Lily. Four of the top 13 girls are nature names. This is a clear, strong aesthetic preference in the data.
🏛
Classic ≠ Favorite
Despite having tons of classic names in the pool, they mostly settle mid-pack. The names that excite are either punchy classics (Dean, Henry) or distinctive modern picks (Sterling, Io, Luna).
🏴
Scottish/Celtic Ceiling
Sterling broke through to Tier A, but Callum, Graham, and Alistair all land 580–665. The vibe is liked in theory more than in practice.
🇫🇷
French Names Underwhelm
Remy (987), Camille (937), Eloise (883), Margot (885). All respectable, none cracked the top tier. The French aesthetic doesn't quite resonate at the gut level.
📖
Biblical Names Are Middlers
Ezra is the standout at #8 boys. Levi, Asher, Shiloh all land mid-pack. Consistently liked but never loved, except for Ezra.

How This Was Built

A multi-stage pipeline from blank slate to ranked list.

1. Initial List Building

Claude generated ~500 names across categories (Classic, Modern-strong, Scottish/Celtic, French, Italian, Hebrew/Biblical, Greek, Dutch, German/Nordic, etc.) filtered for zero negative cultural connotations. No Karens, Berthas, or Chads.

2. Conversational Triage

Names were workshopped live in conversation. Boring names got killed (BIAR), Scottish/Celtic picks got added based on Stewart clan heritage. Phonetically broken names were purged (Aoife, Caoimhe, Saoirse, etc.). Pool stabilized at ~458 names.

3. Yes/No/Maybe Voting

Using the Baby Names Lab app, names were triaged into yes/no/maybe. This filtered 458 down to 302 names (150 boys, 152 girls) that entered the ranker.

4. Paired Comparisons

917 head-to-head matchups using a Bradley-Terry model with active learning, collected through a custom keyboard-driven web app. The algorithm served the most informative pair each time, prioritizing close matchups and coverage.

5. Progressive Elimination

At 5+ avg comparisons per name, the bottom 60% was automatically eliminated, focusing all remaining effort on the top 60 per gender. Tier boundaries determined by natural gaps in the ratings (>60 point gap = new tier).

How to Read the Ratings

1000 is average. Every 100 points represents approximately a 65% expected win rate over a name 100 points lower. Within a tier, the exact ordering is soft. The tier boundaries are where the real signal is. Names marked with ~ were flagged as "maybe" during initial triage.
