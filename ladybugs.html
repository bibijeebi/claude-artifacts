Bug Identification Guide

Those "Ladybugs" in Your House? They're Not Ladybugs.

The definitive guide to telling apart native ladybugs, Asian lady beetles, and Japanese beetles, because one of them is invading your home and the other two have nothing to do with it.

The Short Version

If you have dozens or hundreds of orange-ish "ladybugs" swarming your house in fall or winter, they're Asian Lady Beetles (Harmonia axyridis). They bite a little, smell bad when crushed, and stain your stuff yellow. They're annoying, not dangerous. Japanese beetles are a completely different outdoor pest. Real ladybugs don't invade homes in swarms.

🔍 Meet the Lineup

Three bugs that people constantly confuse, but once you know what to look for, you'll never mix them up again.

The Good One
Native Ladybug
Various Coccinellidae

Bright red, round, distinct black spots. A beloved garden helper that eats aphids. You'll see a few at a time, never hundreds. Doesn't invade homes. The one everyone thinks they're seeing.

The Home Invader
Asian Lady Beetle
Harmonia axyridis

Orange to red, variable spots (or none at all), and the telltale M/W-shaped black marking behind the head. Swarms homes in fall. Bites lightly, smells when crushed, stains yellow. This is your culprit.

The Garden Destroyer
Japanese Beetle
Popillia japonica

Metallic green and copper, about half an inch. Completely different animal. Destroys plants by skeletonizing leaves. Lives outdoors in summer. Does not invade homes. Not part of your problem.

🎯 The One Thing to Look For

The M/W Marking

Flip the bug over and look at the white area directly behind its head (the pronotum). If there's a distinct black M-shaped or W-shaped marking there, it's an Asian Lady Beetle. Native ladybugs have a clean, mostly white pronotum without this marking. That's your instant tell, every single time.

⚠️ Should You Be Scared?

Short answer: no. Here's the breakdown.

No Threat

Native Ladybugs

Completely harmless garden buddies. They eat pests. If you see one, congratulations, your garden is healthy. Leave them alone.

Nuisance Only

Asian Lady Beetles

They can give you a mild pinch-bite, their secretions can stain fabrics yellow, and heavy infestations may trigger allergies or asthma. They don't carry disease, damage structures, or reproduce indoors. They leave on their own in spring.

Garden Threat

Japanese Beetles

Devastating to ornamental plants, roses, grapes, and lawns (grubs eat roots). Zero threat to humans or homes. Purely an outdoor garden/landscape problem.

🛠️ What To Actually Do

If your place is being invaded, here's the game plan.

Eviction Protocol

  1. Vacuum them up. Use a shop vac or a vacuum with a bag you can toss. Don't crush them, the smell and staining isn't worth it.
  2. Seal the sun-facing side first. Caulk around windows, door frames, utility penetrations, and any gaps in siding. Trailers and mobile homes have lots of entry points.
  3. Add weatherstripping to doors and windows that don't seal tight.
  4. Check soffits and vents. Make sure screens are intact and gaps are sealed.
  5. Don't bother with pesticides indoors. Dead beetles attract carpet beetles, which are a worse problem. Vacuum and seal is the move.
  6. Wait them out. They'll leave on their own when it warms up. They're not breeding in your walls, just sleeping there.
