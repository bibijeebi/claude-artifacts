Investigation

Mercury, Fish, Pregnancy
& Vaccines

The evidence on methylmercury in fish versus ethylmercury in vaccines. What the research actually found, what the public was told, and why the gap between those two things matters.

01

The Core Tension

Omega-3 fatty acids, DHA in particular, are critical building blocks for fetal brain and eye development. The best dietary source is fish. Fish also contain methylmercury, a neurotoxin that crosses the placenta and the blood-brain barrier. The question was never "is mercury bad." It's whether the developmental benefits of eating fish outweigh the mercury exposure that comes with it.

The evidence says yes, for the right fish. Large cohort studies found that women who ate moderate amounts of fish during pregnancy had children with better cognitive outcomes than women who avoided fish entirely, even after controlling for mercury exposure. Selenium naturally present in fish binds mercury and reduces its bioavailability, providing a partial built-in offset.

But methylmercury is roughly 95% bioavailable through the gut. Digestion is not a meaningful barrier. It enters the bloodstream, crosses the placenta, and reaches the fetal brain directly.

The cheat code

Algae-based DHA supplements deliver the exact same omega-3 compound with zero mercury exposure. Same molecule, no tradeoff. Pregnant women need roughly 200–300mg DHA daily.

02

The Vaccine Mercury Question

Fish contains methylmercury. Vaccines contained ethylmercury via the preservative thimerosal. These are genuinely different compounds, and the official safety argument hinges on ethylmercury clearing from the blood faster: a half-life of 3–8 days, versus methylmercury's 20+ days.

But thimerosal was not some niche ingredient in a few obscure products. Since the 1930s it was used across a massive range of medical products. The FDA Modernization Act of 1997 compiled a list and found it in 219 regulated products: contact lens solutions, nasal sprays, eye drops, ear drops, anti-venins, immunoglobulin preparations, and other biologics. Within vaccines, before the 2001 phaseout, it was in DTaP, Hepatitis B, Hib, and other routine childhood vaccines.

03

The Blood Clearance Problem

Multiple studies confirm ethylmercury leaves the blood in about a week. That number is reproducible and not in dispute. The problem is what happens next.

The landmark Burbacher 2005 primate study found that ethylmercury doesn't leave the body. It redistributes rapidly into the brain, kidneys, and liver. Once in the brain, it converts to inorganic mercury at dramatically higher rates than methylmercury: 71% inorganic in thimerosal-exposed brains versus 10% in methylmercury-exposed brains.

Conclusions regarding the safety of thimerosal drawn from blood mercury clearance data in human infants receiving vaccines may not be valid, given the significantly slower half-life of mercury in the brain.

— Burbacher et al., Environmental Health Perspectives, 2005

A mouse study broke the organ-specific half-lives down precisely:

Tissue Half-life Note
Blood 8.8 days This is the number cited as proof of safety
Heart 7.8 days
Liver 7.7 days
Brain 10.7 days Converts to inorganic Hg with >1 year half-life
Kidney 45.2 days 70%+ of body mercury concentrates here within 30 min
The key distinction

"Clears from the blood in a week" and "clears from the body in a week" are very different statements. The fast disappearance from blood is better described as rapid tissue uptake, not rapid elimination. The public-facing messaging consistently conflated the two.

04

The Policy Timeline

1930s
Thimerosal introduced as a preservative. Eventually found in 219+ regulated medical products including vaccines, contact lens solutions, nasal sprays, eye drops, and immunoglobulin preparations.
1997
FDA Modernization Act compiles comprehensive list of mercury-containing products. Reveals cumulative infant exposure from the vaccine schedule may exceed EPA safety guidelines.
1999
Public Health Service and American Academy of Pediatrics jointly recommend removing thimerosal from vaccines as a "precautionary measure." An odd thing to do for something you're confident is safe.
2001
Thimerosal removed from most childhood vaccines. The public message becomes "we removed mercury from vaccines." The fine print: multi-dose influenza vaccines, recommended annually for infants 6 months and older and pregnant women, still contain it.
2005
Burbacher publishes primate study showing ethylmercury rapidly deposits into brain tissue and converts to inorganic mercury with a half-life exceeding one year. Explicitly warns blood clearance data shouldn't be used to assess safety.
2024–2025
~4% of flu vaccines administered in the US still contain thimerosal as a preservative. These are routinely given to pregnant women and young children.
June–July 2025
Newly appointed ACIP panel votes to recommend thimerosal-free flu vaccines only. RFK Jr. signs it into federal policy. Thimerosal-containing vaccines remain FDA-approved but are no longer recommended.

I actually don't know any pediatric practices that even use that multidose influenza vaccine.

— Dr. Sean O'Leary, Children's Hospital Colorado, former AAP liaison to ACIP

The Bottom Line

The methylmercury versus ethylmercury distinction is real chemistry, not a fabrication. But the conclusion that ethylmercury is therefore harmless was built primarily on blood-level measurements that don't track where the mercury actually ends up. The researchers who generated that data said as much.

Meanwhile, the same health authorities warning pregnant women about mercury in a tuna sandwich were, until mid-2025, recommending those same women receive injections containing a mercury compound that rapidly deposits into organ tissue including the brain, where it converts to inorganic mercury with a half-life exceeding a year.

Thimerosal was not a minor additive in a few obscure products. It was a ubiquitous preservative across hundreds of medical products and the entire childhood vaccine schedule for decades. The messaging was technically accurate and practically dishonest: "removed from childhood vaccines" meant "still in the flu shots we recommend for your 6-month-old every year."

Whether thimerosal causes measurable harm at vaccine doses remains genuinely unsettled science. But the institutional refusal to honestly characterize the uncertainty is what makes the whole thing corrosive to public trust.
