IThe Manifesto

What we mean when we say yuppie.

Yuppie isn't a class so much as a longing. It is the costume of inherited summers, of boarding-school weekends and tennis-club summers and sailboats with names like Persephone. It is the late-American belief that the right polo shirt, the right dog, the right wagon in the drive could prove that you belong.

The acronym, of course, is from young urban professional: Reagan-era money, Wall Street suspenders, the BMW in the garage and the cocaine in the desk drawer. But the aesthetic survived its decade. It went underground, faded like a Polaroid left on a sunlit windowsill, and resurfaced in 2010 on the cover of an indie record. Two flavors. One uniform. Eternal nostalgia.

IIThe Two Flavors

One look. Two centuries of longing.

The same wardrobe carries two different ghosts. One says acquire; the other says remember.

Flavor One · Hot

The 80s Power Yuppie

Patrick Bateman in a corner office. Pinstripes from Brooks, suspenders in arterial red, hair laid down like wet tile. The Filofax open, the Cristal chilling, the Rolodex spinning. This is yuppie as verb: to acquire, to leverage, to dominate brunch. Cold, ambitious, Reagan-era opulence.

Soundtrack · Genesis, Phil Collins solo, Whitney Houston

Flavor Two · Faded

The Contra-Era Indie Yuppie

Same uniform, but the photograph is twenty years old and someone you loved is in it. This is the Vampire Weekend version: ironic Columbia worship, ironic-not-ironic Polo Ralph Lauren, polaroids on a kitchen wall in Brooklyn. The longing is for a summer that may have only ever existed in the picture.

Soundtrack · Vampire Weekend, Paul Simon, Talking Heads

IIIHer Wardrobe

Four adjacencies, one wardrobe.

The women's version of the look isn't one outfit, it's four. Each one borrows the language of an activity she may or may not actually do. The same closet rotates through these moods over the course of a single Saturday: sail in the morning, tennis at noon, stables at three, dinner at the club. The clothes do most of the saying.

i · Sailor / Nautical

Newport, August.

Navy and white, gold buttons, salt air. The most enduring of the four. Works at eight, twenty-eight, and eighty.

  • Breton stripe top. Navy on cream, three-quarter sleeve.
  • Navy blazer with gold buttons. Worn open or draped over the shoulders.
  • White sailing trousers or canvas shorts, cuffed.
  • Gold rope bracelet or anchor pendant. Red lipstick optional.
  • White Keds or brown Sperry Top-Siders, sockless.

Brands: Vineyard Vines · J.McLaughlin · Tory Burch · Sperry · Nautica (vintage).
Setting: yacht clubs · the Vineyard · regatta day.

ii · Safari / Expedition

Out of Africa.

Khaki, brass, leather. The most performative of the four — owns its costume, doesn't apologize. Meryl Streep energy.

  • Khaki shirt-dress with epaulets and patch pockets.
  • Wide tan leather belt with brass buckle.
  • Tall leather riding boots or lace-ups.
  • Silk neckerchief, knotted at the throat.
  • Tortoiseshell aviators + brass cuff bracelet.

Brands: Banana Republic (the original 80s safari version) · J.Peterman · Ralph Lauren Safari · RRL.
Setting: film set, hunting lodge, anywhere with a Land Rover.

iii · Tennis / Country Club

Forest Hills, '86.

All white. Pleated. Crisp. Reads as athletic but won't actually sweat. The vocabulary of capable.

  • Pleated white tennis skirt. Six pleats minimum.
  • Lacoste polo. The crocodile is the whole point.
  • Knee-high white socks + Tretorns or K-Swiss.
  • Terry headband or white visor, blonde ponytail.
  • Cable-knit V-neck draped between matches.

Brands: Lacoste · Sergio Tacchini · Fila (vintage) · Tretorn · Tory Sport.
Setting: the club · Forest Hills · Newport Tennis Hall · Wimbledon.

iv · Equestrian / Polo

Hampton Classic.

The most aspirational of the four. Quietest money. Oldest references. Hunt-club correct, never costume.

  • Tall riding boots in tan or black leather.
  • Cream jodhpurs or breeches, knee-patched.
  • Navy hunt coat with brass buttons.
  • White ratcatcher shirt + stock tie or Hermès scarf.
  • Velvet-trimmed helmet + leather gloves in hand.

Brands: Hermès · Ralph Lauren Purple Label · Burberry classic · Pikeur (real riders).
Setting: stables · the Hampton Classic · Tuxedo Park · Lake Forest.

The Sister Adjacencies These four are the load-bearing ones. The wardrobe also flirts with: Lilly Pulitzer garden-party (sundress + kitten heels + hydrangeas), Aspen après-ski (cashmere + fur + cognac), and Slim Aarons poolside (caftan + gold sandal + martini). The structure is the same: borrow the activity's vocabulary, opt out of the work.

IVThe Uniform

The uniform, by the catalog.

Every aesthetic has a kit. The yuppie kit is a closed system: pieces signal each other, a polo invites khakis, khakis invite Top-Siders, Top-Siders invite a signet ring. None of it is invented. All of it is inherited, even when bought new at Bloomingdale's last Tuesday.

  • ARalph Lauren polo. The pony is required. Collar may be popped, never both points.
  • BKhakis or chinos. Pleated front by 1988, flat front by 2010. Cuffed if you sail.
  • CSperry Top-Siders. Brown, broken in. White rope laces. Worn sockless from May to September.
  • DCartier Tank watch. Or a Timex if you're being honest. Either reads correctly.
  • ESignet ring. Pinky, gold, ideally inherited. A bare crest beats invented heraldry.
  • FPearls or Tiffany. Studs, single strand, or the silver bean. Restraint is the point.
  • GCable knit, draped. Tied around the shoulders. Never actually worn.
  • HRay-Ban Wayfarers. Black, square. Risk Persol if you've earned it.
Color Palette Mahogany · Oxblood · Brooks-Brothers Navy · Kelly Green · Butter Yellow · Nantucket Red · Pale Pink · Cream

VIThe Polaroid Look

How the photograph itself reads.

The aesthetic is half wardrobe, half film stock. A yuppie in a sharp digital photo is just an investment banker with a haircut. The polaroid does the work: it ages the present, photographs the longing, makes everyone in the frame already a memory.

The look, technically.

  • iPolaroid 600 / SX-70. The square frame, white border, slight aging halo at the edges.
  • iiDirect flash. No bounce, no diffusion. Eyes catch the strobe.
  • iiiSlight overexposure. Skin glows, walls go cream, the world looks generous.
  • ivWarm yellow cast. Kodachrome memory in instant-film clothing.
  • vSoft focus. Always a touch out, the way an actual happy memory blurs.
  • viCaption strip. A handwritten date in blue ballpoint at the bottom is the closer.

VIIIThe Kit, Acquired

You don't inherit it. You hunt it.

The dirty truth of the aesthetic: ninety percent of the people who wear it correctly bought it for ten cents on the dollar. The pony shirt at Bloomingdale's is for tourists. The pony shirt that an actual yuppie's son outgrew in 1994 and his mother dropped at the Nantucket Hospital Thrift Shop is the relic. Faded beats new. Inherited beats bought. The aesthetic rewards patience.

Where to source.

  • $Goodwill, Salvation Army. $4–8 polos. Always check the men's rack, then the women's. The pony does not discriminate.
  • $Estate sales. Gold mine. Monogrammed handkerchiefs, signet rings, watches with patina. Look for zip codes starting in 06 or 02.
  • $$eBay. Search "Ralph Lauren polo vintage L." Filter by “Used.” Sort by lowest price. Faded is the point.
  • $$Brooks Brothers Outlet. OCBDs at $35, sport coats at $89. The 346 line is fine; nobody is checking.
  • $$Polo Factory Store. Last-season at half price. Avoid anything with a shield over the chest.
  • $$Etsy. Vintage gold signet rings $25–60. Filter for “9k” or “heirloom.”
  • $$$The Boathouse, Mystic, CT. Or any consignment shop in a coastal New England town. They’re selling actual yuppie estates.

The substitutions that work.

  • →Cartier Tank → Timex Weekender on a brown leather strap. $40. Silhouette is the signal; the brand is for insurance.
  • →Sperry → Sperry, used. Buy them broken-in or break them in yourself with rain, salt, and time. New leather is the giveaway.
  • →Cable knit → any 100% cotton or merino crew in navy or cream. Drape it. Never wear it.
  • →Khakis → Dockers, ironed. Yes. The crease is doing more work than the brand.
  • →Pearls → costume pearls. No one is biting them. Fifteen dollars at any antique mall.
  • →Madras → any cotton plaid in faded jewel tones. Old Navy will do in a pinch.
The Three Rules of the Hunt Older beats newer.   Smaller logo beats bigger.   Faded color beats fresh.

IXThe Tells

How to spot the counterfeit.

The aesthetic has a thousand ways to go wrong, all of them rooted in the same error: trying. The look only works when it appears unconsidered, accumulated, slightly indifferent. The following are the dead giveaways. Avoid them in your own kit. Spot them on others and feel the small private satisfaction of knowing.

  • ✕The oversized logo. The pony is one inch tall, in a color two shades from the shirt. The horse-and-rider that takes up a third of your chest is for theme parks.
  • ✕Brand-new Sperry Top-Siders. Crisp leather, white-white laces, pristine soles. They should look like they survived an Atlantic crossing.
  • ✕White athletic socks above the ankle. No-shows from May to September. Argyle from October. Never the gym pair.
  • ✕Tucked-in polo with a belt at brunch. Polo is untucked unless you're at a country club, on a tennis court, or attending a funeral. None of which is brunch.
  • ✕More than one visible logo. Pony on the chest or croc on the belt. Not both. Never both.
  • ✕The Apple Watch. A computer on your wrist is the opposite of inheriting time. Even a $20 Casio reads correctly.
  • ✕The whole outfit, fresh from the bag. Layers should look accumulated, not assembled. Mix decades. Mix conditions.
  • ✕Vineyard Vines anything, post-2015. The Whale was acceptable in 2007. Now it signals you are trying. The brand has gone full mall.
  • ✕An “old money” aesthetic Instagram account. If you have to caption it, you don't have it.
  • ✕A real Rolex. Submariner says hedge fund associate. The watch you inherited from your grandfather, even if it's a $30 Bulova, says everything.
The Single Test Could this outfit pass for something you've owned since college, even if you bought it last week? If yes, you've understood. If no, take something off, age what's left, and try again next summer.
