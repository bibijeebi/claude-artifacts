Chum Bread

A cheap, stinky, fall-apart loaf made for one job: filling the minnow trap and pulling bait.

1.5 lbLoaf

This isn't sandwich bread. It's bait. You want it cheap, you want it loud with garlic and cheese, and you want it to crumble apart in the water and lay a scent trail that pulls minnows into the trap. Run it on the basic white cycle, dry it out, bag it, freeze it. Done.

Why this works

four things that matter for bait bread, not for breakfast
01

Cheap flour, dense crumb

Don't waste good bread flour. Plain all-purpose is perfect. You want it to break apart wet, not hold together like a sandwich.

02

Garlic powder, not fresh

Powder spreads scent through the whole loaf and out into the water. Fresh garlic stays put and can gum things up.

03

Powdered parm beats shreds

The green-can stuff is cheap, pungent, and bakes clean. Fresh cheese scorches on the long bake and gums the paddle.

04

Dry it out after

The pro move. Stale, dry chunks cloud the water and crumble far better than fresh bread, which turns to gum and fouls the trap.

What goes in

a standard 1.5 lb loaf, juiced up
  • 1 cupwarm waterabout 110°F
  • 2 tbspvegetable oil
  • 3 cupsall-purpose flour
  • 1 tbspsugar
  • 1 tspsalt
  • 3 tbspgarlic powder
  • 1 tbsponion powder
  • ¾ cupgrated parmesangreen-can style
  • 2¼ tspbread machine yeastone packet
Everything goes in the pan in order. No mixing bowl needed.

How to make it

load the pan, push start, walk away
1

Wet first

Pour the water and oil into the bread machine pan.

2

Dry on top

Add the flour. Then sprinkle the sugar, salt, garlic powder, onion powder, and parmesan over the flour so it covers the water.

3

Yeast last

Poke a small well in the center of the flour and pour the yeast into it. Keep the yeast off the water until the machine starts.

4

Run the cycle

Set the machine to Basic / White, 1.5 lb loaf, medium crust. Hit start.

⏱ about 3 hours, hands-off
5

Cool it

Pull the loaf, turn it out onto a rack, and let it cool all the way down.

⏱ about 45 minutes
6

Dry it out — the chum move

Tear the loaf into chunks. Dry them on a sheet pan at 200°F until stale and crumbly, or just leave them out a day or two. Dry bread clouds the water and breaks apart in the trap way better than fresh.

⏱ 30–45 minutes in the oven

Then what?

Bag the dried chunks and freeze them in portions. They pull straight from the freezer to the lake. At the water, crumble a handful into the minnow trap or stuff in a fist-sized chunk. It softens, clouds the water, and pulls minnows right in.

Dried chunks at the trap. The crumbs cloud the water and lay the scent trail.

Pro tips & tweaks

reality checks and ways to make it nastier

Cheese swap. Green-can parm is ideal, but a bag of shredded cheddar or mozzarella works too. It just melts into the crumb instead of staying powdery. Still stinks up the water fine.

Make it nastier. Add 1–2 tbsp of canned mackerel or tuna oil to the wet ingredients, or a few drops of anise oil. Fish love the funk.

Skip the sugar if you want. Minnows don't care. A little just helps the yeast and adds a fermentation funk fish like.

One loaf is a lot of chum. A dried, bagged loaf runs many trap sets. Portion and freeze so it doesn't mold.
