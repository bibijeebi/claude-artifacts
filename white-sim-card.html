سیم کارت سفید
/SIM kart-e safid/ — literally "white SIM card"
A SIM card registered on a government "whitelist" with Iranian mobile carriers, granting unrestricted access to the global internet without filtering or VPN circumvention. Holders can directly access Twitter/X, YouTube, Instagram, Telegram, and other blocked platforms while ordinary citizens cannot.
◈

Key Capabilities

White SIM cards provide privileged network access that bypasses Iran's national censorship infrastructure entirely. They are not "jailbroken" or hacked—they are officially whitelisted at the carrier level.

▸ No filtering: Direct access to all blocked platforms (X, YouTube, Instagram, Telegram, WhatsApp) without VPN
▸ Shutdown immunity: Remain functional during nationwide internet blackouts (e.g., the "12-day war" period)
▸ Better performance: Faster speeds and lower latency because traffic doesn't route through filtering/DPI equipment
▸ Tied to national ID: Access is linked to the holder's کد ملی (national ID code), not the SIM itself
◈

NOV 25, 2025 The X "Account Based In" Exposure

Twitter/X rolled out a new feature showing which country an account is managed from. Since X is blocked in Iran, most users connect via VPN and show foreign locations.

The tell: Any Iranian account showing "Account based in: Iran" is connecting directly—meaning they have unfiltered access. Screenshots flooded social media exposing politicians, pro-regime influencers, and state journalists all tweeting from direct Iranian IPs while preaching the virtues of filtering to ordinary citizens.

"The worst thing we're dealing with in this country is filtering. I've ordered the white SIM cards to become black."
— President Masoud Pezeshkian, December 2025

Historical Context

The white SIM system originated during the Rouhani administration (2013-2021) when the Communications Ministry opposed blanket filtering. Unable to prevent censorship, they created exemptions for "professional" users—journalists, academics, diplomats.

Under Rouhani, white SIM holders had unlimited international bandwidth and connectivity even during the November 2019 nationwide shutdown. When Raisi took office in 2021, many exemptions were revoked. The current Pezeshkian administration inherited ~50,000 active white SIMs and faces public pressure to either eliminate the two-tier system or lift filtering entirely.

A 2020 plan to expand white SIMs to ~7 million users (all university students, all journalists, all tourists, all business cardholders) was never implemented due to "lack of cooperation from relevant agencies."
