Whole-Home Dehumidifier

The Problem

Kong has a diagnosed dust mite allergy that causes intense itching. Dust mites thrive above 50% humidity and cannot survive below 45%. Our house regularly hits 80% humidity in summer and struggles in spring/fall too. The root cause is the AC being undersized for the home's volume, especially with 13-foot ceilings creating a massive air volume that the unit can't dehumidify fast enough during cooling cycles.

We've been managing symptoms: frequent bedding washes in 130°F+ water, steam cleaning the ottoman, keeping up with vacuuming. That helps, but it's fighting the symptom, not the cause. The humidity itself is the breeding ground.

This is a health issue, not just comfort. Kong's quality of life is directly tied to indoor humidity. Portable dehumidifiers can't handle a whole house with 13-foot ceilings. A whole-home unit ducted into the HVAC system is the real fix.

What a Whole-Home Dehumidifier Does

A whole-home dehumidifier ties into the existing HVAC ductwork and runs independently of the AC. It pulls humid air from the return duct, removes moisture, and pushes dry air back into the supply. It runs on its own humidistat, so even when the AC isn't calling for cooling, it keeps pulling moisture. Target: maintain 35-45% humidity year-round.

This also reduces the load on the AC (drier air feels cooler at higher temps), which helps with the undersized unit situation. Two birds, one stone.

Rough Cost Estimate

Unit: $1,200-1,800 for a quality whole-home dehumidifier (Aprilaire 1850, Santa Fe Compact70, or similar). Sized for 2,500-4,000 sq ft.

Installation: $500-1,200 depending on ductwork complexity. An HVAC pro ties it into the existing return/supply, runs a condensate drain, and wires it up. Half-day job typically.

Total installed: $2,000-3,000.

Benny works HVAC (Carolina Quality Air). Possible to get contractor pricing on the unit and/or DIY the install with guidance from coworkers. Could significantly reduce cost. Worth asking Meredith about employee/contractor equipment pricing.

What Needs to Happen First

Get the home's square footage and ceiling volume calculated. Have the existing HVAC system assessed: what tonnage is the AC, what's the ductwork layout, where would the dehumidifier tie in. Carolina Quality Air may be able to do this assessment for free given Benny works there. Then get unit recommendations and an install quote (or a plan to self-install).

Decision for Bekah

This is the single highest-impact project for Kong's health and overall home comfort. The cost is significant ($2-3K) but it's a permanent fix for a recurring problem. The alternative is continuing to fight humidity with portable units, steam cleaning, and laundry, which works "enough" but doesn't solve it. If we're going to do it, doing it before summer 2026 is ideal so we're not dealing with 80% humidity again.
