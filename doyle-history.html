Day 1

The Rosa Parks Story Is Not Even Approximately True

Montgomery Bus Boycott, 1956. Library of Congress.

The standard story goes like this: 1955, Montgomery, Alabama. Rosa Parks, a tired seamstress, refuses to give up her bus seat to a white man after a long day's work because her feet hurt. She gets arrested, it sparks a bus boycott, and she becomes the mother of the civil rights movement.

The entire thing was a planned publicity stunt from the beginning. By 1955, Rosa Parks was already an experienced left-wing activist. She and her husband were attending Communist Party meetings. Her husband owned a car.

This wasn't even the first time a black person had been arrested for refusing to give up a seat. Other incidents had occurred, but the NAACP and activists ignored them. Rosa Parks was specifically selected as the perfect subject for the agenda because she was polite, employed, and married, making her sympathetic to white people.

The Rosa Parks incident was actually a reproduction of a prior plan that fell through when the woman they originally chose got pregnant out of wedlock, so they subbed in Parks. She even had to ride around on a few different buses before the incident happened, since the rules weren't really enforced on female passengers.

She wasn't even exhausted. She admitted in her autobiography that she refused the seat not because she was physically tired, but because she was "tired of giving in" to injustice. She'd also had a prior run-in with the same bus driver, James F. Blake, in 1943, when he kicked her off entirely. That earlier incident got zero attention because the activists hadn't chosen her yet.

Montgomery law didn't actually require black people to give up seats to white people if no other seats were available.

Parks was sitting in the middle section. The bus had a white section, middle section, and black section. The driver was moving the dividing sign to expand the white section because more white passengers were boarding. He asked Parks and a few black men to move from the middle section to the black section. The men complied. Parks, following the plan, refused, and was arrested.

After the bus incident, Parks didn't stop there. She supported Malcolm X and the Nation of Islam. She marched in Farrakhan's Million Man March in 1995. She backed the Black Power movement, defended black rioters in the 1967 Detroit riots, and endorsed domestic terrorist groups like the Black Panthers, the Black Liberation Army, and the Weather Underground. Despite all of that, she still spoke at the 1988 Democratic National Convention and was hailed as a hero.

Day 2

Black People Were Enslaving Other Black People on American Soil Before George Washington Was Born

Tobacco cultivation in colonial Virginia, ca. 1670. Library of Congress.

The first legally recognized slave owner in the American colonies was a black man named Anthony Johnson. Historian Philip Foner called him "the patriarch of black property owners in America."

Johnson was kidnapped by African slave traders in southern Africa, sold to the Portuguese, and sent to Virginia, where he eventually earned his freedom. He then went on to purchase slaves himself and even used the legal system, which we're told was systemically anti-black, to take a black slave away from a white man. He became an extraordinarily wealthy tobacco farmer.

Johnson had five indentured servants: four white men and one black man. On the subject of indentured servants, because indentured servitude was a temporary labor contract rather than lifetime ownership, indentured servants were often treated far worse than slaves. They were worked harder, beaten worse, and starved more, because their masters had no long-term interest in their health. The mortality rate of indentured servants was actually much higher than that of slaves. Indentured servants in America were overwhelmingly white.

The one black indentured servant Johnson had was a man named John Kayser. Kayser claimed his contract was only seven years and wanted to leave. His wife and kids begged Johnson to let him go. Johnson said there was no contract and that he simply owned Kayser.

Kayser left anyway to work for a white man's plantation, claiming to be free. Johnson chased him down and got the courts to rule that he owned Kayser for life. Because of Anthony Johnson, John Kayser became one of the first Africans in America enslaved for life as a result of a civil suit rather than criminal punishment. It's literally on his Wikipedia page.

Day 3

Europeans Didn't Have to Go Capture Africans in the Jungle

Historical depiction of the slave trade. Library of Congress.

The standard story is that Europeans ran around the jungles of Africa, snatching up Africans and taking them across the ocean, away from their brothers and sisters who were living together in perfect harmony. That literally never happened.

Nobody had to go out of their way to find slaves in Africa. They were already there, waiting in slave markets set up by African kingdoms which had been running for almost a thousand years. And when you look at the map of where these slaves were going, most of the movement went to South America, some to the Caribbean, and only a small fraction made it to America. Only 2–5% of transatlantic slaves ended up in America. Apparently that's the one everyone is supposed to talk about.

America is the only country that has to apologize for its history when it was doing what everybody else was doing, except on a much smaller scale. Before the transatlantic slave trade, there was the trans-Saharan slave trade, where the same African kingdoms sold African slaves to Muslim countries in the Middle East. Those countries famously castrated their African slaves, which is why there are no significant black populations in those countries today.

The only thing that changed for the Africans was that instead of sending slaves across the desert, they were now sending them out on boats. African kingdoms would take captured men and women from weaker and rival tribes, load them onto boats, and receive trade goods in exchange. What they valued most were guns and liquor, apparently especially liquor. They wouldn't even begin negotiations with Europeans unless they were given rum first.

This slave trade went on until the early 1800s, when Britain and America both made it illegal to trade slaves from Africa. The Africans did not like this. Kingdoms like Dahomey, the one Netflix made the black queen "girl boss" movie about, continued trading slaves against the ban.

Britain deployed the West African Naval Squadron specifically to stop Africans from enslaving each other and selling them all over the world. Britain spent decades trying diplomacy with the Kingdom of Dahomey, attempting to convince their king, Gezo, to stop raiding and attacking other Africans to get slaves. Those conversations were essentially the British offering alternative commerce like palm oil, and the Dahomey king refusing. This went on until the French went to war with Dahomey, put down their army with some of the most lopsided casualty reports of all time, and finally stopped Africans from enslaving other Africans.

Thomas Jefferson Didn't Have a Kid with His Slave

Monticello, Jefferson's estate in Virginia. Library of Congress.

The story that Thomas Jefferson had sex with his slave Sally Hemings and fathered children with her is something you learn in school and hear about constantly at Monticello. It's used to tar the image of the Founding Fathers. But it's not true.

Sally Hemings wasn't even black as they portray her. She was three-quarters white, one-quarter black. She had fair skin and straight hair. They need you to believe she was fully black because that's what suits the narrative.

The actual relationship is minimal in the historical record. Thousands of letters exist from Jefferson, and the only thing he ever says about her is that "Marie's servant is doing well after having a child." The only time Sally was with Jefferson outside Monticello was in Paris, and she wasn't even his choice of companion. He wanted an older woman to accompany his daughter, but Sally was the only one available because she was inoculated against smallpox. And when she stayed with the Adams family in France, they described her as even more childish than Jefferson's nine-year-old daughter.

Jefferson was going after highly intelligent, mature, aristocratic English women, not 14-year-old immature slave girls.

In Paris, Jefferson was pursuing two women: a relative of Alexander Hamilton and Maria Cosway, to whom he wrote his famous love letter, "Dialogue Between My Head and My Heart." He was a highly intelligent aristocrat pursuing highly intelligent, aristocratic English women.

The evidence comes from a paternity test showing that only one of Hemings' sons, Eston Hemings (not her other son Thomas Woodson), matched Jefferson's Y chromosome. But that DNA also matched Randolph Jefferson, Thomas's younger brother, and his five sons. Randolph was described by a Monticello slave as "a mighty simple man" who "used to come out among black people, play the fiddle and dance half the night" and was "known for going to bed with them." Randolph Jefferson and his sons were invited to Monticello nine months before Eston Hemings was born.

Day 5

Liberia Was Going to Be Wakanda, and It Turned Out to Be the Opposite

Monrovia, Liberia. Library of Congress.

We hear a lot about how "the white man just wants to keep the black man down." The opposite actually happened: a group of wealthy, influential Americans worked to give black people their own country back in Africa. The American Colonization Society was founded in 1816 with the purpose of giving free black people their own land and government. Even Abraham Lincoln supported it.

Millions of dollars went into providing guns, supplies, construction materials, and passage to what would become Liberia. Its capital, Monrovia, was named after James Monroe, who had the federal government provide millions of dollars for the project. When the Liberians arrived, they were attacked by hostile tribes trying to stop them from colonizing, even though all parties on both sides were African. The Liberians beat them thanks to weaponry provided by the United States.

By the mid-19th century, Liberia declared independence. It adopted a constitution nearly identical to the United States, with two exceptions: slavery was illegal, and only people of African descent could be citizens, vote, or hold office. It was one of only two countries on the continent, along with Ethiopia, not colonized by Europeans, as it was being protected by America.

Around the 1900s, Liberia became almost a magical place in the minds of black nationalists in America. Marcus Garvey, head of the Universal Negro Improvement Association (UNIA), sent a delegation to Liberia to explore moving more black Americans there to build the real-life Wakanda.

When the delegates arrived, they were shocked. The Liberians were wearing suits and sipping tea while running rubber plantations with Africans in various forms of forced labor. The delegates were immediately arrested and deported when they protested.

America conducted a State Department investigation known as the Christy Report, which found that Liberians had developed a system of debts and contract labor that was slavery in all but name: "a large proportion of the contract laborers recruited under conditions of criminal compulsion scarcely distinguishable from slave raiding and slave trading."

The Americo-Liberians continued ruling over other Africans until around the 1980s, when a coup launched the country into civil war, rendering it one of the poorest and most dangerous countries on the planet.

The Harriet Tubman Legend Is a Myth Invented by Two Writers

Harriet Tubman. Library of Congress.

We've all heard the legend of Harriet Tubman: the Moses of the Underground Railroad who led hundreds of slaves to freedom. She's on stamps, schools are named after her, Confederate monuments are replaced with her statues, and she may replace Andrew Jackson on the $20 bill. The problem is it's mostly made up.

At age 13, around 1834, Araminta Ross (her birth name) was shopping in a general store when an overseer threw a 2-pound iron weight at an escaping slave. It missed and smashed into her skull. She almost died and suffered severe head trauma, leading to lifelong headaches, seizures, and random blackouts.

How did someone with chronic blackouts lead nighttime raids and guide groups through swamps? Historical records show she struggled with basic tasks.

The specific claims about Tubman have virtually no primary sources from her time to back them up. The extravagant bounty that slaveholders supposedly put on her head? No evidence. Her 19 rescue trips freeing 300 slaves? Totally made up. Even a current Google search puts the number at around 60, tops.

The myth came from two key writers. The first was Sarah H. Bradford, whose books on Tubman are, according to scholars, "pseudobiographical narratives" with "no standing as sources of historical information." Bradford cobbled together uncorroborated stories, exaggerating them for drama and sales. She later admitted to relying on whatever Tubman said with zero verification, from a woman with lifelong severe brain damage.

The second was Earl Conrad (born Earl Cohen), a Marxist journalist for the black newspaper Chicago Defender. In the 1940s, he wrote "history" books on Tubman with zero primary sources, motivated by his dislike of white people. As a radical, he twisted Tubman's story into propaganda for civil rights, ignoring her disabilities to create an invincible superhero icon.

The point of these stories is not history. It's creating propaganda against America.

Day 7

Top 10 Things Black People Claim They Invented but Actually Didn't

Edison's light bulb. Library of Congress.

10. Ice Cream — Augustus Jackson. He claims to be the father of modern ice cream, but modern ice cream using salt in the process appears in Mary Eale's Cookbook from 1711. What made ice cream available to everyone was Nancy Maria Johnson's hand-cranked ice cream freezer. Jackson literally just sold ice cream.

9. The Cell Phone — Henry T. Sampson. Sampson's name is on a patent for the Gamma Electric Cell, which has to do with nuclear reactors, not cell phones. Sampson himself had to say: "Contrary to what you read on the internet, I had literally nothing to do with inventing the cell phone." That was Martin Cooper at Motorola.

8. Central Heating — Alice Parker (1919). Central heating existed for over 100 years prior in various forms. Parker had a patent for a kind of heating using natural gas, but it never got used or commercialized because it had no safety features and could easily start a fire with no shutoff valves.

7. The Gas Mask — Garrett Morgan (1914). John Stenhouse invented the gas mask in 1854. Morgan just made a different version of something that had already been around for 60 years.

6. Traffic Lights — Garrett Morgan again. No. John Peak Knight made the first traffic signal in 1868. Lester Wire made the first electric signal in 1912. William L. Potts made the modern traffic signal used worldwide today. Morgan made a hand-cranked version in 1923.

5. Blood Banks — Charles Drew. Blood preservation was invented by Oswald Robertson working for the British Army during World War I. Drew didn't invent blood banks; that was Bernard Fantus in 1937. Drew helped with blood collection efforts.

4. Refrigerated Trucks — Frederick Jones. Also gets attributed to air conditioning, which he also didn't invent. Gustavus Swift already had cooled rail cars in 1878, revolutionizing the meat industry. Refrigerated trucks existed in the 1920s.

3. The Pencil Sharpener — John Lee Love (1897). The first pencil sharpener was made by Frenchman Bernard Lassimon and popularized by another Frenchman, Thierry des Estivaux, in 1847, fifty years before Love.

2. The Light Bulb — Lewis Latimer. No. That was Thomas Edison.

1. Peanut Butter — George Washington Carver. Carver did not invent anything to do with peanuts. The claim of 300 different uses for peanuts, one being peanut butter, is attributed to him, but peanut butter was actually made by Marcellus Edson, a French Canadian, who is also sometimes claimed to be black through the use of Carver's photos being misattributed as Edson.

John Brown Was Not a Hero

John Brown's Fort, Harpers Ferry. Library of Congress.

John Brown is considered one of the "good" white people because he started killing normal white Christians in service of his political agenda. Today, Antifa and far-left militant groups name themselves after him, displaying his name on their merchandise and uniforms, because his name has become synonymous with "direct action."

Brown was a complete loser. He became a radical only after decades of failed businesses and bankruptcy caused by his generally stupid and erratic behavior. He moved to Kansas, where there was already tension over whether the state would adopt slavery, and he made it worse.

He kidnapped and murdered five innocent settlers, claiming the attack was revenge for attacks by pro-slavery forces, which had literally nothing to do with the victims he killed. He brutally executed these people with swords when they couldn't even defend themselves. None of the victims even owned slaves.

The raid on Harpers Ferry, the thing he's most well known for, was not a heroic attempted slave uprising. It was basically "suicide by cop with extra steps." His raiders failed to secure the routes in and out of town and were quickly discovered.

The first person Brown's men killed was Hayward Shepard, a free black person who was shot in the back while running to get the police. They couldn't have picked a worse target: Harpers Ferry had a large black population, 75% of whom were free and prosperous, and the remaining slaves were relatively well-treated house slaves. Nobody wanted to join Brown.

Brown's men kept killing innocents, including the mayor, Fontaine Beckham, who was unarmed and renowned for his generosity to the town's black community. The Marines arrived, led by Robert E. Lee and Stonewall Jackson, stormed the building, took minimal casualties, and easily overcame Brown's men in hand-to-hand combat.

It was a total failure. Brown was captured alive and was so embarrassed that during the trial he lied and claimed he hadn't been attempting a slave uprising at all, only a peaceful protest march. Northern abolitionists repeated this lie after Brown was executed, which helped convince patriotic Southerners that there was no chance of national reconciliation. The resulting Civil War cost hundreds of thousands of American lives and probably could have been avoided were it not for people like Brown fanning the flames.

"Black Wall Street" Was Not Real and the Tulsa "Race Massacre" Didn't Happen Like They Say

Smoke over Tulsa, 1921. Library of Congress.

The standard story: the Tulsa Race Massacre, 1921. A thriving "Black Wall Street" community burned to the ground by a bunch of racist white people. Hundreds killed, another tale of oppression. It's in movies, books, TV shows, and demands for reparations.

What was "Black Wall Street"? It was not some black economic powerhouse. Greenwood was just a few blocks in Tulsa's segregated north side. It had a barber shop, a grocery, a communist newspaper office. Not too different from a modern inner-city ghetto. The name "Black Wall Street" was coined by Booker T. Washington, a black writer who never even lived in Tulsa. There were no banks, no stock exchanges. It was just a normal black neighborhood.

On May 30, 1921, Dick Rowland, a 19-year-old black delivery boy, was arrested for attempting to rape a 17-year-old white elevator operator named Sarah Page. Page testified that Rowland grabbed her arm, scratched her, tore her clothes, and tried to assault her. Rowland denied harm but said he may have touched her arm because he tripped.

Tensions exploded in Jim Crow Tulsa. Word spread that there might be a lynching, partly because a white man named Roy Belton had been lynched a year prior after being suspected of murder. Angry white people showed up outside the courthouse but were kept at bay by the sheriff and six armed men. A few walked inside and asked the sheriff to hand Rowland over. The sheriff said no. They left.

Seeing the white mob, a group of black men armed themselves with rifles and shotguns and marched to confront it, even though the sheriff had everything under control. The white people, seeing armed black men, went and got armed too.

The standoff ended with black people attacking first, opening fire on whites. The initial result: 12 dead, 10 of whom were white.

Total chaos erupted. Riots, shootings, fires. Over 24 hours, Greenwood burned. Up to 300 people died, most of them black, and thousands became homeless. But the violence started because of black aggression, not random white rage.

Black Wall Street was never rebuilt because it never existed as a lost Atlantean utopia in the first place. It was an ordinary black district. The myths grew, amplified by civil rights narratives wanting to ignore the attempted rape that triggered the event and the black initiation of violence.

Day 10

"Redlining" Was Not a Real Thing

HOLC residential security documentation. Library of Congress.

The story: redlining was an evil racist policy where banks drew red lines around black neighborhoods to prevent them from getting loans, trapping them in poverty forever. This is the go-to example of systemic racism. It's taught in schools and written about everywhere. It turns out it's just a lie.

Redlining started in the 1930s with the federal Home Owners' Loan Corporation, a New Deal agency created to help poor people refinance mortgages. It was essentially an early credit score for mortgage seekers. Banks and insurers rated areas based on risk assessment: green was safe for investment, red was high risk. Red zones were risky and got fewer loans because of poverty, high crime, old housing, etc. Not race.

Scholar John McWhorter called it "race-neutral," and extensive data backs him up. When all relevant economic factors are accounted for, non-whites were not actually more likely to be redlined. Looking at the maps themselves: in Philadelphia, redlined spots were racially mixed with blacks and whites jumbled together. In Boston and Seattle, many red zones had no black people at all, just poor Irish, Italians, and Jews crammed into slums.

Nationwide, 82% of people in redlined areas were white.

Ethnic whites were the main targets, facing the same loan denials. Black people were actually disproportionately favored as loan recipients. During redlining's peak (1930s–1960s), home loans to black people increased relative to white people. Federal data shows black homeownership rose from 15% in 1940 to 42% by 1960, faster than national trends. You wouldn't see that if the government were going out of its way to prevent black people from getting loans. It seems the opposite was happening.

The myth grew in the wake of the civil rights era and is still weaponized to push for reparations and increase white guilt. Redlining may have been too general in its approach, but it was class-based, not race-based. It explained urban poverty across all groups. Redlining was formally outlawed about 60 years ago, and the behaviors that made these neighborhoods risky haven't changed much.

The Tuskegee Airmen Is Just a Myth

P-51 Mustang in production, WWII. Library of Congress.

In school, we're told that black people did so much for America in World War II only to continue being oppressed. The Tuskegee Airmen: supposedly the best pilots of the war, they get Netflix shows. But what was the actual black service record in World War II?

America had 1.2 million black soldiers, of which only 708 actually died in combat because they were almost entirely kept from combat. Their scores on the Army General Classification Test were lower on average, so they were assigned to labor units rather than combat units.

For comparison, only 33,000 Japanese Americans served in WWII and had 800 killed in action. Literally more people from the country America nuked twice died for America.

The 92nd Infantry Division, the only black infantry division in WWII (the "Buffalo Soldiers"), first saw combat in Italy in 1944. They were known for low morale, immediate retreating, malingering, and losing equipment. They were eventually withdrawn from combat and reconstituted by adding white and Japanese soldiers.

The Black Panthers (the tank unit, not the political group) only had one major battle. During a specific engagement at Tillet in the Battle of the Bulge, 11 Shermans crewed by black soldiers took on three German anti-tank guns and a single Mark IV Panther tank. They lost nine tanks to the single Panther. While German tanks were superior, white crews generally had about five or six Shermans lost for every four German tanks.

The Tuskegee Airmen themselves actually lost 27 bombers over the course of the war, despite the "never lost a bomber" myth from the movie Red Tails. Flying one of the most advanced long-range fighter escorts (the P-51 Mustang), they lost three planes for every two Germans they shot down. Every other white-only P-51 fighter group shot down over two Germans for every one they lost. They did have low bomber losses while escorting, but most German aircraft at that point were already out of the picture.

Despite being only 10% of the military, black male soldiers comprised 56% of convicted rapists. One such criminal was Lewis Till, father of Emmett Till, who after choosing military service instead of jail time for domestic violence, went on to rape two women and murder one of them in Italy, was court-martialed, found guilty, and executed by hanging.

Day 12

Emmett Till Sexually Assaulted Carolyn Bryant

Country store in Mississippi. Library of Congress.

The story told a million times: 14-year-old Emmett Till just whistles at a white woman in 1955 Mississippi, gets lynched by racists, and becomes a martyr of the civil rights movement. Iconic photo, open casket. Total injustice. But that's not the whole story.

In her 1955 trial testimony, Carolyn Bryant, the store clerk, said Till didn't just whistle at her. He grabbed her waist, tried to pull her behind the counter, and said "I've [been with] white women before." She fought him off, terrified he was about to rape her. This was an isolated country store. Police could take hours to arrive. It was no small thing to threaten a woman in a place like that.

Contrary to popular belief, Bryant never went back on her story, even in old age. Black people even tried breaking into her nursing home to lynch her before she died in 2023, motivated by fake rumors that she had admitted the whole thing was a lie.

That rumor was started by a liberal professor named Timothy Tyson. Tyson did a recorded interview of Bryant for a book about the case and claimed she confessed to making up Till's attack. However, although the rest of the interview was recorded, Bryant's supposed confession wasn't. The author made the claim she recanted her story 10 years after interviewing her, but he didn't record that part. Bryant and her daughter, who was present, denied it.

Tyson told the FBI and journalists that his recorder was "broken" that day, then that it was recorded but "lost." He confused important dates, claiming the confession occurred before he'd even met Bryant. He even edited Bryant's book after the supposed confession but didn't object to her giving the same story she'd always given. The DOJ concluded that Tyson's story "lacked credibility."

Bryant didn't even tell her husband Roy about the assault because she feared he'd beat the kid. Roy found out from a customer at the store who was also black. Roy Bryant and J.W. Milam kidnapped Emmett Till with the help of two black men. In a 1956 Look magazine interview, they said they originally only planned to assault and beat Emmett, but he kept insulting them and bragging about sexual assaults on white women. They snapped, killed him, and dumped his body in the river.

This was a horrific killing. But it was not just racism against an innocent boy.

If this were purely about race, why were black people involved in what happened to Till? And why do we hear a limited version of this story from 70 years ago to prove America is racist today, but never hear about cases that go the other direction? Cannon Hinnant, a 5-year-old shot in the head by a black man while playing in his front yard. Julian Wood, a 3-year-old stabbed to death by a black woman in a grocery store parking lot. These happened in just the past few years. None of these kids will be learned about in school.

Day 13

Gaps in Black "Generational Wealth" Are Not Caused by Slavery or Racism

We hear all the time that it's the fault of white people and white supremacy that black Americans don't have generational wealth the way white families do. The obvious starting point is that black generational wealth has lagged because of slavery: they couldn't build wealth because they were enslaved, so after abolition they were starting at a disadvantage.

First, the South was much poorer in general for both blacks and whites, and most ex-slaves lived in the South. Research shows that after controlling for southern regional poverty, economic gaps between descendants of enslaved and descendants of free black Americans largely disappeared within two to three generations. Differences in schooling, income, occupation, and homeownership were statistically insignificant by the grandchild generation. Slavery's direct economic effects diminished relatively quickly.

The idea that racism explains group differences in employment levels doesn't square with the fact that there was no unemployment gap between the races in the early 20th century, when white people were far more likely to be racially biased. For 60 years, hiring discrimination has been illegal. Unequal pay has been illegal. There's been affirmative action, diversity initiatives in the workforce, government, and academia.

Research shows that wealth transmits less strongly across generations for black Americans. Doubling the wealth of grandparents predicts a 60% increase in wealth for white descendants, but only a 9% increase for black descendants. IRS data shows that black children born at the top 20% of income are about as likely to stay there as they are to fall to the bottom 20%. White children born at the top are far more likely to remain.

Studies show that black households save at lower rates than white households even with similar incomes, and spend more on luxury items like fancy cars and clothing. Federal Reserve research tracking financial habits found the lowest "financial health scores" among black families, measuring habits like paying bills on time, low debt-to-income ratio, and saving money. This pattern held even among highly educated households. Asians scored at the top.

There's even data showing that black Americans are less likely to pay bills than white Americans even when they have the same credit score. If the solution to generational wealth gaps is paying reparations, there's no evidence that would even work. Over $4 trillion has been spent on this since the civil rights movement, and the wealth gaps have only gotten worse.

Day 14

"White Flight" Is Literally Just a Myth

Abandoned building, Detroit. Library of Congress.

Remember that one time racist white people teamed up with the government to flee major cities that black people were moving into because they were racist and judging people based on skin color? It's taught in schools. It is a lie.

The initial reason for the Great Migration of black people into northern cities was manufacturing jobs, but also generous welfare payments. At the same time, government efforts were breaking down segregation and moving black people specifically into white neighborhoods and schools. The 1948 Shelley v. Kraemer case banned enforcement of racial covenants. The 1954 Brown decision banned school segregation. Green v. New Kent required cities to take affirmative action to racially balance schools. The 1968 Fair Housing Act and other 1970s legislation banned formal and informal redlining. The federal government was fully committed to facilitating this.

In every case, the arrival of black populations in these cities was accompanied by a massive explosion in violent crime. This was something already happening in the South, which even W.E.B. Du Bois acknowledged in 1904.

Between 1950 and 1980, the homicide rate increases: Baltimore +350%, Boston +270%, Chicago +320%, Cleveland +230%, Detroit +1,500%, Louisiana +620%, New Orleans +500%, New York City +700%, Philadelphia +380%.

People generally don't just want to leave their homes, churches, and communities, places they've been in for centuries, unless they're under pretty extreme duress. And that's exactly what they experienced. A book called Left Behind in Rosedale documents what happened in Dallas-Fort Worth. The area went from 98% white in 1960 to 12% white in 1990. White residents faced extreme black-on-white violence, particularly the elderly. In just 1982, this area reported 79 rapes. In 1981: 116 robberies, 105 aggravated assaults, 766 burglaries, 643 property thefts, and 120 auto thefts. It was a nonstop onslaught of assault, rape, murder, and burglary.

Elderly residents had to turn their homes into fortresses. City leadership discussed the complaints and concluded that the elderly white residents who had built their lives there would either move or "be dead soon," and it wasn't worth investing time into the situation.

Liberals then, just like today, either denied this was happening or blamed it on white people themselves. They made up propaganda terms like "white flight" to avoid acknowledging who was doing the raping and murdering.

Studies have consistently shown that diversity erodes community bonds everywhere. Robert D. Putnam's research famously confirms this, and he even felt he had to apologize for his findings.

Civil rights era. Library of Congress.

After prison, he discovered the Nation of Islam, a religion teaching that an evil wizard named Yakub created white people thousands of years ago, leading to the destruction of a highly advanced black civilization. The NOI teaches that white people are literal devils destined for extermination by God.

With this as his belief system, Malcolm X advocated violence against white people to achieve black nationalism. He preached "by any means necessary," meaning guns and revolution to carve out a black ethnostate. This inspired groups like the Black Panthers to ambush and kill innocent white police officers. He celebrated JFK's assassination, calling it "white America's chickens coming home to roost."

Under Elijah Muhammad, the Nation of Islam was a cult. Muhammad was a serial rapist who impregnated multiple teen secretaries, justifying it through his reading of the Bible. Malcolm knew about all of this but stayed loyal until a bitter split in 1964 when he could no longer deny Muhammad's corruption.

In his famous 1964 "The Ballot or the Bullet" speech, he urged black people to take up arms against white people. Nine days before he was shot, he compared minorities' plight in the UK to Jews under the Nazis, calling for armed uprising against the British. He never renounced anti-white aggression.

People say he became a moderate after his trip to Mecca. This just means he stopped talking about using violence against every white person and limited it to "white supremacy as a system."

Elijah Muhammad grew increasingly jealous of Malcolm, leading to Malcolm leaving to start his own black power group. On February 21, 1965, members of the Nation of Islam confronted Malcolm at a meeting and shot him to death. Three NOI members were convicted, motivated by jealousy over Malcolm's fame and fury over his exposure of Muhammad's rapes and fraud.

The system didn't kill Malcolm X. A violent separatist tried to separate from his violent separatist group, and they became violent.

That didn't stop his family from suing the FBI, CIA, and NYPD in 2024.

Day 16

Everything About Martin Luther King Was a Lie

March on Washington, 1963. Library of Congress.

To many Americans, even conservatives, Martin Luther King was a virtuous Christian pastor who wanted peace and for society to judge people by the content of their character rather than the color of their skin. Schools are named after him. He has his own federal holiday, plus monuments, statues, and streets.

First, MLK rejected the divinity of Christ. He wrote an essay saying that calling Christ divine is "detrimental and harmful." He criticized the Bible for containing legends and myths, and described the virgin birth as "downright improbable." He was specifically selected to be the figurehead of the civil rights movement rather than other influential black figures like Jackie Robinson, because it was thought he could use Christian language to appeal to America, even though his actual agenda was completely different.

He plagiarized nearly everything he wrote. Scholars from Stanford University and the King Papers Project found in 1990 that MLK plagiarized nearly all of his works, including his doctoral dissertation. Boston University refused to revoke his doctorate, even though in any other case they would have.

King was a communist for his entire career. He attended the Highlander Folk School in 1957, a Communist Party USA front for training radicals. His right-hand man, Bayard Rustin, who co-founded the Southern Christian Leadership Conference with him, was a Young Communist League member who attended CPUSA conferences. One of his main advisors was Stanley Levison, a major financer and supporter of CPUSA who ghost-wrote a lot of King's material, including the famous "I Have a Dream" speech. King's biographer David Garrow revealed that King frequently called himself a Marxist in conversation with close friends.

King supported reparations and affirmative action. In a 1964 Playboy interview, he essentially called for a $50 billion program to give black people free money.

According to his close friend Ralph Abernathy's memoir, King liked to embezzle donations for alcohol, drugs, and hookers. On speaking tours, he would hire prostitutes for hotel orgies lasting days. He was caught on tape laughing while a woman was sexually assaulted in front of him in a hotel room. Even the night before his assassination, Abernathy said he bedded two women and beat a third in his Memphis motel. The FBI has all the tapes. A judge ordered them sealed until 2027.

The claim that MLK was a peace-loving figure isn't exactly true either. He went to great lengths to present himself as peaceful, but it was mostly an optics thing. In one of his most famous speeches, he rationalized riots by calling them "the language of the unheard." He knew that the fear of rioting and looting could be used to coerce white people into accepting his agenda. Contemporary newspaper cartoons made fun of how ridiculous this was.

MLK was not a real Christian. He was not a virtuous man. He was not a real doctor. He was not some colorblind conservative. He was not committed to peace.
