October 2020 — Internet Forever

The Burger King Crown Saga

A schizophrenic man, a Jamaican love scam, a paper crown, and the meltdown that became the internet's most infamous airline moment.

↓
Chapter I

The Dream

Ryan Brewer was a lonely guy from the Michigan/Ohio area, diagnosed with schizophrenia, struggling through life with severe mental health episodes. At some point he connected with a Jamaican woman online, possibly through Facebook or a dating app, and fell hard. She was going to be his wife.

On September 12, 2020, Brewer bought a one-way ticket to Kingston, Jamaica. He'd been telling everyone back home he was going to "bring home a Jamaican princess." Before he even left, he'd already sent her around $1,000 USD, which she told him was for wedding preparations.

He landed in Jamaica full of hope, expecting a new life.

The before and after of Ryan Brewer's Jamaican dream.

Chapter II

Stranded in Paradise

It became clear almost immediately that the whole thing was a scam. The woman took his money and ghosted. No wedding. No princess. No nothing. Ryan Brewer was now stranded in Jamaica with no money, no return ticket, and a serious mental illness with no support system.

He was homeless in a foreign country for over a month. The place he found refuge? A Burger King restaurant. Some accounts say he worked there, others that he just survived by eating there. Either way, this is where he got the paper crown. It wasn't a random prop or an ironic fashion statement. It was a souvenir from the restaurant that kept him alive while he was stranded, broke, and heartbroken in Jamaica.

A lonely Burger King in Jamaica became his lifeline.

He was so broke that the U.S. Embassy eventually had to purchase his return airline ticket to get him home. That's the level of dire we're talking about.

"He was going to bring home a Jamaican Princess." — Ryan Brewer, before the trip

Then Came the Flight.

Chapter III

JetBlue Flight 1760

October 20, 2020. JetBlue Flight 1760, Kingston to JFK. Ryan Brewer finally boards the plane home, Burger King crown still on his head. He's late getting to his seat and the crew is rushing him.

Then he sees her. A Black woman sitting near his assigned seat who, according to Brewer's own account to airport security afterward, resembled the woman who had scammed him. That's when something snapped.

He accused her of stealing his seat. He claimed she kneed him in the stomach. Then he started screaming the N-word at the top of his lungs. When other passengers called him racist, his defense was: "Excuse me, I'm part-West African, I can say n----r anytime I want!"

He dumped water on a flight attendant. Passengers attacked him. Other passengers pulled out their phones. The whole thing was captured on multiple videos from multiple angles. Eventually police boarded the plane and dragged him off, crown askew, still screaming.

Chapter IV

The Aftermath

JetBlue permanently banned Brewer from their airline. He was taken to Bellevue Hospital in Jamaica for psychiatric evaluation before being allowed to fly home. His family had contacted Jamaican authorities explaining his schizophrenia diagnosis, trying to get him help.

But the story didn't end there. Reports surfaced that Brewer caused similar incidents on subsequent flights with American Airlines and Southwest, suggesting the JetBlue meltdown wasn't a one-time thing but part of a pattern of psychotic episodes in high-stress environments.

Someone later started a crowdfunding campaign called "BringTheKingHome" to fly to Jamaica, find Brewer, and get him proper psychiatric help. The campaign page described him as "a PERSON with severe mental illness" whose "incidents are not his fault" and who "can't control his outbursts." It mentioned he missed his daughter and needed help reconnecting with her.

"What those viral videos don't show is that he is a PERSON with severe mental illness. These incidents are not his fault. He can't control his outbursts." — BringTheKingHome crowdfunding page
Chapter V

The Birth of Kingposting

Within days of the video going viral, 4chan's /pol/ and /tv/ boards latched onto it. They dubbed Brewer "The King" and the meme format "Kingposting." The absurd visual of a grown man in a children's paper Burger King crown screaming racial slurs was just too perfectly internet.

The paper crown became an ironic internet artifact.

👑 Kingposting

Any image or reference to wearing a BK crown during confrontation. Became a reaction format for public meltdowns and absurd entitlement.

🛬 The King's Return

When Italian footballer Chiellini posted a photo on a plane wearing a crown with a BK-related caption, the internet exploded with connections.

🍔 Crown = Racist

Singer Peach PRC once joked about tattooing a BK crown on herself, then woke up to hundreds of messages explaining it was "now a symbol for racists."

💀 Burger King's Nightmare

The brand's decades-old paper crown, an innocent marketing asset, became permanently linked to a racist meltdown. No good way to respond without amplifying it.

The meme eventually detached from the original incident entirely. Anyone wearing a BK crown in any confrontation automatically invokes the reference. It became a shorthand for a very specific kind of unhinged public freakout.

Chapter VI

The Tragedy Under the Crown

Here's what makes this story hit different from your average "racist gets filmed" video: Ryan Brewer wasn't some ideological white supremacist making a political statement. He was a severely mentally ill man who got catfished, scammed out of everything he had, stranded in a foreign country, and then had a psychotic break on the plane ride home when he saw someone who reminded him of the woman who destroyed him.

His YouTube channel, which people dug up after the incident, showed a guy who mostly just talked about missing his daughter and dealing with his problems. Not a hatemonger. A broken person.

None of that excuses screaming racial slurs at strangers on a plane. The people on that flight didn't deserve that regardless of what was going on in Brewer's head. But understanding the full picture turns it from a simple "racist gets owned" clip into something way more complicated and, honestly, way sadder.

The Burger King Crown Saga is simultaneously one of the internet's funniest memes and one of its most depressing origin stories. A mentally ill man's worst moment, captured on multiple phones, turned into an eternal joke. The crown he wore because a fast food restaurant was his only refuge became the punchline. And every time someone new goes viral doing racist shit while wearing a crown, the cycle starts again.
