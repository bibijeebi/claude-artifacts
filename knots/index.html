Key takeaways

Fish N Fool is OP on braid. #2 overall, 138.9% on braid. It's a uni knot with one extra wrap through the eyelet. If you tie a uni, you can tie this in 10 seconds. Should probably be your default braid terminal knot over Palomar.

Improved Clinch on braid is a fish-losing habit. 51.6% efficiency. Your 30 lb braid breaks at 15 lb. Widespread beginner knot, actively dangerous with modern slick braid.

Palomar is fine, not elite. #8 overall. Fish N Fool and Burke both beat it by 10%+ on braid for similar tying effort.

Fluoro drops almost every knot 15-30%. Brittle, hates sharp bends. If you're fishing fluoro leader, knot choice matters more than mono.

Loop knots trade strength for lure action. Single-line architecture means they test lower, but you run them for jig/topwater action, not raw strength.

San Diego Jam is tier 2. #6 average, 115.8%. Solid but not king. The doubled version (line run through the eye twice before wrapping) likely climbs this chart but wasn't tested separately in this dataset.

What's missing: Double San Diego Jam, FG knot (line-to-line, different category), Alberto, Bimini Twist, PR/GT knots (big-game connections), J knot, Micah knot (Fred Hall contest winners). These are all separate categories or require doubled-line prep.

Caveats

Single-line lab pulls with slow tension. Real-world shock loads from a striking fish can break knots that hold fine on a machine. John Merwin (Field & Stream) acknowledged this and planned a shock-tower rig but died in 2013 before completing it.

The strongest knot is the one you tie perfectly every time. A 100% knot tied sloppy breaks at 60%. Wet it, cinch slow, seat gradual. Retie after every fish.
