🎣 Fishing Knots Field Guide

Fifteen knots ranked by actual freshwater usefulness. Images, strength ratings, and field tips.

Bottom Line

If you only memorize four, make it Palomar, Non-Slip Loop, Uni, and Trilene. They cover 95% of freshwater fishing. Add Double Uni or FG the moment you start running a fluoro leader off braid.

Priority Ranking

Tier 1 — Daily Workhorses

#1 Terminal Palomar Strongest all-around. Braid's best friend. #2 Loop Non-Slip Loop Free lure action. Game-changer for jerkbaits/topwater. #3 Versatile Uni Terminal + line-to-line. Swiss army knife. #4 Mono/Fluoro Trilene Classic mono terminal. Already in your muscle memory.

Tier 2 — Situational

Backup Terminal Improved Clinch Trilene's older cousin. Everyone knows it. Line-to-Line Double Uni Braid to fluoro leader. Gateway to FG. Slip Float Bobber Stop Deep crappie, live bait suspending. Circle Hook Snell Forces hookset into corner of mouth. Spool Arbor Line to reel. 15 seconds to learn.

Pro Leader FG Knot Gold standard for braid-to-fluoro. Heavy Fluoro San Diego Jam Saltwater leaders, jigs. Leader Loop Perfection Loop Loop-to-loop connections. Fly fishing. The Knots

T1Palomar Knot

~95% strength Best for: braid, terminal

When to tie it

Your #1 terminal knot. Works on any line type but is especially strong on braid where most other knots slip.

Weaknesses

Lure has to fit through a doubled loop. Gets awkward with bulky crankbaits and treble hooks.

Field tips

  • The whole lure passes through the loop in step 4. First-timers miss this.
  • Wet the knot before final cinch. Every time.
  • On braid, leave a 1/4" tag end. Flush cuts can let it slip.

T1Non-Slip Loop Knot (Lefty Kreh Loop)

~90-95% strength Best for: lures needing free action

When to tie it

Jerkbaits, topwater, small crankbaits, soft plastic swimbaits. Anywhere a lure fishes better with free swing versus cinched tight to the eye.

Wrap count (counterintuitive)

  • Under 8 lb line: 7 wraps
  • 8-14 lb line: 5 wraps
  • 15-40 lb line: 4 wraps
  • 40+ lb line: 3 wraps

Critical detail

The tag end must exit the initial overhand loop on the same side it entered. Exit the wrong side and the knot will never seat properly.

Why it matters

Night-and-day difference in lure action. The biggest skill upgrade you can make past basic terminal knots.

T1Uni Knot

~85-90% strength Best for: anything, terminal or line-to-line

When to tie it

Universal terminal knot. Works on mono, fluoro, braid. Double it up (see Double Uni) for line-to-line.

Wrap count

  • Mono/fluoro: 5-6 wraps
  • Braid: 7-9 wraps (braid is slicker, needs more bite)

Field tips

  • Cinch slow. The coils need to stack neatly, not pile up.
  • Slide the knot down to the eye after tightening. That's where its holding power comes from.

T1Trilene Knot

~90% strength Best for: mono, fluoro terminal

When to tie it

Solid mono/fluoro terminal knot. The double pass through the eye is the whole point, it cushions the line and boosts strength over a regular clinch.

Weaknesses

Don't use on braid, it slips. Stick to Palomar or Uni there.

Field tips

  • Pass through the eye TWICE in step 1. Missing this makes it a regular clinch.
  • 5-6 wraps. Don't shortcut to 3-4.

T2Improved Clinch Knot

~85-90% strength Best for: mono/fluoro terminal

When to tie it

Classic mono/fluoro terminal. The most recognized fishing knot in the world. Backup if Trilene feels fiddly.

The "improvement"

The extra tuck through the big loop (step 4) is what separates it from a regular clinch. It locks the knot. Never skip it.

Weaknesses

Slips on braid. Slightly weaker than Trilene. Use Palomar on braid.

T2Double Uni Knot

~90% strength Best for: line-to-line, braid to fluoro

When to tie it

Connecting two lines together. Mono to mono, mono to fluoro, or braid mainline to fluoro leader. Replaces the blood knot in every category.

How it works

Two Uni knots tied on each other's standing line, then pulled together. The knots slide and jam against each other under load.

Wrap counts (critical)

  • Braid side: 8 wraps (slippery, needs more bite)
  • Fluoro/mono side: 5-6 wraps

Field tips

  • Overlap the two lines 8-10 inches before starting. Short overlap = fumbled knot.
  • Cinch each Uni down individually BEFORE pulling them together. Step 2 and step 5 in the diagram.
  • Lube before the final opposite-direction pull. The two knots slide hot.

Why over blood knot

Handles mismatched diameters way better. Also simpler to tie, especially with cold fingers.

T3Blood Knot

~85% strength Best for: line-to-line, similar diameters

When to tie it

Joining two similar-diameter lines. Old-school standard for mono-to-mono leader builds and fly fishing tapered leaders. Your stepdad's knot.

Real talk vs Double Uni

Blood Knot works, but Double Uni beats it in every practical category: easier to tie with cold fingers, stronger with mismatched diameters (like braid to fluoro), and less finicky about the wraps stacking neatly. If you're starting from scratch, skip to Double Uni.

When Blood still wins

  • Fly fishing tapered leaders where a slim, straight profile matters for casting.
  • Very similar diameter mono-to-mono where the symmetry gives a clean finish.
  • Talking shop with your stepdad. Cultural knot.

Field tips

  • 5 wraps each side is standard. Both sides should mirror each other.
  • Fails on braid. Braid is too slick, the wraps slip loose. Use Double Uni or FG for braid.
  • Lube heavily before the opposite-direction pull. Lots of friction as the two halves pull together.

T3FG Knot

~98-100% strength Best for: braid to fluoro leader

When to tie it

The pro-level line-to-line knot. Braid mainline to fluoro leader. Nearly 100% line strength and the slimmest profile of any leader knot, shoots through rod guides without catching.

Why it's hard

Not intuitive. The braid has to wrap AROUND the leader under tension while the leader stays taut. Most guys practice 20+ times before it clicks.

The diagram in 3 phases

  • Phase 1 (steps 1-4): 23 alternating wraps of braid around the fluoro leader. This is the core of the knot.
  • Phase 2 (steps 5-7): Half-hitches with the braid tag to lock the wraps. 9 additional wraps.
  • Phase 3 (steps 8-10): Finishing knot on the fluoro tag to prevent pull-through.

Field tips

  • Stretch the fluoro tight. Clip in teeth, foot on the butt, or use a leader-tying tool. Tension management is 80% of this knot.
  • Alternate wrap directions. One clockwise, one counter-clockwise. The X pattern is what makes it lock.
  • Count out loud. Losing track of 23 wraps is the #1 fail.
  • Trim fluoro tag flush, trim braid tag to 1/8". Different shrink behavior.

Worth learning when

You start running a dedicated braid-to-fluoro setup for finesse bass, clear-water crappie, or any time you're seeing line-shy fish. Until then, Double Uni works fine.

T2Bobber Stop Knot

N/A (stopper, not load-bearing) Best for: slip float rigs

When to tie it

Slip bobber / slip float fishing. Lets you fish deeper than the rod is long. Clutch for deep crappie, suspending live bait for bass, dock fishing.

Critical detail

Tie the knot with a separate piece of heavy mono or Dacron, NOT with your mainline. Thread mainline through it. This way the knot is small enough to reel through guides but won't cinch to mainline.

How it works

  • Slides up or down mainline with finger pressure.
  • Holds position when cast (bobber stops at the knot).
  • Reels through rod guides without catching.

Rig order (top to bottom)

Bobber stop knot → small bead → slip bobber → split shot → hook with bait.

T2Snell Knot

~95% strength Best for: circle hooks with live bait

When to tie it

Circle hooks with live bait: catfish rigs, bottom rigs for crappie on minnows, anything where you want the hook to rotate into the corner of the fish's mouth automatically.

Why it works

The line pulls from BEHIND the hook shank, not from the eye. That pulling angle forces the hook to turn as the fish runs, setting in the corner of the mouth instead of the gut.

Hook eye requirement

Only tie this on hooks with an up-turned or down-turned eye. Straight-eye hooks don't hold the snell properly.

Pro tip

Pre-tie snelled hooks at home in good lighting. Way easier than trying to snell on the water with cold fingers.

T2Arbor Knot

N/A (reel attachment) Best for: tying fresh line to the spool

When to tie it

Every time you spool new line. Takes 15 seconds to learn and saves you from the embarrassment of watching line peel off the spool on a long run.

How it works

First overhand knot grips the standing line. Second overhand knot acts as a stopper. When the fish pulls, the first knot slides down and jams against the stopper, which jams against the arbor.

Tip for braid

Braid is slick and can slip on the spool even with an arbor knot. Either put down 20 yards of mono backing first, or tape the braid to the arbor with electrical tape before spooling.

Field tip

Leave the initial loop (step 1) loose enough to slide around the arbor freely before tying the knots. Too tight and you can't get it seated properly.

T3San Diego Jam Knot

~95% strength Best for: heavy fluoro leaders, saltwater

When to tie it

Built for heavy fluoro leaders (20-80 lb). Developed on San Diego tuna boats for iron jigs. Great knot in its domain.

Real talk for freshwater

On 14 lb line for bass/crappie, you're not gaining anything over Palomar or Trilene. Skip unless you're targeting stripers with 20+ lb leaders.

When you'd actually want it

  • Stripers on the coast with heavy fluoro.
  • Saltwater jig fishing.
  • Muskie leaders (though wire is more common).

T3Perfection Loop

~95% strength Best for: end-of-leader loop

When to tie it

Creating a fixed loop at the end of a leader for loop-to-loop connections. Standard in fly fishing (butt section to tippet). Also useful for pre-rigged leader systems.

Why "Perfection"

The loop sits perfectly straight, inline with the standing line. No offset, no wobble. Other loop knots tend to angle off to one side.

NOT a terminal knot

This doesn't tie to a hook or lure directly. It makes a loop that connects to another loop. Different tool, different job.

Freshwater bass/panfish relevance

Low unless you're fly fishing or running pre-tied leader setups. Not something you'll use with standard spinning/baitcasting rigs.

T3Rapala Loop Knot

~90% strength Best for: Rapala lures (or any loop-knot application)

When to tie it

Rapala's official recommendation for their lures. Functionally the same as a Non-Slip Loop, different construction.

vs Non-Slip Loop

Slightly more complex, roughly equivalent strength. Most anglers pick one and stick with it.

Verdict

Learn Non-Slip Loop first. It's simpler and does the same job. Only learn this one if you want redundancy or find the non-slip fiddly to tie.

T3Haywire Twist

N/A (wire-specific) Best for: single-strand wire leader

When to tie it

Single-strand wire leader to hooks or lures. For fish with teeth that cut mono and fluoro: king mackerel, barracuda, pike, musky, sharks.

NC freshwater relevance

Pike and musky if you chase them. For Harris, Jordan, Bass Lake, essentially zero use.

Critical finishing technique

The L-shape break-off is mandatory. Never cut wire with nippers, the stub will stab you every time you handle the rig. Bending back-and-forth breaks the wire clean.

Pro tip

The first 5 loose twists should look like a barber pole (two strands twisting around each other evenly). If one wire just wraps around the other, pull apart and restart.

T3Regular Clinch Knot (reference only)

~80% strength Best for: nothing (use Improved Clinch)

Included for recognition only

This is what not to tie. The regular clinch skips the final tuck through the big loop. That missing tuck is the difference between 80% and 90% line strength.

Rule

Always do the extra tuck. Always tie the Improved Clinch instead.

Universal Pro Tips (apply to every knot)

  • Lube before cinching. Spit works. Dry friction burns the line and costs 10-20% strength.
  • Leave 8-10 inches of tag end before starting. Short tags lead to fumbled knots.
  • Cinch slow and steady. Yanking creates heat and uneven coils.
  • Trim tag to 1/8", not flush. Flush cuts can unravel, especially on fluoro.
  • Pull-test against the rod after tying. Break it now, not on a fish.
  • Use nippers, not teeth. Teeth crush line fibers right next to the cut.
  • Retie after every decent fish. The knot takes abuse even if you don't see it.
  • More wraps on braid, not fewer. Slippery line needs extra bite.
  • Cold fingers ruin knots. Hand warmers in winter fishing aren't optional.
  • Practice with paracord at home. Easier to see what's happening than with clear 14 lb mono.

Drive-Home Practice Priority

  • Non-Slip Loop until automatic (biggest skill upgrade past basics)
  • Palomar blindfolded (your #1 workhorse)
  • Uni with both 5 wraps and 7 wraps (feels different, both useful)
  • Double Uni if you plan to run leaders
  • Skip Haywire, Perfection, and Rapala Loop for this trip, specialty or redundant
