Does It Actually Matter?

The no-bullshit guide to what's worth spending on in your kitchen. Save money. Skip the marketing hype. Cook better.

Spirits Fats & Oils Vinegars Salt Sweeteners Spices Dairy & Eggs Pantry Cocktails

The Golden Rules

  • Flavor that cooks off: Cheap is fine (vodka, brandy, cheap wine)
  • Flavor that concentrates: Medium quality (wine reductions, stocks)
  • Flavor tasted directly: Worth spending (finishing oils, bitters, fresh citrus)
  • Texture differences: Often real (butter fat, panko vs breadcrumbs, bronze-cut pasta)
  • Old spices: Replace anything older than a year. Biggest upgrade for the money.
  • Fakes exist: EVOO, balsamic, saffron, parmesan, San Marzano. Verify.

Spirits & Alcohol

Vodka Brand Irrelevant

By definition, vodka is distilled to be as neutral as possible. Blind tests consistently show people can't distinguish premium ($40) from bottom-shelf ($12) in mixed drinks. Even neat, trained tasters fail.

For cooking: You're using it for one chemical property: ethanol extracts flavor compounds water can't. Any vodka does this identically. Popov = Belvedere.

Buy Costco Kirkland, Svedka, Tito's, whatever's on sale. Save money for better mixers.

Whiskey Context Dependent

Whiskeys have actual flavor compounds from barrel aging, mash bills, char levels. These differences ARE detectable neat or in spirit-forward drinks (old fashioned, manhattan).

But in a whiskey sour or jack & coke? The citrus/sugar/cola overwhelms most nuance. The $50 vs $25 gap is real. The $25 vs $15 gap is smaller than people claim.

Mixing: Buffalo Trace, Evan Williams, Wild Turkey 101. Don't waste good stuff in coke. Sipping: This is where spending matters.

Wine for Cooking Quality Matters (To a Point)

The real rule: Don't cook with wine you wouldn't drink, but don't cook with wine you'd savor.

Wine has acids, tannins, sugars, and flavor compounds that survive cooking and concentrate as liquid reduces. Off or corked wine = amplified off flavors. But subtle complexity burns off, so that $40 pinot's delicate notes are gone after 20 minutes of simmering.

Use CaseWhat to Buy
Deglazing, pan sauces$8-12 bottle you'd actually drink
Braises, stews (long cook)Same tier, nothing fancier
Wine reductionsSlightly better, flavor concentrates
Coq au vin, beef bourguignonUse what you'll serve alongside
"Cooking wine" from the grocery aisle is salted garbage. Never buy it.

Keep a box wine (Bota Box, Black Box) for cooking. Stays fresh for weeks, always on hand. The $20 box = ~4 bottles.

Beer for Cooking Style > Brand

The honest answer: "Craft" is a marketing term, not a quality indicator for cooking. What matters is style and flavor intensity.

StyleCooking UseCraft vs Macro?
Light lager (Bud, Coors)Beer batter, beer can chickenNone. Macro is better here.
Stout/PorterStews, braises, chocolate dessertsMinimal. Roast is roast.
IPAsGenerally avoidBitterness concentrates badly.
Belgian stylesCarbonnade, musselsSome, but Chimay works great.

A $3 Modelo and a $8 craft lager both reduce to approximately the same thing in chili.

Irish Cream Brand Irrelevant

Kirkland vs Baileys: Closer than you'd think. Both are cream + Irish whiskey + cocoa/vanilla. In coffee or mixed drinks: nearly indistinguishable. Neat: Baileys is slightly smoother. The price gap ($13 vs $25+) isn't justified.

For cooking (Irish cream cheesecake, truffles): zero meaningful difference.

Fats & Oils

Olive Oil — The Big One Fraud Alert

TypeUseQuality Matter?
Extra virgin (EVOO)Finishing, dressings, dippingYES. Spend $15-25, look for harvest date.
"Pure" or "light" olive oilHigh-heat cooking, fryingNo. It's refined, flavor stripped.
EVOO for sautéingMedium heatModerate. Don't use fancy stuff.
Fake EVOO is rampant. Most Italian grocery store bottles are cut with seed oils or rancid. Verified legit: California Olive Ranch, Kirkland Organic, Cobram Estate.

Butter Context Dependent

Salted vs unsalted: Matters for baking (salt amounts vary between brands). Unsalted for baking, salted for spreading/finishing.

European-style (Kerrygold, Plugra): Higher butterfat, less water. Noticeably better for finishing dishes, pastry, eating on bread. Not worth it melted into a sauce.

Generic vs premium for cookies: You'll taste the difference. Worth it.

Butter vs margarine for baking: Not interchangeable. Butter has water content and milk solids that affect texture. Margarine makes rubbery cookies.

Other Oils Quick Reference

Coconut oilRefined (no coconut taste) vs unrefined (coconut flavor). Pick based on desired flavor.
Lard/tallowQuality varies. Leaf lard (around kidneys) is cleanest for pastry.

Salt

The Truth About Salt Texture, Not Chemistry

Salt is sodium chloride. The chemistry is identical. What varies: crystal size/shape (affects dissolving, measuring, adhesion), trace minerals (minimal flavor impact), texture pleasure on tongue.

SaltWhen It MattersWhen It Doesn't
Kosher (Diamond Crystal or Morton)Default cooking salt. Pick one, learn it.—
Table saltBaking (dissolves fully)Don't use for cooking, hard to control.
Flaky finishing (Maldon)Crushed on steak, chocolate chip cookiesDissolved in anything
Pink HimalayanInstagram aestheticsAlways. Trace minerals are homeopathically small.
Diamond Crystal vs Morton kosher are NOT interchangeable by volume. Diamond Crystal is half as dense. 1 tsp Morton = ~2 tsp Diamond Crystal. Recipes usually assume Diamond Crystal. This screws people up constantly.

Sweeteners

Sugar Mostly Irrelevant

White granulated: All identical. Cheapest wins.

Brown sugar: Light vs dark matters (molasses content). Brand doesn't.

Powdered/confectioners: All identical.

Honey Variety Matters

Clover (mild) vs buckwheat (intense, molasses-y) vs wildflower (variable). Brand within variety matters less. Avoid ultra-processed stuff that's basically corn syrup (check ingredients). Real honey crystallizes, that's fine.

Maple Syrup Real vs Fake

Real maple syrup vs "pancake syrup" (corn syrup + flavoring): Completely different. Never substitute.

Grade A Golden vs Amber vs Dark: Darker = more maple flavor. Personal preference. All real. Costco's is perfectly good.

Vanilla Context Dependent

Extract vs imitation: In baked goods where vanilla is cooked, difference is minimal-to-undetectable in blind tests (Serious Eats tested this).

Imitation works fine: cookies, cakes, anything baked.

Real extract worth it: custards, ice cream, anything where vanilla isn't heated.

Spices

The #1 Rule Freshness > Brand

Freshness matters more than brand. Ground spices lose potency in 6-12 months. That 3-year-old cumin? Basically sawdust.

Whole vs ground: Whole lasts years. Toast and grind yourself for dramatic improvement. True for cumin, coriander, peppercorns, cinnamon sticks, nutmeg.

Buy from ethnic grocery stores (Indian, Mexican, Middle Eastern). Higher turnover = fresher spices at 1/3 the price of McCormick.

Key Spice Notes

SpiceWhat to Know
Black pepperPre-ground is stale. Get a grinder. Single biggest spice upgrade.
CinnamonCeylon (delicate) vs Cassia (punchy, "classic"). Both valid but different.
Red pepper flakesFresher = hotter. Old flakes are dull. Turkish Urfa/Aleppo are great alternatives.
SaffronExtremely variable quality. Cheap saffron is often safflower (does nothing). Pay $8-15/gram from reputable sources.

Dairy & Eggs

Eggs Depends on Use

Brown vs white: Zero difference. Shell color = hen breed.

Farm fresh/pastured vs store: More orange yolk, richer flavor. Noticeable in eggs that star (fried, poached). Irrelevant in baked goods.

Egg size: Matters for baking. Recipes assume large eggs.

Cheese Know the Fakes

Parmesan:

• Parmigiano-Reggiano (real, PDO): Aged 12+ months, complex, crystalline. The good stuff.

• Domestic "parmesan": Younger, simpler. Fine for melting, less good for finishing.

• The green can: Cellulose filler, processed. Avoid.

For shaving over dishes: real Parm matters. For melting into baked pasta: less critical.

Other Dairy

ProductWhat to Know
MilkWhole vs 2% vs skim matters (fat content). Brand doesn't.
Heavy creamHigher fat brands make better whipped cream. 36% vs 40% is noticeable.
Sour cream vs crème fraîcheCrème fraîche is richer, doesn't break when heated. Worth it for sauces.

Pantry Staples

Canned Tomatoes DOP Is Real

San Marzano (DOP): Premium Italian plum tomatoes, sweet, less acidic. Worth it for simple sauces where tomatoes star.

Many "San Marzano style" are fake. Look for DOP certification.

For chili, stew, heavily spiced dishes: regular canned tomatoes are fine. Best value: Cento, Tuttorosso, even Hunt's is acceptable.

Soy Sauce Brewed vs Hydrolyzed

Naturally brewed (Kikkoman) vs hydrolyzed (La Choy): Different products. Naturally brewed has more depth. This matters.

Light vs dark (Chinese): Different uses. Japanese vs Chinese vs Korean: Different profiles, not interchangeable for authentic dishes.

Stock & Condiments

ItemWhat to Know
Chicken/beef stockBetter Than Bouillon (paste) is the best commercial compromise. Boxed: Pacific > Swanson.
Fish sauceQuality varies wildly. Red Boat is gold standard. Cheap = harsh/ammoniac.
WorcestershireLea & Perrins is original and still best. Generics are noticeably worse.
Hot sauceFrank's ≠ Tabasco ≠ Cholula ≠ Sriracha. Not interchangeable.

Pasta & Grains

Dried pasta: Bronze-cut vs teflon-cut is real. Bronze is rougher, holds sauce better. De Cecco, Rummo, Garofalo are good bronze-cut. Barilla is teflon, perfectly acceptable.

Rice: Long vs medium vs short grain are completely different starches. Jasmine vs basmati: different aromatics. Not interchangeable.

Bread crumbs: Panko (Japanese) is larger, crispier, best for most breading. This matters.

Cocktail Specifics

The Non-Negotiables Don't Cheap Out

Bitters: Angostura is the default, no substitute. Bitters last forever.

Citrus juice: Fresh only. Bottled lime/lemon tastes completely different. This is not optional.

Vermouth: Opened vermouth goes bad. Refrigerate, use within 1-2 months. Cheap vermouth ruins a martini. Dolin, Cocchi are good mid-shelf.

Maraschino cherries: Luxardo vs Red #40 supermarket cherries. Night and day. The neon red ones are barely food.

Where Quality Shows

ItemWhat to Know
Tonic waterThis matters in G&T. Fever-Tree, Q Tonic are noticeably better than Schweppes.
Simple syrupMake your own. 1:1 sugar:water, shake. Bottles are overpriced sugar water.
Triple sec/Cointreau/Grand MarnierCheap triple sec is just orange sugar syrup. Cointreau is drier, better. Grand Marnier is cognac-based, richer.

The Master Rules

  1. Flavor that cooks off: Cheap is fine (vodka, cheap wine, brandy)
  2. Flavor that concentrates: Medium quality (wine reductions, stocks)
  3. Flavor that's tasted directly: Worth spending (finishing oils, bitters, fresh citrus, good chocolate)
  4. Texture differences: Often real (butter fat content, panko vs breadcrumbs, bronze vs teflon pasta)
  5. Old spices: Biggest bang-for-buck upgrade is replacing spices older than a year
  6. Fake versions exist: EVOO, balsamic, saffron, Parmesan, San Marzano. Verify.
