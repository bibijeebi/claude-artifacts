North Carolina

Raw Milk Laws
In Plain English

Everything you need to know about legally obtaining unpasteurized dairy in the Tar Heel State

Current as of: January 2026 · G.S. § 106-266.35

The Bottom Line

Selling raw milk for human consumption is illegal in NC. However, two legal workarounds exist: herd shares (you own part of the animal) and pet milk (labeled "not for human consumption"). The 2025 legislature tried to change things both ways—failed both times. Status quo remains.

1 The Two Legal Methods

🐄 Herd Share

EXPLICITLY LEGAL

You purchase partial ownership of a cow, goat, or other lactating animal. As an owner, you receive your share of what the animal produces—not buying milk, receiving your property.

  • Initial buy-in fee ($50–$200 typical)
  • Monthly boarding/care fee ($25–$75)
  • Regular pickup of your milk allocation
  • Written ownership agreement with farmer

🐾 Pet Milk

LEGAL GRAY AREA

Raw milk sold as animal feed with required labeling. Ostensibly for your pets. What you do at home is your business—the farmer has legal cover.

  • Must be labeled "NOT FOR HUMAN CONSUMPTION"
  • Must state sale for humans is illegal in NC
  • Labels submitted to DENR
  • No permit required for farmer

💡 Pro Tip

Herd shares provide better legal protection. You have documented ownership of the animal. Pet milk technically puts the legal gray area on you if questions arise.

2 What the Law Actually Says

"This labeling requirement does not apply to raw milk or raw milk products dispensed for personal use or consumption to the independent or partial owner of a cow, goat, or other lactating animal."

— G.S. § 106-266.35(b)

The key distinction: you're not buying milk—you're receiving product from an animal you partially own. The farmer is boarding and milking the animal on your behalf. This exemption has been explicitly protected in NC law since 2004 and strengthened in 2018.

"Any raw milk or raw milk product dispensed as animal feed shall include on its label the statement 'NOT FOR HUMAN CONSUMPTION' in letters at least one-half inch in height."

— G.S. § 106-266.35(b)

3 2025 Legislative Drama

Both sides tried to change the law. Both failed. Here's what happened:

March 2025

HB 609 Introduced

Bipartisan bill to legalize on-farm raw milk sales within 100 miles. Would have created a licensing system with testing requirements. Stalled in House Rules Committee.

May 2025

Farm Act Attempts Ban

Ag Commissioner Troxler pushed to close both loopholes, citing bird flu concerns. Over 100 raw milk supporters showed up. One person arrested for bringing a gun.

May 7, 2025

Ban Provisions Removed

After bipartisan outcry (Morrow and Chitlik united), the Senate stripped raw milk language from SB 639. Pet milk loophole survives.

June 2025

Farm Act Passes Senate

SB 639 passes 31-14 with all raw milk provisions removed. Stalls in House. HB 609 also dead.

January 2026

Status Quo Remains

Both herd shares and pet milk remain legal. Expect the fight to resume in the 2026 session.

Health considerations: Raw milk can contain pathogens including Salmonella, E. coli, Listeria, and Campylobacter. Higher risk for pregnant women, young children, elderly, and immunocompromised individuals. Know your farmer, ask about testing protocols.

6 What to Ask Your Farmer

Good questions when evaluating a herd share or raw milk source:

  • ✓ What breed are your animals? (Jersey and Guernsey have higher butterfat)
  • ✓ What do you feed them? (Grass-fed vs grain-finished affects nutrition)
  • ✓ Do you test for pathogens? How often?
  • ✓ Are animals tested for TB and brucellosis?
  • ✓ Can I visit the farm and see the milking operation?
  • ✓ How is milk handled and cooled after milking?
  • ✓ What's included in the herd share agreement?
