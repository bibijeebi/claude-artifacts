Proposal #008

Accord Subwoofer Install

Hook up the Kicker sub and amp kit that have been sitting in the trunk of the 2017 Accord EX for months. DIY driveway job, one afternoon. ANC disable included.

The Situation

There is a Kicker subwoofer (in an enclosure) and an amp wiring kit sitting in the trunk of the Accord right now. They've been there for a while. The car has the stock head unit, which works fine and doesn't need to be replaced. The sub just needs to be wired up to the amp, the amp needs power from the battery, and the whole system needs an audio signal tapped from the factory speakers.

The stock head unit has no RCA outputs, so we need a way to get a clean signal to the amplifier. There are two options: use an amp with high-level (speaker wire) inputs, or add a Line Output Converter (LOC) to convert the speaker signal to RCA. Either way works. The LOC route (something like the AudioControl LC2i) gives you a cleaner signal with bass boost and remote level control, but a basic amp with high-level inputs works fine for a single sub setup.

The Install (Step by Step)

Step 1: Run Power Wire Battery → Trunk
  1. Disconnect the negative battery terminal before touching anything. This is non-negotiable.
  2. Locate the pre-drilled firewall grommet on the driver's side, near the gas pedal area. It's a rubber plug. Pop it out with a flat screwdriver.
  3. Feed the main power cable (the thick red/yellow wire from the amp kit) through the firewall hole from the engine bay into the cabin.
  4. Install the inline fuse holder on the power cable within 18 inches of the battery. Do not connect to battery yet.
  5. Route the power cable along the driver's side under the door sill trim (just pop the plastic trim off, tuck wire under, snap back). Run it all the way to the trunk.
  6. Keep the power cable on the opposite side from any speaker or RCA cables to avoid interference.
Step 2: Remote Turn-On Wire Fuse Box → Trunk
  1. Open the fuse box under the steering wheel (driver's side kick panel).
  2. Find an accessory fuse slot that only has power when the ignition is on. On the 2017 Accord, the front accessory power outlet fuse works well.
  3. Plug the Add-a-Fuse tap into that slot. This gives you a 12V signal that turns on with the car and off when you shut it down.
  4. Run the remote wire (thin blue wire from amp kit) from the fuse box, along the same path as the power cable, all the way to the trunk.
Why this matters: The remote wire tells the amp to turn on only when the car is running. Without it, the amp either stays on and kills your battery, or you'd have to manually flip a switch every time.
Step 3: Get the Audio Signal Speakers → LOC/Amp
  1. Since the EX has a factory subwoofer on the rear deck, the easiest signal tap is the factory sub wires in the trunk. This saves you from pulling the head unit or rear door panels.
  2. If your amp has high-level inputs: Tap the factory sub wires (or rear speaker wires) directly and run them to the amp's speaker-level inputs.
  3. If using an LOC: Tap the wires, run them to the LOC. From the LOC, run RCA cables to the amp. Mount the LOC somewhere accessible in the trunk near the amp.
  4. Use proper splice connectors or solder + heat shrink. No scotch locks if you can help it, they corrode.
EX-specific tip: Some owners get a cleaner, fuller signal by tapping the rear 6.5" speaker wires instead of the factory sub wire. The factory sub signal is heavily filtered by the factory amp. Try the sub wire first since it's easiest, but if it sounds thin or weak, switch to tapping the rear speakers and use the amp's low-pass filter to cut the highs.
Step 4: Ground, Connect, Tune The Fun Part
  1. Ground the amp: Find bare metal in the trunk. Sand any paint down to shiny metal. Bolt a ring terminal with a short ground wire (under 18 inches) tightly to the bare metal. A trunk bolt works, but avoid seatbelt bolts.
  2. Connect all wires to the amp: power, ground, remote, and audio signal (RCA or speaker-level).
  3. Connect amp output to the subwoofer enclosure's terminals.
  4. Double-check every connection. Make sure the power cable fuse is installed and the inline fuse holder is closed.
  5. Reconnect the negative battery terminal.
  6. Turn the car on. Set the amp gain to minimum. Play music. Slowly turn the gain up until it sounds full but not distorted. Set the low-pass filter to ~80Hz so the sub only plays bass.
  7. Secure the sub enclosure so it doesn't slide around. Velcro strips, a strap, or L-brackets bolted to the trunk floor all work.

ANC Disable (Required)

The 2017 Accord EX has the 7-speaker premium audio system with Active Noise Cancellation. ANC uses cabin microphones and the factory subwoofer to pump out-of-phase sound that cancels engine noise. The moment you add an aftermarket sub, ANC detects the new bass, tries to cancel it, and the more powerful aftermarket sub amplifies the cancellation signal instead. The result is extremely loud, pulsing feedback through the speakers that gets worse when accelerating or decelerating. This is not optional to fix.

This MUST be done before you test the sub. Every single Accord forum thread about "my new sub sounds broken" traces back to ANC still being active. Do not troubleshoot anything else until ANC is disabled.

How to Disable ANC

  1. Remove the center console storage tray below the radio. Two Phillips screws hold it in. Pull it straight out.
  2. Reach behind the opening. You'll feel a silver metal box (the ANC module) mounted to the dash brace.
  3. Disconnect the wiring harness from the ANC module. It's a tight fit. Wiggle it, don't yank it.
  4. That's it. Tuck the harness out of the way and reinstall the tray.

Bluetooth, hands-free calling, steering wheel controls, and everything else still works normally after this. You may notice slightly more road/engine noise with the stereo off, but it's minimal and honestly most people can't even tell.

Bonus: better stock sound too. The ANC module steals power from the head unit to produce cancellation frequencies through all speakers. Disconnecting it frees up that power, and multiple forum users report noticeably improved audio quality from the factory system even before the aftermarket sub is connected.

Tools Needed

Everything here is stuff you already own or can grab from the garage: 10mm socket and ratchet, Phillips screwdriver, flat screwdriver (for trim clips), wire strippers/crimpers, electrical tape and/or heat shrink, zip ties, sandpaper or a wire brush (for the ground point), panel removal tool or a plastic butter knife. A multimeter is nice to have for verifying the remote wire has 12V with ignition on.

Pro Tips

Gain Setting

The #1 killer of subwoofers is clipping from the gain being set too high. Turn your head unit volume to about 75%. Then slowly raise the amp gain until the bass is full but clean. If you hear any crackling, buzzing, or the cone sounds like it's "bottoming out," you've gone too far. Back it down. That's your max. A properly gained sub will sound deeper and punchier than one cranked to distortion.

Low-Pass Filter

Set the amp's low-pass crossover (LPF) to 80Hz. This tells the sub to only play the deep bass frequencies and leave the mids and highs to the door speakers. If the sub sounds boomy or muddy, try dropping it to 60-70Hz.

Phase Switch

Most amps have a 0/180 degree phase switch. Try both. One setting will sound noticeably fuller and more natural from the driver's seat. That's the right one.

Securing the Enclosure

An unsecured sub box in the trunk is a projectile in an accident. At minimum, use heavy-duty Velcro strips. Better is an L-bracket screwed into the trunk floor (use self-tapping screws through the carpet into the metal) with a strap or bungee over the box.

The Bottom Line

You already own everything that costs real money. This is a free-to-$55 afternoon project that's been putting itself off. No new head unit needed. No shop visit needed. Disable ANC, wire up the amp, tune it, and enjoy the bass you paid for months ago.
