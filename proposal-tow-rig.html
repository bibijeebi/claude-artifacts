Proposal #031

The Tow Rig: Boat First, Truck Second

Bekah wants a boat she can get drunk on and a truck to pull it, instead of hitching things to the Tesla forever. The Model Y's 3,500 lb rating covers a 16–18 ft boat in the meantime, which flips the buying order in our favor: boat when winter prices bottom out, used V8 half-ton whenever the right one appears, zero deadline pressure on either.

The Situation

Towing with the Model Y works but it's a compromise: roughly half the range, a 3,500 lb ceiling with no upgrade path, and every hitched mile is wear on the family car. Bekah's position is get a truck. The boat is the second half of the same picture, sized like Jeff's, a 16–18 ft bowrider or aluminum fishing boat, not a pontoon.

The unlock: because the Model Y can tow this class of boat, neither purchase blocks the other. We shop each market at its seasonal bottom instead of buying both under pressure.

Phase 1: The Boat

Boats are brutally seasonal. Listings bottom out December–February when sellers are staring at winter storage bills. Budget $5k–$15k used depending on age; a clean 2000s Sea Ray 180 averages around $6k on the national market, while late-model Bayliner VR4s hold above $20k.

The hard constraint while the Tesla is the tow rig: 3,500 lbs total, boat + trailer + fuel + gear, and ~350 lbs tongue weight. Shopping rule: listed dry weight under ~2,200 lbs. After a 600–900 lb trailer, full fuel (a 20-gal tank is 125 lbs), battery, and coolers, that lands around 3,000–3,200 with real margin. Verify actual weights per listing, brochure dry weights lie by a few hundred pounds.

Where to hunt: FB Marketplace Raleigh — bowrider, FB Marketplace — boats w/ trailer, Craigslist Raleigh boats, Boat Trader for dealer stock. This budget lives on Marketplace, not dealer lots.

Non-negotiables at inspection: compression test or cold-start video on the motor, transom solid (no flex, no soft spots), trailer with surge brakes preferred, title in hand for boat AND trailer. NC titles both separately.

Tesla ramp duty, already sorted: factory tow package with 2" receiver, 4-to-7-pin adapter in hand, Trailer Mode handles the software. AWD plus a heavy car is genuinely good on a wet ramp. Range hit is irrelevant on a 40 min run to Jordan or Falls Lake.

Phase 2: The Truck

The market context: average new full-size transaction is ~$66k, tariffs added $2k–$6k to some segments since late 2025, and prices are trending up. Meanwhile used prices are flat and loan qualification is the easiest it's been in a decade. Every signal says buy used, and the used market has no season, so this phase fires whenever the right truck appears.

Target: 2018–2021 F-150 5.0 V8 or Silverado 5.3 V8$26k–$32k

Crew cab, 4x4, factory tow package (receiver, 7-pin, trans cooler, integrated brake controller), 3.55 or shorter gears, 50–80k miles. Tows 9,000–11,000 lbs, triple anything we'll ever hook up, and both are simple NA V8s that get wrenched on in the driveway, not at a dealer. 4x4 is non-negotiable for ramp duty.

Skip the 2.7 EcoBoost for tow duty (runs hotter, more complexity), skip new midsizes (a new Frontier costs the same $34k as a used half-ton that's twice the machine and already ate its depreciation).

Standing searches: AutoTempest F-150 4x4 '18–'21 near Willow Spring, AutoTempest Silverado 1500 4x4 '18–'21, CarGurus F-150 near Garner (their deal ratings flag over/underpriced listings).

The Driveway

Truck plus boat trailer doesn't fit the current driveway. Fix is a gravel pad extension: geotextile fabric, 4" of compacted ABC stone, a few hundred dollars and one weekend. Gravel generally doesn't count toward impervious surface in unincorporated Wake County the way concrete does, which is exactly why it's the answer. Ties into the existing driveway spur draft (#021), so the pad gets sized for both once the truck's footprint is known.
