Jean-François Gariépy

JF / JFG
b. 1984Saint-Sophie, QuebecEx-NeuroscientistMissing Partner

Jean-François Gariépy is a Canadian former neuroscience researcher turned white supremacist commentator. His story is among the darkest on this list, intertwined with disturbing allegations involving vulnerable women and an unsolved disappearance.

Academic Career

Gariépy studied biology at the Université de Montréal, earning the Society for Neuroscience's Next Generation Award (2008). He completed a PhD in neuroscience studying neural networks in lampreys (2012), then researched social interactions in monkeys at Duke University's Institute for Brain Sciences (2011-2015). He reportedly received $25,000 from Jeffrey Epstein for "NeuroTV." Court records revealed he was not asked to return after a sexually inappropriate relationship with an undergraduate lab assistant he supervised.

Far-Right Media

Gariépy launched The Public Space and later JFG Tonight, hosting Richard Spencer, David Duke, Mike Peinovich, Nick Fuentes, and Greg Johnson. The ADL lists The Public Space among "White Supremacist Channels." He promotes a white ethnostate, antisemitism, and claims of genetic superiority of white people. He published The Revolutionary Phenotype.

Pattern with vulnerable women: Married and divorced three times. His third wife won sole custody after evidence he threatened to abduct their baby to Canada. In 2018, a guardianship lawsuit revealed he had pursued a 19-year-old with autism whom a doctor assessed as having "the social and mental maturity of a 10 or 11-year-old child." Gariépy contacted her online and she traveled to North Carolina, where he reportedly intended to impregnate her. Her parents won guardianship. He stated a white ethnostate "would've prevented these legal issues."
The Disappearance of Élora Patoine

In June 2023, Gariépy's partner Élora Patoine ("Mama JF"), age 30, vanished. He says he dropped her at a gas station June 17; she was last seen June 19 in Moncton, New Brunswick. She left behind her phone and credit cards. Gariépy described embarking on a "deep clean" of their home shortly after. The RCMP investigated as a "high-priority file" and invoked the Missing Persons Act.

In videos, Gariépy drew lessons from her disappearance: "That's the cost of female liberty: 50,000 policemen, full-time salary, nationwide searches... Just allow some form of control of husbands over their wives."

Élora Patoine has never been found.
