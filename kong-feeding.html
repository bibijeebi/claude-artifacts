Homestead Dog Feeding

Kong's Game & Fish
Feeding Reference

Putting wild-caught fish and harvested game into a 150 lb Saint Bernard, safely, without screwing up the nutrition.

Bottom line: Keep a complete base (Farmer's Dog or good kibble) doing the real nutrition. Feed your fish and game as toppers on top of it. Do that and you can't screw up balance, because the base is already complete. You only need the full ratio and calcium math below if game ever becomes a primary calorie source, which for a 150 lb dog is a lot of harvesting.

01The two models

  • PMR (Prey Model Raw): mimics a whole prey animal. 80% muscle meat / 10% bone / 5% liver / 5% other organ. No veg. This ratio is the gospel number.
  • BARF: same idea plus some veg/fruit and supplements.

Hit the ratio averaged over a week or two, not every single meal.

02How much food

Only matters if game ever goes primary instead of topper. Adult dog: 2-3% of body weight per day, raw weight.

  • Kong at 150 lb = 3 to 4.5 lb/day. Start at 3, adjust by waistline.
  • That's the bar for topper vs. staple: a couple panfish or a squirrel puck is a rounding error against 3-4 lb. It's a garnish. Don't sweat balance on garnishes.

03Cook vs. freeze-raw, by animal

The whole game: fish you can freeze-and-feed-raw. Land game you cook. Freezing reliably kills fish parasites; it does NOT reliably handle mammal pathogens.

04Bone & the calcium fork

Bone is the calcium source. This is the piece people miss.

  • Feeding raw? Raw bone is IN. Soft, bends, doesn't splinter. It's your ~10% and your calcium. Done.
  • Cooking the meat (all your game)? Bones come OUT, cooked bone splinters. But now you removed the calcium, so you add it back if the meat is a real part of the diet.
Calcium replacement for cooked boneless meat
  • Ground eggshell, ~1/2 tsp per pound of boneless meat. (1/2 tsp ≈ one large eggshell ≈ ~800-1000 mg calcium.)
  • Make it: rinse shells, dry them out, blitz to fine powder in a spice grinder, store in a jar.
  • Stir into the meat or sprinkle on the bowl.

Again: only matters when cooked meat is a dietary chunk, not a topper. A puck on top of complete food doesn't need eggshell.

05Organ dosing

Organ meat is nutritional gold, and it's exactly the 5%+5% in the ratio.

  • Liver: max ~5% of diet. Loaded with vitamin A. Amazing as dehydrated treats, but a small-dose thing, not a main course. Don't free-feed a pile of liver jerky.
  • Other secreting organ (kidney, spleen, pancreas, brain): the other ~5%.
  • Heart and gizzard are MUSCLE, not organ. Lean, great, feed freely as part of the 80%. Heart especially is excellent.

When you get bigger game: dehydrate liver and heart into treats, keep liver to that ~5% slice, heart can be as much as you want.

06Your actual playbook

  1. Base: Farmer's Dog (it's gently cooked fresh food, not raw, which is why cooking your game matches what Kong already loves) or solid kibble. Carries the complete nutrition.
  2. Fish: freeze 1 wk, feed raw occasionally, or pressure cook for soft-bone toppers.
  3. Squirrel/small game: pressure cook → debone → toss every bone → freeze pucks → topper.
  4. Big game (deer) later: cook the meat, dehydrate organ into treats. If it becomes a primary calorie source, then add eggshell calcium and balance toward 80/10/5/5.
  5. The waste-not outlet happens entirely at the topper level. No risk, no balance math, because the complete base has your back.

You only graduate to real ratio and calcium discipline if you're replacing the base diet with game. For a 150 lb Saint Bernard, that's a serious harvest volume, so you've got runway before it matters.
