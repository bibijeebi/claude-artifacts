RIDER'S GUIDE

      
    

    

      
How to ride

      
Tap a fare card or the gate display to preview a move. Tap again to commit. The board above the gate is your proof, growing line by line. Reach a terminal on every branch and the gate opens.

    

    

      
The rules

      

    

    

      
Ponens vs Tollens

      
MODUS PONENS — hold A, tap A→B, receive B. One move; it's a primitive here. First ride: station 11.

      
MODUS TOLLENS — from A→B and ¬B conclude ¬A. Not a primitive: you build it yourself out of boarding and ponens, because ¬A is just A→⊥.

      

    

    

      
No through service

      
This system is intuitionistic: it only opens a gate when it can print the ticket. Some classically valid routes have no constructive path:

      
✕A ∨ ¬A — excluded middle

      
✕¬¬A ⊢ A — double negation elimination

      
✕((A→B)→A) → A — Peirce's law

      
✕¬(A∧B) ⊢ ¬A ∨ ¬B — De Morgan, this direction only

      
Yet the ghost of excluded middle still runs: ¬¬(A ∨ ¬A) is provable. Station 38.

    

    

      
Every ticket is a program

      
Curry–Howard: a proof of A→B is a function, a proof of A∧B is a pair, a proof of A∨B is a tagged choice, and ⊥ has no proof at all. Every Q.E.D. here exports its proof as a Lean 4 term you can run and check yourself.

    

    

      
Planned service — ∀ and ∃

      
Quantifier lines are surveyed but not built. First-order logic is undecidable in general, so the dispatcher that prices par and certifies every route cannot exist for it. Quantifiers need different rolling stock: a bounded fragment with hand-built stations. v3 territory.
