§1 The concept in plain English

Spanish has two grammatical genders: masculine and feminine. Every noun is one or the other, even objects with no biological sex. El libro (the book) is masculine. La mesa (the table) is feminine.

Quick heuristic: words ending in -o are usually masculine, words ending in -a are usually feminine. Words ending in -ción, -dad, -tad, -tud are feminine. Words from Greek ending in -ma (problema, sistema, clima) are masculine. Memorize the irregulars: el día, la mano, el mapa, la foto, la moto.

The articles bend to match:

 MasculineFeminine
Definite (the)el / losla / las
Indefinite (a/some)un / unosuna / unas

Plurals: add -s after a vowel (libro → libros), -es after a consonant (ciudad → ciudades), and -z → -ces (lápiz → lápices). Words ending in -ión drop the accent in plural (lección → lecciones).

Adjectives agree in both gender and number. El gato negro / la gata negra / los gatos negros / las gatas negras. Adjectives ending in -e or a consonant don't change for gender, only for number: la casa grande, las casas grandes.
