Bottom Line Up Front

You already own the expensive part. Zigbee bulbs are in every room, which means the lighting layer is bought and paid for, it just has no brain that can talk to it. Apple Home can't speak Zigbee, so the move is Home Assistant as the engine with the HomeKit Bridge mirroring everything into the Apple Home app you and Bekah already use, plus Siri on a HomePod. HA runs as a VM on the Proxmox box from Proposal #023, your GPU runs Frigate for local AI cameras at zero monthly cost, and the UniFi network from #023 carries the PoE cameras. Lean entry is one $25 dongle and one HomePod mini, about $125, and every bulb in the house comes online under one roof. Full whole-house buildout with cameras, a doorbell, sensors, and a smart thermostat lands near $1,200, phased over months.

Chains with Proposal #023. This rides on the #023 server + network. Home Assistant runs as a VM on that Proxmox box and Frigate runs as a container with the GPU passed through, so both cost $0 in hardware. The PoE cameras hang off the UniFi switch, and IoT gets its own VLAN. You can do this fully standalone instead with a $99 Home Assistant Green and a cheap PoE switch, the parts list flags both paths. Net: do #023 first and this layer is nearly free to stand up.

Why Now

Right now you've got Zigbee bulbs scattered across the house sitting on their own little islands, run through whatever app shipped in the box. That's a half-built smart home with no brain. The point of this is one brain that runs everything locally: it survives the internet going down, costs nothing per month, and reaches every corner of the property instead of just the living room.

It also folds in pieces already on the board. Kong's dust-mite humidity problem (the dehumidifier, #004) becomes an automatic humidity hold instead of you squinting at a hygrometer. The outdoor rabbitry (#001) gets temp alerts so Rabbi Yosef and Rabbi Thump don't cook or freeze. And the fact that you're out of town for work constantly while Bekah holds down the fort turns into remote eyes on the whole place from a thousand miles away.

The Architecture: Brain and Face

Home Assistant is the engine. Apple Home is the pretty front end. HA does the heavy lifting, Zigbee, cameras, and any automation you can dream up, all running locally on hardware you own. The HomeKit Bridge integration then mirrors every device into the Apple Home app, so you and Bekah keep the clean interface and Siri, and a HomePod gives you voice. You get HA's power with Apple's polish, no compromise.

The Parts

Grouped by layer. The headline number is small because the lights, the server, and the network are either already bought or already proposed. The only true new spend to get rolling is a dongle and a speaker.

Brain & Zigbee bridge

Home Assistant — $0 to $99

Free as a HAOS VM on the Proxmox box from #023, which is the cleanest path and what the pros recommend (virtualize it, never run it on an SD card).

$99 alternative: a Home Assistant Green, a dedicated always-on box, if you want HA independent of the big server being up.

Recommendation: HAOS VM on #023. Proxmox snapshots make backups and rollbacks trivial.

Sonoff ZBDongle-E — ~$25

The Zigbee coordinator, the single USB stick that lets HA talk to every Zigbee bulb regardless of brand.

The most-recommended HA coordinator in 2026. Brand-agnostic, handles 100+ devices, works with ZHA or Zigbee2MQTT, fully local with no cloud. Passes through to the HA VM. Mount it on a short USB extension, away from the router and off USB 3.0 ports, to dodge 2.4 GHz interference.

Voice & the Apple hub

HomePod mini — $99 each (1–2)

Gives you Siri voice control and satisfies Apple Home's remote-access hub requirement. Place one central, add a second at the far end of the house for coverage.

Your Rokus stay exactly as they are, they have nothing to do with this. The HomePod is purely the voice and Apple-hub layer.

The eyes: cameras & doorbell (Frigate)

Frigate NVR — $0 software

Open-source local NVR built for Home Assistant. Runs real-time AI object detection on your GPU, identifies people, cars, packages, and animals, records to the 6TB, and fires events into HA.

No Coral TPU needed since your GPU handles inference. A $35 Coral USB is only relevant if you go standalone without the #023 GPU box.

PoE cameras + doorbell — ~$50–100 each, doorbell ~$100

Reolink or Amcrest PoE cameras (they output RTSP, which Frigate needs) plus a PoE doorbell. One Ethernet cable does power and data, far more reliable than Wi-Fi cameras in a storm.

Ring and Nest don't support RTSP and won't work with Frigate, skip them. A $40 PoE switch powers it all, or use the UniFi PoE switch from #023.

Sensors & control (all Zigbee, all on the one dongle)

Cheap Zigbee sensors & buttons — ~$10–20 each

Contact sensors (doors, garage, freezer), motion sensors, temp/humidity sensors (humidor, rabbitry, shop), water-leak sensors (under sinks, water heater, washer), and wall buttons for physical control. They all join the same dongle, no extra hubs.

Optional: an ecobee or Zigbee smart thermostat (~$130) and a DIY ESP32 + LD2410 mmWave presence sensor (~$15) running ESPHome for true "someone's in the room even if still" detection.

What It Actually Does

This is the part that matters. A pile of smart gear is worthless until it does work for you without being asked. Here's what the system runs once it's built, every one of these tied to how this house actually lives.

Lighting that runs itself

  • Sunset auto-on, sunrise auto-off, per room, so the house is never dark when you walk in and never burning lights at noon.
  • Motion-triggered halls, bathrooms, and closets at night, dimmed low so a 3 a.m. trip doesn't blind you.
  • One-tap scenes: "Movie" drops the living room, "Goodnight" kills everything and locks up, "Leaving" shuts the house down as you pull out.
  • Vacation randomization while you're out of town, lights cycle like someone's home.
  • Adaptive lighting, warm at night and cool by day, automatically, plus a gentle wake-up fade in the bedroom.

Security & cameras (Frigate, all local)

  • Local AI detection of person, car, package, and animal, running on your GPU, no cloud and no monthly fee.
  • Instant phone alert with a snapshot the moment a person hits the driveway or porch, not a generic "motion."
  • Package detection logs the delivery with a timestamp and photo.
  • Pet-aware: ignores Kong and Ginny crossing the yard and only alerts on humans and vehicles, which is the whole reason most people stop turning their cameras off.
  • LLM Vision upgrade: alerts read "FedEx driver with a package" or "unknown person at the side door, no uniform" instead of "person detected."
  • Recordings on the 6TB with smart retention, keep person events for 90 days, throw away empty footage.

Away from home (you're out of town constantly)

  • Full remote view and control of the whole place from your phone in Wisconsin or wherever the job lands.
  • Auto away-mode: arms when both your and Bekah's phones leave, disarms when you're back, no buttons.
  • Bekah-home-alone safety: alerts to both phones, and a one-tap "Hey Siri, show me the front door."
  • Leak, freeze, and smoke alerts reach your phone a thousand miles away, so you catch a burst pipe before it floods the house, not after.
  • Garage-left-open alert plus one-tap remote close via the Meross controller, and a check that the doors locked from the airport.

Homestead & pets

  • Rabbitry temp/humidity monitoring (#001): alerts if the hutch gets too hot or freezing for Rabbi Yosef and Rabbi Thump.
  • Freeze warnings for the garden, the water lines (#016), and the outdoor spigots before a hard night.
  • Kong's dust-mite allergy: humidity sensors trigger the dehumidifier (#004) or HVAC to hold the house under the threshold automatically.
  • Coop and run lights/door on a schedule, plus feed and water reminders.

The shop (welding & woodworking)

  • Shop lights on motion or a wall button, hands full of stock or a stinger, no problem.
  • Dust collection or tool power on a smart plug, schedule or button-triggered.
  • Garage door status and remote close (Meross), and a "did I leave the shop open" check.
  • Shop temp/humidity monitoring to keep rust off the tools and the MIG.

Cigars, bar & climate

  • Humidor sensor with a drift alert, catch a humidor sliding off 70/70 before it dries out a box of Liga Privadas.
  • Bar-area lighting scenes for when you're mixing.
  • Presence + humidity-driven HVAC, don't heat or cool an empty house while you're OOT, and tie climate to the Kong humidity rule.
  • Whole-home energy monitoring, including Tesla charging visibility.

Voice & announcements (Siri / HomePod)

  • "Hey Siri, goodnight" kills the lights, locks the doors, and arms the cameras in one shot.
  • Spoken announcements: "garage door left open," "laundry's done," "someone's at the front door," "freezer door open."
  • Ask Siri for any camera, light, or sensor by name, from any room.

Rollout

Phase it so it's useful from day one and you never carry a big bill at once.

  1. Foundation (a weekend): HA VM on the #023 box (or an HA Green), plug in the Sonoff dongle, migrate every Zigbee bulb onto it, flip on the HomeKit Bridge, add one HomePod mini. Result: every light is local, in Apple Home, and voice-controlled.
  2. Sensors & control: add contact, motion, temp, and leak sensors plus a couple of wall buttons. Write the first real automations, sunset lights, motion halls, garage-open and leak alerts.
  3. The eyes: stand up Frigate on the GPU box, hang PoE cameras and a doorbell off the UniFi switch, wire person/car/package detection to phone alerts.
  4. The fun: LLM Vision smart alerts, mmWave presence, smart thermostat, humidor and rabbitry monitoring, energy, dashboards, and the full scene + voice layer.

Reality Checks

No perfect solution, here's the honest part.

  • HA has a learning curve and ships breaking updates now and then. That's the tax for total control and zero subscriptions. Pin versions and lean on Proxmox snapshots from #023 to roll back in seconds.
  • Camera RTSP URLs are the single fiddliest thing in the whole build, every brand formats them differently. Budget one evening for it.
  • Don't put your only mesh-routing bulbs on wall switches people flip off, a dead bulb kills that branch of the Zigbee mesh. Leave the switches hot, control in software, add buttons for the wall.
  • One dongle replaces a shelf of brand hubs (Hue, Aqara, IKEA). Don't buy a hub per brand.
  • Segment the IoT junk onto its own VLAN on the #023 UniFi network. Security, plus it keeps the chatter off your main network. You've already got the gear for it.
  • HomeKit Secure Video is one-way, an HSV camera like the Aqara G400 over at Laurel's place won't hand video to HA. Go RTSP/PoE on the HA side; you can still push HA cameras into Apple Home, just not pull HSV out.
