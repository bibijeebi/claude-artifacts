First, what's a “venus tummy”

Because the entire joke dies if you don't know the term.

It's the soft lower-belly pouch, rebranded as a goddess feature. The little roll that sits above the waistband and shows up under a tight skirt or dress. The term got coined around September 2025 and detonated across TikTok and X through late 2025 into 2026.

The name does the heavy lifting. It points at the mons veneris, literally the “mound of Venus,” and at the marble Venus and Aphrodite statues the ancients carved with soft, rounded stomachs instead of flat ones. The pitch: that pooch isn't a flaw, it's the most classically feminine thing on the body. Roughly 8.8 million posts ran with it, plus the inevitable “I believe in venus tummy supremacy” meme.

Naturally it became a war. One camp treats it as divine, the other insists it's just a sign of being unhealthy, and the discourse got dumped gasoline by a viral “my gf has a pouch that won't go away, is surgery my only hope” post. So by mid-2026 “venus tummy” is loaded vocabulary that every terminally online person recognizes instantly.

“The Mound of Venus. It protects the vital organs that create life. And it makes a neat pillow to bury one's face in.” actual top reply on the explainer post

Which sets up the punchline perfectly: when you say “venus tummy” out loud to a cop, you are describing the soft lower belly. A region that is anatomically right next door to the part the sketch artist immediately assumes you mean. Hence the lean-in.
