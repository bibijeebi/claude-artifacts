The School

Western Governors University (WGU) is a private, nonprofit, accredited university based in Salt Lake City. It was founded in 1997 by 19 U.S. governors specifically to provide flexible, affordable higher education. It's regionally accredited by the Northwest Commission on Colleges and Universities (NWCCU), the same level of accreditation as traditional universities.

What makes WGU different is the competency-based model. Instead of sitting through 16 weeks of lectures, you study the material and take the assessment when you're ready. Pass the test, get the credit, move on. There's no seat time requirement and no waiting for semesters. You pay a flat rate per 6-month term (~$4,300), and you can complete as many courses as you can handle during that term. People regularly finish entire degrees in one or two terms.

WGU is one of the largest universities in the country with over 180,000 enrolled students. It's VA-approved and has a dedicated Military Support team for veterans using education benefits.

The GI Bill pays WGU's tuition directly and sends me a monthly housing allowance (MHA) of $1,169/month while enrolled, plus up to $1,000/year for books and supplies. Since WGU is 100% online, the MHA is the national flat rate rather than the local BAH rate.

Potential bonus: As a veteran, I may also qualify for the Pell Grant through FAFSA, which stacks on top of the GI Bill. That's up to ~$7,395/year in additional cash, completely separate from tuition coverage.

Why Banking Extra Benefits Matters

If I finish both degrees with GI Bill time remaining (even just one day), it keeps the door open for VR&E (Chapter 31), also known as Vocational Rehabilitation and Employment. Having remaining GI Bill entitlement is one of the eligibility pathways for VR&E, which provides up to 48 additional months of education benefits. VR&E covers tuition, books, supplies, and still pays the GI Bill housing rate.

This means banked months aren't wasted. They're optionality for future grad school, professional certifications, career pivots, or anything else down the road. The faster I complete these two degrees, the more flexibility I preserve.

How WGU Actually Works

There are two types of assessments at WGU:

Objective Assessments (OAs)

Multiple-choice and knowledge-based tests. Study the material, schedule the exam, pass it. Similar to certification exams. Most Accounting courses use this format since the content is factual and rules-based.

Performance Assessments (PAs)

Papers and projects graded against a rubric. Write to the rubric requirements, submit, get evaluated. Common in business and management courses. Usually straightforward once you understand what they're looking for.

Every student gets a Program Mentor who helps plan course scheduling and provides support throughout the program. There are also Course Instructors available for each class if you need help with specific material. It's self-paced but not unsupported.

The flat-rate tuition means there's a built-in incentive to move quickly: whether I complete 3 courses or 15 courses in a term, the price is the same. This is what makes the "speedrun" approach possible, and it's a well-known strategy among WGU students, particularly veterans.
