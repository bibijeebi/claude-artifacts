Proposal #028

Bedroom Built-In Bed Wall

Floor-to-ceiling dark wood built-ins flanking a king bed alcove under an arch. Every freestanding dresser, nightstand, and storage piece gets absorbed into one wall. Pitched by Bekah. Built by Benny.

Total Cost$2,500–$4,500
Difficulty4/5
Build Time4–6 weekends, phased
CategoryHome

The Inspiration

The Problem

The bedroom is huge, and it's filling up the lazy way: dressers, side tables, and storage pieces accumulating one at a time along the walls. Each piece works, but the room reads as furniture parked against paint instead of a designed space.

A built-in wall flips that. Dressers become drawer bases. Nightstands become alcove cabinets. Gun storage becomes a recessed safe bay sized to the safe we already own. Everything else goes behind doors or on lit shelves. One architectural element replaces five freestanding pieces, and the floor comes back.

Why Benny says yes: this is the biggest woodworking project on the board. Face frames, panel work, a laminated or kerf-cut arch, trim carpentry, in-wall wiring for sconces and shelf lights. Every skill he's been collecting, on one wall he sees every day.

The Layout

Keeping the standard king (76" wide). The center bay needs roughly 8 ft including alcove walls, which leaves real width for flanking towers and window-seat bases even on a 14–15 ft wall. Five zones, left to right: tall tower, drawer base with seat top, center arch + bed alcove, drawer base with seat top, tall tower with safe bay.

Existing bedroom palette already cooperates: dark wood built-ins against the light pastel green walls, or paint the wall behind to match the cabinetry for full tone-on-tone. The planned wall-hung leather headboard dies in this plan, the paneled alcove IS the headboard.

The Plan (4 Phases)

Phase 1: Flanking Towers$800–$1,200

Two towers per side using IKEA PAX frames (39¼" wide × 93⅛" tall, ~$100 per frame) as carcasses, plus interior shelves and drawers. IKEA is running 10% off PAX orders over $500 through Sep 7, 2026, so the clock helps here.

Frames get anchored to studs, leveled, and gapped for scribe strips. One tower gets sized around the safe: frame a recessed bay, trim it out, done. No cost beyond lumber since the safe is already owned.

Phase 2: Window Seats + Drawer Bases$700–$900

Four unfinished Hampton Bay drawer base cabinets from Home Depot (~$154–$200 each, paint-ready), topped with a plywood seat deck and cushions. Buy-10-get-10%-off applies if the order is combined with filler cabinets.

Bases sit between towers and alcove, giving the window-seat look from the inspo plus 12 more drawers of storage.

Phase 3: Center Arch + Alcove$400–$700

The scratch-built showpiece. Stud-framed alcove walls, ¾" plywood skins, and the arch cut from doubled plywood or kerf-cut MDF, wrapped in panel molding. Sconce wiring (off the existing outlet circuit) and LED shelf strips go in before panels close up.

Phase 4: Unify + Finish$300–$450

Continuous face frames and crown tie all the boxes into one piece. PAX foil won't take stain, so the whole wall gets bonding primer plus dark enamel (~$150), then pulls, knobs, and seat cushions. This phase is what makes stock boxes read as custom.

The Fork: Stock Carcasses vs Full Scratch Build

Recommended

Stock carcasses, custom skin

PAX + Hampton Bay boxes, Benny builds face frames, arch, and trim. ~$2,500–$4,500 total, 4–6 weekends. 90% of the look for 40% of the shop hours.

The purist route

Full scratch build

Every box from ¾" plywood. Materials drop to ~$1,500–$2,500 but add roughly 60 shop hours. Maximum woodworking project, maximum timeline risk.

For reference, a cabinet shop would quote $15k–$25k for this wall. Either DIY route is a rout.

Open Questions (Blocking)

  • Wall width, ceiling height, window positions and sizes. Nothing gets drawn until these are measured.
  • Stock carcasses or scratch build. This is the money-vs-shop-hours argument to have in person.
  • Outlet and switch locations on that wall, for sconce and LED circuits.
  • Which safe lives in the tower bay, and its exact dimensions.
