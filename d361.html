Framework Cribcondensed, unit order
SMART goals

Specific · Measurable · Attainable · Relevant/Realistic · Time-bound. Mission = purpose/how you deliver value. Goal (= "objective" in the sim) = specific outcome in a timeframe. Strategy = approach to hit the goal. Tactic = the tool you use to execute the strategy.

Global market entry modes

Export/Import = cheapest, fastest, least control, less local insight. Outsourcing = contract out a process (domestic or foreign). Offshoring = relocate a process (usually operational, e.g. manufacturing) to another country. Both cut cost but add coordination/quality risk.

Product design

Customers buy benefits, not components. Features have positive or negative correlation to a benefit. Design = pick the feature mix that maximizes benefit satisfaction for your target segment.

Truth-in-advertising (ethics)

FTC standard: ads must be truthful, non-deceptive, backed by evidence when needed. Express claim = stated directly ("prevents colds"). Implied claim = inferred from wording ("kills germs that cause colds"). Both require a "reasonable basis" of proof.

Fair / living / minimum wage

Minimum wage = legal floor. Living wage = enough to actually live on in that geography. Fair wage = weighs both plus the value the employee's work brings to the company. Matching industry norms ≠ automatically fair.

Pro forma statements

Projects future cash flow, revenue, taxes from current decisions before you commit. Sim order: Cash Flow → Modify → Load Data → Save, then check Income Statement, then Balance Sheet. Compare pro forma projection vs. actual next quarter to calibrate.

Capacity: fixed vs. operating

Fixed capacity = max units the facility (3-D printers) can produce. Operating capacity = what you actually schedule. Just-in-time means no excess inventory risk, but under-scheduling → stockouts (reputation + next-quarter penalty); over-scheduling → wasted capacity cost.

Balanced Scorecard

Holistic performance view mixing quantitative (financial) + qualitative (non-financial) metrics, built from strategic goals. Takes a stakeholder view (investors, employees, customers, regulators) vs. a pure stockholder (profit-only) view. Cumulative version in the sim covers Q3–Q6.

Accounting fundamentals

Assets = Liabilities + Equity — always balances. Equity = investment + retained earnings; profit ↑ equity, expenses ↓ equity. Every transaction needs ≥2 entries (double-entry) to stay balanced. Balance sheet = snapshot at a point in time. Income statement = flow over a period (revenue − costs/expenses = net income).

Financial forecasting

Estimate of future outcomes from historical data + market/economic indicators + assumptions. Hardest part is forecasting revenue (price × quantity); costs are easier to model from historical data.

Profitability / activity-based costing

Tie costs to specific activities/brands to find true per-brand profitability, then shift spend (marketing, sales staff) toward the most profitable brand rather than spreading evenly.

Change management

External forces: demographic, social, political, tech, economic. Internal forces: low performance, low satisfaction, conflict, new leadership/mission. Learning-org response: commit to a strategy, flatten structure, keep adapting continuously.

SWOT

Strengths/Weaknesses = internal, within your control. Opportunities/Threats = external market factors, outside your control. For Task 2: pick 1 unmet opportunity and 1 unresolved threat specifically (not ones you've already captured/fixed).
