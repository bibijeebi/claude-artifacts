MacBook Air Repair: Speakers + F Key

The M1 Air works fine except the speakers sound blown and the F key feels mushy. Both are cheap DIY fixes: the key is a pop-off-and-clean job, the speakers are one of the easiest internal repairs on this machine. Diagnose first though, because there's a known M1 Air gotcha where "blown speakers" is actually chassis rattle and new drivers fix nothing.

Est. Cost $10–75
Difficulty 2/5
Time One evening
Machine A2337 (M1, 2020)

The Problem

Two independent faults on the M1 MacBook Air. One: the speakers distort, described as blown-out. Two: the F key is mushy, it presses but the tactile response is gone or gummy. Neither stops the laptop from working, both make it annoying to use daily, and both are squarely in DIY territory. Apple would quote a top-case-adjacent repair bill; the actual parts cost is lunch money.

Diagnose the Speakers First

The resonance trap. There is a documented M1 Air issue where speakers rattle and distort (piano music is the classic trigger, even at 25% volume) and the cause is not the speaker drivers. Multiple owners on iFixit replaced their speakers and got the exact same distortion from the new ones. One found the speakers sounded perfect when connected but not screwed into the chassis, meaning something in the case resonates. If that's our failure mode, buying speakers is burning $30.

Two-minute test before ordering anything: play a bass-heavy track or a sine sweep at the volume where distortion shows up, then press firmly around the keyboard edges and palm rest. If the buzz changes or stops under pressure, it's resonance, and the fix is the software mitigation below, not parts. Also A/B against headphones: distortion through headphones too means audio board, not speakers (unlikely, but free to rule out).

Genuinely blown drivers sound like: crackle at all volumes, one side dead or clearly quieter, distortion that doesn't change no matter where you press. That's a parts order.

The Plan: F Key

Power off. Fully shut down, not sleep.
Pop the keycap. Slip a fingernail or thin pick under the top edge and lift gently. Scissor-switch caps release with light force, the clips are along the bottom edge. If the scissor mechanism comes off with the cap, unclip it and set it aside, it goes back in first.
Clean. Isopropyl on a Q-tip: the key well, the rubber dome, the underside of the cap. Compressed air for crumbs. Mushy almost always means drink residue or grit under the dome, not a broken part.
Inspect the dome. Torn or collapsed dome = the rare bad outcome. Domes aren't sold alone but a full keycap/scissor set is $8 and the dome usually survives, it's the residue.
Reassemble. Scissor clips into the metal hooks first, flat side down. Then press the cap on until it clicks at all four corners. Test feel before closing the lid and calling it done.

The Plan: Speakers (If the Press Test Says Drivers)

Follow the iFixit guides step-by-step, they're written for the exact A2337 board layout:

Guides: Right speaker · Left speaker
Parts: Right speaker unit · Left speaker unit · Full A2337 device page
Prep. Full shutdown, unplug, lid closed, flip it. iFixit recommends disabling Auto Boot first since opening the lid mid-repair can power it on.
Open the bottom case. Pull the P5 pentalobe screws (track which hole each came from, lengths differ), then pull the lower case free.
Disconnect the battery. First thing after the case is off, before touching anything else.
Swap the speakers. They sit right under the bottom panel, no logic board removal. Disconnect cable, pull screws, pry the old unit free of its adhesive, seat the new one on fresh adhesive strips, screw down. Repeat other side.
Reassemble and test. Battery back on, case on, screws home. Play the same track that exposed the distortion before declaring victory.

If It's Resonance, Not Drivers

No parts fix known for the chassis rattle, so mitigate in software: cap max volume, and use eqMac (free) to notch out the band that excites the buzz, typically somewhere in the 200-600 Hz range on these. Gets the speakers to maybe 80% listenable. External speakers or headphones for anything that matters. Not satisfying, but it's the honest answer and it costs nothing.

Decision for Bekah

Low stakes. The key fix is free and happens regardless. The only spend decision is the ~$30 speaker pair, and that only gets ordered after the press test confirms the drivers are actually the problem. If the test says resonance, total project cost is zero and we live with EQ.
