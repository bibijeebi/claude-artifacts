The Crossbow Pivot

The Darton Viper setup is dead. It died at the archery shop, along with part of Benny's right shoulder. New plan: buy a crossbow, be sighted in before the September 12 opener.

Total Cost $300–$450
Difficulty 1/5
Time 1 buy + 1 range day
Category Firearms
Status Proposed

What Happened

August 1: Benny took the Viper to Dunn Outdoors to get it set up. The shop confirmed what we suspected, it's old and only adjusts down to 70 lbs. They handed him a 70 lb test bow to try. Mid-draw, his right shoulder partially dislocated. Big audible crunch, freaked out the staff. He walked out with the shoulder back in and a decision made.

This was always the risk with the Viper plan. 70 lbs is the bow's floor, and Benny's shoulders were the known weak link. The shop trip was supposed to answer "is this bow worth $200 in accessories." It answered a different question instead.

The shoulder math: a vertical bow makes you pull and hold the full draw with your shoulders every single shot, and let it down under load if you don't shoot. A crossbow gets cocked once with a rope aid (about half the draw weight, split between both arms, legs braced) or a crank (a few pounds of effort). Then it holds itself. No draw at the moment of truth, no let-down, no re-injury window while a deer is standing there.

Why This Isn't a Downgrade

Crossbows are fully legal in NC during all archery seasons, no special permit or disability waiver needed. Same seasons, same land, same deer. And a $300 crossbow package arrives with a scope, bolts, and quiver already on it, where the "free" Viper still needed $175-225 in parts plus an eventual $80-150 string job on 30-year-old cables. The free bow was never free. The crossbow costs about the same as finishing the Viper would have, without gambling the shoulder.

Rope cocking works fine for Benny (tested), so a crank is optional, not required. Nice-to-have line item below.

The Clock

NC archery opens statewide Saturday, September 12, 2026. Wake County sits in the zone that keeps archery open through October 30. Buying by early September leaves a range day to assemble, sight the scope at 20/30/40, and confirm broadhead point of impact before opening morning. Jeff and the neighbor have been pushing Benny to be ready for this season. This is the last purchase between him and a deer.

What Happens to the Viper

Nothing lost, it was free. Options: wall-hanger (it's a genuinely cool early-90s American-made bow), or hand it back to Dillon. Zero dollars were spent on it, which means the shop trip did its job, it just answered with a crunch instead of a quote. The original proposal is preserved here for the record.

Setup Steps

Buy the Boss 405 package. Sub-$300 target. Check Rural King and Walmart in person, Sportsman's Guide and KI direct online.
Assemble and lube the rail. Stirrup, limbs, scope come mostly pre-mounted. Rail lube every 10-15 shots.
Sight in with field points. 20 yards first, then let the scope's speed-ring hash marks handle 30/40. Backyard has the distance.
Confirm with broadheads. Fixed blades usually hit close to field points at crossbow speeds, but verify at 20 and 30. Never re-shoot broadheads into the good target spots.
Hunt September 12. Cock it once at the truck, decock via decocking bolt or a shot at day's end. Shoulders stay out of the transaction entirely.

Decision for Bekah

The Viper plan was $175-225 now plus $80-150 later, riding on shoulders that just proved they can't cash that check. The crossbow is $340-435 all-in, arrives basically ready, removes the injury risk from every future shot, and beats the season opener by weeks. It also stays useful forever: ground blinds, guests, and any year the shoulders get worse instead of better. One purchase closes out the entire "archery deer" capability gap.
