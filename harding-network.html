The Goal

A phone that works on every acre

The fiber at the house tested at 768 Mbps down. A phone feels fast at 25 Mbps. The pipe is not the problem, and a faster plan from Craw-Kan is not the fix. The project is to spread the signal that is already paid for.

Cell service dies past the barn. When Wi-Fi covers the land, phones move calls and texts onto Wi-Fi on their own. The dead zones close, and every covered acre can also hold a camera.

In 1936 the power lines reached the farms of Bates County. In 2026 the signal follows the same idea: run the utility out to where the people are.

Bulletin No. 1

You cannot buy range

  • 01A faster internet plan moves more data through the same radios. It does not move the signal one foot farther.
  • 02The FCC caps Wi-Fi transmit power. Every router already sits at the cap. No provider and no expensive router can raise it.
  • 03Wi-Fi is a conversation. The router can shout across a field. The phone must answer with a radio the size of a fingernail. Range ends where the phone’s answer dies.
  • 04The house has metal walls. A signal born inside the metal mostly stays inside. Outdoor radios must hang outside the metal.
The fix is radios near people, not louder radios far away.
Bulletin No. 2

Power is the real project

Data crosses the property free, through the air. Electricity does not. Every radio in the field needs power, so the design question is not which gadget to buy. The question is which spots get power, and how.

A

One buried cable

A buried Ethernet cable carries power and data together (PoE). The far radio needs no outlet at all.

The data signal dies past 328 feet. Inline extenders stretch a run to about 600 feet, then copper is done.

FIT: nodes within a few hundred feet of the house.
B

A real power line

Trench electric service to a pole or a pedestal. That spot then has power forever, and every connection method works there.

The entrance may get this for free. See Bulletin No. 3.

FIT: permanent spots worth a trench.
C

Solar on the pole

A 100 W panel, a 100 Ah battery, a charge controller, and a small box feed one radio with about three cloudy days of reserve.

You have built solar before. This is that skill, pointed at a pole.

FIT: bare field. No wires of any kind.

The radios then talk to each other through the air. For the goal of usable phone internet, that mesh link is plenty. If one station must someday carry full speed, a dedicated beam (two small dishes) upgrades that link to near wire speed.

Decision for the demolition

Kill the building. Keep the meter. The electric service at the condemned house is worth more than the house. If the pedestal or the pole service survives the demo, Station B costs about $330 instead of about $880, and the entrance has power forever.

Standing rules

  • Never bolt equipment to a building that will be demolished.
  • If any trench ever runs near the foundation slab, lay a spare conduit in it. Conduit in an open trench costs almost nothing. A new trench later costs everything.
  • Full speed lives at the stations. Between stations, the phone’s own radio sets the ceiling. Usable everywhere is the honest promise, and usable is all a phone needs.
  • Copper Ethernet dies past 328 feet. Plan around that number or go through the air.
