Build Doc — Shed #015

Cut List & Shopping List

Full material takeoff for the 12x12 stick-built storage shed. Gravel pad foundation, 4:12 gable roof, T1-11 siding, double barn doors, partial rear loft.

Footprint12' x 12'
Walls~8' (precut studs)
Roof4:12 gable
Materials Est.$2,000–$3,200

How To Use This

The Shopping List below is grouped the way a lumberyard thinks, hand it over or work down it aisle by aisle. The Cut List after it breaks every piece down by assembly with finished cut lengths. This is a takeoff to price and shop from, not stamped engineered plans. Lumber sells in 2-foot increments, so a few quantities are rounded up. Buy roughly 10% extra on dimensional lumber and fasteners, you will miscut, and a second trip costs more than a spare stud.

Before the first board: get the Wake County Land Use Permit, confirm your 5-foot setbacks from property lines and septic, and stake out the actual 12x12. A 12x12x12 build needs no inspection, but the layout has to be right because everything downstream inherits it.

Cut List by Assembly

Finished cut lengths. Where a piece equals a stock length, no cut is needed, buy it and frame it.

1. Foundation & Skids

Excavate and compact a level 14x14 gravel pad, 4 to 6 inches deep, landscape fabric underneath. Set skids dead level and parallel.

5. Siding, Trim & Roofing

T1-11 goes straight to the studs, it is rated as combined sheathing and siding, so there is no separate wall sheathing layer. Caulk every seam, prime, paint. Then underlayment, drip edge, shingles.

Tools You'll Want

Circular saw, drill/driver, framing square and speed square, 25' tape, 4' level, chalk line, hammer, pry bar, tin snips (drip edge), utility knife, sawhorses, a string line and stakes for the pad, a hand tamper or rented plate compactor for the gravel, and an extension ladder for the roof. A framing nailer is a nice-to-have, not required if you screw the framing.

Reality check: best case, you hit the low end on a calm lumber-pricing week. Realistically budget the middle. The two phases that eat the most time are the gravel pad (getting it dead level) and the roof (cutting rafters and learning shingles). Everything between those two is fast and satisfying. Expect a stud or three of waste, that is what the 10% is for.
