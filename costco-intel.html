01
Primary Target

The $4.99 Rotisserie Chicken

Here's what I found out: Costco actually loses money on every rotisserie chicken they sell. It's a loss leader. They price it at $4.99 specifically to get people through the doors and walking past everything else in the store. They've kept this price unchanged for years even as chicken costs have risen.

Which means we should be buying one every single trip. We're basically exploiting their business model. Here's the math on what one bird gives us:

$4.99
Cost per chicken
~$1.50
Per lb cooked meat
2–3
Meals for two people
$10–12
True value per bird

For context, boneless skinless breast alone runs $3–4+/lb at the store and you still have to cook it. We're getting fully cooked, seasoned meat for under $1.50/lb.

The Broth Multiplier

The carcass makes 8–12 cups of bone broth, which replaces $4–6 worth of the boxed stuff we used to buy. Homemade bone broth is also nutritionally superior — more collagen, more minerals, better taste. This is where we stopped buying the Costco containers of broth, and honestly, we're winning.

The Better Than Bouillon is still great for quick stuff, but the real broth from the carcass is next level.

Freezer Bag Protocol

Shred all the meat the day we buy it, portion into freezer bags, freeze flat. They thaw fast and are ready to throw into anything — tacos, pasta, rice bowls, sandwiches, soups.

Pro tip: We can also freeze the carcasses. Stack up 2–3 and do one big broth batch instead of making it every time.

02
Targets of Opportunity

Every Loss Leader They Run

The chicken isn't the only thing they're losing money on. Costco's actual profit comes from membership fees, not from selling us stuff. Their product markup is capped around 14–15% compared to 25–50% at normal grocery stores. These are the items where they actively take a loss to keep us renewing:

Item Price Why It's Insane
Rotisserie Chicken $4.99 Below cost to produce. 3+ lbs cooked meat plus carcass for broth.
Hot Dog & Soda $1.50 Same price since 1985. The CEO told the old CEO he'd kill him if he raised it. Not a joke.
Gas Station 20–40¢ off Per gallon cheaper than surrounding stations. They break even or lose money on fuel to drive membership renewals.
Food Court (All) Razor thin Pizza, chicken bake, churros — all priced at near-zero margin. Exists to make you happy so you renew.
Kirkland Signature 20–40% under Not a loss leader per se, but intentionally undercuts name brands at equivalent or better quality. The vodka, the olive oil, the diapers.
03
Special Mission

The Irish Cream Problem

North Carolina is an ABC state, meaning all liquor has to be sold through state-run ABC stores. That means no Kirkland liquor at our Costco — beer and wine only. The Kirkland Irish Cream is a 1.75L bottle for around $15–17, which is more than double the volume of a standard 750ml Bailey's knockoff (Carolan's, Riley's) that costs the same price or more. It's absurdly good value.

So if we want it, we need to cross state lines. South Carolina Costcos sell beer, wine, and liquor to members.

Nearest Costco Liquor Runs

Two options from Willow Spring, both in South Carolina:

Option A ~3 hrs Beach Trip Combo

Myrtle Beach — 1021 Oak Forest Ln, Myrtle Beach, SC 29577. About 3 hours. Perfect excuse for a beach day and stock up on the way back.

Option B ~3.5 hrs Dedicated Run

Columbia — 505 Piney Grove Rd, Columbia, SC 29210. About 3.5 hours. Has a dedicated "Columbia Liquors at Costco" section.

Virginia is also an ABC state, so that's no help. South Carolina is the move.

Stock Up Strategy

Irish cream keeps a long time unopened, so whenever we're near either of those locations — beach trip, road trip, whatever — grab 2–3 bottles. At $15 for 1.75L, it's perfect for White Russians, espresso martini riffs, on the rocks, or just the cocktail experiments we've been doing on snow days.

04
Sustainment Ops

Bone Broth From Scratch

Every rotisserie chicken carcass is essentially a free batch of bone broth. We already stopped buying the boxed stuff from Costco, and that's the right call. The homemade version from the carcass has more collagen, more minerals, and just tastes better. The Better Than Bouillon still works great for quick stuff where we don't want to thaw a bag.

Between the meat for meals, the broth from the bones, and the fact that we're freezer-bagging the excess, a single $4.99 chicken is honestly returning $10–12 in usable food. Every trip, no question.
