French Macarons Crispy shell. Chewy center. Ruffled feet.

The fancy almond meringue sandwich cookies. First batch will be ugly. They'll still taste incredible.

The folding (step 7) is the make-or-break moment. Everything else is just measuring and waiting. Expect batch 1 to look rough. Batch 2 will look great.
⚠️
Three things that will ruin your macarons: (1) Any grease or yolk in the egg whites, (2) not letting them rest until the tops are dry, (3) over-folding or under-folding the batter. That's it. Nail those three and you're good.

Ingredients

Shells
  • 3 egg whitesRoom temperature. Separate carefully. ANY yolk = start over.
  • 1 cup almond flourMust be fine, blanched almond flour. Not almond meal.
  • 1 cup powdered sugar
  • 1/4 cup regular granulated sugar
  • pinch cream of tartarStabilizes the meringue. Just a tiny pinch.
Filling (pick one)
  • - Nutella, jam, or buttercreamNutella straight from the jar is the easiest win.
🥣
Equipment you need: Electric mixer (hand mixer or stand mixer), fine mesh strainer/sifter, baking sheet, parchment paper, spatula, piping bag or ziplock with corner cut off.

Steps

1
Separate eggs and temper
Separate 3 eggs. Whites go in a big, clean, dry bowl. If even a speck of yolk gets in, toss it and start with a new egg. Let the whites sit on the counter for 30 minutes to come to room temperature.
The bowl must be completely grease-free. Wipe it down with a paper towel and a drop of vinegar if you're unsure.
2
Process the dry ingredients
Measure 1 cup almond flour and 1 cup powdered sugar. Blend them together in a food processor for about 30 seconds (makes finer texture). Then sift the whole thing through a fine mesh strainer into a bowl. Throw away any chunks that don't go through.
No food processor? Just sift the almond flour and powdered sugar together twice.
3
Beat the egg whites
Start beating the egg whites with an electric mixer on medium. When they get foamy (about 1 minute), add a pinch of cream of tartar.
4
Add sugar slowly
With the mixer still running, slowly pour in the 1/4 cup of regular sugar. Not all at once. A little at a time over about 30 seconds. Then crank the mixer to high and keep beating.
5
Beat to stiff peaks
Keep beating on high until you get stiff peaks. This means when you pull the beater out, the peak stands straight up and doesn't flop over. The ultimate test: flip the bowl upside down. If nothing moves, you're there. If it slides, keep beating.
Don't under-beat. "I think it's done" is not done. It should be thick, glossy, and stiff.
6
Add food coloring (optional)
If you want colored macarons, add gel food coloring now. Gel, not liquid. Just a tiny dab. Fold it in gently before adding the dry ingredients.
7
THE FOLD (macaronage)
Dump all the sifted almond flour/powdered sugar into the egg whites. Now fold. Press the spatula down through the center, scrape along the bottom, fold up and over. Rotate the bowl a quarter turn. Repeat. About 50-60 folds total.
This is the hardest part. The batter should flow off the spatula like thick lava and settle back into itself within about 10 seconds. If it sits in a blob, fold more. If it's completely runny, you went too far (still bake them, they'll just be flat).
8
Pipe the circles
Scoop the batter into a piping bag or ziplock bag with a corner snipped off. Pipe quarter-sized circles onto parchment paper on a baking sheet. Leave about an inch between them. Then pick up the whole baking sheet and bang it on the counter 3-4 times to knock out air bubbles.
Pop any visible surface bubbles with a toothpick.
9
Rest until dry (CRITICAL)
Leave the piped macarons sitting on the counter for 30-60 minutes. Don't skip this. Touch the top of one. If it's dry and doesn't stick to your finger at all, they're ready. If it's still tacky, wait longer.
This rest is what creates the "feet" (the ruffled edge at the bottom). Skip it and you get flat sad cookies.
Turn the oven on to 300°F now while they rest.
10
Bake at 300°F for 15-17 minutes
Put them in the oven. Don't open the door for the first 10 minutes. At 15 minutes, try to gently wiggle one. If it's firmly attached to the parchment and doesn't slide around, they're done. If the top wiggles separately from the base, give them 2 more minutes.
11
Cool and fill
Let them cool completely on the baking sheet. Don't touch them. Once cool, they'll peel right off the parchment. Match up same-size pairs. Spread filling (Nutella, jam, buttercream) on the flat side of one, sandwich the other on top. Done.
They're actually better after sitting in the fridge for 24 hours. The filling softens the shell slightly and it all melds together.

Pro Tips

🥚 Aged egg whites: Some people separate the eggs the night before and leave the whites in the fridge uncovered. This dries them out slightly and makes them whip better. Optional but nerdy.
😤 Batch 1 will be ugly. Lopsided, cracked, no feet, whatever. They still taste amazing. Don't get frustrated. Batch 2 you'll understand the fold and it'll click.
🌡️ Oven accuracy matters. If your oven runs hot, macarons will crack on top. If it runs cool, they won't develop feet. An oven thermometer is worth $5 at Walmart.
💧 Humidity is the enemy. If it's a rainy or very humid day, the resting step takes way longer. The tops have to be fully dry before baking or you'll get cracked shells.
🧊 They freeze great. Filled macarons in an airtight container in the freezer last 2-3 months. Thaw in the fridge for a couple hours before eating.
