Executive Summary

Verdict

The 2023 solar purchase is a sound, unrepeatable position that has reached cash-flow break-even in month 33 and turns clearly positive over the hold. The household did not overpay and did not get had. Three actions determine how much of the upside survives the January 2027 net metering transition, and all three are cheap or free.

Every deal term is now extinct for new buyers: the 30% federal credit (dead for purchases after 2025), the 3.99% 25-year loan, the $3.36/W turnkey price, and legacy 1:1 net metering (application window closed 9/30/2023, beaten by eight days). Meanwhile the utility has raised the reference residential bill roughly 45% since 2020 and has another ~9.5% settling in for 2027-28, which is precisely the risk this system insures against. The honest characterization: the household bought a 25-year fixed-price electricity contract at an effective ~7-8¢/kWh in a territory sprinting away from that price.

Rate Environment

Duke Energy Progress residential increases: 6.8% (2023-24), 3.4% (2024-25), 3.7% (2025-26), with rate changes alone lifting the reference 1,000 kWh bill about 45% between 2020 and 2025. The pending 2027-28 case opened at an 18.5% two-year ask; the July 2026 settlement framework lands near 9.5% cumulative, effective January 2027, subject to NCUC approval. Effective all-in residential price today is ~16.4¢/kWh and plausibly ~18¢ by 2028. Every scenario in this report that uses a rate escalator assumes 4%/yr, which is conservative against the realized record.

Implication: the system's value is levered to exactly the thing the utility keeps doing. Rate hikes that cost a non-solar neighbor $15–25/mo accrue to this household as increased offset value at zero marginal cost.

6.0

The January 2027 Transition

On 1/1/2027 export compensation drops from ~16.4¢ (retail, banked) to ~3.4¢ (avoided cost, cashed monthly), and a $28 minimum bill applies. At the current 34% export share (~6 MWh/yr), the unmitigated haircut is roughly $780/yr. Mitigation is entirely behavioral: self-consumed kWh retain full retail value, so every kWh moved from the grid into the 10am–4pm production window recovers ~13¢. The household's schedulable load happens to be enormous (§7), so most of the haircut is recoverable without hardware.

Pre-transition noteBanked 1:1 credits do not carry across the transition on the same terms. Run any accumulated credit balance down toward zero through December 2026 rather than stockpiling exports into the changeover.

Battery storage: declined. At 3.4¢ export value, arbitrage on ~$10k+ of uninstalled storage with no remaining federal credit returns $150–200/yr. It pencils only as outage insurance, and vehicle-to-home from the household Tesla is not an alternative: the 2023 Model Y lacks the bidirectional hardware that newer trims and the Cybertruck carry, and no software update adds it. Revisit only if an EV truck enters the fleet or Duke introduces a compensated demand-response program.

Set scheduled charging to 10am–4pm for every day the car is home. Post-2027, each shifted kWh is worth ~13¢. Highest ROI action available; implement now so the habit predates the rate change.

$8–12k avoided + $250–400/yr · paperwork only
