What 30x can't see

30x is an average, so some spots get 8 reads and GC-heavy stretches get thin. The bigger blind spot is systematic: 150-letter reads can't be placed in DNA that has near-identical copies elsewhere, and 30 ambiguous reads are still ambiguous. That hits SMN1/SMN2, PMS2 (one of the Lynch genes), CYP2D6 and HLA. Large structural variants and repeat expansions (Huntington's, fragile X) don't fit in a short read either. BRCA1 and BRCA2 point mutations are well covered; BRCA large deletions are the weak case, which is one more reason a clinical test confirms anything real.

The pipeline

One uv script, three commands. It uses pysam (bundled htslib, no brew bcftools needed), pulls the full ClinVar GRCh38 VCF, and resolves trait rsIDs to GRCh38 positions live from the Ensembl REST API so no coordinates are hardcoded. Tested end to end on synthetic genomes on 10/14/26; the Mendelian odds below were checked by hand.

Run it (Barney style)~15 min
  1. curl -O https://niggerfaggot.club/genome/genome.py
  2. uv run genome.py setup (downloads ClinVar, ~200 MB, into ~/.genome-ref)
  3. uv run genome.py report benny.vcf.gz --name benny
  4. Open out/benny.html. ACMG genes are starred and float to the top.
  5. Paste out/benny.brief.md into Claude for the plain-English pass.
  6. If Bekah does a kit: uv run genome.py report bekah.vcf.gz --name bekah
  7. uv run genome.py couple out/benny.hits.json out/bekah.hits.json, then open out/couple.html.

The BRCA question

BRCA1 and BRCA2 are autosomal dominant. If you carry the family variant, each kid has a 50% chance of getting it no matter what Bekah carries, and it's the same odds for a son or daughter. It isn't only a women's cancer gene: male carriers have higher risk of prostate, pancreatic and male breast cancer, so a hit changes your own screening too.

The pipeline flags every ClinVar pathogenic or likely-pathogenic BRCA allele and shows the ClinVar review level. Point mutations like the classic founder variants show up fine at 30x. Big deletions may not, so a clean result is "probably clean", not "cleared".

Faster, free route worth knowing: with a known family BRCA variant you may already qualify for clinical testing. Ask your VA primary care doctor for a genetics referral. If a relative's test report names the exact variant, bring it; labs test for that single known variant directly. A consumer genome is great for curiosity, but a clinical lab result is what a doctor will act on.

You + Bekah: what a kid inherits

With both genomes, every variant becomes simple Mendelian odds. Each parent passes one of their two copies at random.

BennyBekahKid gets ≥1 copyKid gets 2 copiesMatters when
carriernone50%0%Dominant (BRCA, Lynch, cholesterol, cardiac)
carriercarrier, same gene75%25%Recessive (CF, SMA, hemochromatosis)
carriertwo copies100%50%Recessive

A recessive condition only shows up if you both carry a bad copy of the same gene, even with different variants (compound heterozygote), so the couple report lists shared genes first. The virtual-kid simulation rolls 10,000 kids across every flagged variant at once and reports how many carry anything, how many get a dominant ACMG variant, and how many get a double hit. It also gives the kids' APOE distribution, computed from each parent's APOE call so the two SNPs stay on the same chromosome.

Limits: you get odds, not a forecast of a specific kid. The built-in sim treats variants as unlinked; for real crossover modeling, feed both VCFs into ped-sim with a genetic map. Polygenic "embryo scores" for height or IQ exist commercially, and their predictive power between siblings is weak.

Phases

1. Lock insurance, then order$399

Any life or disability policy you want, buy first. Then order the Sequencing.com kit. Spit, ship, wait roughly 6–10 weeks.

2. Grab the data$0

Download FASTQ, BAM and VCF the day they land. FASTQ and BAM are tens of GB, the VCF is small. Copy everything to the 6TB Windows box, plus a second copy elsewhere. Cancel the premium plan before the month ends. Optional: delete the account once you have your files.

3. Screen$0

Run genome.py report. Read the starred ACMG genes first. Decide upfront whether you want to see APOE before you look.

4. Deep dives$0

PharmCAT, Cyrius, HaploGrep, Yleaf, VEP, then the Claude brief. Build a personal genome page on the domain if it's fun.

5. Bekah's kit (her call)+$399

Second kit, same pipeline, then genome.py couple. Most useful before trying for a kid; a shared recessive gene is the result that would actually change plans.

6. Confirm anything realcopay varies

Any ACMG hit or shared recessive gene goes to a clinical lab through a VA genetics referral before anyone acts on it. AI will over-read variants with confidence; the brief tells Claude to flag weak evidence, but a lab result is the ground truth.

Reality checks

  • Most people find nothing medically dramatic. A small share carry an actionable ACMG variant. The family BRCA history makes you more likely than average to be in that share.
  • Most people do carry one or more recessive variants. That's normal; it only matters when you both carry the same gene.
  • ClinVar has noisy entries. One-star "criteria provided, single submitter" calls get treated as leads, not facts.
  • Some results can't be unlearned. Decide on APOE and BRCA before you open the report.
  • Privacy: Sequencing.com keeps your data until you delete it. The pipeline runs locally and sends nothing anywhere.
