Two-Flag House Display

Bottom Line Up Front

The hard part is done. The 2-flag wall bracket is already drilled into the brick return wall, the recessed face between the garage door trim and the columned front entry. That spot is good, keep it. What remains is hardware: two 6ft tangle-free spinning poles, a 3x5 embroidered nylon U.S. flag, and a 3x5 embroidered nylon Marine Corps flag. Fly the U.S. flag on the honor side, the USMC flag on the other. About $60–$95 all in, Amazon overnight. The single tradeoff to accept up front: a fixed 2-flag bracket cannot be set to half-staff.

What We Already Have

A 2-position wall bracket is mounted in the brick "pocket" on the garage-return wall, the recessed brick face that sits between the white garage door trim and the front entry columns. It went into solid brick with the hammer drill. Brick is the correct substrate for this, so the install itself is done and done right.

One important distinction, because it reverses an earlier worry: a 2-flag bracket holds two separate poles fanned apart, and that works fine. What does not work is stacking two flags on a single 6ft pole, the pole is too short and the lower flag drags. The bracket already installed is the two-pole kind, which is exactly the correct way to fly two flags off a house. One flag per pole.

Is That The Right Spot? Yes.

The brick pocket between the garage and the entry is the best realistic location on this house, and it should stay there. Reasons it works:

  • Solid brick. The correct anchor for a flag bracket, already done with the hammer drill.
  • Street-visible. It reads clearly from the driveway approach and the road, which is the point of flying colors.
  • Open air to fly into. The poles fan out over the driveway and front walk, nothing to tangle on. No tree limbs, no gutters, no roofline in the flight path.
  • Right next to the entry. A flag display belongs by the front door. This is the natural spot.

The alternatives are all worse. The flat brick to the left of the garage doors flies the flags out over where vehicles park, so a flag can slap a car. The brick to the right of the front door would put the flags straight across the big arched window. The entry columns themselves are not a structural mounting surface and would look cluttered. The pocket wins.

U.S. flag goes to its own right, which is the observer's left when facing the house. If the bracket stacks the poles instead of fanning them, the U.S. flag goes on top.

Three Things To Verify Before Final Mounting

The bracket is in. Before the poles go in for good, sight-check these three. Five minutes, saves a redo.

1. Garage door clearance
With a flag flying toward the garage, it must not reach the garage door, the door track, or a vehicle parked in the bay. Angle the inner pole away from the door.
2. Fan angle
Spread the two poles enough that the flags clear each other and clear the entry columns at full flap. The wider the fan, the less the flags beat on each other.
3. Flying height
The lowest point of a flag in the wind should clear roughly 7 ft over the walkway and stay above the foundation bushes, so nobody walks into it and it doesn't drag the shrubs.

Fly It Right — U.S. Flag Code

Two veterans in the house, so the display should be correct, not just up.

  • U.S. flag highest, honor side. On two separate staffs of equal height, the U.S. flag goes to its own right, which is the observer's left facing the house. Left hole gets Old Glory, right hole gets the Marine Corps flag. If the bracket stacks the poles, the U.S. flag goes on top.
  • USMC flag is never larger than the U.S. flag. Two equal 3x5s is correct. Do not put a bigger Marine Corps flag next to a smaller national flag.
  • Both embroidered nylon. Embroidered stars and sewn stripes on nylon fly in light wind and last. The Marine Corps flag is the scarlet field with the gold Eagle, Globe, and Anchor.
  • Lighting. Flag Code says the U.S. flag flies sunrise to sunset. If it stays up overnight it should be lit. A small solar spot aimed at the pocket covers it.

Install — Barney Style

  1. Order the two poles and both flags. Overnight shipping.
  2. Clip each flag onto its own pole. U.S. flag on one pole, Marine Corps flag on the other. Grommet flags clip on fastest.
  3. Slot the U.S. flag's pole into the honor-side hole of the bracket, observer's left. Marine Corps pole goes in the other hole.
  4. Set the fan angle so the two flags clear each other, clear the garage door, and clear the entry columns when the wind pushes them. Wider fan is better.
  5. Check the lowest flying point clears about 7 ft over the walkway and rides above the bushes.
  6. Walk out to the street and the end of the driveway. Confirm it looks square and the U.S. flag reads as the dominant one.

The One Tradeoff: Half-Staff

A fixed 2-flag bracket cannot fly at half-staff. The poles seat at a set angle. On the mandated half-staff days, Memorial Day morning, Patriot Day, and others, this display can't be lowered. The honest options: live with it, which is what most two-flag house displays do, or, if half-staff matters enough, add one separate single bracket elsewhere for the U.S. flag so it can be lowered on its own. Not a recommendation to redo good work, just the tradeoff stated plainly so the call is made on purpose.

Maintenance Reality

Outdoor flags flown daily are consumables. Expect 6 to 12 months before sun and wind fade and fray them, which is why a spare of each is worth buying now. The scarlet Marine Corps flag will likely fade first, red dye gives out before the others. These poles are not wind-rated, so both flags come down for real storms and hurricane remnants, which in central NC summers is a few times a season.

Decision for Bekah

The bracket stays where it is, the brick pocket between the garage and the entry is the right spot and the install is already done. The ask is one Amazon order: two 6ft tangle-free aluminum poles and two 3x5 embroidered nylon flags, a U.S. flag and a Marine Corps flag, for $60–$95 all in. Fly the U.S. flag on the honor side, observer's left, over the Marine Corps colors. Accept that a fixed bracket can't go half-staff. Total commitment after the order lands: about 30 minutes to clip the flags on, seat the poles, and set the fan angle.
