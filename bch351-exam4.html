K vs Q

K = equilibrium constant, ratio of products/reactants at equilibrium.
Q = reaction quotient, same ratio at any moment.
• Q < K → forward (need more product)
• Q > K → reverse (excess product)
• Q = K → equilibrium

Multienzyme Complex (PDH)

A complex of multiple enzymes physically associated so substrate is channeled between active sites without diffusing away. PDH = E1 (pyr DH, TPP) + E2 (dihydrolipoyl transacetylase, lipoamide) + E3 (dihydrolipoyl DH, FAD).

TPP role in PDH: Cofactor on E1 that performs the decarboxylation of pyruvate — TPP carbanion attacks pyruvate's keto group, CO₂ leaves, leaving hydroxyethyl-TPP intermediate which then transfers acetyl to lipoamide.

Glucose-Alanine Cycle (Muscle ↔ Liver)

  • Muscle: AA breakdown → NH₄⁺ → captured by α-KG → glutamate → transfers NH to pyruvate → alanine
  • Blood: Alanine travels to liver (NH₃ is toxic, can't transport free)
  • Liver: Alanine → pyruvate (transamination, NH goes into urea cycle via Glu) → gluconeogenesis → glucose
  • Blood: Glucose returns to muscle

Why this cycle exists: muscle can't do gluconeogenesis. This dumps NH₃ AND restocks glucose in one move.

Why Categorize?

Glucogenic AAs degrade to: pyruvate, OAA, fumarate, succinyl-CoA, or α-KG → can be exported as glucose via gluconeogenesis (vital during fasting).

Ketogenic AAs degrade to: acetyl-CoA or acetoacetate → cannot make glucose, become fatty acids or ketone bodies. Excess (starvation, diabetes) → blood acidosis → coma/death.

Only Leu and Lys are exclusively ketogenic. The "both" category (Phe, Ile, Thr, Trp, Tyr) gives both depending on which carbons.

🧪 Key Structures (Memorize)

Urea

Two NH₂ groups: one from glutamate (mitochondria), one from aspartate (cytosol). Both originate from amino acid breakdown.

Uric Acid (uricotelic — birds, reptiles)

Purine derivative. Insoluble paste, conserves water. Made via more ATP than urea but excreted as solid.

Glutamine Synthetase Reactions

#Reaction
1Glutamate + ATP → γ-glutamyl phosphate + ADP
2γ-glutamyl-P + NH₄⁺ → glutamine + Pi + H⁺
SumGlutamate + NH₄⁺ + ATP → Glutamine + ADP + Pi + H⁺

Regulation: 8 allosteric inhibitors (His, Trp, AMP, CTP, glucosamine-6-P, carbamoyl-P, Ala, Gly). Covalent adenylylation on Tyr increases sensitivity to those inhibitors. Adenylylation itself is inhibited by high [Gln] and [Pi].

Glutamate Synthetase (GltS) — only in bacteria/plants, NOT animals

Reactionα-KG + Gln + NADPH + H⁺ → 2 Glu + NADP⁺
Sum w/ GSα-KG + NH₄⁺ + NADPH + ATP → Glu + NADP⁺ + ADP + Pi

Animals lack this enzyme. We rely on dietary protein → AA breakdown → transamination onto α-KG → Glu.

Transamination (general)

Amino acid₁ + α-keto acid₂ ⇌ α-keto acid₁ + amino acid₂

Cofactor: PLP (pyridoxal phosphate / vitamin B6) — bound to active-site Lys via Schiff base. Mechanism: AA forms aldimine with PLP → ketimine → hydrolyzes to α-keto acid + pyridoxamine → reverse with second keto acid.

In catabolism, most AAs funnel their NH to glutamate via aminotransferases. Glu can then either feed urea cycle (via deamination by glutamate DH) or be transaminated to aspartate via Glu-Asp aminotransferase (Asp donates the 2nd N of urea).

Glutathione (γ-Glu-Cys-Gly)

Tripeptide w/ γ-peptide bond (not normal α!) between Glu and Cys. Synthesized by 2 ATP-using enzymes:

  1. γ-glutamyl cysteine synthetase (Glu + Cys + ATP)
  2. Glutathione synthetase (γ-Glu-Cys + Gly + ATP)

Roles: maintains 5 mM reducing intracellular environment, drug conjugation for excretion (feces/urine), GSSG ↔ 2 GSH redox cycle.

N₂ Fixation Stoichiometry

N₂ + 8 H⁺ + 8 e⁻ + 16 ATP → 2 NH₃ + H₂ + 16 ADP + 16 Pᵢ

Nitrogenase complex:

  • • Dinitrogenase reductase (Fe protein) — donates electrons, ATP-dependent conformation
  • • Dinitrogenase (MoFe protein) — has MoFe₇S₉ + homocitrate active site where N₂ binds
  • • Electrons originate from pyruvate oxidation → ferredoxin/flavodoxin
  • • O₂ inactivates nitrogenase — solved by anaerobic conditions (heterocysts in cyanobacteria) or leghemoglobin (root nodules)
  • • Plant supplies up to 20% of ATP needed
