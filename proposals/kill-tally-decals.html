Why Decals At All

Kill tally stickers have been a hunting tradition for decades, the vehicle equivalent of notches on a rifle stock. A yellow leaping buck on the rear glass signals harvest for the year, starts conversations at the gas station, and looks clean against the Tesla paint. Long-term, the same system scales to turkey fans, bass silhouettes, brookies, whatever gets tagged. One design system, one workflow, one set of tools.

Why Not Print Shops

StickerMule's "die-cut" product leaves a visible white vinyl border around every sticker. That's fine on a laptop but makes a Tesla rear window look like it has a laptop sticker slapped on it. StickerYou's Image Transfers product is aesthetically right (no border, only the silhouette transfers) but is quote-based with minimums pushing $40+ for a single design and a week of back-and-forth. Both lose to cut vinyl on every axis except novelty color printing.

Why a Cricut Wins

Cut vinyl applies flush to the surface like paint. No background, no border, just the yellow shape sitting on glass. Oracal 651 is the same material professional sign shops use, 6-year outdoor rated, Tesla clearcoat friendly. Once the machine and vinyl are in hand, every subsequent decal is ~5 minutes of work and about 15 cents of material.

Oracal 651 Glossy Yellow 021, 12" x 6ft roll. Frisco Craft Clear Transfer Tape, 12" x 50ft, medium tack. Total $24.28. Arrives before the Cricut pickup window closes.
First cut & weed
Install Cricut Design Space (free), upload the buck SVG, size to 6" wide for the Tesla rear window. Cut takes 2 minutes. Weed the excess vinyl with an X-Acto knife - antler tines are the fiddly part. Lay transfer tape over the design and burnish with a credit card.
Apply to the Tesla
Wash surface, wipe with 70% isopropyl, park in shade at 60-85°F. Hinge decal with painter's tape, squeegee from center outward, peel transfer tape at 180° angle. Leave untouched for 24 hours before washing.
Iterate
Every harvest earns a new silhouette. Turkey for May 9 season closer. Bass for summer. Brookie for trout creek trips. Same machine, same software, same vinyl roll for years.
