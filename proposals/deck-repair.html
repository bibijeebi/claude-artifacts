Deck Rehab

Diagnosis, root cause, and repair plan for the deck railing, screen framing, and surface.

Status Proposed

TL;DR The wood is pressure-treated, so the type isn't the problem. The deck was never sealed or stained, causing years of moisture damage. Screen framing was glued instead of fastened. Cheap lumber + no finish + lazy joints = everything failing at once. Fix is straightforward DIY: replace the worst boards, properly fasten screen frames, fill bee holes, seal everything.

What We Assumed

Assumed

Carpenter bees destroyed the deck, and maybe the previous owner didn't use pressure-treated wood.

What's Actually Going On

Reality

The wood IS pressure-treated southern yellow pine. PT protects against fungal rot and termites only. It does nothing against carpenter bees, which bore into any softwood regardless of treatment.

The real damage comes from a combination of failures by whoever built or "repaired" this deck before we bought the house:

  • Never sealed or stained. Years of rain/sun cycles caused all the splitting, warping, and bowing as the wood swells and shrinks repeatedly.
  • Screen frame pieces glued on with construction adhesive instead of being screwed or bolted. Adhesive fails outdoors from UV and moisture, which is why the frames are separating.
  • Lower-grade lumber (#2 or construction grade) used instead of #1 or select, making it more prone to warping.
  • Wood likely installed wet/green and left to dry unevenly, accelerating the bowing.

Repair Plan

Replace worst rails and screen frame boards
Swap out split, bowed, and separated boards with new pressure-treated lumber. Use proper structural screws and carriage bolts for the screen frames, not adhesive.
Address carpenter bee damage
Plug existing bore holes with steel wool pushed into the holes, then seal over with exterior caulk or wood putty. Treat with residual insecticide (permethrin) to deter new boring.
Sand and prep surfaces
Hit the worst UV-damaged areas with 80-grit to open up the grain. Clean the whole deck with a deck brightener/cleaner to remove gray oxidation and prep for stain adhesion.
Seal and stain the entire deck
Apply a penetrating exterior stain/sealant to the whole deck, railings, and screen framing. This is what should have been done from day one and will prevent future damage cycles.
