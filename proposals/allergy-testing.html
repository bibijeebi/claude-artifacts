The Problem

Starting in May 2025 during the Marine Corps Half Marathon in the DC area, Benny experienced an acute respiratory allergic reaction around mile 5. Pollen granules were visibly hitting his nose and throat, and his airways began to constrict. The same type of reaction has recurred in spring 2026 at Honeycutt Road park and at home in Willow Spring, NC on windy high-pollen days.

Without knowing exactly which allergen is causing it, treatment is guesswork. A positive IgE test for specific allergens opens the door to targeted antihistamine timing, potential immunotherapy (allergy shots/sublingual drops), and knowing which pollen seasons to prepare for.

Action Plan

Order the panel online
Go to healthlabs.com/region-2-allergy-testing and purchase the Region 2 panel ($429). Alternatively, go to walkinlab.com for the Quest expanded panel ($319), or pick individual allergens for ~$100. A lab requisition is emailed immediately.
Walk into a local lab
No appointment needed. HealthLabs uses Quest and Labcorp locations. Nearest options are in Fuquay-Varina, Holly Springs, and Garner. Blood draw takes about 5 minutes. No fasting required. No need to stop Zyrtec or any antihistamines.
Get results online
Results arrive in 1-3 business days via secure portal. Each allergen gets a specific IgE level (0.10 to 100+) and a class rating (0-6). Class 0 = no sensitization, Class 3+ = clinically significant. No need to interpret alone; HealthLabs includes a free physician consult.
Act on results
If oak or grass pollen comes back positive, start a targeted plan: pre-dose cetirizine (Zyrtec) 10mg daily before pollen season hits, add fluticasone (Flonase) nasal spray, and consider allergy immunotherapy (shots or sublingual tablets) through VA or private practice for long-term desensitization.
Timing Matters

IgE levels are highest when you're actively symptomatic. Testing right now, during peak oak pollen season while you're reacting, gives the most accurate results. A test done in December when nothing's pollinating could miss a borderline positive.

Why This Matters

This isn't just seasonal sniffles. Airway constriction during a half marathon is a performance and safety issue. If the reaction worsens year over year (which allergies can do), it could progress toward exercise-induced anaphylaxis in a pollen-heavy environment. Knowing what you're allergic to means you can pre-medicate intelligently, avoid peak exposure windows, and have a conversation with a doctor about immunotherapy if it's severe enough.

It's also useful data for Bekah. If Kong's dander, dust mites, or mold species come back positive, that changes how you think about HVAC filtration, cleaning cadence, and whether a HEPA air purifier is worth it for the house.
