Key Question

Why a Gravel Bike Instead of a Hybrid or Mountain Bike?

The short answer: a gravel bike does everything a hybrid does, but better, while also working on trails. A hybrid is the "compromise that compromises everything" bike. Here's the breakdown.

Road Bike Gravel / CX Bike Hybrid Bike Mountain Bike
Handlebars Drop bars Drop bars Flat bars Flat bars
Road speed Excellent Great Okay Sluggish
Gravel / dirt roads Terrible Excellent Okay Excellent
Singletrack trails No Light trails No Built for it
Long distance comfort Good Great Fair Poor
Hand positions 3+ (drops, hoods, tops) 3+ (drops, hoods, tops) 1 1
Tire flexibility 23-32mm only 28-50mm (slick or knobby) 32-42mm 2.0-2.6" only
Ride with road group Yes Yes (swap to slick tires) You'll get dropped No chance
Ride with MTB group No Fire roads + moderate trails No Yes
Weight ~18 lbs ~21 lbs ~28 lbs ~30 lbs

The gravel/CX bike covers about 80% of what every other category does. Put slick tires on it and it keeps up on road rides. Put knobby tires on it and it handles fire roads and gravel trails with the mountain biking crew. A hybrid can't keep up with road cyclists (flat bars and heavy frame) and can't handle trails (no clearance, wrong geometry). It's the "I don't know what I want" bike that people buy at Target and never ride.

The common confusion is that "hybrid" sounds like "best of both worlds." It's not. A gravel bike is actually the best of both worlds. The hybrid is the worst of both worlds marketed to people who don't know the difference.
