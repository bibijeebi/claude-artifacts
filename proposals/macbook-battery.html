MacBook Air M1 Battery Service

TL;DR The M1 chip is still plenty capable. macOS says "Service Recommended," the battery dies in ~2 minutes unplugged, and Safari alone is flagged as using significant energy. Apple will replace the battery for $159 + tax at the Crabtree Valley Mall store in Raleigh. This is 70% cheaper than the cheapest new Mac (MacBook Neo at $539 with military discount) and buys years more life out of a machine that does everything it needs to.

The Problem

The MacBook Air M1 battery has degraded to the point where macOS is showing "Service Recommended" with a warning triangle. The laptop cannot survive more than approximately 2 minutes unplugged from power, effectively turning it into a desktop. Safari alone is flagged as using significant energy. Low Power Mode is permanently enabled as a cope mechanism.

The machine is otherwise fully functional. The M1 chip handles daily workloads (Claude, browser tabs, terminal, code editing) without breaking a sweat. There's no performance reason to replace it.

The MacBook Neo is an interesting machine but it's actually a downgrade from the M1 Air in several ways: no backlit keyboard, weaker USB-C ports (one is USB 2.0), and the A18 Pro is an iPhone-class chip less powerful than the M1 for sustained workloads. The M1 Air with a fresh battery is the better machine.

Action Plan

Book a Genius Bar appointment
Apple Store at Crabtree Valley Mall, Raleigh (~35 min drive). Schedule at getsupport.apple.com or the Apple Support app. Alternatively, mail it in for 5+ business day turnaround.
Back up the Mac
Time Machine to external drive or iCloud. Apple may wipe the machine during service. Takes 30 min with a USB drive while plugged in.
Drop off and wait
In-store battery replacement takes about 2 hours. Crabtree Valley Mall has plenty to kill time with. Bring a book or take Bekah.
Pick up and verify
Confirm battery health reads "Normal" in System Settings > Battery > Battery Health. Comes with a 90-day service warranty.

Why Not Just Replace It?

The M1 MacBook Air was a generational leap when it launched and it remains genuinely capable in 2026. It runs the latest macOS, handles all daily tasks, and the 8-core GPU is still relevant. The only thing wrong with this machine is the battery, a wear item with a known lifespan of ~1,000 charge cycles.

Spending $170 to restore full functionality and portability to a machine that otherwise works perfectly is the obvious play. This laptop can realistically last another 3-4 years after a battery swap, at which point a proper upgrade (not a sidegrade to a Neo) would make more sense.

Budget Impact

$159 + tax (~$170 total) is a one-time expense. No recurring cost. No financing needed. This is a maintenance item, not a capital purchase. For context, one month of HVAC travel work covers this several times over.
