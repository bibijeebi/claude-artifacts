The Foreman College Plan

Why the Kids' College Is Already Paid For

Written for Dad. The short version: because of Benny's permanent VA disability rating, every child we ever have gets their own federal education benefit. We don't need to save for college. The college fund is the rating.

Each kid, individually, gets 36 months of school paid at ~$1,600/month by the VA, on top of state programs that cover the tuition itself. Kid one gets it. Kid nine would get it. It is not a pot they split.
The Main Engine

Chapter 35: the federal benefit doing the heavy lifting

Chapter 35 (the VA's Dependents' Educational Assistance program) pays for school for the spouse and children of any veteran who is permanently and totally disabled due to a service-connected disability.[1] Benny holds exactly that rating.

How it works, plainly:

  • Every child gets their own entitlement. 36 months of full-time benefits, per kid, no family cap. It is not shared or divided among siblings.
  • The money goes to the student, not the school. Currently $1,574 per month for full-time college, adjusted upward every year.[2] The kid uses it for tuition, rent, food, whatever.
  • No expiration. Under current rules, children who become eligible now face no age deadline to use it.[1]
  • No lottery, no essay contest, no income test. It's an entitlement. The kid applies with a one-page form and starts getting paid.

The Tuition Layer

State and federal programs that cover the tuition bill itself

Chapter 35 is cash to the kid. Separately, the tuition invoice gets handled by programs that key off household income, and here's the quirk: VA disability compensation is tax-free and invisible to the IRS, so on paper our income looks low even though the household is fine. That unlocks:

  • Next NC Scholarship. North Carolina covers full tuition and fees at any NC community college, and most-to-all tuition at any public university in the state, for households under $80,000 adjusted gross income. Automatic off the FAFSA, no separate application.[3]
  • Federal Pell Grants. Same low-income math, up to ~$7,400/year, doesn't get repaid.
  • NC Promise. Four NC public universities charge $500 per semester tuition, for everyone, as standing state policy.[4] That's the floor under the whole plan.
  • NC Scholarship for Children of Wartime Veterans. A state scholarship specifically for children of 100%-disabled wartime veterans (Class I-B), eight semesters, awarded to all qualified applicants. Kids born and raised in NC qualify through their own residency.[5] Being honest: this program has had state funding drama recently,[6] so we treat it as a bonus, not a pillar.

Why It's Durable

Why we trust this more than a savings account

  • It's been federal law since 1956. Chapter 35 has paid for school for veterans' kids for 70 years, through every Congress and administration, with rates indexed annually.
  • The rating locks in. A VA rating held continuously for 20 years becomes legally protected from reduction. Benny's clock runs out in 2044, right around when the first kid would start college. After that the benefit is effectively vested for the whole family.
  • Per-kid entitlement scales automatically. Nothing to fund, rebalance, or catch up on. Kid #9 is exactly as covered as kid #1, which no 529 in the world can claim.

The extras: any baby born through 2028 also gets a $1,000 federal "Trump Account" seed deposit invested in an index fund[7], which we'll claim, but that's retirement scaffolding, not tuition. Ordinary savings and investments can stack on top whenever we feel like it. They're garnish. Chapter 35 is the meal.

Straight Talk

The only three ways this fails

  • The rating drops below 100% before 2044. Permanent-and-total ratings are rarely reduced without evidence of major medical improvement, and we manage everything with that protection in mind.
  • Congress kills the program. Possible for anything, but this one has survived 70 years and every budget fight since Eisenhower.
  • The kid demands a $70,000/year private school. That's a parenting conversation, not a funding gap. In-state options make them money.

Two of those are near-zero. The third is a choice we control. That's why the confidence.
