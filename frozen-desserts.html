Taxonomy

The Frozen Dessert Family

There are roughly ten distinct categories of frozen desserts, each defined by fat content, air content, serving temperature, and technique. They range from the butterfat-rich density of frozen custard to the crystalline simplicity of granita. Here's every member of the family.

American Classic

Ice Cream

Cream-heavy, churned with lots of air ("overrun"). Legally must be at least 10% milk fat in the US. The high fat coats your tongue, and the high air makes it fluffy. Cheap brands are 50-100% air. Premium brands like Häagen-Dazs run about 25%.

Custard-style uses egg yolks. American-style (Philadelphia-style) skips eggs entirely and leans on cream. Served at 6-10°F.

14-25% fat 50-100% overrun 6-10°F optional eggs
Italy

Gelato

Milk-heavy instead of cream-heavy, churned with far less air. Denser, silkier, with more intense flavor because less fat coats your tongue and less air dilutes the taste. Modern Italian style often skips eggs entirely, using cornstarch or nothing.

The warmer serving temperature (10-15°F) is critical. Serve gelato at ice cream temps and it's a brick.

4-8% fat 25-30% overrun 10-15°F rare now
Milwaukee, Wisconsin

Frozen Custard

Legally must contain at least 1.4% egg yolk solids plus 10% milk fat. Churned with very little air. This is what Culver's and Shake Shack serve. Extremely dense and creamy, almost chewy.

Served basically fresh from the machine, not hardened in a freezer. The egg yolk gives it that yellow tint and velvety mouthfeel nothing else has.

10%+ fat low overrun ~18°F fresh required
Americana

Soft Serve

Same base as ice cream but served directly from the machine at around 18°F before hardening. Higher air content makes it smooth and airy. The soft texture is about temperature, not recipe.

You can technically soft-serve gelato, ice cream, or frozen custard. It's a serving method as much as a product.

3-10% fat 30-60% overrun ~18°F churned
France

Sorbet

Zero dairy. Just water, sugar, and fruit (or other flavoring), churned in an ice cream maker. Bright, intense, clean fruit flavor. Icier texture since there's no fat to smooth things out.

Good sorbet depends entirely on sugar ratio: too little and it's a popsicle, too much and it won't freeze. The magic number is 20-30% sugar by weight.

0% dairy low overrun 10-15°F churned
The Middle Child

Sherbet

Fruit-based like sorbet but with 1-2% milk fat added. Tangier and lighter than ice cream, creamier than sorbet. Legally must be between 1-2% milk fat in the US.

Rainbow sherbet is the classic. Honestly an underrated category that deserves more respect than it gets.

1-2% fat churned ~10°F
Sicily

Granita

Coarse, flaky, scraped ice. No churning at all. You freeze a flavored liquid in a pan and scrape it with a fork periodically. The texture is the entire point: crunchy ice crystals, not smooth.

Coffee granita with whipped cream is a Sicilian breakfast. Easiest frozen dessert to make at home since it requires zero equipment beyond a fork and a pan.

0% dairy scraped, not churned 15-20°F
Italy

Semifreddo

"Half-cold" in Italian. A mousse or whipped cream base folded with flavoring, poured into a loaf pan, and frozen. Never churned. Slices like a cake.

The whipped cream/egg structure traps air, so it stays creamy without a machine. This is the best no-equipment frozen dessert if you want richness.

high fat usually no churn 5-10°F
India

Kulfi

Milk slowly cooked down (reduced) until thick and concentrated, then frozen in molds. No churning at all. Dense, almost fudgy. The long reduction caramelizes the milk sugars and gives it a flavor nothing else replicates.

Pistachio and cardamom are the classic flavors. Mango and rose are also traditional.

high fat no churn, molded ~10°F
Japan

Mochi Ice Cream

Ice cream ball wrapped in pounded glutinous rice dough. The mochi shell insulates the ice cream and adds a chewy, stretchy texture. The ice cream inside is standard.

Matcha, strawberry, and black sesame are the most popular. The combination of textures is what makes this special.

varies ice cream + rice dough

Make It Yourself

Classic Gelato Recipe

This is the master base. Once you nail this, every flavor is just a variation. Two versions: the traditional egg custard base, and the modern eggless Sicilian style that most Italian gelaterias actually use today.

Custard-Style Gelato Base

Yield~1 Quart
Prep15 min
Chill4-12 hrs
Churn20-25 min

Ingredients

2.5 cups whole milk
1/2 cup heavy cream
2/3 cup sugar
4 egg yolks
1 pinch salt
Flavoring of choice

Steps

  1. Whisk egg yolks and sugar until pale and thick, about 2 minutes.
  2. Heat milk, cream, and salt over medium until steaming. Don't boil it.
  3. Slowly pour half the hot milk into the yolks while whisking constantly (this tempers the eggs so they don't scramble).
  4. Pour everything back into the saucepan.
  5. Stir constantly over medium-low until it hits 170°F or coats the back of a spoon. Drag your finger through it on the spoon — the line should hold.
  6. Strain through a fine mesh strainer into a clean bowl.
  7. Add your flavorings now (vanilla, espresso, melted chocolate, etc).
  8. Press plastic wrap directly on the surface (prevents skin). Refrigerate 4+ hours. Overnight is better.
  9. Churn in ice cream maker. Pull it out at soft-serve consistency, not stiff.
  10. Transfer to a container, press plastic wrap on surface. Freeze 2-4 hours.
  11. Pull out 10-15 minutes before serving. Gelato is served warmer than ice cream.

Eggless "Sicilian" Style

Lighter, cleaner flavor. This is what most modern Italian gelaterias actually use because it lets the flavor ingredient shine without egg competing.

ChangeReplace 4 egg yolks with 1.5 tbsp cornstarch
  1. Whisk 1.5 tbsp cornstarch into 1/4 cup of the cold milk to make a slurry.
  2. Heat the rest of the milk + cream + sugar until simmering.
  3. Whisk in the cornstarch slurry and cook 2 minutes until it thickens noticeably.
  4. Continue from step 6 above (strain, flavor, chill, churn).
Pro Tip The single biggest quality lever is aging the base overnight. The fat globules hydrate, the proteins relax, and you get a noticeably smoother texture vs churning after just 4 hours. Every extra hour of chill time pays dividends.
Variations

Flavor Playbook

All of these use the base recipe above. Just add the flavoring at step 7 (or as noted). The eggless base is better for fruit and citrus flavors; the custard base is better for nut and chocolate flavors.

Pistachio

Blend 1/2 cup roasted unsalted pistachios into the hot milk, steep 1 hour, strain hard. Use custard base.

Chocolate

Whisk 4 oz chopped dark chocolate into the hot custard until melted. Add 2 tbsp cocoa powder for depth.

Lemon

Zest of 3 lemons steeped in the hot milk, strained out. Use the eggless base for this one.

Stracciatella

Drizzle melted chocolate into the churner in the last 2 minutes. It shatters into thin shards. Magic.

Hazelnut

Same as pistachio but with toasted hazelnuts. Or cheat with 3 tablespoons Nutella whisked into the warm base.

Coffee

Dissolve 2 tbsp instant espresso into the hot milk. Dead simple, incredible results.

Fior di Latte No flavoring at all — just the base with good vanilla. This is the "plain" gelato that tests your base recipe. If your fior di latte is good, everything else will be great.

Boozy Frozen Desserts

Alcohol lowers the freezing point of water, which is the central engineering problem. More booze means softer, slushier results. The solution: stay under 1/4 cup of 80-proof spirit per quart of base. Liqueurs are more forgiving than straight spirits because they contain sugar.

Most restaurant versions land around 3-5% ABV, roughly equivalent to a light beer per serving. The alcohol is more about flavor than effect. You'd need to eat a lot to feel it.

The Key Ratio

Add 3 tablespoons of liqueur per pint of base before churning. For straight 80-proof spirits, cut to 2 tablespoons and add an extra tablespoon of corn syrup to compensate for texture.

Amaretto

3 tbsp amaretto + 1/2 tsp almond extract. Toast sliced almonds for topping. The most "fancy Italian restaurant" option.

Kahlúa

3 tbsp Kahlúa + 2 tbsp instant espresso dissolved in the hot milk. Coffee on coffee.

Limoncello

3 tbsp limoncello + zest of 2 lemons. Skip the corn syrup — limoncello has enough sugar already.

Bourbon

2 tbsp bourbon + 2 tbsp maple syrup (replace equal sugar). Tastes like fall in a bowl.

The Boozy Spectrum Beyond gelato: Prosecco sorbet and Aperol spritz sorbetto are Italian restaurant staples. Grappa granita is classic Sicilian. Semifreddo handles alcohol well because it's not supposed to be firm. And any granita is naturally alcohol-friendly since it's meant to be slushy.

What You Need

You don't strictly need an ice cream maker, but it helps enormously. Here are three tiers depending on what you want to invest.

Best Results · $30-50

Cuisinart ICE-21

The standard home recommendation. Freeze-bowl style — you freeze the bowl 24 hours ahead, pour your base in, it churns 20-25 minutes. Makes legitimately good gelato. Only downside: one batch per freeze cycle.

No Equipment · Free

Fork & Pan Method

Pour chilled base into a shallow metal 9x13 pan. Freeze 45 min. Scrape and stir vigorously. Repeat every 30-45 min, 3-4 times. Icier texture, but works. The cornstarch base helps here.

Middle Ground · $0 if you own one

Food Processor Hack

Freeze the base solid in ice cube trays (8+ hours). Pop frozen cubes into a food processor. Blitz 3-5 minutes until smooth. Serve immediately or refreeze 1 hour. Surprisingly close to churned.

Sugar Science Replace 2-3 tablespoons of your granulated sugar with corn syrup or dextrose. This lowers the freezing point slightly and fights iciness. Every serious gelateria does this. Dextrose is available at brewing supply stores.
Colder Is Better If your ice cream maker bowl goes in the freezer, give it 24 hours minimum — not the 12 most manuals say. A colder bowl means faster freezing, which means smaller ice crystals, which means smoother gelato.
Expert Knowledge

Pro Tips

Age the Base Overnight This is the single biggest quality upgrade. During the overnight rest, fat globules fully hydrate, proteins relax into solution, and the entire base becomes more homogeneous. The difference between a 4-hour chill and an overnight chill is dramatic.
The Two Best No-Equipment Options Semifreddo for when you want something rich and creamy (whip cream, fold in flavor, freeze in a loaf pan). Granita for when you want something refreshing (make flavored liquid, freeze, scrape with fork). Both require zero special equipment and zero skill.
Why Gelato Tastes More Intense It's not just the lower fat. Fat coats your taste buds and mutes flavor perception. Gelato's lower fat means your tongue makes direct contact with the flavor molecules. Less air means each spoonful is denser with actual product. And the warmer serving temp means your taste receptors are more active (cold numbs them). Three factors stacking.
Sorbet Sugar Math The magic window is 20-30% sugar by weight. Below 20% and you get an icy popsicle. Above 30% and it won't freeze properly. A cheap kitchen scale pays for itself immediately when making sorbet. If you don't have one: about 1 cup sugar per 3 cups liquid.
The Spoon Test vs Thermometer If you don't have a thermometer, the "coats the back of a spoon" test works: dip a wooden spoon into the custard, run your finger across the back. If the line holds and doesn't drip back together, you're at about 170°F. If it fills back in, keep cooking.
