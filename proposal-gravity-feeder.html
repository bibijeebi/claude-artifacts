Proposal #034

The Feed Tower

The corn pile is live and the squirrels found it, but a bare pile feeds the rain and the raccoons for free, one bag disappears in days and molds in a week. This proposal is the escalation ladder above the pile: the $13 bucket rig already in hand and running first, the $60 PVC tree tower it graduates to, and the $150+ tripod tier we keep on file to remind ourselves why we're not buying it.

The Situation

A 50 lb bag of TSC deer corn is on the ground patterning squirrels (plus whatever coons, possums, and deer find it) ahead of the Oct 12 opener, feeding the very animals #033's suppressed Scoremaster and #006's crossbow exist to shoot. The pile works. The pile is also the least efficient possible delivery system: rain rots what the coons don't vacuum overnight, and every refill is a full re-scatter.

A gravity feeder fixes all of it with zero moving parts: a container holds the bag, corn exits a small opening at the bottom, the animals' own eating pulls more down. The commercial versions run $100–300 because they're built for 250–400 lb of feed on steel legs in a treeless food plot. The backyard needs none of that, it has a good tree and one bag at a time.

Design requirement that shapes everything: the animals must feed from a pile ON THE GROUND, not with their faces in the hardware. The kill zone is the pile; the feeder stays out of the shot picture. This is why chicken-port designs (feeding at the container wall) are the trial tier, not the destination.

Tier 1 — Bucket + Chicken Ports (in hand, running first)

5-gal bucket + chicken feeder ports, strapped to the tree~$13 (kit bought 8/16)

Bucket from the garage, port kit already ordered (comes with its own hole saw). Build: drill 2 holes ~1" up from the bucket bottom, port through, lock nut inside, cut the port hood's inner lip back so corn flows instead of bridging (the one mod every build video agrees on), strap bucket to trunk, bungee the lid against coons.

Capacity ~25 lb, half a bag. Feeding happens at the ports, but squirrels and coons rake corn out constantly, so a spill pile forms below anyway, and a paver under the port side makes that the kill pad. Verdict: a $13 experiment that answers "does rationed corn change consumption?" before the pipe run.

Tier 2 — The PVC Feed Tower (the build this proposal funds)

Full-bag capacity, meters itself, nothing but pipe near the trunk and nothing at all near the pile. Two variants of the same idea, pick at the store:

Variant A: strapped to the trunk, 45° elbow outletsimpler build

Pipe stands vertical against the trunk on two metal straps. Bottom necks 6"→4" into a 45° elbow with the mouth pointing up: gravity holds the corn column at the elbow lip, animals dig it out, spillage builds the ground pile. Fill from a step stool through the top cap.

Variant B: rope-hung, open bottomno elbow needed

Pipe hangs from a limb, completely open bottom 3" above a paver. Corn pours until the pile touches the mouth and self-plugs (angle of repose), eating reopens it. Filling is the party trick: slack the rope so the mouth rests sealed on the paver, pour the whole bag with zero spill, hoist 3", tie off. Diagram above.

Tier 3 — The Tripod (on file, declined)

Commercial tripod / barrel gravity feeder$150–300

What TSC sells: 250–400 lb hoppers on powder-coated steel legs (Moultrie 250 lb gravity, treeline 400 lb protein, Boss Buck 200 lb). Engineering for exactly one problem the backyard doesn't have: no tree at the feed site. It also plants three steel legs in the shooting lane and hauls half a season of feed the coons get to work on nightly.

Recorded here so the next time a $200 tripod looks tempting in the TSC aisle, the answer is already written: the oak is a one-legged tripod with roots, and it was free. Only revisit if a feed station ever needs to live in the open (a future food plot at a treeless corner), in which case a 55-gal drum on a homemade lumber tripod beats the store version anyway.
