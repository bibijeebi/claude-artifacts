Identity is Permanent

In traditional Jewish law, once you're Jewish, you're Jewish forever. There's no mechanism to "un-Jew" yourself.

As a man I've always felt Irish, as an actor I've always felt Jewish.

— Harrison Ford Ford's mother was Dorothy Nidelman, making him halachically Jewish regardless of his personal identity

The Rules

Matrilineal descent: Jewish mother = Jewish child, regardless of upbringing. Conversion: Even if you convert out, traditional Judaism still considers you Jewish (just an apostate). Israel's Law of Return: The one exception—grants citizenship to anyone with a Jewish grandparent, but excludes those who've converted to another religion. This was a political compromise, not a religious ruling.

The Founding Generation

Jewish immigrants from Eastern Europe, fleeing pogroms and poverty, built Hollywood from nothing. Excluded from "respectable" industries, they took a chance on the vulgar new medium of moving pictures.

The Shift

Since the 1980s, Jewish actors increasingly kept their birth names: Barbra Streisand, Dustin Hoffman, Adam Sandler, Seth Rogen, Ben Stiller. Jewish identity moved from something to hide to something unremarkable—or even a comedic asset.

Network Effects, Not Conspiracy

These numbers reflect historical compounding, not coordination. First-generation success created mentorship pipelines, family businesses, and cultural capital. Similar patterns exist in other immigrant-founded industries: Italian-Americans in construction, Korean-Americans in dry cleaning, Indian-Americans in motels. The difference is Hollywood's visibility.
