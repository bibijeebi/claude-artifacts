The Procedure

What Actually Happens

The surgeon doesn't know which of the two techniques you'll need until they're in there looking at fluid under a microscope mid-surgery. This is a critical detail — you can't plan for one or the other in advance.

1

General Anesthesia

Completely asleep. Outpatient — go home same day.

2

Small Incision

Less than an inch on each side of the scrotum. Surgeon locates the two severed ends of the vas deferens.

3

Fluid Check Under Microscope

Surgeon examines fluid from the testicular side. If sperm or clear fluid is present, they proceed with the simpler reconnection. If thick/pasty with no sperm, a secondary blockage has formed and they do the bypass instead.

4

Microsurgical Reconnection

Using sutures finer than a human hair under 30–40x magnification. 6–10 stitches create a watertight two-layer closure. About 2–3 hours total.

Two Possible Techniques

Vasovasostomy (VV) — the simpler, more common one. Direct reconnection of the two cut ends of the vas deferens. 90–95% patency rate. This is what the VA urologist said is most likely given the short interval since vasectomy.

Vasoepididymostomy (VE) — the complex bypass. Only needed if a secondary blockage has developed upstream closer to the testicle. 65–70% patency rate. This is why surgeon selection matters — not all urologists can perform VE, and you need someone who can do both so they can switch mid-surgery if needed.

Recovery

About a week of light duty. 2–4 weeks to full activity. No sexual activity for 3 weeks to protect the delicate reconnection. Sperm typically returns in weeks to months. First semen analysis usually at 6–8 weeks post-op.

VA Coverage

What the VA Covers

The VA covers vasectomy reversal as a fertility/contraceptive service for enrolled veterans under VHA Directive 1332 (Fertility Management) and VHA Directive 1102.2 (Elective Sterilization and Reversal). This doesn't require service-connected disability — just enrollment in VA healthcare.

Durham VA doesn't do the surgery in-house (most VAs don't have microsurgery capability). So they're referring out to Community Care, which means a private surgeon does the procedure and the VA pays the provider directly. Zero cost to the veteran.

The VA confirmed they cover everything except IVF:

✓ Covered: Vasovasostomy (reconnection), Vasoepididymostomy (bypass), IUI (intrauterine insemination), Sperm aspiration (TESA/MESA), All follow-up appointments and semen analyses

✗ Not covered: IVF (requires service-connected infertility)
