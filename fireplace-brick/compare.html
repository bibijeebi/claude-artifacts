Every Brick Option, Compared Honestly

Three different product categories. Different prices because they're different materials. Here's what you're actually buying.

When you searched "brick sheets" at Home Depot you got results across three completely different product categories: real clay tile, polyurethane faux panels, and gypsum stick-on. They're priced differently because they are different. Here's what each one actually is, what it costs, and which one fits your fireplace trim job.

Quick Verdict

For your fireplace trim band:

MSI Red Noble at $106/case is the cheapest real-clay option. Same product category as Old Mill Brickwebb, $9 less. Both are mesh-mounted real fired clay. Either works.

The polyurethane GenStone panels look cheap per sq ft but aren't right for this job — they're 11.75″ tall siding panels designed for big wall coverage, not a 4-inch decorative trim band. Cutting them down to a 4″ strip wastes most of the panel. Also some manufacturers' clearance specs say keep them away from active heat sources.

Why The Prices Look Different

You're comparing apples, oranges, and a plastic apple. The polyurethane GenStone shows a low per-panel price ($31) but it's covering only 1.98 sq ft, so at $15.66/sq ft it's actually more expensive than every real clay option. The gypsum versions look cheap per piece but cover so little ground per box that they're $28+/sq ft.

For real clay mesh sheets, the gap is small ($9 between MSI and Old Mill). That's what should drive your decision. Pick whichever color blend you and Bekah like, ignore the polyurethane and gypsum noise.

What Goes On The Shopping List

The deployed shopping list has MSI Red Noble as the primary pick with both Old Mill alternatives listed as backup if MSI is out of stock at your local store.
