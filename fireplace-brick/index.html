Fireplace Brick Trim

Decorative thin-brick band framing the existing gas insert beneath the cedar mantel.

Location: Eagle Chase Dr, Willow Spring Method: DIY · Single afternoon Budget: $110–130

Executive Summary

The fireplace insert, mantel, and slate hearth are already in place. The job is a decorative thin-brick band roughly 4 inches wide running up the left side, across the top, and down the right side of the existing black firebox surround, capped at the bottom by the slate hearth and at the top by the cedar mantel. Total brick area: about 3.5 to 5 sq ft. One carton of Old Mill Brickwebb covers it with material to spare. No backer board, no permit, no demo. Glue, set, grout, done.

Reality check: This is a one-afternoon job for under $130. A 4-inch band wrapping the firebox opening is exactly the look that complements the cedar mantel without competing with it.

The Plan in Plain English

The black metal trim around the firebox stays exactly where it is. The brick wraps around the outside of that black trim, filling the painted drywall area between the black frame and the surrounding wall. Brick faces go flush against the existing wall surface, with cuts at the inner edge to butt cleanly against the black metal. The cedar mantel above and the slate hearth below act as natural top and bottom borders.

Code & Safety

Brick is non-combustible, so no clearance issues. Your gas insert is a factory-built unit, which means the manufacturer's installation manual governs, not the IRC. The original setup had wood and drywall in this exact zone, so brick in the same zone is automatically safer than what was there. No permit needed for cosmetic non-structural finish work in NC.

One thing to check: Do not cover or block any vents, access panels, or junction boxes built into the gas insert framing. The black metal frame around the firebox is part of the appliance and stays untouched. Brick goes around it, not over it.

Step-By-Step

Phase 1 · Prep · 30 min
  1. Mask off everything. Painter's tape along the bottom edge of the cedar mantel, both sides of the black firebox frame, and the top edge of the slate hearth. Drop cloth on the hearth and floor.
  2. Score the painted drywall. 60-grit sandpaper, scuff the entire brick zone aggressively. You're breaking the paint surface so the adhesive can bite. Wipe dust with a damp cloth.
  3. Snap your reference lines. Pencil in the outer edge of the brick band on all three sides. Use a level on the top header line. This is your target boundary.
Phase 2 · Brick · 1.5 to 2 hrs
  1. Dry-fit the layout. Hold a Brickwebb sheet up to the wall and check the fit. Plan your cuts so the most visible bricks (at the edge of the firebox where eyes go) are full bricks, and any partial bricks fall at the outer edge of the band.
  2. Cut the sheets to width. Outside, with the angle grinder and diamond blade. Wear the respirator. Score the brick face first, then break or cut through the mesh. For a 4″ band you'll be cutting individual bricks longwise.
  3. Glue and press. Squeeze a heavy zigzag bead of BrickBond on the back of each piece, plus the wall surface for big pieces. Press firmly into place, wiggle slightly to bed, hold 30 seconds. Start at the bottom and work up.
  4. Verify with the level. Every few bricks, check the top header line and the inner edge against the firebox frame. Adjust before adhesive sets.
  5. Cure 24 hours. Walk away. Don't grout tonight.
Phase 3 · Grout & Finish · 45 min next day
  1. Mix the Type S mortar. Per package, slightly stiffer than peanut butter. Load the grout bag.
  2. Pipe the joints. Squeeze mortar into every joint, slightly overfilling. Work top down.
  3. Wait for thumbprint-firm. 20 to 45 minutes. Press a thumb in. Dents but doesn't smear means ready.
  4. Strike the joints. Run the brick jointer through every joint with steady pressure. Concave profile.
  5. Brush, then sponge. Stiff brush an hour later to knock off crumbs. Damp sponge wipe next day for haze.
  6. Pull the tape. Slowly, while grout is still slightly damp at the edges. Done.

Pro Tips

Color picks for your room: The light blue-gray walls and warm cedar mantel pull in two directions. A warm rustic red (Old Mill Castle Gate or Bridgewater) ties back to the cedar and grounds the composition. A whitewashed/limewashed brick (German smear style) leans modern farmhouse and plays nice with the highland cow art. A charcoal/black brick echoes the firebox frame for a clean monochrome look. Order the free 4-piece sample pack from oldmillbuildingproducts.com before committing.

Mortar joint color: Standard gray reads classic. Light buff/cream reads warm and farmhouse, especially with red brick. For the limewashed look, use white mortar mixed with a touch of buff pigment. Five bucks of pigment dramatically changes the final read.

Inner edge cuts: When you cut bricks to butt against the black firebox frame, leave a 1/16″ to 1/8″ gap and fill with black silicone caulk instead of mortar. Handles thermal expansion of the metal frame and looks cleaner than brick-to-metal grout.

Best case 90%: A few cut edges will show, a couple joints will look slightly off up close. From across the room nobody sees it. Don't chase perfection on a 3-foot decorative band.
