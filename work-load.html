Load.

Every day for nine weeks, phone activity joined to the hours texted to Meredith. The question was how hard a job day actually is. The answer: a job day moves you as far as a run, at half the intensity, four times as often.

Every day, by what it was

Steps per day. Red is a field job, blue is a run, gray is a van day, class, exam, convention or errand, and the pale bars are days off. Hover any bar.

Where the days land

Each dot is one day. The tick is the median for that group. Field days and run days sit in the same band; everything else sits with the days off.

Distance is equal. Intensity is not.

Median miles per day are about the same for a job and a run. Median active calories per hour of activity are not. Work is walking under load: volume, not cardio.

Miles, running total

Cumulative miles walked on the clock at work against cumulative miles run (run distance from the training log, not the whole day). The job is the base. The runs are the sharpening.

Job sites, ranked

Steps inside the shift, per site. Dorms and multi-story jobs are the big days. Anywhere the phone stayed in the van reads low.
