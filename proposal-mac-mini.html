Bottom line

Buy one M6 Mac mini (32GB memory, 512GB storage, 10-gig Ethernet) from Apple's Veterans & Military store for $1,439, roughly $1,543 out the door with Wake County tax. Apple quoted $295 back for Benny's cracked-lid M1 MacBook Air, which would bring it to $1,144 if they take it as-is. It replaces the Windows desktop as the daily machine, and it's the always-on box that lets Claude run errands (Amazon orders, Marketplace, email) while we're out of town. Nothing else needs buying: the two monitors, keyboard, and mouse on the desk already work with it. You said yes to this in July at $699. The price nearly doubled since then, so this is an honest re-ask, not a sneak.

BIAR — your July call: “No. We're frugal this month.” Plan approved, timing deferred. Then Apple raised Mac prices twice, launched the M6, and the config grew from 16GB to 32GB. The thing you approved at $699 is now $1,439 for a better machine. That's a new number, so it's a new decision, and it's yours.

Why this and why now

Off the Windows box. Benny does his real work on a Mac and hates booting the desktop. This puts a Mac on the desk, on the big monitors, permanently. The Windows PC doesn't get thrown away, it becomes the home server later under #023, but that's a separate decision on a separate day.

The always-on Claude box. Claude Cowork and Claude in Chrome can do things that need a real browser signed into our accounts: buy on Amazon, watch Marketplace for a deal, dig through Gmail. That needs a machine that's awake at home 24/7 while the laptop is in a hotel in New Bern. A laptop with a lid can't be that machine. A mini with no battery, no screen, and a 10-year Apple support life can.

Why now instead of waiting. The M6 launched Aug 25 and ships Sept 22. Apple's CEO said on the July earnings call that memory prices are in a “100-year flood” and to expect more increases after September. Every Mac has gotten more expensive three times this year. There is no cheaper version of this coming; the used market is still priced above new for the last model.

The laptop side of the deal

The M1 Air goes to Apple. It has the hairline crack in the lid housing and the screen glitches when the lid flexes, but the screen itself isn't cracked and the logic board tested fine on an external monitor. Apple quoted $295 in “Good Condition.” Benny will answer the condition questions honestly and see if they take it at the Crabtree pickup. If they grade it lower, we just keep the laptop (it still works fine plugged into a monitor) or sell it locally. So $1,439 is the real budget number and the $295 is the upside.

Benny's laptop becomes Mom's MacBook Air. Instead of buying a new laptop to replace the failing M1, the plan is to take over Mom's Air for the out-of-town work weeks. That still needs a yes from Mom. If that falls through, the mini still stands on its own; the laptop question just comes back as a separate proposal.
