What & Why

The Remington 511 Scoremaster is a bolt-action .22 LR that was Benny's granddad's rifle, passed down through the family. It's the primary squirrel gun. The 511 didn't come from the factory drilled and tapped for a scope, and the receiver is smooth on top (no dovetail grooves for tip-off mounts). To run optics on this rifle, a gunsmith needs to drill four holes in the receiver top, tap them with 6-48 threads, and screw on two Weaver #43 bases.

Once the bases are mounted, any Weaver-style scope rings will bolt right on, and you can mount whatever rimfire scope you want. The bases stay on the rifle permanently. Scope and rings are a separate purchase whenever we're ready.

Action Steps

Call Tar River Arms at 919-307-4530. Say: "I have a Remington Five-Eleven Scoremaster, need the receiver drilled and tapped for two Weaver number 43 bases. Y'all said you'd order the bases and it's about an hour of labor. I'd like to schedule a drop-off."
Drop off the rifle. Bring the 511, unloaded, action open. No need to bring anything else. They'll have the bases on hand or ordered. Confirm the $100 labor + $25-30 parts quote.
Pick up. They drill 4 holes, tap 6-48 threads, screw on both bases. One hour of work. May be same-day pickup or next-day depending on their queue.
Later: scope + rings. Whenever we're ready, buy Weaver-compatible rings (~$20-30) and a rimfire scope. Mount at home with a torque wrench (15-25 in-lbs, blue Loctite). Bore-sight and zero at the range.

Why Weaver #43 Bases (Not Picatinny)

We considered an EGW one-piece Picatinny rail ($30-40) but decided against it. A full-length black anodized Pic rail on a 1950s wood-stock .22 looks ridiculous. Two Weaver #43 bases are tiny, low-profile aluminum blocks that practically disappear on the receiver. They're period-appropriate, proven for decades on rimfire rifles, and there's zero practical advantage to Picatinny rigidity on a gun with no recoil. Weaver rings hold a scope on a .22 literally forever.

Decision for Bekah

This is a straightforward $125-130 one-time expense with zero ongoing cost. Benny drops the rifle off, picks it up, done. The rifle becomes scope-ready for squirrel hunting without any permanent damage to the heirloom (the 4 drilled holes are hidden under the bases and are the standard, accepted way to mount optics on a 511). The only follow-up purchase is a scope and rings whenever we decide to pull the trigger on that.
