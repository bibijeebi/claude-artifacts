Operating agreement

Not filed with the state. Nobody checks it. The bank asks for it when you open the account, and it proves the LLC is a real separate thing if you ever get sued. Sign it, date it, keep a PDF.

Operating Agreement of

A North Carolina single-member limited liability company

This Operating Agreement is made effective by (the "Member"), the sole member of (the "Company").

1. Formation

The Company was formed as a North Carolina limited liability company by filing Articles of Organization with the North Carolina Secretary of State under Chapter 57D of the North Carolina General Statutes.

2. Name and purpose

The Company's name is . Its purpose is , and any other lawful business the Member decides to pursue.

3. Principal office and registered agent

The principal office is . The registered agent is at the same address. The Member may change either at any time.

4. Member

The Company has one member, , who owns 100% of the membership interest. No other person has any ownership, voting, or economic interest in the Company.

5. Management

The Company is managed by its Member. The Member has full authority to run the Company, sign contracts, open and use bank accounts, hire and fire, buy and sell property, and do anything else the Company can lawfully do, without the consent of anyone else.

6. Capital and distributions

The Member has contributed the initial cash and equipment needed to start the Company and may contribute more at any time. The Member is not required to contribute more. Profits and losses belong to the Member, and the Member may take distributions whenever the Company can pay its debts as they come due.

7. Separate finances

The Company keeps its own bank account and its own books. Company money is not mixed with the Member's personal money. The Member's personal money is not mixed with the Company's.

8. Limited liability and indemnification

The Member is not personally liable for the debts or obligations of the Company solely by reason of being a member. The Company will indemnify the Member for any loss or expense reasonably incurred acting on the Company's behalf, except for the Member's own willful misconduct or fraud.

9. Taxes

The Company is treated as a disregarded entity for federal and state income tax purposes unless the Member elects otherwise. Company income and expenses are reported on the Member's personal return.

10. Dissolution

The Company continues until the Member decides to dissolve it. On dissolution the Member pays the Company's debts, then keeps whatever is left, and files Articles of Dissolution with the Secretary of State.

11. Governing law and amendment

This Agreement is governed by North Carolina law. The Member may amend it at any time in writing. This is the entire agreement about the Company's affairs.

, Sole Member,

Then do these, in this order

  1. File the Articles online. $125.

    Go to sosnc.gov online business services, make an account, choose Create a business, LLC, and walk the wizard using the answers above. Online filings clear in about 3 business days. Skip the $100 expedite unless you have a job waiting on it.

  2. Get an EIN. Free. Five minutes.

    Only at irs.gov. Anyone charging you for an EIN is a middleman. Wait until the state approves the LLC so the name matches.

  3. Sign the operating agreement.

    Print the one above, sign it, scan it to your phone. Done.

  4. Open a business checking account.

    This is the step that actually matters. Bring the stamped Articles, the EIN letter, the operating agreement, and your ID. From now on every business dollar goes through this account and only this account. Mixing business and personal money is how people lose the liability protection they just paid for.

  5. Register with NCDOR if your work is taxable.

    NC charges sales tax on repair, maintenance, and installation work on real property, and on most tangible things you sell. Some cleaning-only services are carved out. If yours is taxable, register free at NCDOR online business registration and you'll charge sales tax on invoices. Not sure? That's the first question for Claude below.

  6. Check if you need a trade license.

    HVAC, plumbing, and electrical work need a state license at any dollar amount (NC State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors). General contracting needs one above $40,000 per project (NC Licensing Board for General Contractors). Pressure washing, lawn care, handyman work under the thresholds: no state license. NC has no general business license and no county business license.

  7. General liability insurance.

    Roughly $40 to $80 a month for a one-man service outfit. Most commercial customers and every property manager will ask for a certificate of insurance before you're allowed on site, so this isn't optional in practice.

  8. Put April 15 on your calendar forever.

    Annual report, $203 online, every year starting the year after you file. Miss it and the state sends a warning, then dissolves the LLC. This is the step everybody forgets.

What the LLC actually gets you

Business debts and lawsuits stop at the LLC.Your truck, your house, your savings are off the table if the business gets sued or can't pay a vendor. One catch: it does not protect you from your own screwups. If you personally back the truck through somebody's fence, you still get sued personally. The LLC protects you from the business's liabilities, not your own negligence. Insurance covers that part.

Doors open.Property managers, GCs, and vendor accounts take an LLC with an EIN and a COI seriously in a way they don't take a guy with a truck. Business credit cards and equipment financing start here too.

Same taxes as before, for now.A single-member LLC is "disregarded" by the IRS. Everything goes on Schedule C of your personal return, and you still pay 15.3% self-employment tax, exactly like a sole proprietor. The tax savings only show up later when you elect S-corp treatment, which is worth doing somewhere around $60,000 to $80,000 a year in profit. Not day one.

Ignore all of this

The BOI / FinCEN letters.Beneficial ownership reporting for US companies was permanently killed in August 2026. Any company created by filing with a secretary of state has no obligation to file BOI reports. Letters saying otherwise are scams.

"Certificate of good standing" and "labor law poster" mailers.They show up the week after you file, look official, and want $80 to $150. Trash them.

$299 formation services.You just did the whole thing on this page. The only thing they add is a registered agent, and only if you don't want your home address public.
