The Bottom Line

A 3-year contract. A lifetime of benefits.

The Army offers contracts as short as 2 years for certain MOSs (your military job). Standard contracts run 3–4 years. Most benefits, including the GI Bill, VA home loan, VA healthcare, and disability compensation, require only 90 days of active duty service (or 24 months continuous, depending on the benefit). The GI Bill scales with time served. 90 days gets you 50% of it. 36 months gets 100%. That's exactly why the 3-year contract is the sweet spot. Serve a single enlistment, get out, and you carry these benefits for the rest of your life. The only exceptions: retirement benefits (20 years) and a few programs that scale with service length.

Think about that. Three years of your twenties buys you free college (potentially 6+ years of it), a $0-down home loan you can use forever, VA healthcare (free for life at a 50%+ rating, 10 years enhanced for combat vets), disability compensation for life, and veterans' preference in federal hiring. Plus a whole ecosystem of discounts, programs, and protections. No civilian employer on Earth offers this package.

Section 01

Education: The Crown Jewel

You could become a doctor without paying a single dollar in tuition. Not an exaggeration. Between the GI Bill, VR&E, Yellow Ribbon, STEM extension, VET TEC, tuition assistance, and state-level programs, the military will fund virtually any education you want. The trick is knowing the programs exist and using them in the right order.

Post-9/11 GI Bill (Chapter 33) Tax-Free

36 months of full tuition at any public school (or ~$29,920/yr private school cap), plus Monthly Housing Allowance (E-5 w/ dependents BAH for your school's ZIP), plus $1,000/yr book stipend. Transferable to spouse or kids if you commit to additional service. MHA updates August 1 each year, not January 1 with active duty BAH.

STEM Scholarship (Edith Nourse Rogers) Extra 9 Months

Up to 9 additional months (or $30,000, whichever first) for undergrad STEM degrees requiring 120+ credit hours. Must have completed 60+ credits and have ≤6 months GI Bill remaining. This is effectively a 5th year of GI Bill for qualifying programs. Priority goes to 100% entitlement holders needing the most credits.

VR&E / Chapter 31 Separate Entitlement

Veteran Readiness & Employment. Separate from GI Bill but connected in a clever way. For veterans with service-connected disabilities who have employment barriers. Pays tuition, books, supplies, and subsistence. If you're GI Bill-eligible, elect the Chapter 33 BAH rate instead (almost always more money). Covers graduate degrees, OJT, and self-employment programs.

Here's the key mechanic. You need GI Bill eligibility to get VR&E's higher housing rate. But VR&E never burns your GI Bill months. If you already used some GI Bill before switching, the VA converts those months to VR&E usage and gives them back. Stack it in this order: VR&E first, GI Bill second, STEM extension third. One ceiling: all VA education programs combined cap at 48 months. Your VR&E counselor can approve more if your rehab plan needs it.

VET TEC 2.0 Rules Changed in 2026

Covers coding bootcamps, cybersecurity, data science, IT networking (6–28 week programs). Pays tuition plus housing stipend. Applications opened summer 2026. But know the new rules: unlike the old pilot, VET TEC 2.0 charges your GI Bill 1-for-1 for each month of training, requires 36 months of service, caps at 4,000 participants per fiscal year, and sunsets September 30, 2027. One big exception: if you've fully exhausted your GI Bill, you can still use VET TEC with zero entitlement charge. That makes it a dessert benefit, not an appetizer: burn your GI Bill on the degree first, then use VET TEC for a free bootcamp on top.

CLEP & DSST Exams Free Credits

Free while active duty through DANTES. Test out of gen-ed college courses (English, math, history, sciences) at $0 per exam, credits most schools accept. Knock out a year of a degree before spending a dollar of TA or a day of GI Bill. This is step zero of the education stack and almost nobody uses it.

Credentialing Assistance (CA) & COOL Separate From TA

Each branch funds civilian certifications through its COOL program (Army CA, AF COOL, Navy COOL): exams, prep courses, books, and recertifications. Army CA pays up to $2,000/yr (under the combined $4,500 TA cap, one credential per fiscal year as of 2026, enlisted and warrants only). Think CompTIA, PMP, EMT, NATE, welding certs, CDL. Stack certifications year over year and walk out with a civilian resume already built.

Licensing Exams, Apprenticeships & OJT GI Bill Beyond Classrooms

The GI Bill reimburses licensing and certification exam fees (up to ~$2,000 per test, pro-rated entitlement charge only). Bigger: it pays your Monthly Housing Allowance during registered apprenticeships and on-the-job training. An HVAC, electrician, or plumbing apprentice collects a paycheck from the employer AND a housing check from the VA at the same time. The trades path is funded just as hard as the college path.

Tuition Assistance (TA) — While Active Duty

Up to $250/credit hour, $4,500/year while you're still serving. Use TA for undergrad classes while active, save your GI Bill for after you get out. Each branch has slightly different approval rules.

Yellow Ribbon Program

Schools voluntarily match VA contributions above the GI Bill cap. Some schools cover 100% of tuition this way, even at expensive private universities. Coverage varies wildly by school and program. Check the VA's Yellow Ribbon directory.

Student Loan Forgiveness (TPD Discharge) 100% Forgiven

100% of federal student loans discharged if you have a 100% disability rating or TDIU. VA now does automatic data-matching with Nelnet. Tax-free at the federal level, and Congress made that permanent in 2025. This is a one-time benefit, so time it strategically if you plan to take more federal loans.

Plus: Chapter 35 (DEA), MyCAA, Work-Study, PSLF

Chapter 35: up to 36 months for spouses/children of P&T (100% Permanent & Total) veterans. MyCAA: $4,000 for military spouses. Work-Study: tax-free pay while using GI Bill. PSLF: active duty counts as qualifying employment for student loan forgiveness after 120 payments.

You could get a bachelor's, a master's, a professional certification, and a coding bootcamp. You could become a doctor. An engineer. A lawyer. All paid for.

Section 02

Housing & Home Ownership

VA Home Loan Lifetime, Reusable

$0 down. No PMI. Competitive rates. Reusable. The single most powerful wealth-building tool veterans have. Funding fee (1.25%–3.3%) is waived for veterans receiving VA disability compensation. Works for purchase, refinance (streamline or cash-out), and even construction. You can use it again and again. Some veterans hold two VA loans simultaneously.

BAH (Basic Allowance for Housing) — Active Duty Tax-Free

Tax-free monthly payment based on rank, dependency status, and duty station ZIP. Average 4.2% bump in 2026. In high-cost areas: $3,000–$4,000+/month. Get married, get the "with dependents" rate. Pocket the difference between BAH and your actual housing cost. This is what people mean when they say "the military will literally make your house payments."

SAH/SHA Grants, HISA

SAH: up to ~$109,986 for wheelchair-accessible home modifications. SHA: up to ~$22,036 for less severe adaptations. HISA: up to $6,800 for structural home improvements for service-connected veterans.

Section 03

Healthcare for Life

VA Healthcare

Comprehensive medical, mental health, vision, and prescriptions. Veterans with 50%+ disability get essentially free healthcare. 2026 copays: $15 primary care, $50 specialty. Combat veterans get 10 years of enhanced eligibility.

TRICARE — Active Duty Free

100% free healthcare for you and your family while serving. TRICARE for Life kicks in for 20-year retirees once Medicare-eligible, acting as a supplement with minimal out-of-pocket.

Community Care (Mission Act)

If VA can't provide timely or geographically accessible care, they authorize treatment at civilian providers and pay for it. This is how you access specialists like fertility doctors through the VA system.

Mental Health — No Barriers

No copay for the first year after separation. Vet Centers provide free readjustment counseling in a relaxed, non-clinical environment. Crisis Line: 988, press 1.

Plus: Dental, Fertility, Caregiver Support, PACT Act

Full dental for 100% disabled veterans; everyone else enrolled in VA healthcare can buy discounted private dental through VADIP. IUI covered (IVF not). Caregiver stipend for seriously injured post-9/11 veterans. PACT Act expanded coverage for burn pit and toxic exposure.

Section 04

Disability Compensation

$3,938
Per month, tax-free, for a single veteran at 100% disability in 2026. With dependents, it's over $4,100. For life. With annual COLA increases.

VA Disability Compensation Tax-Free For Life

Monthly payments based on rating (10%–100%). 2026 rates: 10% = $180.42/mo, 100% single = $3,938.58/mo. Rates increase with dependents. 2.8% COLA increase effective December 2025.

TDIU (Total Disability Individual Unemployability) Hidden Gem

If your disabilities keep you from holding a steady job, TDIU pays you at the 100% rate even when your combined rating is lower. Most veterans have never heard of it.

Special Monthly Compensation (SMC)

Additional tax-free payments on top of disability for loss/loss of use of specific organs or extremities. 2026 rates: $4,584–$11,843/mo.

Plus: Aid & Attendance, Clothing Allowance, Auto Grant, CRDP, CRSC

Clothing allowance: $1,053/yr. Auto grant: ~$22,000 one-time. CRDP: military retirees with 50%+ disability collect BOTH full retirement AND full VA disability.

Section 05

Financial & Legal Protections

SCRA (Servicemembers Civil Relief Act) 6% Rate Cap

6% interest rate cap on ALL debts you had before you joined: credit cards, car loans, student loans, mortgages. The mortgage cap runs 1 year past your separation date. SCRA also blocks foreclosure and repossession, lets you break a lease without penalty, and protects you from default judgments. One catch: the cap isn't automatic. Send your lender a written request with your orders, no later than 180 days after you leave active duty.

Life Insurance (SGLI → VGLI)

SGLI: up to $500,000 coverage at ~$31/mo while active. Convertible to VGLI after separation with no medical exam (must act within 240 days). Also: VALife, guaranteed-acceptance whole life insurance up to $40,000 for ANY veteran with a service-connected rating (even 0%), no medical exam, no health questions.

DIC (Dependency & Indemnity Compensation)

Tax-free monthly payment to surviving spouses/dependents. 2026 base rate: $1,699.36/mo.

Veterans Pension

For wartime veterans 65+ or permanently/totally disabled, with limited income. 2026 asset limit: $163,699.

Section 06

Employment & Career

Veterans' Preference 5-10 Extra Points

5 or 10 extra points on federal civil service exams. 10-point preference for disabled veterans, Purple Heart recipients, and spouses/parents of deceased/disabled veterans. This is massive for federal hiring.

Security Clearance

The investigation costs the government $5,000–$50,000+. Having an active Secret or TS/SCI makes you immediately more employable in defense, intelligence, and government contracting. Worth $10,000–$20,000+ in salary premium.

VOSB / SDVOSB (Veteran-Owned Business)

Special procurement preferences for federal contracts. Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business gets sole-source contracts up to $5M.

Entrepreneur Track If You Want to Own, Not Work

The military actively funds veteran business ownership. Boots to Business: free SBA entrepreneurship course during your transition (and B2B Reboot for veterans already out). VBOCs: Veterans Business Outreach Centers give free one-on-one business counseling, plan reviews, and mentorship. VR&E self-employment track: for service-connected veterans, the VA can fund business startup costs, equipment, licenses, and training as your rehab plan. SBA lending: veteran-focused loan programs and fee relief on certain SBA Express loans. Stack that with SDVOSB sole-source contracts, plus federal buyers who are required to spend with veteran-owned firms. One enlistment becomes a permanent structural advantage for a business owner.

Plus: SkillBridge, Helmets to Hardhats, USAJOBS Filter, Career Counseling

SkillBridge: intern with civilian companies during last 180 days while receiving full military pay. Helmets to Hardhats: free path to construction trades. Many federal jobs are veteran-only listings.

Section 07

Travel & Recreation

Space-A Flights Free

Free flights on military aircraft for retirees and 100% P&T disabled veterans (plus accompanied dependents). Category 6 priority. CONUS and between CONUS and territories. Need flexibility. Must have Next Generation ID card (replaced DD-2765 in 2026).

MWR, Armed Forces Recreation Centers, National Parks

On-base rec facilities, ITT discount tickets (30–50% off), Shades of Green (Disney World at military rates), Edelweiss (Germany), Hale Koa (Hawaii). Free lifetime National Parks Access Pass for disabled veterans. Free annual Military Pass for all veterans.

Military Discounts — Everywhere

10–15% off at most retailers, restaurants, hotels, car rentals. USAA/Navy Federal for insurance and banking. T-Mobile/Verizon military plans. Home Depot/Lowe's 10%. Under Armour, GovX, and hundreds more.

Section 08

Shopping & Commissary

Commissary (DeCA)

On-base grocery store at cost plus 5% surcharge, and no sales tax (part of where the savings comes from). Savings of 25–30% compared to civilian stores. All veterans with service-connected disability can access.

Exchange (PX/BX/NEX/MCX)

Tax-free shopping on base. All honorably discharged veterans have online exchange privileges at ShopMyExchange.com. In-store access for veterans with service-connected disabilities.

Section 09

Burial & Memorial

Free burial in a VA national cemetery (grave site, opening/closing, headstone, perpetual care). Spouse and minor children eligible in same cemetery. Burial allowances for service-connected death: up to ~$2,000. Free government headstone or marker for any veteran with other-than-dishonorable discharge. Presidential Memorial Certificate and burial flag.

Section 10

State-Specific Benefits

States stack their own massive layer on top of federal benefits. This varies wildly, but some states make it absurdly good.

Property Tax Exemptions $5K-$15K+/yr Savings

Texas, Florida, and others provide FULL property tax exemption for 100% disabled veterans. NC excludes the first $45,000 of your home's appraised value at 100% P&T (form AV-9 + NCDVA-9). Others offer partial exemptions at various thresholds.

Income Tax, Vehicle, Hunting/Fishing, Education, Cash Bonuses

Many states exempt military retirement and VA disability from state income tax. NC: $100 lifetime sportsman license at 50%+ disability. Texas Hazlewood Act: free tuition at state schools. Some states pay cash bonuses for combat deployments. Free/reduced vehicle registration, special plates, free state parks.

Section 11

Enlistment Incentives

What the recruiter can actually put on the table. Bonuses, rank, job choice, and duty station, all negotiable before you sign.

Signing Bonuses Up to $50K

Enlistment bonuses up to $50,000 depending on MOS, contract length, and manning needs. High-demand or high-attrition fields (infantry, Special Forces, Ranger) get the biggest numbers. Must be written into your contract or it doesn't exist.

Duty Station of Choice (Army Option 19)

Pick your first assignment from a list of participating installations. Available in select MOSs. Can be combined with other bonuses. Navy similarly lets you pick your guaranteed "A School" and fleet preference.

Advanced Rank for Qualifications

You don't start at E-1 if you show up with credentials. 24 college credits = E-2. 48 credits = E-3. Bachelor's degree = E-4 (Army). JROTC: 1–2 years = E-2, 3+ years = E-3. Eagle Scout or Girl Scout Gold Award = E-2/E-3. Referrals in the Future Soldiers Program can earn E-2 or E-3 before you ship. Completing pre-training tasks (MarineNet equivalent) earns advancement too. These can stack. The difference between E-1 and E-3 is ~$400/month.

Branch Job Selection — Know the Difference
Army

Guarantees a specific MOS in your contract. You know exactly what you're doing. Also offers duty station choice.

Navy

Guarantees a specific rating (job). You pick your "A School" at MEPS.

Air Force

Gives you a wish list. You might get #1 or #8. Less control, but better quality of life and nicer bases.

Marines

Guarantees an occupational field (e.g., 26xx) but NOT a specific MOS within it. You find out your exact job during or after training.

Enlisted-to-Officer Programs

Green to Gold (Army): Stay on full pay/allowances for up to 24 months while finishing your degree and doing ROTC. Commission as a 2LT. STA-21 (Navy): Full-time college on active duty pay. MECEP (Marines): Same concept, commission through OCS. OCS/OTS (All branches): Bachelor's degree → officer. The military will literally pay you your full salary to go to college and come back as an officer making significantly more money.

Short Contracts

The Army offers contracts as short as 2 years for certain MOSs, and 3-year contracts are common. Most benefits require only 90 days of active duty service (the GI Bill scales: 90 days = 50%, 36 months = 100%). Serve a single 3-year enlistment and you carry GI Bill, VA loan, healthcare, disability eligibility, and veterans' preference for the rest of your life. Same benefits as anyone else who served, minus the retirement pension (that's the 20-year prize).

National Guard & Reserve Keep Your Business

The path nobody pitches: serve part-time and keep your civilian career or business running. One weekend a month plus two weeks a year (nominally), with drill pay on top of your regular income. The benefits layer is real. TRICARE Reserve Select costs $57.88/mo for you or $286.66/mo for the whole family (2026 rates), and the coverage embarrasses most civilian marketplace plans. Add the Montgomery GI Bill-Selected Reserve for school money, federal tuition assistance, and state tuition programs (NC pays toward state school tuition for Guard members). Deploy once on federal orders and you unlock Post-9/11 GI Bill percentages, VA home loan eligibility, and veterans' preference. If you're building a business and can't disappear for 3 years, this is the hedge: benefits, training, a pension at 20 good years, and a foot in the door if the business dies.

Section 12

Active Duty Compensation

Base Pay

2026: E-1 starts at ~$2,407/mo, increases with rank and time. 3.8% raise across the board. But base pay is just the starting point.

BAH + BAS Tax-Free

BAH: tax-free housing allowance. Getting married effectively doubles your compensation. BAS: ~$460/mo tax-free food allowance. Combined with tax-free BAH, a married E-5's actual compensation is $15,000–$25,000/yr higher than base pay suggests.

Special & Incentive Pays

Flight pay, dive pay, hazardous duty, hostile fire ($225/mo), foreign language proficiency ($100–$500/mo per language), reenlistment bonuses (up to $50,000+). In a combat zone, ALL pay is tax-free for enlisted.

Dependent Benefits While Active

Your spouse and kids get a military ID unlocking: free TRICARE healthcare, commissary and exchange privileges, MWR facilities (gyms, pools, theaters), subsidized childcare, free JAG legal services, DoDEA schools overseas. Military spouses can use MyCAA ($4,000). PCS moves at government expense.

Plus: 30 Days Paid Leave, Free Gym, Free Legal, Free Medical/Dental, TSP

30 days leave/year (accrued 2.5/month). TSP retirement savings with government matching up to 5% and the lowest expense ratios in the industry (0.04%). Free fitness centers, pools, and sports facilities.

Section 15

Disability: The Real Talk

VA disability compensation is NOT the same as SSDI. It's not about being "disabled" the way civilians think of it. It's a legal recognition that your body and mind took damage during service, and the government compensates you for it.

It happens naturally. You don't go in planning to get hurt. Military service is physically demanding. After 4–6 years of running, rucking, carrying heavy things, being exposed to loud noises, breathing in weird stuff, and dealing with extreme stress, most veterans have legitimate, documentable conditions: tinnitus, knee/back/shoulder issues, sleep problems, anxiety, skin conditions, respiratory issues.

It's a legal puzzle, not a scam. The VA rates disabilities on a combined percentage scale. Each condition gets its own rating, combined using "VA math" (not straight addition). The key is documentation: in-service medical records, buddy statements, nexus letters. Everyone who served honorably and has health issues that started or worsened during service should file. Period.

$4.7M
Estimated lifetime value of 100% P&T with COLA averaging 2.5%/year over a 50-year remaining lifespan. Tax-free.

P&T unlocks everything else. 100% Permanent & Total is the keystone: CHAMPVA for spouse, Chapter 35 education for dependents, state property tax exemptions, Space-A travel, commissary access, student loan forgiveness, no VA loan funding fee. The cascading benefits are enormous.

You can work with VA disability. Unlike SSDI, standard VA disability has NO income restriction. Earn $500K/year and still collect your full check. The only exception is TDIU, which has employment limitations because it's specifically for veterans whose disabilities prevent working.

File before you separate. Start a BDD claim (Benefits Delivery at Discharge) 180–90 days before your ETS date, your separation date. Records are fresh and accessible, and a rating decision can be waiting for you when you get out.

Section 16

Retirement: The 20-Year Prize

Serve 20 years and you receive a monthly pension FOR LIFE, starting immediately upon retirement. No waiting until 65. Retire at 38, start collecting at 38.

The Pension For Life

Under BRS: 2.0% × years of service × High-3 average base pay. A 20-year E-7 retiree with a High-3 of ~$5,000/mo gets 40% = ~$2,000/mo for life. An O-5 with High-3 of ~$10,500 gets ~$4,200/mo. At 30 years it jumps to 60%. Annual COLA increases. Under the legacy High-3 system (pre-2018), the multiplier is 2.5% per year, so 20 years = 50%.

TSP (Thrift Savings Plan)

Government contributes 1% automatically and matches up to 4% more (5% total). Lowest expense ratios in the industry (0.04%). A disciplined service member contributing 5% from day one with government match can retire with $500K–$1M+ in their TSP after 20 years. Even if you only do 4 years, your TSP balance keeps growing.

Retiree Benefits — The Lifestyle Package

Full commissary and exchange privileges (in-store). Base access for MWR and recreation worldwide. Space-A travel. TRICARE (transitioning to TRICARE for Life at 65). Legal assistance. Your dependents retain ID cards and access to all of these.

The Real Retirement Math

An E-7, 20-year retiree under BRS: ~$2,000/mo pension + $600K TSP + TRICARE healthcare + commissary savings + 70% VA disability (~$1,800/mo tax-free). Annual income floor of ~$45,600 in pension + disability alone. Healthcare covered for life. $600K+ nest egg growing tax-advantaged. They're 38 years old. Most civilians at 38 are still 27 years from seeing their 401k. This is why people re-enlist.

Section 17

The Human Element

The people are genuinely interesting. Military service throws you into close quarters with people from every state, every background, every walk of life. Farm kids from Iowa, street-smart kids from the Bronx, college dropouts, Eagle Scouts, first-generation Americans, fourth-generation military brats. The diversity of life experience in a single platoon is something most civilian workplaces can't match. Some of these people will become your closest friends for the rest of your life.

You'll do things most people never will. Jump out of planes, fire crew-served weapons, navigate by stars, stand watch on a destroyer at 3 AM in the Pacific, learn a language at DLI, work on nuclear reactors, fly helicopters, build bridges in foreign countries.

Travel and see the world. Army and Navy both station you overseas: Germany, Italy, Japan, Korea, Hawaii, Guam. Navy puts you on ships hitting ports across the Pacific and Mediterranean. Even stateside, you PCS every 2–4 years to different parts of the country, all expenses paid. Many service members rack up 10+ countries visited before 25.

The trade-off is real. You give up personal autonomy. You go where they tell you. You might deploy to places you'd rather not go. Current geopolitics being what they are, you're rolling the dice. The benefits are extraordinary precisely because the ask is extraordinary.

The Draft Insurance Argument

If a large-scale conflict kicks off and the draft comes back, you're going regardless. Draftees historically get slotted into whatever the military needs most, which is almost always infantry. But say you're already serving in a specialized MOS: cryptologic linguist, avionics tech, cyber operations, intel analyst, nuclear reactor operator. The military spent serious time and money training you for that seat. They're not pulling a trained Farsi linguist out of an intelligence shop to clear buildings.

Enlisting voluntarily on your own terms — picking your branch, picking your job, getting your training — is an insurance policy against being conscripted involuntarily into the worst possible assignment. Selective Service registration is still mandatory for all males 18–25. The infrastructure exists.

Section 18

What Most People Get Wrong

The education stacking order matters

Test out of gen-eds with free CLEP/DSST exams → use TA and CA certs while active → use VR&E first (converts prior GI Bill usage back into VR&E months) → use your restored GI Bill entitlement → apply for STEM extension → finish with VET TEC, free once your GI Bill is fully exhausted. Done in the right sequence: 6+ years of funded education, more with VR&E extensions.

BAH + BAS is the hidden salary

A married E-5 gets $1,500–$2,500/mo tax-free BAH plus $460/mo BAS. Actual compensation is $15,000–$25,000/yr higher than base pay suggests. This is why getting married in the military is such a financial game-changer.

File for disability BEFORE you separate

Start a BDD claim 180–90 days before your separation date. Records are fresh and accessible, and a rating decision can be waiting when you get out.

The VA loan funding fee waiver

Saves $3,000–$15,000+ on a typical home purchase if you have any VA disability rating. On a $400K home, that's real money.

State benefits are the hidden layer

Some states stack incredibly well. Property tax exemptions, income tax exemptions, free hunting/fishing licenses, free tuition at state schools for dependents. Nobody advertises these. Research your state's veteran benefits office.

Most veterans don't claim everything they're entitled to

File for disability. Use your education benefits. Apply for property tax exemptions. Get your hunting/fishing licenses. Check CHAMPVA for your spouse. Apply for student loan forgiveness. Every one requires paperwork, and nobody does it for you.

100% P&T is the financial singularity

At 100% P&T with a spouse in 2026: ~$49K/yr tax-free disability + CHAMPVA + no VA loan funding fee + student loan forgiveness + state property tax exemptions + Space-A travel + commissary access + dependent education benefits. Total package value easily exceeds $80K–$100K/yr in equivalent pre-tax civilian income, for life, with COLA increases.
