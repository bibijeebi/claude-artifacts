Proposal #012

2017 Accord Head Unit Replacement

The factory Honda dual-screen is buggy, gets stuck in phantom call states, and locks audio behind a Bluetooth stack that throws fits. Three paths to fix it, from a $100 dongle to a full Tesla-style Android dash.

Total Cost $90–$800
Difficulty 1–3/5
Time 5 min – 1 afternoon
Category Automotive
Status Proposed

The Problem

The 2017 Accord runs Honda's 9th-gen Display Audio system: a dual-screen setup with an 8" upper info display (clock, climate, backup cam overlay, ANC visualizer) hardwired to a 7" lower touchscreen. It's slow, the Bluetooth stack is famously buggy, and it routinely wedges itself into a phantom "call active" state where the audio gets muted because it thinks a call is still ongoing. Hard reboots only sometimes clear it. This is not a phone problem. It's a Honda problem documented across every model year of this gen.

The car already has an aftermarket Kicker sub and amp installed in the trunk (Proposal #008). The factory head unit is now the weak link in the audio chain. The sub deserves a better source.

Bonus annoyance: no wireless CarPlay or Android Auto. Honda didn't add wireless versions until well into the 10th gen. Even if your trim has wired CarPlay, you're hunting for a cable every time you get in the car.

The 2017 Accord Install Gotcha

Crutchfield, the bible of car audio fitment, flatly refuses to sell a head unit kit for this car. The dual-screen setup is not a standard double-DIN install. The upper iMid screen is fed by the lower head unit, so you can't just yank the bottom one and drop in a Pioneer or Kenwood. Two viable options exist:

Option A: Replace just the lower 7" touchscreen with a vehicle-specific Android unit. Keeps the upper iMid intact for climate display and backup cam overlay. Lower risk, slightly less impressive look.

Option B: Replace both screens with one giant 12.3" Tesla-style Android tablet that takes over the entire dash. More dramatic, more features, slightly more install effort.

Both options use Chinese OEM-fit brands (Dasaita, Belsee, Eonon, Joying) because nobody else makes hardware for this dash. The quality has improved dramatically since the 2008 Optima days. Snapdragon chips, 8GB RAM, Android 13/14, wireless CarPlay + Android Auto standard, built-in DSP for tuning the sub.

Trim check before ordering: The 2016-2017 Touring trim has touch-capacitive climate buttons and the premium audio system. Most vehicle-specific units explicitly do NOT fit Touring. Sport, EX, EX-L, and LX trims all work. If unsure, send a photo of the dash to the seller before ordering.

The Plan (3 Paths)

Path 1: Wireless CarPlay Dongle $90–$150

The lazy genius play. A USB dongle plugs into the existing factory CarPlay port and converts wired CarPlay to wireless. You never use the buggy Honda Bluetooth stack again because CarPlay handles calls, audio, and everything else through its own protocol. Five-minute install. Works around the muting bug entirely.

Requires factory wired CarPlay on the head unit. The 2017 Accord has CarPlay standard on Sport, EX, EX-L, and Touring trims. Base LX does not have CarPlay and the dongle will not work. If you have CarPlay (USB port labeled with the icon, or CarPlay shows up when you plug in an iPhone with the right cable), this is the play.

Top picks for the dongle: CarlinKit 5.0 (2air) is the most reliable per multi-month bake-off tests, ~$90 on Amazon. Ottocast U2-X Pro is slightly faster and has a button for switching devices but less stable on auto-connect, ~$120. Both do iOS and Android Auto in the same unit.

What this does NOT solve: The buggy factory screen is still the buggy factory screen. The phantom call mute bug can theoretically still happen if you make a regular Bluetooth call without CarPlay active. If you commit to running CarPlay every drive, the bug is functionally gone. If you sometimes use plain Bluetooth, you're still exposed.

Path 2: Lower Screen Replacement (Dasaita 10.2") $528–$728

Rip out the lower 7" touchscreen, drop in a Dasaita 10.2" QLED Android 13 unit. Keeps the upper iMid functional for climate readout and backup cam overlay. Plug-and-play harness, CAN-bus decoder included, retains steering wheel controls, factory amp, backup camera, and LaneWatch right-side cam.

Specs at the $728 tier: Qualcomm Snapdragon 665 octa-core, 8GB RAM, 256GB storage, 4G LTE SIM slot, wireless CarPlay + Android Auto, TDA7808 amp chip, optical audio output for tuning your Kicker sub through DSP, full Google Play Store (Spotify, YouTube, Waze, anything).

Best fit for: keeping the dual-screen aesthetic, lower install risk, wanting the upper screen's existing climate/backup cam display to keep working exactly as it does today.

Available direct from Dasaita.com or Amazon (same product, sometimes cheaper). Look for SKU referencing "2013-2017 Accord LHD" with the 10.2" QLED. Confirm dual-screen variant compatibility when ordering since some configurations specifically target 2013-2015 dual-screen and 2013-2017 single-screen.

Path 3: Tesla-Style Full Dash Takeover (Belsee 12.3") $500–$800

The dramatic option. A single 12.3" QLED touchscreen replaces both factory screens, taking over the entire dash from glove box to driver instrument cluster. Looks like a Tesla. Runs Android 14 on an octa-core MT8667 with up to 8GB RAM and 128GB storage. Wireless CarPlay + Android Auto, 4G LTE, DAB+, OBD2, optional 360 camera support.

Best fit for: maximum visual impact, willing to commit to one big screen instead of the dual-screen look, want the biggest possible canvas for navigation and split-screen apps.

Belsee is the most established vendor for this specific style on this car. Buy direct from belsee.com with the 8GB+128GB tier ($600-700). The extra RAM tier is worth the $50 upcharge since the 4GB base tier slows down after a year of installed apps.

What's preserved in Paths 2 and 3: Steering wheel controls (volume, source, calls), factory amplifier signal, backup camera, LaneWatch right-side camera, USB charging ports, AUX-in, antenna. The CAN-bus decoder box is included with the head unit and handles all of that automatically.

Installation (Paths 2 and 3)

This is a DIY job. No tools beyond a $10 plastic pry kit. The Accord's center stack pops off cleanly with no screws hidden behind anything weird. YouTube has installation videos for both Dasaita and Belsee units on the 9th-gen Accord. Watch one before you start.

Step 1 Disconnect the negative battery terminal. 10mm wrench. Wait 5 minutes for capacitors to drain. Mandatory to prevent airbag light triggers and electrical surprises.
Step 2 Pry off the surround trim around the gear shifter and center stack with the plastic pry tool. Goes from the bottom up. Listen for the clip pop. No screws.
Step 3 Four 8mm bolts hold the factory head unit assembly in. Remove them. Pull the unit straight out toward the rear of the car. Disconnect all harnesses on the back.
Step 4 Connect the new unit's plug-and-play harness to the factory harnesses. Everything is keyed and color-coded. Connect the CAN-bus decoder box inline if not already integrated.
Step 5 Test BEFORE re-mounting. Reconnect battery, start the car, verify: screen powers on, audio plays through factory speakers, steering wheel buttons work, backup cam triggers when in reverse, AC info displays (if Path 2). If something fails, disconnect and check connections.
Step 6 Disconnect battery again, mount the new unit with the included bracket, reinstall the trim. Reconnect battery, set up wireless CarPlay/Android Auto pairing, install your apps, configure DSP for the Kicker sub.
The "bypass" question: No anti-theft radio code on this car (Honda dropped that requirement years before 2017). The "parking brake bypass" you may have seen referenced only matters if you want to watch video on the screen while the car is moving. Most Chinese Android units ship with this disabled by default or have a settings toggle to enable it.

How the Market Changed Since the 2008 Optima Days

Significantly. The shitty MediaTek chips of 2015-2018 are gone. Modern Chinese Android head units run Qualcomm Snapdragon 665 or comparable octa-cores at 11nm. 8GB RAM is the new baseline (used to be 1GB). Boot times are 10 seconds instead of 45. Wireless CarPlay and wireless Android Auto are default on every unit over $300, not aftermarket dongle add-ons. Built-in 4G LTE slots take any SIM and turn the head unit into its own hotspot. DSP chips with optical output are standard, letting you actually tune your aftermarket sub from the head unit instead of needing an external processor.

Firmware quality varies. Dasaita and Belsee are the two most reputable Chinese OEM-fit brands. Atoto and Joying are solid in the universal-fit space but rarely make this specific Accord kit. Hieha and the cheapest no-name Amazon brands ($150-250 range) still ship buggy firmware and should be avoided. The sweet spot is the $500-700 tier from a known brand.

What hasn't changed: even the best Chinese unit is still not as polished as a Pioneer DMH or Alpine iLX. But no Pioneer or Alpine fits this car. The choice is "buggy Honda factory" or "Chinese Android," and Chinese Android has gotten genuinely good.

Recommendation

Trim confirmed: 2017 Accord EX Sensing. This has factory wired CarPlay (USB icon, iPhone plug-in works). Path 1 is on the table and is the recommended starting move.

Start with Path 1. The $90 CarlinKit 5.0 is the highest expected value move on the board. The dongle bypasses the buggy Bluetooth stack entirely, gives wireless CarPlay, and ships in two days. Five-minute install. If it solves the problem, total spend is $90 and the project is done. If it doesn't (Bluetooth-only audio still gets used, or the factory screen itself keeps acting up), the dongle is still useful as a CarPlay solution and only $90 was spent.

If Path 1 isn't enough, go Path 3. The Belsee Tesla-style 12.3" is the most dramatic upgrade and pairs well with the existing Kicker sub via DSP. The dual-screen factory look is not worth preserving when the alternative is a unified 12.3" Android computer. Total $610-710 all-in including tools.

Skip Path 2 unless the upper iMid display features (specifically the ANC visualizer or some climate readout you actually use) matter to you. Otherwise Path 3 obsoletes it.

Related project: The front windshield camera on this car is uncalibrated after a prior windshield replacement and Honda Sensing ADAS is dead. See Proposal #013 (openpilot port) for the much bigger play of replacing the broken Honda camera with a comma device and porting openpilot to this car directly.
Combined play: Nothing prevents running a CarPlay dongle on Path 2 or Path 3 either. The wireless CarPlay built into the Android units works fine, but some people prefer the CarlinKit's faster connect time. Optional layer if you want it.

Things to Know Before Ordering

Confirm trim compatibility. Send a dash photo to the seller. Touring trim has touch-capacitive climate buttons that don't play nice with most kits. Sport, EX, EX-L, LX are all standard.

Buy direct from the manufacturer's site when possible. Dasaita.com and belsee.com both ship from US warehouses with a 1-year warranty. Amazon listings are often the same hardware at a similar price, but warranty support is harder to chase down.

Pay the RAM upcharge. 4GB tier units chug after a year of installed apps. 8GB is the right tier. The extra $30-50 is the cheapest performance upgrade on the build.

Ask about firmware update support. Reputable vendors push OTA updates that fix bugs and add features for 2-3 years. Confirm this is included.

Software ads are a documented issue on some Chinese units. One Dasaita reviewer reported ad insertion appearing months after install. Worth asking about explicitly.

Decision Point

This is a "can I live with the bug long enough to wait" question. Path 1 is a $90 Amazon order that ships in two days and solves 80% of the pain immediately. Path 3 is a Saturday afternoon project and the dash never feels like a 2017 Accord again. The current Honda head unit is not getting better. Software updates for the 9th-gen Display Audio system stopped years ago. The bug is baked in.

The Kicker sub in the trunk deserves better source equipment than the factory unit can provide, especially because the factory unit aggressively processes audio in ways that fight aftermarket speakers and subs (high-end boost to compensate for missing tweeters, mid-bass roll-off). Path 3's DSP gives full EQ control over the signal going to the sub.
