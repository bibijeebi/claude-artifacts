Ceramic Soap Dish Replacement

Surface-mounted tile installation using construction adhesive and silicone seal. ~45 minutes of work across two days.

Orientation Matters

The loop/curved lip faces DOWN. This is the drainage edge—water drains off soap into the tub instead of pooling. The ridged sides are grip texture and go vertical.

Tools & Materials

Tools

Heat Gun
Softens old adhesive
Oscillating Multitool + Spatula Blade
Primary removal tool—cuts behind dish
Metal Putty Knife
Scrapes residual adhesive
Caulk Gun
Dispenses adhesive

Materials

Loctite PL Premium
Structural bond—holds dish to wall
GE Silicone #2
Waterproof seal—applied after cure
Rubbing Alcohol
Surface prep
Painter's Tape
Clean silicone lines
Why two products? PL Premium creates permanent structural bond but isn't waterproof at seams. Silicone is flexible and waterproof but has zero structural strength. You need both: adhesive for strength, silicone for water protection.
Phase 1

Removal

1

Heat the Old Dish

Apply heat gun on medium setting, moving in slow circles for 2-3 minutes. Goal is softening the adhesive behind it, not melting tile.

2

Cut Behind the Dish

Insert oscillating tool spatula blade at edge where dish meets tile. Work it behind the dish slowly. Start at top, work down sides, then across bottom. Always cut perimeter before prying.

3

Release and Remove

Once perimeter is cut, pry gently with putty knife from one corner. If it resists, apply more heat and cut further. If old dish cracks—fine, it's garbage anyway.

4

Clean the Surface

Scrape all old adhesive off tile. Heat softens stubborn spots. Get it flat. Wipe with rubbing alcohol, let dry 5 minutes.

Phase 2

Installation

5

Dry Fit and Mark Position

Hold new dish in position (loop DOWN), check level. Mark placement with painter's tape at corners.

6

Apply Construction Adhesive

Apply Loctite PL Premium in S-pattern on back of dish, covering ~60% of surface. Stay ½" from edges to prevent squeeze-out.

7

Press Into Position

Align with tape marks, press firmly. Wiggle slightly (¼") to spread adhesive, hold 30 seconds.

8

Secure While Curing

Hold dish in place for 24 hours. Options:

Best: Board Wedge
Wedge 2x4 from opposite tub wall to dish face. Zero tape, foolproof.
Alt: Tape Tension
6-8 strips of tape from dish to tile, pulled taut in star pattern.
9

Wait 24 Hours

Don't touch it. Don't test it. Don't shower. Walk away.

Phase 3

Sealing

10

Remove Support

After 24 hours, remove wedge/tape. Dish should be solid—light finger pressure shouldn't move it.

11

Apply Silicone Seal

For professional-looking clean lines:

  1. Run painter's tape along dish edge (on dish, 1/16" from edge)
  2. Run painter's tape along tile, leaving consistent gap
  3. Apply silicone into gap in continuous bead
  4. Smooth with wet finger in one stroke
  5. Immediately pull both tape lines while wet
  6. Don't touch it again
12

Final Cure

Wait 24 hours before water exposure. Full load (heavy soap bar) after 7 days. Done permanently.

Common Fuckups to Avoid

Prying before cutting behind dish
Cracks surrounding tile. Always cut perimeter first with oscillating tool.
Using only silicone for adhesion
Dish slides down wall over days/weeks. Use PL Premium for structure.
Not cleaning old adhesive residue
Weak bond, eventual failure. Scrape clean, wipe with alcohol.
Skipping support while curing
Dish sags or falls before adhesive sets. Wedge/tape for 24 hours.
Pulling tape after silicone skins
Torn, ugly sealant line. Pull tape immediately while wet.

Expected Outcome

Done correctly, this repair is indistinguishable from original installation. The dish will be permanently bonded, waterproof at the perimeter, and will last decades. The silicone seal may need refreshing in 10-15 years (just scrape and reapply)—the structural bond is permanent.
