Prevention · Recovery · Pipeline

Hair loss, the whole map — built for a woman.

Every real option, graded by evidence and ruthlessly deduped. Female pattern loss is the lens, but the male playbook and the experimental frontier are all here. Diagnose before you spend a dollar.

RESEARCH SYNTHESIS · JAAD / PMC / AAD-tier sources · pipeline status as of JUN 2026 · not medical advice

For pattern thinning in a woman, the highest-yield DIY-leaning stack is low-dose oral minoxidil (1–2.5 mg/day) or topical minoxidil 5%, plus an oral anti-androgen (spironolactone 100–200 mg/day), plus weekly at-home microneedling (0.5–1.0 mm). That combo is the female workhorse.

Non-negotiable first move: a lab panel and an honest diagnosis. Traction alopecia, telogen effluvium, and scarring alopecias look similar and are treated completely differently. Scarring types destroy follicles permanently, so a wrong call costs hair you can't get back.

The pipeline is the hottest in 30 years (clascoterone, PP405, GT20029) but nothing new is FDA-approved yet, hair "cloning" is years out, and Eli Lilly has no pattern-baldness drug — its hair programs are all autoimmune alopecia areata.

⚠ Read this first — you two are planning kids

Finasteride, dutasteride, spironolactone, and bicalutamide are teratogens. They can disrupt development of a male fetus (pregnancy category X / feminization risk). For a reproductive-age woman who could become pregnant, these require reliable contraception, and are best avoided entirely if pregnancy is possible or planned.

The pregnancy-safe levers are topical/oral minoxidil, microneedling, LLLT, PRP, ketoconazole shampoo, and fixing nutrient deficiencies — though even minoxidil is typically paused in pregnancy out of caution. Given the reversal timeline, this matters: anti-androgens are a "not while trying / not while pregnant" tool.

01

The regimen

Staged, so you only escalate if the cheap proven stuff underdelivers. Every medical hair treatment needs 3–6 months to show change and 12 months for full effect. Early shedding is normal — don't quit at week 4.

0
Diagnose before you spend · weeks 0–4
  • Lab panel: ferritin, iron/TIBC, TSH + free T4, vitamin D, CBC, zinc, B12. Add testosterone / DHEA-S / prolactin if there are androgen-excess signs (acne, cycle changes, hirsutism).
  • Stop all traction now — loosen ponytails/buns, pause extensions and tight styles. This alone reverses early traction loss.
  • Any scalp redness, scaling, itch/pain, pustules, or smooth shiny hairline → see a dermatologist fast to rule out scarring alopecia (FFA/LPP). Time-sensitive.
  • Photograph the part line + crown in the same light for tracking.
1
Foundation stack for female pattern loss · months 0–12
  • Minoxidil — topical 5% once–twice daily, OR low-dose oral minoxidil 1–1.25 mg/day if topical adherence/irritation is a problem (needs a prescriber + BP awareness). Oral 1 mg ≈ topical 5% in head-to-head data.
  • Spironolactone 50 mg/day titrated to 100–200 mg — the highest-yield female add-on. Monitor potassium + renal function + BP. Contraception required.
  • Microneedling 0.5–1.0 mm once weekly; apply topical ~24 h later; strict hygiene. Roughly tripled the response vs minoxidil alone in trials.
  • Fix deficiencies: treat low ferritin/iron, vitamin D, thyroid. Skip biotin unless truly deficient — it's mostly marketing and it screws up lab assays.
  • Optional low-effort adjuncts: ketoconazole (Nizoral) shampoo 2–3×/week; an LLLT red-light cap if budget allows.
2
If response is weak at 6–12 months
  • Consider topical or oral finasteride/dutasteride (postmenopausal or strictly-contracepting only — teratogen) or bicalutamide 25–50 mg/day with liver monitoring. Note: a 2025 RCT showed bicalutamide added nothing over minoxidil alone, so the combo evidence genuinely conflicts.
  • Add PRP — 3–4 sessions ~1 month apart, then maintenance. Adjunct, not a cure. ~$1k/session, not covered by insurance.
  • Escalate to a hair-loss-specialist dermatologist for trichoscopy ± biopsy to confirm nothing scarring was missed.
3
Camouflage & surgery
  • Toppers / wigs / scalp micropigmentation — immediate, risk-free coverage at any stage while meds take effect.
  • Transplant only after meds stabilize loss AND a surgeon confirms a stable donor zone. Most women with diffuse thinning aren't candidates; localized loss (traction, hairline, scar) is the better indication.
Trying-to-conceive note

Because the reversal is aimed at having kids, the anti-androgen half of the stack (spiro, fin, dut, bicalutamide) is an "only while not trying and not pregnant" tool. The pregnancy-window plan leans on minoxidil + microneedling + LLLT + nutrient correction, with the anti-androgen reserved for between-pregnancy windows.

02

Diagnose first

Female hair loss isn't one disease. These four look alike and diverge hard on treatment and reversibility. Getting the type right is the whole game.

Pattern / androgenetic (FPHL)

Diffuse crown thinning

Gradual widening part ("Christmas tree"), frontal hairline preserved. Follicles miniaturize but survive. The target of the regimen above.

PREVALENCE ~32% OVERALL · 8% AT 20s → 68% BY 60–75
Telogen effluvium

Diffuse shedding

Dramatic shed 2–3 months after a trigger: childbirth, illness, fever, surgery, crash diet, COVID, new meds, stress. Follicles intact; usually self-limited by ~6 months.

FIX THE TRIGGER · IRON · DON'T PANIC-MEDICATE
Traction alopecia

Mechanical, at the edges

Hairline/temple loss from tension — tight ponytails, braids, weaves, buns. "Fringe sign" early. Reversible if caught early, permanent if chronic.

STOP TENSION → REGROWTH IN 3–6 MO IF EARLY
Scarring (FFA / LPP)

Permanent — urgent

Smooth shiny hairline, no follicle openings, often itch/pain/eyebrow loss. Destroys follicles permanently. Needs a dermatologist + biopsy ASAP to halt progression.

RISING IN POSTMENOPAUSAL WOMEN · ACT FAST

The lab panel a woman should get

  • Ferritin + iron/TIBC — the key iron markers for hair, not hemoglobin alone. Low ferritin is cheap and frequently helpful to correct. A normal value doesn't fully exclude deficiency.
  • TSH + free T4 — both hypo- and hyperthyroidism shed hair. Postpartum: add TPO antibodies.
  • Vitamin D 25-OH, zinc, B12 — common correctable deficiencies.
  • Testosterone / DHEA-S / prolactin — only if androgen-excess signs (PCOS picture, hirsutism, acne, cycle irregularity).

04

Procedures (non-surgical)

Adjuncts, not cures. They work best layered onto the drug stack — and microneedling is the one with the best DIY cost-to-evidence ratio.

Best DIY value

Microneedling

Micro-injury triggers growth factors + opens microchannels that boost minoxidil delivery ~3×. Combo beat minoxidil alone in multiple meta-analyses (+91 vs +22 hairs/cm² in the classic trial).

0.5–1.0 mm · WEEKLY · TOPICAL ~24 H LATER · CLEAN
In-clinic adjunct

PRP injections

Your own concentrated platelets injected into the scalp. 2024 meta-analysis (628 women) shows real density gains; protocols aren't standardized.

3–4 SESSIONS THEN MAINTENANCE · ~$1K EACH · NO INS.
Low-effort home

LLLT red-light

650–670 nm caps/combs, FDA-cleared, modest hair-count gains in trials. Very low side-effect profile. Fine passive add-on, not standalone.

CAP > COMB · CONSISTENCY MATTERS · MONTHS TO JUDGE
Treat as experimental

Exosomes / mesotherapy

Stem-cell-derived growth-factor signals (often with microneedling). Early promise but not FDA-approved, unstandardized, hype-prone. Mesotherapy evidence is thin.

WAIT FOR DATA · DON'T OVERPAY
05

Surgery — and why it's hard for women

The core problem: transplants move DHT-resistant donor hair to thinning zones. Men have a stable occipital donor rim. Women usually don't.

The candidacy catch

Female pattern loss is diffuse — it often includes the donor area itself, so transplanted follicles may thin over time and density may be inadequate. That's the opposite of the stable donor zone that makes male transplants succeed. Good female candidates have localized loss: traction alopecia with viable follicles, hairline lowering, or post-surgical scars — and only after TE/traction/scarring are ruled out or stabilized.

06

Causes & accelerators

What hastens or triggers loss — and the one that's pure mechanics and 100% in your control.

Traction alopecia — the controllable one

From chronic tension: tight ponytails, buns, braids, cornrows, weaves, extensions, plus heat/chemical styling and long heavy hair. Early "fringe sign" (fine hairs retained at the frontal edge) is the tell. Reversible if caught early — stop the tension and fine regrowth follows in 3–6 months, helped by topical minoxidil and short-course topical/intralesional steroids. Goes permanent (scarring) if chronic, after which only transplant or camouflage helps. Prevention is loose low styles, rotating hairstyles, limiting extensions, and heeding early itch/redness.

Everything else that sheds hair

Hormonal

Shifts & cycles

PCOS (hyperandrogenism), thyroid disease, menopause, postpartum estrogen drop, and starting/stopping birth control — especially discontinuing or switching to androgenic progestins.

Nutritional

Deficiencies

Iron/ferritin (most important in menstruating women), vitamin D, zinc, protein. Biotin only helps the genuinely deficient and fouls lab tests.

Telogen triggers

Shocks to the system

Childbirth, high fever, major surgery, COVID, crash diets/rapid weight loss, severe stress, new meds — shed shows up 2–3 months later.

Medications

Common culprits

Anticoagulants, beta-blockers, retinoids/high-dose vitamin A, some antidepressants & anticonvulsants, chemo (anagen effluvium).

Styling damage

Breakage → traction

Heat tools, chemical relaxers, bleach, and chronically tight styles cause breakage and can convert into true traction alopecia.

Scalp / autoimmune

FFA on the rise

Seborrheic dermatitis (treatable), lichen planopilaris, and frontal fibrosing alopecia — a scarring type mostly in postmenopausal women (mean onset ~59), rising sharply in recent decades.

08

The Eli Lilly question

You asked specifically about Lilly's "upcoming hair treatment." Straight answer:

⌁ Lilly has NO pattern-baldness drug

Lilly's genuine hair programs are both in autoimmune alopecia areata, not pattern loss: (1) the already-approved baricitinib (Olumiant), with strong new pediatric data (BRAVE-AA-PEDS, Oct 2025 — 71% of severe adolescents on 4 mg got successful scalp regrowth) and a planned label expansion; and (2) a Phase 2 IV immunology candidate, LY4005130, for severe AA.

Two myths to kill: Lilly's ~$630M Feb 2025 OliX deal was for a liver/MASH drug, not hair. And on June 24, 2026, Absci priced a $100M equity offering "with participation from Eli Lilly" — that's the ABS-201 pattern-hair antibody, but it was a group financing and Lilly holds no development rights to it.

So: any "Lilly baldness pill" expectation is unfounded as of 2026. The genuinely advanced new-mechanism pattern-loss drugs to watch are clascoterone, PP405, and GT20029 — none of them Lilly's.

09

Reality checks

What's proven vs hyped, and the expectations that keep people from quitting too early.

  • Proven / approved: topical minoxidil for FPHL. (JAK inhibitors — baricitinib, ritlecitinib, deuruxolitinib — only for autoimmune alopecia areata, NOT pattern loss.)
  • Off-label, good-to-strong: LDOM, spironolactone, microneedling+minoxidil, fin/dut (strong in men, variable in women), bicalutamide (positive retrospective but a 2025 RCT showed no add-on benefit — genuinely conflicting).
  • Adjunctive, modest: PRP, LLLT, ketoconazole.
  • Hype-prone: exosomes, mesotherapy, stem-cell "cloning" clinics, most supplements beyond fixing real deficiencies. Biotin for non-deficient people is marketing.
  • Read the pipeline numbers carefully: clascoterone's "539%" is a relative increase off a low base, not 539% more hair. All Phase 3 readouts above are company press releases pending peer review.
  • Universal timeline: 3–6 months minimum to see change, 12 months for full effect; initial shedding is normal; everything is suppressive, not curative — stop, and gains reverse.
