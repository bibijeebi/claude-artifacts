Exhibit A

The Weave

The whole complaint in one picture. On a cloverleaf, the ramp that feeds traffic onto the highway and the ramp that takes it off share the same short stretch of pavement. Yellow wants on, white wants off, and they have to swap lanes through each other in a few hundred feet.

Trucks take the loop at 25 mph because the loop is tiny and physics is undefeated. Then they get that little red zone to climb across your lane while you try to leave. Nobody in the picture is driving wrong. The pavement is wrong. Engineers call it a weaving section, and they have spent 70 years inventing ways to never build one again. Those inventions are below.

The Cloverleaf

b. 1929, Woodbridge NJ · the defendant
WEAVE PAIN ▮▮▮▮▮ TRUCK SPEED ▮▯▯▯▯ STRESS ▮▮▮▮▯

Born in 1929, when 25 mph was a thrilling speed and the loops seemed clever: nobody ever crosses oncoming traffic, no signals needed. Genius, for 1929. The catch is baked in. Every loop dumps entering traffic exactly where the next loop pulls exiting traffic off. Four red zones, four little knife fights, every single pass.

One mercy exists: some cloverleafs got retrofitted with collector-distributor lanes, a parallel side road that takes the loop traffic and the weave off the mainline. If the cloverleaf you are on has a skinny road running alongside handling the ramps, someone tried to apologize.

The Parclo

partial cloverleaf · Canada's favorite child
WEAVE PAIN ▮▯▯▯▯ TRUCK SPEED ▮▮▯▯▯ STRESS ▮▮▯▯▯

A cloverleaf that went to therapy. Keep two loops, delete the two that caused the weaving, cover the missing moves with normal ramps and a traffic light on the smaller road. The freeway itself stays clean. Ontario builds these by the hundred, Highway 401 through Toronto is basically a parclo museum, and NCDOT sprinkles them wherever a freeway meets a big surface street.

The Diverging Diamond

DDI · briefly, legally, on the wrong side
WEAVE PAIN ▯▯▯▯▯ TRUCK SPEED ▮▮▮▯▯ STRESS ▮▮▯▯▯

The road over the bridge briefly swaps sides, so every left turn onto the freeway happens without ever crossing oncoming traffic. No loops, no weave, half the signal phases. Feels illegal the first time, then it feels obvious.

And here is the thing: you already survived it. Exit 312 at NC 42 got converted to a diverging diamond in 2024, and Jones Sausage Road at Exit 303 got one in 2023. Every time the crossover happened and nothing bad occurred, that was the design working.

The Turbine

the whirlpool · a budget masterpiece
WEAVE PAIN ▯▯▯▯▯ TRUCK SPEED ▮▮▮▮▯ STRESS ▮▯▯▯▯

Freeway meets freeway, and instead of tight loops, four sweeping ramps spiral around the center like a pinwheel. Nobody weaves, nobody drops to 25, everybody keeps rolling. It is the stack's cheaper younger sibling and it still embarrasses the cloverleaf.

Plot twist: you drive one constantly. The big new junction where I-40 meets I-42 and the 540 toll road by Garner opened as a turbine in 2024. That easy sweeping ramp feeling on the way to Clayton? That is the whirlpool doing its job. Charlotte got North Carolina's first at I-85 and I-485 back in 2015.

The Stack

the final form · built by adults
WEAVE PAIN ▯▯▯▯▯ TRUCK SPEED ▮▮▮▮▮ STRESS ▮▯▯▯▯

Every single left turn gets its own private flyover bridge, sweeping and fast, stacked in layers. No loops. No lights. No sharing. The Dallas High Five is five levels tall and moves a half million vehicles a day like it is nothing. The only downsides are that it costs a fortune and blocks out the sun, and honestly, worth it.

This is what the cloverleaf wants to be when it grows up. Next time an on-ramp lifts you three stories into a smooth banked left with nobody merging at you, that is the stack, and that is the standard she should hold every other interchange to.

Sidebar

About The Roundabout Thing

She's right, at the right scale

On surface streets the taste is validated: a roundabout has a fraction of the conflict points of a signal, and every possible crash is a glancing angle instead of a T-bone. Statistically one of the safest things you can pour out of a concrete truck. So yes, the roundabout love is real and correct.

The asterisk: feed a roundabout freeway volume and it locks up solid, so it cannot replace the big interchanges above. The compromise does exist though, and it is called the dogbone: two teardrop roundabouts hugging the freeway bridge. Carmel, Indiana went all in and built dozens, they run beautifully at suburban volume, and the town is now a roadgeek pilgrimage site.
