What & Why

Dedicated prescription polarized sunglasses for fishing and outdoor use. The current setup of clear Rx aviators with no sun protection is a dead end for someone who fishes 2-3 times a week. Polarized lenses cut horizontal glare off water surfaces, which is the whole game for sight-fishing: you stop seeing reflections of the sky and start seeing the fish, the structure, and your line twitching.

The alternative, clip-on polarized lenses that attach to the current Ottoto Magnus frame, was considered and rejected. Stacking two lens surfaces creates ghost reflections and degraded optical clarity. For a guy fishing 8-hour days on Harris Lake, the contrast difference between clip-ons and dedicated polarized is real and costly. This also avoids the weight of a clip-on pressing on the nose bridge all day.

Why Amber Tint, Not Gray or Mirror

Amber/copper boosts contrast on water. It enhances the visibility of structure, weed lines, drop-offs, and fish silhouettes under the surface. This is the fishing-specific choice. Costa 580G Sunrise Silver Mirror, Maui Jim HCL Bronze, and every other serious fishing lens leans amber for a reason.

Gray is good for driving because it preserves color accuracy and dims everything evenly. On water, it just dims the scene without adding contrast. Worse for spotting fish.

Mirror coatings look flashy but work against the polarized film. They add internal bounce-back reflections and make the lens darker overall, which reduces visibility on shaded shorelines at dawn and dusk when fish actually bite. Pure cosmetic upgrade, not functional for fishing.
