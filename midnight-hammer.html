Situation Overview

What Happened on June 21-22, 2025

On the night of June 21, 2025, seven B-2 Spirit stealth bombers launched from Whiteman Air Force Base in Missouri on an 18-hour, one-way flight to Iran. Their mission: deliver 14 GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrator bunker-buster bombs onto Iran's most critical nuclear enrichment facilities. Six additional B-2s flew to Guam as decoys. It was the largest B-2 strike in U.S. history, the first combat use of the MOP, and the longest B-2 mission since 2001.

The operation, codenamed Midnight Hammer, involved over 125 aircraft, 4,000 personnel, and more than 75 precision-guided weapons. A U.S. submarine simultaneously launched 24+ Tomahawk cruise missiles at Isfahan. F-35 and F-22 fighters entered Iranian airspace to suppress air defenses. Iran's fighters never launched. Their SAMs never saw the strike package.

Duna's Front Row Seat

Why B-2s keep flying over Hume, Missouri

Whiteman AFB in Knob Noster, MO is the ONLY operational home of the entire B-2 fleet on the planet. All 20 B-2 Spirits live there. Hume is ~50 miles away. Every B-2 you see is either training, proficiency hours, or heading to/from an actual mission.

On June 21, 2025, 13 B-2s launched from Whiteman just after midnight. They left unnoticed. When seven returned the next afternoon, news crews were staged in Knob Noster. Anyone within 50-100 miles would have seen the largest B-2 deployment in history.

B-2s are not spy planes. They're heavy strategic bombers. "Stealth" means enemy radar can't detect them, not that they're sneaking around domestically. If the government wanted to watch someone, they'd use a satellite or a tiny drone, not a $2 billion bomber doing laps.

When you see increased B-2 activity over Hume, it means routine training or heightened readiness for a potential operation. In February 2026, it's likely the latter.
