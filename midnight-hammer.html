Situation Overview

What Happened on June 21-22, 2025

On the night of June 21, 2025, seven B-2 Spirit stealth bombers launched from Whiteman Air Force Base in Missouri on an 18-hour, one-way flight to Iran. Their mission: deliver 14 GBU-57 Massive Ordnance Penetrator (MOP) bunker-buster bombs onto Iran's most critical nuclear enrichment facilities. Six additional B-2s flew to Guam as decoys. It was the largest B-2 strike in U.S. history, the first combat use of the MOP, and the longest B-2 mission since 2001.

The operation, codenamed Midnight Hammer, involved over 125 aircraft, 4,000 personnel, and more than 75 precision-guided weapons. A U.S. submarine simultaneously launched 24+ Tomahawk cruise missiles at the Isfahan nuclear facility. F-35 and F-22 fighters entered Iranian airspace to suppress air defenses. Iran's fighters never launched. Their SAMs never saw the strike package.

Operation Midnight Hammer — Key Locations

The B-2s launched from central Missouri, flew east across the Atlantic and Mediterranean, refueling 6-7 times mid-air, before entering Iranian airspace. Three nuclear facilities were struck simultaneously between 6:40 PM and 7:05 PM ET (2:10-2:35 AM Iran time).

B-2 Spirit

The only aircraft on Earth that can carry the MOP.

Flying wing stealth bomber. 172 ft wingspan. Crew of 2. Range: 6,900 miles (11,100 km). Two internal bomb bays. Near-invisible to radar due to shape and radar-absorbent coating.

Only 20 operational B-2s exist. All based at Whiteman AFB, Missouri. Cost: ~$2 billion each.

Mission Profile

30+ hour round trip from Missouri to Iran and back.

Route: Whiteman → Atlantic → Mediterranean → Middle East → Iran → reverse. 6-7 mid-air refuelings. Strict crew rest/diet protocols. Pilots in cockpit for the entire mission with no autopilot for combat phases.

Decoy flight: 6 B-2s flew west to Guam to confuse OSINT trackers and foreign intelligence.

Local Perspective

Duna's Front Row Seat

Why B-2s keep flying over Hume, Missouri

Whiteman Air Force Base in Knob Noster, MO is the ONLY operational home of the entire B-2 fleet on the planet. All 20 B-2 Spirits live there. Hume is roughly 50 miles away. Every B-2 you see flying is either training, doing proficiency hours, or heading to/from an actual mission.

On the night of June 21, 2025, 13 B-2s launched from Whiteman just after midnight local time. They left unnoticed. When seven of them returned the next afternoon, local news crews were staged in Knob Noster. Anyone living within 50-100 miles of Whiteman would have potentially seen or heard the largest B-2 deployment in history rolling out overhead.

The B-2s are not spy planes. They're heavy strategic bombers. The "stealth" means enemy radar can't detect them effectively. It has nothing to do with domestic surveillance. If the government wanted to watch someone, they'd use a satellite or a small drone you'd never notice, not a $2 billion bomber doing laps. Actual reconnaissance aircraft (U-2, RQ-4 Global Hawk, RC-135) fly at 60,000-70,000+ feet where you'd never see them.

When you see increased B-2 activity over Hume, it means one of two things: routine training flights (pilots need flight hours), or heightened readiness for a potential operation. Right now in February 2026, it's likely the latter.

That sounds like a lot until you remember the annual defense budget is $886 billion. A training flight is literally 0.00001% of the budget. The Pentagon does not care about jet fuel costs.

"Can't they just use simulators?"

There are only about 14 pilots in the entire world qualified to fly B-2 combat missions. The colonel who led Operation Midnight Hammer said he selected crews based on who had the most real-flight experience, "Red Flag after Red Flag, major exercise after major exercise." You cannot simulate a 30-hour mission with 7 mid-air refuelings where you're flying 10 feet below a tanker at 400+ mph for 30 minutes at a time. Simulators are used extensively, but nothing replaces stick time in the actual aircraft for the specific physical and mental demands of these missions.

And here's the bigger picture: the United States is the world's reserve currency. The Federal Reserve literally creates money. The defense budget is funded by Treasury bonds that the entire world buys because the U.S. dollar is the backbone of global trade. When it comes to maintaining the ability to drop a 30,000-pound bomb through 300 feet of mountain on the other side of the planet, the budget is "whatever it takes."

Trump just ordered 25 more B-2s (upgraded variants). Each one costs approximately $2 billion. That's $50 billion in new bombers alone. A few thousand gallons of jet fuel per training flight is not something anyone in the Pentagon is losing sleep over.
