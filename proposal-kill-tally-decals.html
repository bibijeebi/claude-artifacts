Proposal #011

Kill Tally Decals

Fighter-pilot-style kill marks on the Tesla. Leaping deer silhouettes, one per confirmed harvest, stacked clean on the rear quarter panel. Built to grow into a multi-species trophy tally.

Total Cost $15–$45
Difficulty 1/5
Time Ship + 20 min
Category Automotive
Status Proposed

The Concept

WWII fighter pilots painted small silhouettes on the fuselage of their aircraft to mark confirmed enemy kills. Each silhouette was small, uniform, and stacked in a clean row just below the cockpit. Aces wore a trail of them like a resume. This is the exact same idea applied to the Tesla: each confirmed big game harvest gets a small leaping deer silhouette on the rear quarter panel. Low-key enough that casuals just see a sticker. Hunters immediately know what they're looking at.

The leaping deer silhouette deliberately evokes the John Deere logo because that's the iconic profile everybody associates with a deer in motion. For trademark reasons, the actual order will use a generic leaping deer SVG (Freepik, Vecteezy, or SVGRepo) that looks 95% the same without being the trademarked logo. Most big print shops flag real John Deere artwork and reject the order. Etsy sellers are less picky, but the generic silhouette is cleaner, scales better, and nobody can ever send a cease and desist.

Design Spec

Uniform, small, monochrome. Fighter-ace authenticity depends on consistency: every decal is the same size, same style, same color, same spacing. The moment one is different, it reads as a random sticker collection instead of a tally.

Why 2 inches: Big enough to be recognizable from 5-10 feet away. Small enough that 10-15 decals still fit in a clean horizontal row under the rear quarter window without crowding. Matches the scale of real WWII kill marks on fighter aircraft.

Layout on the Tesla

Classic ace placement: rear driver-side quarter panel, just ahead of the rear wheel well, below the beltline crease. This is the cleanest flat body panel on a Model Y and the area that gets the most drive-by visibility. A horizontal row of 10 decals at 2" wide + 0.3" gap is 22.7 inches long, which fits comfortably on the quarter panel.

Placement rules: Stay on a flat panel (curves distort the decals). Keep 4 inches of clear space around the group so it reads as intentional. Align the bottom edges of every decal to the same horizontal line. Use painter's tape + a small laser level or a phone level app.

Multi-Species Expansion

Fighter pilots had different silhouettes for different aircraft types shot down (Messerschmitt, Zero, Stuka, etc.). Same idea works for different game species. Build a "species legend" where each silhouette corresponds to a game category. Over years, the side of the Tesla becomes a visual trophy board.

Install (Barney Style)

Step 1 Wash the rear quarter panel with dish soap. Rinse. Dry with microfiber.
Step 2 Wipe install area with 70% isopropyl alcohol. Let dry 60 seconds.
Step 3 Pick a horizontal reference line (just below the beltline crease is ideal). Run a strip of blue painter's tape along that line as your bottom-edge alignment guide.
Step 4 Mark 2.3" increments along the tape with a pencil dot or small tape tick. This sets the spacing for all 10 decals in a row (2.0" decal + 0.3" gap).
Step 5 Peel the white paper backing off the first decal. The vinyl stays on the clear transfer tape. Align the decal's bottom edge with the tape line and the left edge with your first tick mark.
Step 6 Squeegee with a credit card wrapped in microfiber (Tesla paint is soft, no bare plastic). Center outward, firm pressure.
Step 7 Peel the transfer tape off slowly at a 180-degree angle (fold flat against itself, not up). The vinyl stays on the car.
Step 8 Repeat for decals 2-10. Pull the painter's tape guide off last. No car wash for 72 hours. No pressure wash for a week.

Pro Tips & Reality Checks

Install between 65-85°F with no direct sun on the panel. Cold vinyl tears, hot vinyl melts into the transfer tape. Garage install is safest.

Tesla paint is notoriously soft. Use a felt-edge or microfiber-wrapped squeegee, never raw plastic. A bare credit card edge will leave micro-scratches visible in certain light.

Order 2-3 extra decals in the batch. Install mistakes happen, and you'll want spares to match exactly when adding future kills (batch dye lots on black vinyl are basically identical but not guaranteed years apart).

Reta may make the next season or two leaner body-wise but won't slow the tally. Plan to outgrow 10 slots. Two rows of 10 handles 20 kills, which is realistic over a decade of NC hunting.

If the Tesla ever gets sold, decals come off clean with a heat gun on low (200°F, 30 seconds per decal) and a plastic razor. Adhesive residue wipes off with Goo Gone. The car will show no marks if the paint is in good shape.

Magnetic option exists if leasing becomes a concern later, but adhesive vinyl looks better and sits flatter. Magnetic on a Tesla is also a non-starter since the body panels aren't ferrous steel.

Decision for Bekah

This is a $15-25 Etsy order and a 20-minute Saturday install. The tally starts small and only grows with real harvests, so it's inherently earned rather than showy. Fighter-pilot style keeps it tasteful instead of redneck-decal-wall. The leaping deer silhouette is subtle enough that it reads as a design choice to non-hunters and as a badge to anyone in the know.

Possible BIAR pushback: "It's a Tesla, not a hunting truck." Valid concern. Counter: placement is small (22 inches total), on the driver side only, below the beltline, and removable cleanly with a heat gun. If it doesn't land, it comes off in 10 minutes with zero paint damage. Proposing a 30-day trial period. BIAR applies.
